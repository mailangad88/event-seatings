**Quick answer**

Chicago's earliest sunset of the year comes in early December at about 4:19 p.m., and the winter solstice sunset is about 4:21 p.m. Sunsets do not return to after 5 p.m. until late January. Any event that starts after 3 p.m. in December will be in the dark for most of its length, so plan entrance, path, ceremony and photo lighting as if it were a night event.

</div>

A December wedding or holiday party in the Chicago area has a different lighting problem from a June one. In June, light is a decoration. In December, light is the whole atmosphere, and it begins before guests have finished their coat check. This guide lists the sunset milestones, shows how daylight shapes a timeline and explains what to light.

## Sunset milestones

These figures come from NBC Chicago's seasonal sunset coverage and are rounded. Exact times shift by a minute or two from year to year and from suburb to suburb, so check a sunrise and sunset table for your date and location.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Milestone | Approximate sunset | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Early August | Last sunsets after 8 p.m. | Long summer evenings end |
| Mid-September | First sunset before 7 p.m. | Evening events start in dusk |
| Early December (about Dec. 7) | About 4:19 p.m. | Earliest sunset of the year |
| Winter solstice (Dec. 21) | About 4:21 p.m. | Shortest day, roughly 9 hours of daylight |
| Late January (about Jan. 28) | After 5 p.m. again | Evenings begin to open up |

## What the short days change

**1. Guests arrive in the dark.** A 5 p.m. ceremony is after sunset in December. Light the curb, the door, the walkway and the coat check.

**2. Photos need a plan.** If portraits must be outdoors, shoot them before sunset, which can mean a first look at 2 or 3 p.m. See [lighting for night event photos](/blog/lighting-for-night-event-photos).

**3. Cold meets dark.** Lantern-lit paths feel festive, but ice makes them risky. Salt or sand the paths and light the steps.

**4. Heat is part of the lighting.** Guests at outdoor ceremonies in early dusk need warmth; see [tent heater vs patio heater](/blog/tent-heater-vs-patio-heater).

**5. Battery life drops.** Cold weather shortens the life of many batteries. Bring spare batteries and keep them warm in a pocket or a bag until needed. Read [battery-powered event lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting).

## A December timeline

Here is a sample schedule for a Saturday in early December with sunset near 4:20 p.m.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>1:30 p.m.</strong> Portraits outside while there is daylight.</li>
<li><strong>3:30 p.m.</strong> Lights on at the entry and path, 50 minutes before sunset.</li>
<li><strong>4:00 p.m.</strong> Ceremony begins, with candlelight or warm lighting.</li>
<li><strong>5:00 p.m.</strong> Cocktail hour with uplights and lanterns.</li>
<li><strong>6:00 p.m.</strong> Dinner, with pin spots or candles on tables.</li>
</ol>

Adjust it for your date and venue. Ask your photographer for their preferred schedule.

## What to light

- **Arrival route.** Street, drop-off, steps and door. Use lanterns, string lights or battery stakes. See [lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events).
- **Ceremony.** Warm light on the officiant and couple. Candles are beautiful if allowed; see [candles and fire code](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules).
- **Tables.** Soft light on faces and a glow on centerpieces; see [pin spot lighting](/blog/pin-spot-lighting-centerpieces).
- **Room walls.** Uplights in warm colors. Check [how many uplights](/blog/how-many-uplights-for-a-wedding).
- **Exit.** Guests leave in the dark and the cold. Light the parking and the valet area.

## Color for winter

Winter palettes lean toward warm white, deep green, gold and burgundy. Use 2200K to 2700K bulbs for a cozy look; see [color temperature](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events).

## Power in cold weather

Cold affects power in several ways. Generators need winter-ready fuel and batteries drain faster. Outdoor outlets may be frozen, covered in snow or not working. Test power well ahead. Use GFCI protection and keep connections out of snow and slush; see [extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events). If you need a generator, plan its placement from [quiet generator placement](/blog/quiet-generator-placement-events).

## Chicago notes

The first lake-effect snow and a stiff wind can happen in November. Winter events at lakefront or suburban venues should plan for parking lot ice, early darkness and delivery windows for rentals. Our [winter wedding seating guide](/blog/winter-wedding-seating) covers the seating side.

## Frequently asked questions

### What time is sunset in Chicago in December?

About 4:19 to 4:21 p.m. in early to mid December. The earliest sunset of the year comes around the first week of December.

### When do Chicago sunsets get later again?

The sunset stays near 4:20 p.m. through late December and returns to after 5 p.m. in late January.

### How do I light a winter wedding?

Use warm lighting for the entrance, ceremony, tables and exit. Plan outdoor portraits early.

### When should lights turn on for a winter event?

About 45 to 60 minutes before sunset, so the transition feels natural.

### Do batteries work in cold weather?

They work but drain faster. Bring spares and keep them warm.

### Should I use candles at a winter event?

If the venue allows them, yes. Otherwise use LED candles and lanterns.

### Is outdoor lighting safe in snow?

Yes, with outdoor-rated equipment, GFCI protection and connections kept off the ground.
