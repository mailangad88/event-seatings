**Quick answer**

To plan a party on a budget, set a total first, then split it across food, drinks, space, rentals, decor and a cushion. Save most by trimming the guest list, picking an off-peak day and time, choosing a simpler menu, using your own space and renting only what you cannot borrow. Get at least two quotes for every paid item.

</div>

The fear with a tight budget is that the party will feel cheap. In practice, guests rarely remember the napkins. They remember whether there was enough good food, whether they had a place to sit and whether the host was relaxed. A budget party works when you spend on those things first and cut everything else.

## Start with a number, not a wish list

Write down the total you are comfortable spending, then write down the number of guests. Divide to get a per-guest number. That number tells you what is possible. If it feels too low, you have three levers: fewer guests, a simpler menu or a different space. Do not try to shave a little off everything. Pick one big change.

## A common budget split

Many planners start with a split like the one below, then adjust. Food and drink go first, followed by space and rentals, then decor, entertainment and a cushion. Use the [event budget spreadsheet](/downloads/event-budget-spreadsheet) to track real quotes against the plan.

## The biggest savers, in order

**1. Trim the guest list.** Every guest adds food, drinks, a chair, a plate and a favor. Cutting ten guests from a party of 60 can save more than any other change.

**2. Choose an off-peak date and time.** Friday nights and Saturday evenings in prime season cost the most. A Sunday afternoon or a weekday evening often costs less for a venue or caterer. Brunch costs less than dinner.

**3. Use a free or low-cost space.** Your home, a backyard, a park district pavilion or a community room often costs less than a banquet hall. Ask about nonprofit or resident discounts. See [suburban park district pavilion rentals](/blog/suburban-park-district-pavilion-rentals).

**4. Simplify the menu.** One or two mains, three sides, a dessert and simple drinks go a long way. A potluck, taco bar, pizza or grill is fine for most parties. Drop-off catering costs less than full service. See [drop-off catering equipment checklist](/blog/drop-off-catering-equipment-checklist).

**5. Skip alcohol, or simplify it.** Beer, wine and one signature drink cost less than a full bar. See [beer and wine only bar setup](/blog/beer-and-wine-only-bar-setup) and [dry hire vs full service bar](/blog/dry-hire-vs-full-service-bar).

## Where to spend and where to save

<div class="table-wrap">

| Category | Spend on | Save on |
|---|---|---|
| Food | Enough quantity and a good main | Fancy plating, too many options |
| Drinks | Water, one signature drink | Full open bar |
| Seating | A seat for most guests | Matching chairs for every table |
| Decor | Lighting and flowers on the table | Elaborate backdrops |
| Entertainment | A good playlist or one activity | Hiring several vendors |
| Rentals | Tables, chairs, shade, linens | Specialty items you will use once |

</div>

This is a rule of thumb, not a quote. Your priorities matter more than any table.

## Rent smart, not less

Rentals often save money when compared with buying, especially for tables, chairs and linens. They also reduce your cleanup. The key is to rent only what you need. Count seats for the share of guests who sit at once, and pick basic styles over premium ones. See [how to save money on event rentals](/blog/how-to-save-money-on-event-rentals), [rent vs buy event chairs and tables](/blog/rent-vs-buy-event-chairs-and-tables) and [event rental budget by guest count](/blog/event-rental-budget-by-guest-count). Ask about delivery fees, setup fees and damage waivers so there are no surprises. See [event rental delivery fees explained](/blog/event-rental-delivery-fees-explained).

## DIY with limits

DIY works for decor, signs, centerpieces and playlists. It does not work for food safety, big-batch cooking for 50 or more, or last-minute setup. Value your time. A weekend of crafting costs less in dollars but more in stress. Borrow what you can: serving platters, coolers, speakers and string lights.

## Plan the details early

The most common budget busters are last-minute orders, rush fees and extra headcount. Order rentals and food at least two to three weeks ahead. Confirm the final guest count and stick to it. See [final headcount deadline for event rentals](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals).

## Track as you go

Update the spreadsheet every time you spend. Compare it with the plan weekly. If one line goes over, trim another. At the end, record what you actually spent so the next party is easier.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area prices vary a lot by neighborhood, season and day of the week. Downtown and North Shore venues often cost more than south suburbs and outer collar counties, and May through October is peak season. Winter and early spring are often more affordable. See [peak season event rentals in Chicago](/blog/peak-season-event-rentals-chicago) and [January and February event planning](/blog/january-february-event-planning-chicago). Park district pavilions are often a bargain for residents, but they book early and have rules, so check with your village or park district. Consider parking and transit, which can add cost for guests.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a party on a tight budget?

Set a total, divide by guests, and pick one big saver such as fewer guests, an off-peak date or a simpler menu. Get quotes for every paid item.

### What is the biggest way to save on a party?

Trimming the guest list. Each guest adds food, drink, a seat and a plate.

### How much should I budget per person for a party?

It varies by venue and menu. Many planners work backward from a total and divide by guests. Get quotes for your area.

### Is it cheaper to rent or buy party supplies?

Renting is usually cheaper for items you use once, such as tables, chairs and linens. Buying makes sense for items you will reuse many times.

### What day is cheapest for a party?

Weekday evenings, Sundays and afternoons are often less expensive than Saturday nights in peak season. Ask venues for off-peak rates.

### How can I save money on food?

Choose a simpler menu, a potluck, a grill or drop-off catering. Skip a full open bar.

### What should I never cut from a party budget?

Enough food and seating, safety items such as shade and rain cover, and a small cushion for surprises.
