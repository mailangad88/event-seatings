**Quick answer**

A bar or bat mitzvah party needs two seating zones: round tables of 8 to 10 for adults and family, and a separate teen area close to the dance floor. Plan a dance floor of about 20 x 20 feet for 150 guests, a stage or DJ riser, a candle lighting or tribute table, a guest book table and a head table or family table for the honoree. The ceremony is usually at a synagogue, so rentals are mostly for the party space.

</div>

A bar or bat mitzvah marks a young person's coming of age in Jewish tradition, and the celebration afterward varies from a kiddush lunch to a full evening party. In Chicago and the suburbs, many families hold the party at a banquet hall, hotel, country club or the synagogue's social hall. The planning challenge is balancing two audiences: adults who want to talk and eat, and teens who want to dance. A good layout keeps both happy without anyone feeling trapped in the middle.

## Seat by age group

Most parties include a mix of the honoree's friends, family friends and relatives from several generations. Seat them so everyone has a place:

- **Family tables near the front.** Parents, grandparents, aunts, uncles and godparent-like friends sit close to the honoree's table or head table.
- **Adult friends and neighbors** at rounds of 8 to 10, away from the speakers.
- **Teen tables** close to the dance floor, with the honoree's friends seated together. Many parties set teen tables near the DJ.
- **A kids table** for younger siblings and cousins, close to parents.

The ratio of teens to adults depends on the family. Count each group before you size the tables, and ask your DJ how much space the stage and equipment need. Our [dance floor size guide](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count) gives more detail.

## Special tables and moments

Programs vary, but many parties include a few special elements that need table space:

- **Candle lighting.** A table with candles or a stand for lighting ceremony. Check venue rules on open flame. See [candles at venues](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules).
- **Hora or circle dances.** The honoree is often lifted on a chair. Plan two sturdy chairs and a larger open floor, as in our [Jewish wedding seating guide](/blog/jewish-wedding-seating-chicago).
- **Photo or video tribute display.** A table or easel area. See [signage and easels](/blog/signage-and-easels-for-events).
- **Guest book or signature item.** Many families use a decorated item for guests to sign. See guest book table setup.
- **Candy or dessert station.** A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth.

## Chairs for the honoree and the room

For the honoree, a statement chair adds fun to photos. A [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) ($18 to $25 estimated) in the party color or a [throne pair](/chairs/royal-throne) ($75 to $150 per pair) works. For guests, [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) ($10 to $14) keep the room open and modern, and [Gold Infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity) ($9 to $13) give a classic ballroom look. Mix them with the venue's standard chairs and use the better chairs where photos are taken.

## Chicago notes

- Many synagogues and Chicago-area venues have their own rules about food, music and decor on Shabbat. Check with your synagogue and venue before booking.
- Kosher catering rules may affect which tableware you can use. Ask your caterer first.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many guests attend a bar or bat mitzvah?

Guest counts vary from under 50 to over 250. Many families invite relatives, friends from school and family friends. Plan chairs and tables from your confirmed list.

### Should teens sit with adults?

Usually not. Most parties seat teens together near the dance floor, with a few parents or chaperones at nearby tables. Ask the honoree who they want to sit with.

### How big should the dance floor be?

About 4 to 5 square feet per dancing guest. With teens dancing most of the night, plan on the larger side, such as 20 x 20 feet for 150 guests.

### Do I need a head table?

Not always. Many families use a family table for the honoree, parents and grandparents instead of a long head table. Choose what feels comfortable.

### What if the party is at a synagogue hall?

Ask the synagogue what furniture is included, whether outside rentals are allowed and when you can set up. Many halls have their own tables and chairs.

### Can I rent tables for a candy station?

Yes. A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth works. Use our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).
