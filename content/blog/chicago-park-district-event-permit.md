**Quick answer**

The Chicago Park District requires a Special Event Permit for any outdoor gathering of 50 or more people, and for smaller groups that add a listed feature such as alcohol, a tent or canopy, amplified sound, a generator, an inflatable or a ceremony. The application fee is $45, plus rental fees for the space. Groups of 49 or fewer with none of those features do not need a permit.

</div>

A birthday picnic at a Chicago park looks simple until someone asks about the speaker, the canopy or the cooler of beer. Each of those flips a casual hangout into a permitted event. This guide explains how the Park District decides, what the permit covers, and how to line up tables, chairs and shade so your rental order matches what the permit allows.

## The two tests: headcount and features

The Park District's own permit page uses two tests. If either one is true, you need a Special Event Permit.

1. **Headcount.** Gatherings of **50 or more people** need a permit.
2. **Features.** Even a group of 49 or fewer needs one if the event includes any listed feature.

## Features that trigger a permit

According to the Park District, a permit is required when your event includes:

- Alcohol
- Tents or canopies (the page's own example is a 10 x 10 pop-up)
- Inflatables
- Amplified sound, including a DJ speaker
- Generators
- Ceremonies
- Food, merchandise or services for sale
- Stages, drones, animals or exercise classes
- Guaranteed use of a space (a reserved spot, not first come)

That last item matters for rentals. If you want a specific lawn or shelter held for you and a delivery truck able to unload at it, the permit is what guarantees it. Without one, another family can claim the same spot.

## Parks managed separately

A few Chicago sites do not go through the standard Park District application. The Park District lists these as independently managed and asks you to call them directly: the Alfred Caldwell Lily Pool, Lincoln Park Zoo, Maggie Daley Park and the Cancer Survivors Garden, Millennium Park and Olive Park. Do not assume the standard $45 process covers them.

## What it costs

The Park District states the permit price as a **$45 application fee plus rental fees**. The rental fee depends on the site and event type, and the district lists it on its Permits & Rentals 101 page. We do not estimate it here. Check the current fee for your site before you set a budget.

## Park Permit vs. City Permit

A Park District permit covers the park. It does not cover closing a city street, and it does not replace a city special event permit when your event also meets that threshold. The City's Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events (DCASE) says events on park property, including most green space and the lakefront trail, need a separate Park District permit with separate fees. Our guide to [Chicago special event permits](/blog/chicago-special-event-permit-guide) shows when both apply.

## How to line up your rentals with the permit

Your permit is a list of what will be on the grass. Your rental order should match it line for line.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Pick the site and date.</strong> Confirm whether it is a standard Park District site or an independently managed one.</li>
<li><strong>List every feature.</strong> Tents, speakers, generators, bar, food service and any ceremony.</li>
<li><strong>Apply and pay the $45 fee.</strong> Use the district's online application.</li>
<li><strong>Build the rental list.</strong> Chairs, tables, linens, shade, only after the features are approved.</li>
<li><strong>Plan delivery.</strong> Ask the Park District where a vehicle can enter and how long it can stay.</li>
</ol>

Add the rental items to your application if the form asks. A tent you did not list is the most common surprise at a park. For a rough tent size, see [what size tent you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

## Choosing furniture for park grass

Chicago park lawns are uneven and often soft after rain. Narrow chair legs sink, so garden-friendly designs help. The [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is the practical pick for a big gathering, and the [rattan garden chair](/chairs/rattan-garden) suits a smaller, styled picnic. Round tables are easier to level on grass than long banquet tables, and lighter linens that you tie down handle the wind that sweeps most open Chicago parks.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Guessing the headcount low.** If your guest list is near 50, count everyone you invited and apply for the permit.
- **Treating a pop-up as too small.** The district's page names a 10 x 10 pop-up as needing a permit.
- **Waiting for good weather to decide.** Permits are tied to dates. Apply first, then watch the forecast.
- **Skipping the fieldhouse option.** For groups under 50 without features, you can call the park fieldhouse about indoor rooms.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit for a birthday party in a Chicago park?

You need one if 50 or more people attend, or if you add alcohol, a tent or canopy, a speaker, a generator, an inflatable or a ceremony. A group of 49 or fewer with none of those features does not need a permit. When in doubt, call the park fieldhouse before you invite people.

### How much does a Chicago Park District permit cost?

The application fee is $45, plus rental fees for the space. The rental portion depends on the site and the type of event, so check the Park District's current pricing page for your location. Independently managed sites set their own terms.

### Do I need a permit to bring a pop-up tent to a Chicago park?

Yes. The Park District lists tents and canopies as permit features and gives a 10 x 10 pop-up as its example. Bringing one without a permit can get it taken down.

### Can I serve alcohol at a Chicago park event?

Only with a Special Event Permit, since alcohol is one of the listed features. The permit application asks about it, and city rules for liquor licenses apply to larger public events. Ask the Park District how it applies to your plan.

### Does the Park District permit cover Millennium Park or Maggie Daley Park?

No. Millennium Park, Maggie Daley Park, Lincoln Park Zoo, the Lily Pool and Olive Park are managed independently, and the Park District asks you to contact each directly.

### Can a rental company deliver to the park?

Often yes, but the Park District controls vehicle access. Ask where trucks may enter, how long they can stay and where to stage items. Give your rental company that answer before you confirm a delivery window.
