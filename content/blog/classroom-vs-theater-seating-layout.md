**Quick answer**

Theater seating (rows of chairs, no tables) takes 8 to 10 square feet per person and holds the most people. Classroom seating (rows of tables with chairs) takes 14 to 18 square feet per person and gives each person a writing surface. In the same 30 x 40 foot room, theater seats about 120 and classroom about 70.

</div>

If you are planning a workshop, a board meeting, a training session or a ceremony in a Chicago-area meeting room, church hall or conference space, the first decision is the room style. The same room can seat almost twice as many people in theater style as in classroom style, and neither is wrong. It depends on what your attendees need to do: listen, write, eat or move.

## Side by side

<div class="table-wrap">

| Factor | Theater | Classroom |
|---|---|---|
| Square feet per person | 8 to 10 | 14 to 18 |
| Capacity of a 30 x 40 ft room (1,200 sq ft) | About 120 to 150 | About 65 to 85 |
| Writing surface | None | Yes |
| Row spacing | 32 to 36 in back to back | 5 to 6 ft front to back |
| Best for | Ceremonies, talks, performances | Training, workshops, exams |
| Table rentals | None | 6 ft x 18 in (or 24 in) training tables |
| Chair count | One per person | One per person |

</div>

These are rules of thumb. Fire and building codes set legal capacities, so confirm the number with the venue.

## Row spacing and aisles

For theater seating, set rows 32 to 36 inches back to back, with chairs linked or aligned. Leave a center aisle of 5 to 6 feet and side aisles of 3 to 4 feet. No seat should be more than a few seats from an aisle: a block of 10 to 14 chairs between aisles is typical. For a ceremony, see [wedding ceremony floor plan and aisle width](/blog/wedding-ceremony-floor-plan-and-aisle-width).

For classroom seating, tables are usually 6 feet long and 18 or 24 inches deep, with two or three chairs per table. Leave 5 to 6 feet between the front edge of one row of tables and the back of the next row's chairs, so people can sit and stand. Leave aisles of 4 feet on each side.

## Screens, stages and sightlines

A projector screen should be roughly one-sixth of the distance to the back row in height, a standard rule of thumb for presentations, so a 60-foot-deep room wants a tall screen. The first row should sit at least 1.5 to 2 times the screen height away. Place the screen at the narrow end of the room to keep the audience closer. See [projector and screen size for events](/blog/projector-and-screen-size-for-events) and [sound system for speeches](/blog/sound-system-for-speeches).

## Accessibility

Reserve wheelchair spaces in both styles, spread across rows with companion seats next to each, and keep a 36-inch minimum path to them. In classroom style, leave a table without a chair in the first or second row. See [ADA accessible event seating and pathways](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways). Reserved rows for VIPs are covered in [reserved seating and signage layout](/blog/reserved-seating-and-signage-layout).

## Chicago-area meeting rooms

Hotels, libraries and park district rooms around Chicago often include tables and stackable chairs. Ask what is included, how many are available and how long the room takes to turn. If your event needs more chairs, rent a matching set rather than mixing; stackable styles like the [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) or the [shell chair](/chairs/shell) work well for conferences. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between theater and classroom seating?

Theater seating is rows of chairs with no tables. Classroom seating adds a table in front of each row for writing.

### Which layout fits more people?

Theater. It uses 8 to 10 square feet per person, versus 14 to 18 for classroom.

### How far apart should theater rows be?

32 to 36 inches back to back. Less than 30 inches makes it hard to pass.

### How wide should aisles be?

A center aisle of 5 to 6 feet and side aisles of 3 to 4 feet.

### How many chairs per row in theater seating?

Up to 10 to 14 between aisles. Longer rows trap guests in the middle.

### What size table for classroom seating?

Six-foot by 18-inch or 24-inch training tables, with two or three chairs each.
