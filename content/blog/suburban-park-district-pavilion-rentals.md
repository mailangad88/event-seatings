**Quick answer**

A park pavilion is a roof, a slab and some picnic tables. Everything else, including chairs, tables if you need more, linens, power, shade walls and a rain plan, is usually yours to arrange. Before you reserve, ask the park district for capacity, power, alcohol and tent rules, setup time and the weather policy, then plan tables and chairs to match the posted capacity.

</div>

Park pavilions are the most affordable event spaces in the Chicago suburbs, and the most misunderstood. People see a covered structure and assume it is a venue. It is closer to an outdoor room with no furniture. This guide shows how to size the space, what to ask your park district and what to rent.

## What a pavilion usually gives you

Each park district and village sets its own rules, so treat this as a list of questions rather than a promise. A pavilion typically has a roof, a concrete or paved floor, some picnic tables, grills nearby and restrooms in the park. It may or may not have power, lighting, water or a kitchen. Reservation windows, fees and rules vary. In Chicago itself, the Park District's page says outdoor gatherings of 50 or more people, or any event with a tent, alcohol, amplified sound or inflatables, need a Special Event Permit, and that review takes at least 14 days. Suburban park districts have their own forms and timelines.

As a rule of thumb, a seated meal needs about 10 to 12 square feet per guest. A 40 by 24 foot pavilion has 960 square feet, which suits roughly 48 to 60 seated guests at long tables if you keep food service on the edge. Check the posted capacity first; it is the real limit. ## What to rent for a pavilion

- **Tables**: 8-foot banquet tables usually fit better under a roof than rounds. See [picnic tables for casual events](/blog/picnic-tables-for-casual-events) if you want to use the park's tables.
- **Chairs**: backs make a big difference at a three-hour party. The [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) (about $5 to $7) is a practical choice; the [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) (about $8 to $11) suits a nicer birthday or shower.
- **Linens**: cloths hold down with clips. Paper rolls are common for casual events; for cloth sizes, use our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).
- **Shade walls or sidewalls**: ask the park whether you can add them.
- **Lights**: a pavilion ceiling does not light a table; see [backyard party lighting ideas](/blog/backyard-party-lighting-ideas).
- **Trash**: add extra bins. See [trash, recycling and composting stations](/blog/trash-recycling-composting-stations-for-events).

## Setup and access

Many parks limit when you can access the pavilion and whether vehicles can drive on turf. Ask where a delivery truck can park and how far it is from the truck to the slab. Rental crews carry tables and chairs by hand, and a long walk adds time. If your reservation starts at 10 am for a noon party, there may be little room to set up, so ask whether you can book earlier or add an hour.

## Weather and backup

A roof does not make a pavilion weatherproof. Wind-driven rain, sun angle and heat all get in. Ask whether the park refunds for weather. In some jurisdictions, fees are nonrefundable even for rain; for example, DuPage County Forest Preserves state that. Plan for tarps, clips for linens, and a backup indoor space, such as a fieldhouse or recreation center. See our [rain-on-event-day plan](/blog/rain-on-event-day-chicago).

## Alcohol, music and decorations

Park policies vary. Some parks forbid alcohol, some allow it with a permit, and some require licensed providers. The Chicago Park District, for example, requires an alcohol add-on, a properly licensed company such as a caterer and security at events with alcohol. Some parks ban balloons, confetti, taping to structures or nails. Free-standing signs and tabletop decor are usually the safest choice. Ask before you buy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do park pavilions come with tables and chairs?

Usually they include picnic tables or benches but not chairs. Ask the park what is included and the posted capacity, then rent any additional chairs and tables you need.

### How many people fit in a park pavilion?

Use the posted capacity from the park district. As a rule of thumb, a seated meal needs about 10 to 12 square feet per guest, so a 960-square-foot pavilion suits roughly 48 to 60.

### Can I serve alcohol at a park pavilion?

It depends on the park. Some forbid it and some allow it with conditions. The Chicago Park District, for example, requires an add-on, a licensed provider and security. Ask your park district.

### Can I bring a tent to a park pavilion?

Ask the park. Many districts regulate tents and stakes. In Chicago parks, tents and canopies, even 10 by 10, are a permit feature.

### Is there electricity at a pavilion?

Some have outlets and some do not. Ask about power and circuit limits before bringing a DJ, warmers or lights.

### When can I set up?

Only as early as your reservation allows. Ask for access time and request extra hours if you need them.

### What if it rains?

Ask about the weather policy and backup space. Don't count on a refund unless the park states it.
