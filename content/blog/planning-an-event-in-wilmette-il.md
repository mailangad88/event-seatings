**Quick answer**

For a Wilmette ceremony, set rows about 36 inches apart with a center aisle of 4 to 5 feet. A 100-guest ceremony fits 10 rows of 10 chairs and needs roughly 30 feet of depth. Teak garden folding chairs run about $5 to $7 each. If you hold the ceremony on park or beach property, ask the Wilmette Park District about reservations first.

Wilmette sits on Lake Michigan and is known for its lakefront parks, such as Gillson Park, and for the Baha'i House of Worship, one of the area's best known landmarks. Ceremonies in the village often happen outdoors on a lawn, in a garden or at a club, and the key decisions are the layout, the chair count and the wind. This guide covers each.

Allow roughly 18 inches of width per chair and 36 inches of depth per row. A block of 5 chairs is about 7.5 feet wide. For other sizes, see our [chair count guide](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and the post on [ceremony versus reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs).

## Wind and sand on the lakefront

Wilmette's lakefront parks are open and breezy. A few planning notes apply to any lakeshore ceremony.

1. **Anchor the arch.** Arches and signs catch wind; use weights, not just stakes.
2. **Choose stable chairs.** The [teak garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) and the [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) hold their position on firm lawn.
3. **Avoid loose decor.** Paper programs, chair ribbons and aisle petals blow away.
4. **Bring warm layers.** Lake breezes cool evenings even in summer.

Before you pick any park, beach or pavilion space, contact the Wilmette Park District about reservations, capacity and decor rules. Do not assume you may bring chairs onto sand or a protected lawn.

## Flipping from ceremony to reception

Many hosts use the same chairs twice. The flip is a choreographed move: guests head to cocktail hour while the crew sets rounds, then pulls chairs to the tables. It saves money and keeps chairs consistent, but it only works if the crew and timing are clear.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Ceremony ends.</strong> Guests move to cocktails.</li>
<li><strong>Crew clears rows</strong> and stacks chairs by the carts.</li>
<li><strong>Tables go in,</strong> then linens and place settings.</li>
<li><strong>Chairs move to tables,</strong> eight or ten at a time.</li>
<li><strong>Final check:</strong> centerpieces, candles and place cards.</li>
</ol>

Allow 60 to 90 minutes for a 100-guest flip, and tell the venue the plan in advance. If your venue cannot flip, rent separate sets for each space.

## Sunset and timing

In the Chicago area, sunset is around 8:30 pm near the summer solstice and before 5 pm in mid-winter, so ceremony time shapes your photos. Ask your photographer for golden hour on your date, and choose a ceremony start that leaves time for portraits before dinner. If you book a lakeside time, aim for a window when the sun is not directly in guests' eyes.

## Programs, accessibility and comfort

A ceremony is often the longest stretch your guests sit still, so comfort matters more than it seems. Reserve the aisle-end seats in the first two rows for grandparents or guests who need to step out. Leave a gap in a row for wheelchairs or walkers, so no one is parked in the aisle. Chairs with arms are a kindness for older guests, though they take more width, about 22 to 24 inches instead of 18. If your ceremony is longer than 30 minutes, mention cushions when you request a quote.

Place a small sign or an usher at the entrance to direct guests to the right side. Skip loose programs on the chairs if wind is likely; hand them out at the door or print one large sign instead. Provide a shaded or sheltered waiting area for early arrivals, since guests often arrive 20 to 30 minutes before the start.

## Photography and the view

Chair placement affects photos. Keep the first row at least 6 feet back from the arch so the couple has room and the photographer can shoot from the front. Avoid parking chairs in front of power lines, trash bins or neighboring buildings, which show up behind the couple. Check the sun direction at your ceremony time, because a strong low sun makes guests squint. A quick visit at the same time of day, a week before, shows you where shadows fall and where the wind is strongest.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs fit in a row for a ceremony?

Each chair takes about 18 inches of width, so a block of 5 is about 7.5 feet wide. A center aisle splits the rows in two.

### How wide should the aisle be?

Between 4 and 5 feet works for most processions and accessibility needs. Wider aisles are better for large dresses and wheelchairs.

### Do I need a permit for a ceremony at a Wilmette park?

Most likely you need a reservation, and perhaps a permit. Contact the Wilmette Park District for current rules.

### Can I reuse ceremony chairs at the reception?

Yes, if the venue and your crew plan the flip. Allow 60 to 90 minutes.

### What if it rains?

Pick a backup space or tent before the day, and set a decision time. Do not wait until guests arrive.

### Is a cushion needed for folding chairs?

Not for a 30-minute ceremony, but a cushion helps on longer services. Ask for options in your quote.
