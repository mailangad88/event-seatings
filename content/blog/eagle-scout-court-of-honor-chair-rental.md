**Quick answer**

For an Eagle Scout court of honor, set a head table at the front for the scout, parents and scoutmaster, with room behind it for the troop flags, and rows of chairs facing it for family, friends and fellow troop members. Plan one seat per guest on the invite list, since most courts of honor run 50 to 100 attendees in a VFW hall, church basement or scout building that rarely has that many matching chairs. An Eagle Scout court of honor is a formal recognition ceremony, but it's almost never held somewhere formal. Most Chicago-area troops book a VFW hall, a church basement or the troop's own scout building, and those spaces are set up for weekly meetings, not a sit-down ceremony for 50 to 100 guests. That gap between the room's everyday setup and what a court of honor needs is the main reason chairs get rented.

1. **Set a head table at the front** for the Eagle Scout, their parents and the scoutmaster, with enough room on or near it for the Eagle badge, certificate and any mentor pins being presented.
2. **Leave space directly behind the head table for the troop and any ceremonial flags**, since most courts of honor include a flag presentation as part of the program.
3. **Face family and troop rows toward the head table**, with a center aisle wide enough for the mentor pin ceremony and handshake line that typically follows the main presentation.
4. **Reserve the front rows for immediate family and any honored guests**, such as former scoutmasters or an Eagle Scout who's returning to speak.

## How many chairs to rent

Count by invited guests, not by troop roster size:

- **Ask the family for their guest count early**, since courts of honor often draw extended family and family friends well beyond the troop itself.
- **Add seats for the full troop and a few leaders**, even if not every scout confirms, since turnout for a fellow scout's ceremony is usually strong.
- **Check what the venue already has before ordering**, since VFW halls and scout buildings sometimes have a stack of folding chairs that covers part of the count.

## Chairs for a court of honor

The tone of a court of honor is respectful and a little formal, but not fancy. A simple, dignified chair works better here than anything ornate.

## What the venue usually has, and what it doesn't

- **VFW halls** often have folding tables and some chairs, but counts vary a lot by post, and a weeknight meeting setup is rarely sized for a full court of honor crowd.
- **Church basements and parish halls** usually have enough chairs for a Sunday crowd, but a court of honor on a weekend afternoon can clash with another group's booking, so confirm chair availability isn't already spoken for.
- **Troop-owned buildings** are built for scout meetings, not ceremonies, and often have the fewest spare chairs of the three.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for an Eagle Scout court of honor?
Plan one seat per invited guest. Most courts of honor in the Chicago area run 50 to 100 attendees once extended family, troop members and family friends are counted, well beyond the troop roster alone.

### How should seating be set up for a court of honor?
Set a head table at the front for the scout, parents and scoutmaster, with room behind it for troop flags, and rows of chairs facing the head table with a center aisle for the mentor pin and handshake line.

### Does our VFW hall or church hall already have enough chairs?
Sometimes partway. VFW halls and church basements often have some folding chairs, but the count is usually sized for a regular weekly crowd, not a full court of honor guest list. Confirm the exact count with the venue before you order.

### What's an appropriate chair for a court of honor?
A simple, dignified chair fits the tone best. A teak-look garden folding chair is a step up from a plain metal folding chair without looking out of place at a modest ceremony, and it's easy to reserve one nicer seat just for the Eagle Scout.
