Winnetka is a lakefront village on the North Shore with large homes, tree-lined streets and a strong tradition of hosting at home. It also has a village-run community house and lakefront parks. Whether your guest list is 30 or 130, the biggest risk is not style. It is timing: a delivery that arrives late, a setup that runs long or a pickup that conflicts with the next day. ## Large lots, long carries

Winnetka homes often have long driveways, gates, steps and side yards. Ask the delivery team to look at photos in advance.

- **Measure the carry** from the truck to the final table position.
- **Check gates and doors** for width, since a banquet table is 30 inches wide.
- **Plan wet ground.** Lawns near the lake can stay soft after rain; use boards or mats for carts.
- **Protect finishes.** Ask the crew to use floor protection indoors.

Delivery fees depend on access and distance, so send photos with your [quote request](/quote).

## Community spaces and the lakefront

Winnetka has a village-run community house and lakefront parks. If you want to host in a village or park district facility, contact the Village of Winnetka or the Winnetka Park District to ask about availability, capacity and outside vendor rules. Some public spaces limit how early you can arrive, which affects your delivery window. Confirm the details in writing.

On the lakefront, wind is a concern. Use weights on tents and signs, avoid tall unsecured decor and bring a wrap basket for evening events.

## Lighting for a village evening

Winnetka evenings can feel dark under mature trees. Plan lighting before the sun sets: string lights over the dining area, path lighting at steps and a light at the bar. In Chicago-area summers sunset is about 8:30 pm, but in fall it drops fast. Our [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events) show spacing and heights.

## Working with the neighbors and the street

North Shore streets are quiet, and a large event can change that for a day. Give neighbors a heads-up with the date, the expected hours and a phone number. Ask the caterer and DJ to wrap up by the time your village or venue requires, and confirm that time yourself. If guests will park on the street, ask the village about parking rules for the block, since some streets limit parking by permit or time. A shuttle from a nearby lot can solve the problem for larger parties, but check the rules for any lot you plan to use.

## What to ask before you sign

1. Who is the day-of contact for the rental delivery, and what is their number?
2. How early can the truck arrive, and how long will setup take?
3. Is setup included in the quote, or priced separately?
4. What happens if the weather forces a change the day of?
5. When is pickup, and what must be ready for the crew (stacked chairs, cleared tables)?
6. How are damaged or missing items handled?

Get the answers in the written quote. We would rather answer these in the planning call than on the day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How early should rentals arrive for a 6 pm event?

Aim for a morning or early afternoon delivery so setup is done 2 hours before guests arrive. Late arrivals leave no time to fix problems.

### How many chairs for a 60-guest dinner?

About 63 chairs. Add extras for a head table or lounge area.

### Can I have a tent on a Winnetka lawn?

Possibly. Ask the Village of Winnetka whether a permit or inspection is required, and read our [tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).

### What happens if it rains on the lawn?

Use a tent or an indoor backup. Soft lawns can need mats or boards for carts.

### Do you offer setup service?

Setup details are confirmed in your quote. Ask when you request it, since services and fees depend on the order.

### What chairs suit a modern dinner?

The wishbone and shell chairs suit modern settings, and bentwood suits a bistro look.
