**Quick answer**

Plan an Indian wedding 12 to 18 months ahead, longer for peak season. Start with the muhurat (auspicious date) if your family uses one, then book venues for each event, the caterer, decorator and photographer. Send save-the-dates 6 to 9 months out, formal invitations 2 to 3 months out, and track every event separately, since guest lists differ for each. Traditions vary widely by region, religion and family.

An Indian wedding is rarely one event. Depending on the families, it may include a roka or engagement, a mehndi, a haldi, a sangeet, the wedding ceremony and a reception, often over several days and with guest counts that run from 150 to 600. The real pain point is coordination: different events, different venues, different guest lists, and relatives flying in. This timeline puts the pieces in order, and it flags where your own traditions will change the plan.

## Start with the families and the calendar

There is no single Indian wedding. A Punjabi Sikh wedding (Anand Karaj), a Gujarati Hindu wedding, a Tamil or Telugu ceremony, a Bengali wedding, a Muslim nikah, a Jain wedding or a wedding that blends two traditions will all look different. Begin with a family conversation about which events you want, how long each should be, and which rituals matter. Ask a priest, pandit, granthi, imam or other officiant early about calendars and any restrictions on dates.

Many families consult a priest for a muhurat, an auspicious day and time. That date, sometimes limited to a few days in a season, can narrow your options for venues, so start there if it matters to you. Then confirm that your top venue is available, and have a backup day. For seating specifics, see [South Asian wedding seating](/blog/south-asian-wedding-seating).

## The timeline, month by month

**18 to 14 months out.** Set the budget and the number of events. Agree on guest list limits for each side of the family. Ask the priest or officiant about dates. Visit venues, and ask whether outside caterers, decorators and a baraat (the groom's procession) are allowed. Many hotels and banquet halls have rules about open flame, a sacred fire (havan or agni) and outdoor processions.

**14 to 10 months out.** Sign the venue contracts. Hire a planner or decorator if you want one, and choose the caterer, photographer and videographer. Book the DJ, dhol player and any live musicians. Reserve a hotel block for out-of-town guests.

**9 to 6 months out.** Order outfits and jewelry, and book hair and makeup. Send save-the-dates. Start designing the mandap and stage with your decorator. Plan the mehndi artists, haldi setup and sangeet performances and rehearsals.

**5 to 3 months out.** Send formal invitations. Finalize menus, including vegetarian, Jain or halal needs. Arrange transportation, and plan the baraat and milni.

**2 months to 2 weeks out.** Track RSVPs for each event separately. Build seating charts, rehearse sangeet dances, finalize the run of show, and confirm rentals.

**Wedding week.** Confirm vendors, arrange a family point person for each event, and prepare gift envelopes and ritual items such as flowers, rice and puja supplies.

## How to keep multiple events organized

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>List every event.</strong> Name, date, venue, expected guests and start time.</li>
<li><strong>Make a guest list per event.</strong> Some guests attend only the ceremony and reception.</li>
<li><strong>Assign a lead for each event.</strong> A relative or friend who knows the plan and can answer vendor questions.</li>
<li><strong>Build one master schedule.</strong> Show where each vendor and family member needs to be, with travel time.</li>
<li><strong>Order rentals per venue.</strong> Match chair and table counts to each event, not to the total guest list.</li>
<li><strong>Plan transitions.</strong> Allow time to reset a room between ceremony and reception.</li>
</ol>

## Details people forget

Allow time for the baraat. A procession with a horse, dhol and dancing needs a route, a permit question to answer, and a staging area. Ask venue management well in advance. Account for ritual needs: a sacred fire may require a ventilated space or an approved outdoor area, and some venues restrict incense and flower petals. Plan for shoes, since many guests remove them in ceremony spaces, so arrange a shoe area. Consider elders: provide chairs with arms and clear paths to the mandap. Think about food: families often serve multiple cuisines and need clear labels for vegetarian, vegan, Jain and halal items.

## Chicago-area notes

The Chicago area has a large South Asian community and many banquet halls, hotels and suburban venues that host multi-day weddings, especially in the western and northwestern suburbs. Peak wedding seasons here are summer and early fall, so book early for those months. Parking for 300 or more guests is a real concern, so ask about lots, shuttles and valet. Outdoor baraats and processions may need approval, so check with the venue and your village or park district. In winter, plan for coats and cold-weather transitions between buildings. ## Frequently asked questions

### How far in advance should you plan an Indian wedding?

Most families begin 12 to 18 months ahead, and sooner for peak summer and fall dates. Venues that allow outside caterers, sacred fire or a baraat can be limited, so start early.

### How many events are in an Indian wedding?

It varies. Many couples host between three and five events, such as a mehndi, haldi, sangeet, ceremony and reception, while others host a single day. Regional and religious traditions differ.

### What is a muhurat?

A muhurat is an auspicious date and time, often chosen with a priest or astrologer in Hindu families. Not every family uses one, and other traditions use different methods. If yours does, confirm it before booking venues.

### When do you send Indian wedding invitations?

Many couples send save-the-dates 6 to 9 months ahead and formal invitations 2 to 3 months before. Out-of-town guests may need earlier notice.

### How many guests are typical at an Indian wedding?

Guest counts often range from about 150 to more than 500. Each event may have a different list, so track them separately.

### Do I need a wedding planner for an Indian wedding?

Not required, but many families hire a planner or decorator because the number of events and vendors is large. A trusted relative can also act as a coordinator if they have time.

### What should I tell my venue about a baraat or sacred fire?

Ask early whether processions, drums, open flame and incense are allowed and where. Ask about insurance and any fire marshal requirements, and ask for the rules in writing.

### How do I seat guests at a multi-event wedding?

Create a separate layout for each event. A sangeet and reception use round tables, while a ceremony uses rows facing the mandap. Reserve front seating for elders and immediate family.
