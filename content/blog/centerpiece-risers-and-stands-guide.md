A centerpiece sits at the center of the table and often tries to do three jobs at once: look good, stay out of the way of conversation and not tip over. Risers, stands and pedestals solve the middle problem. They lift flowers or candles above eye level or fill a long table, and they let you reuse the same stems in different heights. Here is how to pick the right stand for your tables.

## The sightline rule

A seated adult's eyes are roughly 40 to 45 inches above the floor, and a table is 30 inches tall. A centerpiece that fills the space from about 12 to 24 inches above the table blocks faces across the table. Keep arrangements **under 12 inches** or lift them **above 24 inches** on a thin stem or stand.

## Risers and stands: what they do

- **Cylinder vases and pedestals** lift the arrangement.
- **Tall stands and trumpet vases** give height for galas and high ceilings.
- **Mirror trays** reflect candlelight and anchor a group of votives.
- **Cake stand style risers** lift candles and small arrangements for a layered look.
- **Candelabras** add height and a classic look.

## Fit to the table

On a 60-inch round, a single center arrangement of about 12 to 18 inches in diameter leaves room for plates, glasses and bread. For 8-foot banquet tables, use a line of three to five smaller pieces, or one long garland with candles. On a 72-inch round, a tall stand or a cluster looks better than a single bud vase. Ask your rental planner how much room the place settings need, because plates, glasses and bread plates take up most of a 60-inch round.

## Fire and venue rules

Many Chicago-area venues limit open flames or require candles in glass. Ask your venue and your florist about candle policy and whether battery candles are better. Tall stands need a heavy base in tents, and wind can tip them. Ask your venue about taping items to tables and moving arrangements.

## Chairs and centerpieces

Tall centerpieces look best with simple chairs. A [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) keeps sightlines open, and a [Louis medallion](/chairs/louis-medallion) can carry a lot of detail by itself, so keep centerpieces low. See also [mirror seating chart displays](/blog/mirror-seating-chart-display-guide) for more reflective decor.

## Mistakes to avoid

- A medium-height arrangement that blocks faces.
- Pedestals with narrow bases outdoors.
- Too many items competing for plate space.
- Forgetting that a tall piece must be moved for the cake cutting or toast.


## Setup day tips

Ask the florist to deliver arrangements on stands or risers fully assembled when the stands are light, and to set the heavy bases early. Mark each table's number on the bottom of the riser so the right one lands at the right table. Test one table first: sit in a guest chair and look across to see what the arrangement blocks. Bring spare wire, floral tape and a few extra votives to fix last-minute tips. In a Chicago winter, flowers brought in from a cold truck need time to relax before they look their best, so schedule delivery with enough time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How tall should centerpieces be?

Either under 12 inches or above 24 inches above the table. The middle range blocks conversation.

### What is a centerpiece riser?

A stand, pedestal or tray that lifts flowers, candles or decor. Risers add height and layers.

### How big should a centerpiece be for a round table?

About 12 to 18 inches across on a 60-inch round, leaving room for place settings.

### Can I use candles in centerpieces?

Often yes, but many venues restrict open flames. Ask your venue or consider battery candles.

### What goes under a centerpiece?

A mirror, a runner or a tray. Mirrors reflect candlelight and anchor small votives.

### How many centerpieces for long tables?

Plan a line of three to five smaller pieces or a garland with candles, spaced evenly.
