Highland Park sits on the lakefront in Lake County, with wooded ravines, a walkable downtown and a lot of people who entertain at home. It is also home to Ravinia, the outdoor music venue that draws picnickers every summer, and it is near the Chicago Botanic Garden in neighboring Glencoe. That mix of settings creates a wide range of events, from small garden parties to large family celebrations. This guide helps you size and style the seating for each.

## Ravinia-style picnic seating at home

Ravinia lists its own rules for picnic seating and rentals on its website, so check there if your plan involves the venue itself. At home, the same idea works well for a summer party: long tables, casual chairs and a relaxed menu. An 8-foot banquet table seats 8 on the long sides and 10 if you add the ends, so three tables serve 24 to 30 guests.

## Celebrations that need special planning

Highland Park hosts many life-cycle events. Each has a seating pattern worth planning for.

- **Bar and bat mitzvah receptions.** Expect a mix of adults and teens, a dance floor, a candle-lighting or speech moment and a head table or family table near the floor. Rounds of 10 are common. Plan a kids' table area, and count a DJ table and gift table.
- **Weddings.** Add a ceremony set of chairs, a sweetheart or head table and a cocktail area. See our [sweetheart table ideas](/blog/sweetheart-table-chair-ideas).
- **Graduation and milestone parties.** Casual seating, lots of cocktail tables and a buffet. A [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) at about $5 to $7 keeps costs in check.
- **Memorials and community gatherings.** Comfortable chairs with arms or cushions for older guests, and a ramp-friendly path.

For any celebration, ask the family or the venue about customs that affect furniture, such as a chuppah, a head table for family or a space for a ceremony. Have that conversation before you finalize the floor plan.

## Weather, ravines and ground

Highland Park has wooded lots and sloping ground. Before you place a table, check the grade. A table that wobbles on a slope needs shims, and chairs can tip on soft soil. Rain can leave low spots wet for days. If your yard slopes, level an area for tables or use a platform. Chicago-area weather brings sudden storms, so keep a covered plan ready.

## Rules to confirm locally

Public places such as parks and beaches have their own rules. If you plan to use a city park or beach, check with the City of Highland Park or the Park District of Highland Park for permits and restrictions. Tents may need a permit, and alcohol, music and food service often have separate rules. Do not rely on a general guideline; ask the office that manages the site.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a bar or bat mitzvah?

Plan one chair per guest plus about 5% spares, then add seating for the DJ, family areas and any lounge. A 120-guest party needs about 126 chairs plus extras.

### How many people sit at an 8-foot banquet table?

Eight on the long sides, and up to 10 if you add the ends. Allow 24 inches of table edge per guest for a seated meal.

### What chair is best for a lakeside party?

Wood chairs like the cross-back and Windsor sit solidly in a breeze, and the garden folding chair is a light, low-cost choice. Use foot pads on soft ground.

### Can I rent chairs for Ravinia?

Ravinia has its own rules and policies for concert seating and private events, so check with Ravinia directly. We can help with seating at private events and backyard parties.

### Do I need a permit for a tent in Highland Park?

It depends on size and location. Ask the City of Highland Park building or fire department before you reserve a tent, and review our [tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).

### How far ahead should I book?

For summer and fall weekends, 6 to 9 months is a good target. Smaller parties can book closer, but styles go quickly.
