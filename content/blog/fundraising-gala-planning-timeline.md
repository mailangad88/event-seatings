**Quick answer**

Start planning a fundraising gala about nine months ahead. Months nine to seven cover the committee, goals, date and venue. Months six to four are for sponsors, auction items and the program. Months three to one handle invitations, ticket sales, rentals and logistics. The final weeks are for seating, scripts and a rehearsal. Smaller events can compress the plan to five or six months.

</div>

A gala feels like one night, but it is really a year-long fundraising campaign with a party attached. The volunteers who run it are usually juggling jobs, the donors have crowded calendars and the venue wants a commitment early. A timeline turns the large goal into small, dated tasks, so the committee isn't scrambling to sell tables in the last month.

Treat these numbers as habits many nonprofits use, not rules. Your donors, calendar and board will shape the real schedule.

## Goals, committee and date

Begin with the money. Set a net revenue goal, not just a gross one, then work backward to the number of tickets, tables, sponsorships and auction revenue required. Many galas rely on a mix of sponsor income, ticket sales, an auction or fund-a-need appeal, and sometimes a paddle raise.

Form a committee with defined roles: chair, sponsorship, auction, program, guest experience, and volunteers. Choose a date that avoids major holidays and competing local fundraisers, and check with your board and key donors before announcing it. For help with sponsor packages, see our [event sponsorship packages guide](/blog/event-sponsorship-packages-guide).

## Venue, program and the auction

Book the venue early, with attention to capacity at rounds, stage space, an auction display area and the load-in path. The program should run about two to three hours total, including cocktails, dinner, remarks and the ask. Keep speeches short and make the appeal emotional and specific.

If you plan a silent auction, give it its own timeline: item solicitation, valuation, bid sheets or software, display, and checkout. Our [silent auction planning guide](/blog/silent-auction-planning-guide) goes through each step, and the [fundraiser auction setup rentals](/blog/fundraiser-auction-setup-rentals) article lists tables and display pieces.

## Tickets, seating and the last month

Open ticket sales as soon as sponsors are secure. Offer tables as well as single seats, because a company or group will often buy a whole table and fill it. Track names and meal choices in a spreadsheet or the [Guest List and RSVP Tracker](/downloads/guest-list-rsvp-tracker), and plan seating with the [Seating Chart Planner](/downloads/seating-chart-planner).

In the final month, confirm vendors, write the script, prepare bid numbers and check-in lists, assign volunteers and rehearse. Our [charity gala rental checklist](/blog/charity-gala-rental-checklist) covers the furniture and linens you will need, and [volunteer coordination](/blog/volunteer-coordination-for-nonprofit-events) explains how to staff the night.

## Common timeline mistakes

The usual problems are launching ticket sales too late, waiting to collect auction items until the last month, and forgetting that a printer, a caterer and a rental company all need final numbers well before the night. Another is putting every task on the chair. Spread work across the committee, set deadlines in the calendar, and review progress at each meeting so a slipped task is caught in week three, not week thirty-three.

Set a regular committee meeting, usually monthly at first and weekly in the last six weeks. Keep one shared tracker so tasks don't live in people's inboxes.

## Gala tasks by phase

This table organizes the plan by phase so each committee area knows what is due when.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Phase | Fundraising | Event logistics |
|---|---|---|
| Months 9 to 7 | Set revenue goal, form committee | Choose date, tour and sign venue |
| Months 6 to 4 | Launch sponsorship, request auction items | Hire caterer, entertainment, AV |
| Months 3 to 2 | Open ticket sales, send invitations | Design layout, order rentals |
| Month 1 | Follow up with donors, finalize bidding | Seating, script, volunteer training |
| Event week | Check-in, payment, thank-you plan | Walkthrough, load-in, rehearsal |

After the gala, send thank-you messages within a few days and record what worked for next year.

## How to run the plan

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Set the revenue goal.</strong> Net goal, ticket price and sponsor targets.</li>
<li><strong>Form the committee.</strong> Give each area a chair and a deadline.</li>
<li><strong>Book the venue and date.</strong> Confirm capacity at rounds with a stage.</li>
<li><strong>Secure sponsors and auction items.</strong> Use clear packages and request letters.</li>
<li><strong>Sell tables and tickets.</strong> Open early and track names and meals.</li>
<li><strong>Finalize layout and rentals.</strong> Build a floor plan with tables, stage and auction area.</li>
<li><strong>Rehearse and thank.</strong> Run the program and send quick thank-yous.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago has a busy gala calendar, especially in spring and fall, so check competing events before you pick a date. Downtown ballrooms often require food and beverage minimums, and loading docks have strict schedules. Winter galas need coat check and plans for snow. Suburban venues have easier parking but may have limits on amplified sound and capacity, so confirm with the venue or village. If you plan an outdoor reception, check with your park district about permits and read our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit). Many nonprofits ask for in-kind donations of services, which can lower costs, but always get written confirmation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far in advance should I plan a fundraising gala?

About nine months is common for a 150 to 400 guest gala. Smaller events can come together in five or six months.

### What is the timeline for planning a gala?

Set goals and form a committee, book the venue, secure sponsors and auction items, open ticket sales, then handle rentals, seating and the program in the final months.

### When should I start selling tickets?

Many nonprofits open sales three to four months out, after sponsors are in place. Offer tables as well as single seats.

### How many people should be on a gala committee?

A committee of six to twelve works for many galas, with a chair for sponsorship, auction, program and logistics. Smaller organizations can combine roles.

### How long should a gala program be?

Two to three hours total is typical, including cocktails and dinner. Keep speeches short and the fundraising ask clear.

### What should I do first when planning a gala?

Set a net revenue goal, form the committee and pick a date. Those three decisions guide the rest.

### How do I keep gala costs under control?

Seek sponsors to cover major costs, ask for in-kind donations, compare quotes, and track expenses against the budget weekly.
