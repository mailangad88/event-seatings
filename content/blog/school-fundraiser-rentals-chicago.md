**Quick answer**

A school fundraiser usually needs adult seating for about half the crowd, kid-height tables for food, booth tables for games and raffles, and shade or cover for weather. For a 200-person event, plan 100 chairs, 15 to 20 tables and a tent or two. Ask the school what furniture it already has, then rent only the gap.

</div>

School fundraisers have a different budget logic than corporate events. Volunteers do the setup, the gym or lawn is free, and every dollar spent on rentals is a dollar not raised. That makes planning the rental list more important, not less. Rent too little and volunteers are hauling folding tables from the basement; rent too much and the margin is gone. Here is a practical approach for a Chicago-area PTA, PTO or school foundation event.

Walk the site with the facilities manager, list what's available, and photograph the storage room. Then rent only to fill the gap.

## Event types and what they need

- **Spring carnival or fall festival:** game booths, food, bounce areas. Tables, canopies, a few chairs, trash stations. See [carnival games for parties](/blog/carnival-games-for-parties) if you want ideas.
- **Gala or auction night:** adult seating, linens, a stage and a silent auction setup. See [fundraiser auction setup](/blog/fundraiser-auction-setup-rentals).
- **Fun run or walkathon:** registration tables, a stage, water stations and shade.
- **Bingo or trivia night:** long tables with chairs and a stage for the caller.
- **Restaurant night or pancake breakfast:** tables, chairs and a serving line.

## Seating math for a family crowd

Families do not all sit at once. Plan seats for **50% of attendees** plus lawn space. Kids eat quickly and move on, while adults stay, so an adult-sized chair per two guests works. Use 6-foot rectangular tables for kids' lunch and 8-foot tables for parents who gather. If your school has picnic tables, count them first.

## Shade, weather and safety

Spring in Chicago is unpredictable, and a rainy Saturday can wipe out a carnival. Rent at least one tent or a few canopies as a weather fallback. Ask the school district about its insurance requirement for rentals and whether outside vendors need a certificate; see the [COI guide](/blog/corporate-event-coi-and-vendor-requirements). Make sure rentals are placed away from fire lanes, and check with the school for rules about bounce houses, generators and food service.

## Save money without cutting corners

- Borrow tables from the parish hall, park district or another school, if allowed.
- Rent chairs only for the dinner, not for the entire event.
- Use paper for kids' food and real glassware only for adults.
- Choose a Saturday afternoon over an evening to avoid extra lighting.
- Ask rental companies about nonprofit options; see [nonprofit rental budget tips](/blog/nonprofit-rental-budget-tips).

## Plan the volunteer shifts

Rentals only help if volunteers can place them. Split the crew into setup, event and teardown shifts, and give each person a role: tables, chairs, signs, food, first aid. Tape a simple map to the door of the storage room. Label the tables with numbers that match the layout so deliveries land in the right spot. Bring work gloves, a dolly and zip ties for banners. Keep a short list of who has keys, who handles cash and who can authorize changes, so a missing table at 3 pm does not stop the event.

## Make the money visible

Parents give more when they can see what the money does. Place a short sign by registration and the stage with the fundraising goal and the project, and update it during the event if you can. A simple thermometer board on an easel works well. Keep donation boxes covered and staffed, and set card payments near the exit. Tell the rental company if your event is for a school so they can tell you what options exist.

## Setup day

Ask for delivery the day before if the school can store items securely. Otherwise schedule early delivery on the morning of the event. Assign a volunteer lead to meet the driver, show them where to place items and sign for the order. Do an inventory count at pickup so you aren't charged for missing items.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do PTAs usually rent for a fundraiser?

Tables, chairs, tents or canopies, and sometimes a stage and sound. Food service gear is rented when volunteers cook or serve.

### How many chairs for a school carnival?

About half the expected attendance. Families rotate through seating during the day.

### Do schools need insurance for rentals?

Often yes. Ask your district what certificate of insurance it requires from the school and from vendors.

### Can I use school tables for the event?

Usually yes, with approval from the facilities manager. Ask how many are available and whether they can leave the building.

### What if it rains on carnival day?

Have a tent or gym backup. Rent canopies for food and registration, and move games indoors where possible.

### How early should I book for spring events?

As soon as the date is confirmed. Popular spring weekends are shared with graduations, so earlier is safer.
