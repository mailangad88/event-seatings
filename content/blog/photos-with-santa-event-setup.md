**Quick answer**

A Santa photo set needs a throne-style chair on a small platform or rug, a backdrop about 8 to 10 feet wide, soft light in front of Santa, and a stanchion line that holds 8 to 10 families without blocking the door. Plan 3 to 5 minutes per family, so 12 to 20 families an hour, and add a 10-minute break for Santa every hour. Set the camera 8 to 10 feet from the chair.

</div>

A photo with Santa is one of the easiest holiday events for a school, church, business or HOA to run, and one of the easiest to run badly. Lines back into the hallway, kids cry, the light is harsh, and the parent with a baby waits 40 minutes. In Chicago and the suburbs, where families go from the car to the building in the cold, a warm, organized line matters even more. This guide covers the chair, the backdrop, the line, the light and the timing. For the larger corporate side, see [corporate event photo backdrop guide](/blog/corporate-event-photo-backdrop-guide).

## The floor plan

Think of three zones: a check-in table at the door, a waiting line, and the Santa set. Put the set at the far end of the room, so the line does not block the door.

A room of about 30 x 40 feet fits this plan. In a school gym or church hall, a stage or a corner can work as the set. Leave an exit path that is not blocked by the line, and an accessible route. See [ADA accessible event seating and pathways](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways) and [church hall and community center event planning](/blog/church-hall-and-community-center-event-planning).

## The throne chair

Santa's chair is the star. A throne-style chair, with a tall back and arms, looks right in a photo, and is comfortable for hours. Make sure it is stable, with a wide base, because kids climb. Add a small platform or rug so Santa is easy to see and the chair does not slide. A bench next to Santa works for families, since three kids can sit on it. Keep a small stool or a step for little ones.

See the [royal throne chair](/chairs/royal-throne), [throne chair rental guide](/blog/throne-chair-rental-guide) and [lounge rentals](/rentals/lounge) for a sofa or bench. Ask the rental company what is available, since style and size vary.

## The backdrop

- **Width.** 8 to 10 feet wide so a family of five fits. Taller is better than wider.
- **Look.** A snow scene, a fireplace, a plain red or green drop. Avoid shiny paper that glares.
- **Pipe and drape** is an easy option and hides a messy wall. See [pipe and drape uses and sizes](/blog/pipe-and-drape-uses-and-sizes).
- **Props.** A small tree, a few wrapped boxes, a lamp post. Keep it uncluttered so children stay in the frame. See [photo backdrop ideas](/blog/photo-backdrop-ideas-for-weddings-and-parties).
- **Weights.** Use sandbags or weights on stands. Kids pull on things.

## Line management with stanchions

A line is where families spend most of their time, so it matters.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Check-in</strong> A table by the door with a list or sign-up, so you can see the day's schedule.</li>
<li><strong>Stanchions and rope</strong> A switchback of 8 to 10 families, with 4 to 5 feet between stanchion lines for strollers.</li>
<li><strong>Wait spot</strong> A small area with chairs for older guests and a snack or craft table for kids.</li>
<li><strong>Staging</strong> One family waits just behind the set while another sits with Santa.</li>
<li><strong>Exit</strong> A different path out, with the picture table or the print station.</li>
</ol>

Fencing and stanchions are easy to rent. See [fencing and crowd control](/rentals/fencing-crowd-control) and [event registration and check-in setup](/blog/event-registration-and-check-in-setup). Keep rope lines at least 36 inches wide at the narrowest point, and wider for strollers and wheelchairs.

## Light

- **Soft light in front.** Two lamps with soft diffusers on each side of the camera, at about face height. See [lighting for night event photos](/blog/lighting-for-night-event-photos) and [lighting rentals](/rentals/lighting).
- **No window behind Santa.** Backlight makes faces dark.
- **Warm color.** Most holiday scenes look best with warm white bulbs. See [lighting color temperature](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events).
- **Power.** Run cords along the wall and tape or cover them. See [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist).

## Timing per family

<div class="table-wrap">

| Setup | Time per family | Families per hour |
|---|---|---|
| Quick snap, one photographer | 3 minutes | about 20 |
| Standard, a short chat | 4 to 5 minutes | 12 to 15 |
| Slow, with gifts or sensory-friendly | 8 to 10 minutes | 6 to 7 |

</div>

These are rules of thumb. A 2-hour event with 4-minute slots handles around 25 to 30 families, after a break for Santa. If you expect more, add a second photographer, or run two sessions. A sign-up with time slots cuts the line. A quiet hour for children who find crowds hard is a kind option. Ask a school or church to check what families need.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Santa needs water and a break.** A suit is hot and 2 hours is long. Put a chair and a drink behind the backdrop.
- **A line in the cold.** A Chicago December line out the door is not fun. Keep the line inside. See [coat racks](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).
- **Check your venue's rules.** For a school, park district building or HOA clubhouse, check with the building about rental pickup times and decorations. For a business, check with the property manager. See [HOA and neighbors party](/blog/hoa-and-neighbors-tent-party).
- **Book early.** The first two weekends of December are busy. See [December holiday party rentals](/blog/december-holiday-party-rentals-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a Santa photo take per family?

About 3 to 5 minutes. A quick snap can be 3 minutes. A longer visit or a sensory-friendly session can be 8 to 10 minutes. Plan a break for Santa every hour.

### How many families can visit Santa in an hour?

About 12 to 20, depending on how long each visit runs. Time slots and a check-in table help keep the line moving.

### How far should the camera be from Santa?

About 8 to 10 feet. That fits a family of four or five in the frame. Use soft lights on both sides of the camera.

### What kind of chair should Santa use?

A throne-style chair with a tall back and arms, with a stable base. Add a platform or rug, and a bench for kids.

### How do I manage the line?

Use stanchions with rope, a switchback of 8 to 10 families, and a check-in table. Keep a 36-inch minimum aisle, and make the path accessible.

### What lighting works best for Santa photos?

Soft light in front, two lamps at face height, and no window or bright light behind Santa. Warm white bulbs look best.
