**Quick answer**

A 20x40 tent is 800 square feet. It seats about 32 to 40 guests at rounds with a small dance floor, 48 to 56 at rounds with no dance floor, 60 to 70 at a cocktail party, or about 80 in theater rows. Plan for 14 to 16 square feet per seated guest, and treat the numbers as a rule of thumb.

</div>

The 20x40 is the most common backyard tent in the Chicago suburbs. It fits on many lots, goes up in a few hours and feels generous for a graduation, birthday or small wedding. But it is smaller than people expect once chairs, poles and the buffet go in. This guide shows what actually fits.

## Capacity by setup

<div class="table-wrap">

| Setup | Capacity | Sq ft per guest | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rounds, dance floor | 32 to 40 | 20 to 25 | Tight aisles; bar outside |
| Rounds, no dance floor | 48 to 56 | 14 to 16 | 6 or 7 rounds of 8 |
| Long tables | 40 to 50 | 16 to 20 | Two 36-foot tables is too wide; use one or two shorter rows |
| Cocktail, standing | 60 to 70 | 11 to 13 | Highboys and a bar outside |
| Theater, rows | 70 to 80 | 10 to 11 | No tables |

</div>

Why so few at rounds? A 60-inch round needs a footprint of about 8 x 8 feet with chairs, and 20 feet gives you only two rows with a 4-foot aisle between them. Our [round table sizes guide](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch) lists the footprints.

## Why a 20x40 feels smaller than 800 square feet

Tent size is measured edge to edge, but the usable floor is smaller. Frame legs and sidewall fabric take a few inches on each side, and any guest sitting next to a sidewall needs room to push the chair back. A 60-inch round with eight chairs measures about 8 feet across, so two rows across a 20-foot width leave a single 4-foot aisle between the tables and about 2 feet to each wall. That works for seated guests, but a server with a tray will brush chairs.

The best way to gain space in a 20x40 is to move things out of it. Serve food from a table under the edge of a separate canopy, put the bar on the patio, and keep the cake and gift tables near the entrance so they do not claim floor. If your guest count is on the edge, either choose a 20x50 or 20x60, which many tent companies offer in 10-foot increments, or move to a 30x40. The jump from 800 to 1,200 square feet changes the party from tight to comfortable.

## What does not fit inside

A 20x40 does not fit a buffet and a bar and a band. Put these outside:

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Catering prep.</strong> A 10 x 10 or 10 x 20 canopy or the garage, away from guests.</li>
<li><strong>Bar.</strong> Under the tent edge or a separate canopy, so the line stays outside.</li>
<li><strong>Gift and guest book table.</strong> At the entrance.</li>
<li><strong>Restrooms.</strong> In the house or a trailer; see our restroom guide.</li>
<li><strong>Lounge.</strong> On the lawn, with a couple of sofas or chairs.</li>
</ol>

## Space around the tent

The tent footprint is only part of the footprint. Stakes or ballast extend beyond the edge, and the crew needs access to set up. Ask the tent company how much clearance they need around the tent. Many backyards in the Chicago suburbs are 50 to 60 feet wide, which leaves room on each side, but trees, fences and sheds can block setup. See [space around a tent](/blog/space-around-a-tent) and [tent anchoring](/blog/tent-anchoring-stakes-vs-ballast).

## Permits and utilities

Municipalities set their own rules for tents, often based on size. Some require a permit for tents over a certain square footage, others for any tent with heaters or lighting. A 20x40 is 800 square feet, which may exceed the threshold in your village. Call your village or city hall's building or fire department, and check with your HOA. See [tent permit rules in the Chicago suburbs](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs) and [Chicago tent permit](/blog/chicago-tent-permit). Also call 811 (JULIE in Illinois) before stakes go in; see [JULIE utility locate](/blog/julie-utility-locate-tent-illinois).

## Weather in Chicago

A tent is protection from sun and light rain, not a storm shelter. If the forecast shows high winds or lightning, the tent company or venue may ask you to take it down, and guests should go indoors. Cold evenings in spring and fall may need heat; see [heating a tent in fall](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall). In summer, ventilate with open sides or fans; see [cooling a tent in summer](/blog/cooling-a-tent-in-summer).

## Chairs for a small tent

In a tight tent, a slim, light chair helps. A [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is compact and stacks; a [bentwood bistro chair](/chairs/bentwood) feels dressy without adding bulk. Use [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) to count the extras.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people fit in a 20x40 tent?

About 32 to 40 seated at rounds with a small dance floor, 48 to 56 without dancing, and 60 to 70 standing.

### How many tables fit in a 20x40 tent?

Four to six 60-inch rounds, depending on whether you include a dance floor.

### Can I fit 50 guests in a 20x40 tent?

Seated, with no dance floor, yes if you use 6 or 7 tables, but it will be snug. For dinner and dancing, choose a 30x40 or larger.

### Is a 20x40 tent big enough for a wedding?

For 30 to 40 guests, yes. For more, a 30x60 is safer.

### How much space do I need in the yard for a 20x40 tent?

The tent plus several feet on each side for stakes or ballast and crew access. Ask your tent company for their number.

### Do I need a permit for a 20x40 tent?

It depends on your municipality. Call the village or city, since requirements vary and may depend on size, heaters and lighting.
