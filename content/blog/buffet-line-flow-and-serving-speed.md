**Quick answer**

A single-sided buffet serves about 2 guests per minute, so 100 guests take about 50 minutes. A double-sided line cuts that to roughly 25 to 30 minutes. Put plates first, then salads and cold items, hot entrees, starches and sides, with napkins and flatware at the end. Allow 36 inches of clear walkway in front of the table and keep the line out of the path to restrooms and the bar.

</div>

A buffet is only as good as its line. Poor layout creates a 45-minute queue, cold food and guests who feel like they waited more than they ate. Chicago-area banquet halls, church halls and backyards all tend to have the same problem: not enough space around the table. Here is how to avoid it.

The pace numbers are rules of thumb used by many caterers: expect about 2 guests per minute through a well-run single line, and adjust if you have heavy sauces or carving stations. Guests who take small portions go faster; carved meat or made-to-order food slows the line.

## The order foods should appear in

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Plates</strong> stacked at the start, with extra stacks on the other side if double-sided.</li>
<li><strong>Salads and cold items</strong> so guests don't build tall plates.</li>
<li><strong>Starches and sides</strong> before the protein.</li>
<li><strong>Protein</strong> last among foods, so it sits warmest and on top.</li>
<li><strong>Bread, sauces and condiments</strong> near the end.</li>
<li><strong>Napkins and flatware</strong> at the finish, ideally rolled, so guests aren't juggling.</li>
</ol>

Putting napkins and flatware at the end is a small change with a big result. Without it, guests balance silverware on a loaded plate and slow the line to look for room.

## Table math

Each hot dish takes about 30 inches of table. Cold bowls and platters take 20 to 24 inches. A 6-foot banquet table fits 2 hot dishes plus a utensil plate, while an 8-foot table holds three. For a 7-item buffet you need about 15 to 18 feet of table on each side of a double-sided line. See our guide to [how many chafing dishes you need](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet) for the count.

## Release tables in waves

Rather than call everyone at once, release tables one at a time, starting with the head table, elders and families with small children. This turns a crowd into a steady flow. A DJ or emcee can announce it, and a table number card on each plate works well. For a seating plan that fits this, see [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Space around the line

- **Walkway:** 36 inches minimum in front of a wall-side buffet, and 48 inches where lines face each other.
- **Staff side:** 24 inches behind the table for refilling, or a back aisle through a tent flap.
- **Drink service:** Keep beverage tables away from the buffet. A separate station avoids a second queue.
- **Dessert:** Put the sweets on a separate table so guests who finished can graze while others eat. See [dessert table equipment](/blog/dessert-table-equipment-checklist).

## Chicago layout notes

Older banquet halls and church basements in the city often have a fixed pass-through or a narrow kitchen door. Measure the doorway if the chafers are traveling on a cart. In the suburbs, backyard tents rarely give you more than a 20 x 40-foot footprint, so use the tent's long side for the buffet and leave the short side for bar and dessert. Always ask your venue where staff can plug in warming equipment and where they can stage.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to serve 100 guests at a buffet?

About 50 minutes on one line and 25 to 30 minutes on a double-sided line. Releasing tables in waves keeps the line moving evenly, and a second dessert or drink station lets finished guests step away.

### Should a buffet be one-sided or two-sided?

Use one side up to about 50 guests and two sides for 75 or more. Two sides need a 6-foot-wide table or two matching tables, with a staff aisle behind them for refills.

### What goes first on a buffet line?

Plates, then salads and cold items, then starches and sides, then the main protein. Napkins and silverware go at the end. This order keeps hot food warm and prevents guests from overloading plates early.

### How far apart should buffet tables be?

Keep at least 36 inches between the table and anything behind guests, and 6 feet around a free-standing island. If two lines face each other across a walkway, 48 inches avoids bumping.

### How many serving utensils do I need for a buffet?

One per dish, plus a spare for each hot item in case one is dropped or contaminated. Our [serving utensils checklist](/blog/serving-utensils-checklist-for-catering) lists the right tool for every dish.

### Where should the buffet go in a tent?

Put it along a long side near the kitchen or prep tent, away from the main door and bar. Wind can disrupt the line, so avoid open tent sides, and see [catering prep tent and back of house space](/blog/catering-prep-tent-and-back-of-house-space).
