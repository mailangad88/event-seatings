**Quick answer**

Plan about half a gallon of drinking water per guest for a 4-hour summer event and about a quarter gallon in cooler weather, served from 2-gallon glass dispensers on a 6-foot table. One dispenser serves about 25 guests, so 100 guests need 4 to 6 dispensers, plus ice and cups or glasses. Infuse with citrus, cucumber, berries or herbs for 2 hours or more before the event.

</div>

Water is the drink everyone wants and nobody budgets for. At an outdoor wedding in July or a summer fundraiser along the Chicago lakefront, a thirsty crowd empties a water station quickly. A well-planned station keeps guests comfortable, gives non-drinkers something special and reduces lines at the bar.

## How much water to plan

These are planning rules of thumb, not published standards. Adjust for heat, the length of your event and whether people will be dancing.

## What to rent

- **Glass beverage dispensers.** 2 or 3 gallon with a spigot and a stand. Pick glass over acrylic if the dispensers will sit indoors; acrylic is safer on a lawn or a tent floor.
- **A 6-foot banquet table** with a floor-length cloth. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).
- **Glasses or cups.** Plan 1 cup for every 2 guests per hour and spares. For outdoor events, see [plastic vs glass outdoors](/blog/plastic-vs-glass-outdoor-events-parks).
- **An ice bin or ice bags** and a scoop. Read [how much ice for a party](/blog/how-much-ice-for-a-party).
- **A drip tray or towels** under the spigots.
- **Small labels** on the table, listing the flavors.
- **A trash bin** for used cups.

## Four infused water recipes

Slice fruit and herbs thin and add them 2 hours before guests arrive. Strain citrus after 4 hours, because the rind turns bitter.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Recipe | Ingredients per 2 gallons | Season |
|---|---|---|
| Classic | 4 lemons, sliced, and fresh mint | Summer |
| Spa | 2 cucumbers and a bunch of mint or basil | Summer |
| Berry | 2 pints strawberries, sliced, and orange slices | Late spring |
| Winter | Orange slices, cranberries and rosemary | Winter |
| Citrus | Grapefruit, orange and lime slices | Any |

</div>

These are common household amounts, so scale up or down to taste. Use a fruit basket or infuser tube if your dispenser has one, to keep seeds out of the spigot.

## Layout

Put the station away from the bar so lines do not merge. Near the dance floor, near a tent exit or beside the cocktail area works well. Allow 4 feet of standing room in front. Shade it if possible, because water warms and ice melts quickly in direct sun. For layout ideas see [bar placement in the floor plan](/blog/bar-placement-floor-plan).

## Food safety and venue notes

Keep infused water cold, and discard anything left out for more than a few hours in the heat. Public parks and forest preserves may have rules about what you can bring in and about glass; check with your park district or venue. If tap water is not available at the location, ask your caterer or a water supplier to deliver jugs.

## Make it a feature

A water station can double as a decor piece. Mix tall glass dispensers with fresh flowers, a lace runner or a hand-lettered sign. For a kids' table, serve water in small glasses; see [kids' glassware](/blog/kids-glassware-and-toasts). For a wedding, a water station is a thoughtful touch for older guests waiting for the ceremony. Seating nearby gives people a place to rest, and [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) are a simple option.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much water do I need for 100 guests?

About 25 gallons in cool weather, 40 in mild weather and 50 or more on a hot day for a 4-hour event. That is a planning rule of thumb, so round up.

### How many water dispensers do I need?

One 2-gallon dispenser serves about 25 guests, so 4 to 6 dispensers for 100 guests. More dispensers mean shorter lines and fewer refills.

### How long should fruit infuse in water?

At least 2 hours in the fridge for a good flavor, and up to 8 for fruit. Remove citrus rinds after 4 hours, because they get bitter.

### Should I use glass or acrylic dispensers?

Glass looks better indoors and does not scratch. Acrylic is lighter and safer on lawns and in tents, so many outdoor events use it.

### Can I serve infused water for a winter event?

Yes. Use orange, cranberry and rosemary, and offer warm water with lemon too. Cold water is less popular in winter, so reduce the quantity.

### Do I need ice in the dispenser?

Use an ice core or add ice to the cups instead of the water. Loose ice melts and dilutes the flavor.
