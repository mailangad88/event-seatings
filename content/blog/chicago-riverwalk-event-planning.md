**Quick answer**

The Chicago Riverwalk is a public promenade, not a rental venue. The City's Riverwalk FAQ does not list private-event or wedding rentals on the walk itself, and says each vendor plans its own events. If you want a river setting, book a nearby restaurant, hotel, boat or private terrace, and ask them what furniture they allow or provide.

</div>

Photos of the Chicago Riverwalk make it look like the perfect party backdrop: steel bridges, tall glass and water below. Planners search for how to reserve it. The honest answer is that it works differently from a park shelter or a banquet hall. This guide explains what we could verify, how to find the right contact and how to plan an event with a river view.

## What the City says

The City of Chicago's Riverwalk FAQ page states that DCASE (the Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events) participates in programming events and public art. It says vendors plan their own schedules, and to reach out to individual vendors to inquire. It also notes that vendor areas may have separate policies. On private events and weddings, the FAQ gives a short answer that suggests they are not currently offered on the walk, and "planned in future builds." The page does not describe a permit or reservation process for private parties.

Because rules and offerings change, treat this as a starting point. Call the City or the specific vendor before you promise guests a Riverwalk setting.

## Questions to ask any river-view venue

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Who owns the space?</strong> Landlord, operator or City.</li>
<li><strong>What is provided?</strong> Chairs, tables, linens, glassware.</li>
<li><strong>Can outside vendors deliver?</strong> Many venues have rules or a preferred list. Ask before you order.</li>
<li><strong>What is the load path?</strong> Dock, stairs, elevator, hand carts.</li>
<li><strong>What is the weather plan?</strong> Wind and rain near the river.</li>
</ol>

## Downtown logistics

Downtown deliveries are the hard part. Streets near the river carry heavy traffic, loading zones fill, and big events change the picture. The Chicago Marathon closes Michigan Avenue from 4 a.m. on race morning, and Lollapalooza closes streets near Grant Park for weeks. Read [planning around the Marathon weekend](/blog/planning-around-chicago-marathon-weekend) and [Lollapalooza weekend](/blog/planning-around-lollapalooza-weekend). ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I book the Chicago Riverwalk for a private event?

The City's Riverwalk FAQ does not describe a booking process for private events or weddings and suggests they are not currently offered on the walk. Vendors run their own events, so ask them directly. Call the City before you rely on any plan.

### Can I get married on the Chicago Riverwalk?

We could not verify a wedding reservation process for the Riverwalk, so we do not recommend counting on one. Ask the City and consider a nearby venue with a river view.

### Who runs events on the Riverwalk?

Individual vendors plan their own schedules, and DCASE participates in programming events and public art, according to the City's FAQ. Contact the vendor you are interested in.

### Do I need a permit for a party near the river?

On public land, a Park District or DCASE special event permit may apply. On private property, the owner's rules apply, plus any tent or liquor rules. See our special event permit guide.

### What furniture works for a river-view terrace?

Sturdy, wind-ready pieces such as bentwood chairs, fitted table covers and low centerpieces. Ghost chairs keep sight lines clear for the view. Ask the venue if outside rentals are allowed.

### How do deliveries work downtown?

Through a dock, freight elevator or loading zone arranged by the venue. Ask for hours, height limits and insurance requirements, and never assume curb access.
