**Quick answer**

A product launch works best as a hybrid layout: a presentation area with theater seating for about half the guests, demo stations around the edges, and standing cocktail space in between. Plan seats for 40 to 60% of attendees, a 10 x 10 to 12 x 12 foot stage, and 5 feet of clearance around demo tables. Reserve a press and photo corner near the entrance.

</div>

A product launch is not a conference and not a party. The goal is for guests to see the product, hear the story and talk to the team, usually within 90 minutes. The layout that serves that goal is a flow, not a grid of rounds. Here is a plan that fits a 50-person press event in a Chicago loft or a 200-person customer launch in a suburban hotel.

1. **Arrival zone:** registration, name badges, a branded backdrop.
2. **Experience zone:** demo tables or stations where guests handle the product.
3. **Presentation zone:** a stage, screen and seats for the reveal.

## Seating math

Not everyone needs a seat at the same time. A launch usually runs in waves: networking, a 15 to 30 minute presentation, then more demos. Seats for **40 to 60% of attendees** let people rotate. For a 100-guest launch, plan 50 to 60 chairs in rows facing the stage and a few lounge seats. For 200, plan 100 to 120 chairs. Theater spacing is about 6 to 8 square feet per person; see [how many chairs fit in theater seating](/blog/how-many-chairs-fit-theater-seating).

## Demo stations

Demo tables should be 6 or 8 feet long, covered in a floor-length black or white cloth, with 5 feet of clearance on every side so a small crowd can gather without blocking others. Keep power in mind: you may need cords and surge protectors, and your venue controls what you can plug in. Put the most popular demo away from the entrance to pull people through the room, and never put a demo against the only path to the restrooms.

## Chairs that suit a launch

Modern products need modern chairs. [Ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) keep sightlines open. [Wishbone chairs](/chairs/wishbone) and [shell-style seating](/chairs/shell) feel contemporary. For a luxury launch, [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) at VIP tables add weight.

## The press corner

If journalists or creators are invited, give them a table with power, Wi-Fi if the venue has it, and a chair per person. Put it near the entrance, with a clear view of the stage. Photographers need space at the front of the room. Plan a second row of standing room behind the chairs for video.

## Branding without clutter

A launch tells a story, and the room is part of it. Keep branding to three or four moments: a backdrop at arrival, a screen on stage, signage at demo tables and one custom item such as a napkin or cup sleeve. More than that, and the room feels like a trade show. Use neutral linens (black, white or gray) so the product's colors stand out. If you need pipe and drape to hide a back wall or a staging area, plan for it in the rental list from the start, since it affects the floor plan. Set up the product tables first; chairs go in last so staff can move freely.

## Run-of-show tips

Walk the room with the team the day before. Check that each demo table has power, that sightlines to the screen work from the last row and that the bar line doesn't block the path to the stage. Have a staff person for each demo, one at the door and one with the press. Plan a five-minute buffer after the reveal for guests to find seats and drinks before the next segment starts.

## Chicago note

Many launches in Chicago happen in lofts, galleries and hotel ballrooms with freight elevators and restricted load-in times. Ask about delivery windows, stairs, and whether you can paint, hang or tape anything. For lighting and AV, the venue may have in-house requirements, so ask early. If you are hosting for a hybrid audience, see [hybrid event AV and seating](/blog/hybrid-event-av-and-seating).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many seats do I need at a product launch?

Seats for 40 to 60% of attendees. Guests arrive, mingle, sit for the presentation and then move around again.

### How big should the stage be?

About 10 x 12 feet for 100 guests and 12 x 16 for 150 or more. Add space if the product is large or if multiple speakers will demo.

### Should I use rounds or theater seating?

Theater, because it holds more people in the same area and keeps the room open for demos. Use rounds only if you serve a meal.

### Where should the bar go?

Away from the entrance and the stage, so lines do not block the arrival or the presentation. A wall near the demo zone works.

### How much clearance do demo tables need?

About 5 feet on all sides, more if you expect crowds.

### What do I do with coats in winter?

Add a coat rack near the entrance, one per 40 to 50 guests. Do not use the audience seats.
