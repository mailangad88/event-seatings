**Quick answer**

A 100-guest plated wedding with salad, entree and cake needs about 110 dinner plates, 110 salad plates, 110 dessert plates, 100 cups and saucers if coffee is served, and 100 bread plates if bread is served. A buffet wedding cuts that to about 110 dinner plates and 110 dessert plates. The extra 10 pieces per line is a rule-of-thumb cushion for breakage and late additions.

</div>

A hundred guests is the most common wedding size we hear about in Chicago, and it is a number that makes counting easy. Ten tables of ten, or twelve tables of eight to nine. This post gives a counted list that you can paste into an email to a rental company, with notes on what to change for your menu. It is not a price sheet: we do not publish dinnerware prices, and the list is meant to help you ask for an accurate quote.

## Buffet version

With a buffet and cake, the list shrinks to about 110 dinner plates, 110 dessert plates, and optional cups and saucers. You may add a stack of 20 extra dinner plates at the buffet for second trips. The buffet itself needs serving pieces and chafing dishes, which come from the caterer or a catering equipment order. See [dinnerware sizes for buffet, plated and family-style](/blog/dinnerware-sizes-buffet-plated-family-style).

## The head table and extras

Include the head table or sweetheart table in the guest count; most couples forget that the couple eats too. Add vendor meals if the photographer and DJ are fed by the caterer. Include the kids' table if kids are in the 100 or are extra.

## Tables for 100 guests

For 100 guests, that is about 10 rounds of 10 at 72 inches or 12 rounds of 8 to 9 at 60 inches, plus cake, gift and guest book tables. Check how many tables you need before you count place settings. Our [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) guide handles chairs, and the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) handles linens.

## Choices that change the count

- **Skip the soup bowl** if the menu has no soup.
- **Use salad plates for dessert** to cut one line (see [dessert plates](/blog/dessert-and-cake-plates-how-many)).
- **Skip chargers** to save a line (see [chargers](/blog/are-charger-plates-worth-it)).
- **Skip bread plates** (see [bread plates](/blog/bread-and-butter-plates-do-you-need)).
- **Serve coffee from a station** to cut cups used at the table.

Each skip cuts about 100 pieces from the order, which also cuts crate count and delivery weight.

## Delivery weight and crates

A 100-guest plated order is many crates of china. Plates are heavy: a stack of 20 dinner plates is a significant carry. Tell your venue how many crates to expect and where the loading dock or entry is. See [delivery and packing crates](/blog/dinnerware-delivery-and-packing-crates).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many plates do I need for a 100-guest wedding?

For a plated three-course dinner, you need about 330 plates: 110 each of dinner, salad and dessert. For a buffet with cake, about 220.

### How many chargers do I need?

One per seat, so 100 for guests plus the couple's seats, roughly 105 to 110 with a cushion. Chargers are optional.

### Do I need to rent cups and saucers?

Only if you serve coffee or tea at the table. If coffee is at a station, ask whether mugs or paper cups are better.

### Should I order extra for breakage?

Yes, about 5 to 10% extra. Read the [breakage fee guide](/blog/rental-breakage-and-loss-fees-explained) for how it is charged.

### Does my caterer provide china?

Many do or can supply it for a fee. Ask for what is included, then rent only what is missing so you do not pay twice.

### What if my headcount changes?

Tell the rental company as early as possible. Most allow changes up to a deadline in the contract.
