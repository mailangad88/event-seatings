**Quick answer**

A straight single-sided buffet needs about 1 foot of table per 8 to 10 guests per dish, and one line serves about 50 to 75 guests in 20 to 30 minutes. Use a double-sided line to halve the wait for 100 guests or more. Allow at least 4 to 5 feet in front of the table, and keep plates at the start and silverware and napkins at the end.

</div>

A buffet looks simple and hides a lot of math. Too short a table and food has no room. Too narrow an aisle and the line stalls. The layouts below work in Chicago's banquet halls, church basements and backyard tents, and they follow the same logic.

## The rules of thumb

- **Table length:** about 2 feet per dish. A 6-dish buffet needs a 12-foot line.
- **Aisle in front:** 4 to 5 feet, so a person with a plate can pass someone in line.
- **Speed:** a guest spends a minute or two at the line. With 100 guests on one line the last tables wait a long time, so use two lines or stations.
- **Seating first:** release tables in turn so the line does not form at once.

These are planning rules, so ask your caterer to confirm for their menu.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Plates first</strong> at the start of the line, stacked.</li>
<li><strong>Salads and cold items</strong> Then the main dishes.</li>
<li><strong>Proteins and sides</strong> Hot items in the middle.</li>
<li><strong>Sauces and bread</strong> Near the end.</li>
<li><strong>Silverware and napkins</strong> at the end or at the tables, so hands are free.</li>
</ol>

## Heights and risers

Raising dishes on risers or crates lets guests see the food and creates a better photo. Keep the highest items at the back, the lowest at the front. Add a sneeze guard if the venue requires one. Ask the caterer what equipment they bring, such as chafing dishes and fuel, so you do not rent doubles. See [catering equipment](/blog/topic/catering-equipment).

## Linens and skirting

A floor-length cloth hides power cords and supplies under the table. A pleated skirt looks finished on the guest side. See [pleated skirting for head tables and buffets](/blog/pleated-table-skirting-head-table-buffet). Heat sources and fabrics need care, so check with your caterer and venue about fuel placement.

## Layouts by room type

- **Ballroom:** double-sided, with food in the middle of a wide space.
- **Tent:** an island or a straight line against a wall away from the entrance.
- **Backyard:** a single line, with a cover for shade and insects.
- **Church basement:** a single line down a hallway, with plates at the door.

Chicago winter parties often need a coat rack by the door, so keep the buffet away from the entrance to avoid a bottleneck.

## Mistakes to avoid

- A single line for 150 guests.
- Placing the buffet where guests have to cross the dance floor.
- Putting drinks on the buffet table. Use a separate bar.
- Forgetting a cleanup table for dirty plates.

## A worked example: 120 guests at a wedding in a suburban banquet hall

A couple has 120 guests, 8 dishes and a banquet hall with 60-inch rounds.

1. Dishes: 8 dishes at 2 feet each is a 16-foot line, which means two 8-foot tables per side.
2. Lines: 120 guests is too many for one line, so choose a double-sided line with the food in the middle.
3. Table count: four 8-foot tables back to back, or two 8-footers with the dishes repeated on both sides.
4. Aisles: 5 feet on each side of the buffet, which makes the buffet footprint about 12 x 18 feet.
5. Release: call tables to the buffet in groups of 3 or 4 so the line never exceeds 20 people.

Repeating the dishes on both sides of a double line means your caterer needs two sets of chafing dishes and serving spoons. Say so early so the quote covers it.

## Questions to ask your caterer

- Do you bring tables, or should we rent them?
- How many dishes are on the menu, and how long a line do you need?
- Do you need a separate table for plates and napkins, and one for dirty dishes?
- Where do you set up hot boxes, and do you need power?
- Do you serve or do guests serve themselves?

If the caterer uses their own tables, you only need linens. If you rent tables, tell the caterer what size they are before they plan the line.

## Safety at the buffet

Hot food and fabric need distance. Fuel cans sit in chafing dishes, so keep cloths from touching the flame, and never place the buffet where wind can blow cloth into a burner. Outdoors in Chicago, a gust off the lake can flip a loose napkin; weigh down linens. Cold foods, such as salads and desserts, need shade or ice in summer.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a buffet table be?

About 2 feet per dish. Six dishes need roughly 12 feet. Add table space for plates, silverware and napkins.

### How many guests can one buffet line serve?

One line serves 50 to 75 guests in about 20 to 30 minutes. For more, use a double-sided line or stations.

### How much room do I need in front of a buffet?

4 to 5 feet, so guests can pass each other with plates.

### Should a buffet be one-sided or two-sided?

One-sided for under 75 guests; two-sided for 100 or more.

### Where should plates go?

At the start of the line. Silverware and napkins can go at the end or at the tables.

### What size tables are used for buffets?

6 or 8-foot banquet tables. See [banquet table sizes](/blog/banquet-table-sizes-6-ft-vs-8-ft).
