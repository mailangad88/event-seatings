Late October in the Chicago area is cold and damp more often than not, which changes what you can do outside. A good plan balances atmosphere with comfort, and keeps kids and adults both having fun.

## Pick your crowd and theme

**Kids' party (ages 4 to 10).** Short, early, with a craft, a costume parade, simple games and snacks. Keep decor playful, not scary. Two hours is plenty.

**Teen party.** Music, snacks, a scary movie or haunted house, and a photo spot.

**Adult party.** Cocktails, a costume contest, a spooky food spread and music. Often 40 to 80 guests.

**Family party.** A mix of kid-friendly games early, then adult activities later.

**Block party or neighborhood trunk-or-treat.** See [block party rentals in Chicago](/blog/block-party-rentals-chicago).

Themes can be as simple as classic monsters, haunted house, witches' brew, or a costume theme such as movies or decades. Pick a theme that makes decor and food easy to match.

## Food and drink ideas

Build a spooky menu with easy touches: "witch's brew" punch, "mummy" hot dogs, "graveyard" dip, deviled eggs as eyeballs, popcorn, candy and a dessert table. In cool weather, offer hot cider, chili and a hot cocoa station. See [hot cocoa bar for winter events](/blog/hot-cocoa-bar-winter-event) and [food station ideas and equipment](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment). For adult parties, add a signature cocktail. See [signature cocktail station ideas](/blog/signature-cocktail-station-ideas).

## Activities

Costume contests with categories (funniest, scariest, most creative), pumpkin carving or decorating, a photo booth with props, a haunted trail, trivia, a scary story circle and bobbing for apples if everyone is comfortable with it. For kids, a treasure hunt and a candy hunt. See [photo backdrop ideas](/blog/photo-backdrop-ideas-for-weddings-and-parties).

Seats are for a casual party where guests mingle. For a seated dinner, plan a seat for every guest. Use the lower bite count if dinner is served.

## Layout for costumes

Costumes can be bulky, with wings, hats and capes. Leave 36 to 48 inches between tables, avoid cluttered walkways and keep the dance floor open. Put the food on one side and the bar on another so crowds spread out. See [table spacing and aisle clearance](/blog/table-spacing-and-aisle-clearance) and [backyard party layout plan](/blog/backyard-party-layout-plan).

## Lighting and atmosphere

Warm lighting is easier and safer than total darkness. Use string lights, lanterns and battery candles. Add one or two accent lights such as orange or purple. Fog machines work for short moments, but check that they are allowed and safe near guests. See [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events), [lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events) and [battery powered event lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting).

## Safety

Keep candles enclosed or use battery candles, since costumes can be flammable. Keep walkways lit for trick-or-treaters and guests. Check that cords are not a trip hazard. Label food for allergies. If kids are trick-or-treating, plan an adult for every small group. See [candles at venues fire code rules](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules).

## Keeping warm

Tents, heaters and blankets make an outdoor party possible. See [heating a tent in fall](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall) and [patio heaters: how many per guests](/blog/patio-heaters-how-many-per-guests).

## Costume contest and the guest list

A costume contest gives guests a reason to dress up and breaks the ice early. Keep categories friendly: funniest, scariest, most creative and best group. Choose judges ahead of time, or let guests vote with a slip of paper. Small prizes such as candy baskets or a gift card are enough. For mixed groups, remind guests that masks and face paint are optional, and that costumes should be comfortable for dancing and eating. If kids are coming, set a clear time for the scarier parts of the party to begin after they leave.

## Candy, treats and trick-or-treaters

If you live on a street with trick-or-treaters, plan a separate candy station near the door so the party is not interrupted. Keep a bowl outside and assign one guest to watch it. Check treats for common allergens and offer non-food options such as stickers. Keep walkways lit and clear of cords and decor. Plan trash cans near the food and the candy table, since wrappers pile up fast.

## Chicago-area notes

Late October in the Chicago area can be anything from mild to freezing, with wind and rain. Plan for warmth and shelter: a tent with sidewalls, heaters, blankets and hot drinks. Check with your village about trick-or-treating hours and any rules for decor or noise. Many neighborhoods have community events and block parties, and some require a permit for street closures. See [Chicago block party permit](/blog/chicago-block-party-permit). Daylight ends early, so plan lighting for walkways and parking. If you host at home, tell neighbors about the party and parking.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a Halloween party?

Pick your crowd and theme, set the guest list, send invitations three weeks ahead, and plan food, activities, lighting and a layout. Plan for cold weather.

### What food should I serve at a Halloween party?

Serve easy spooky snacks, a main such as chili or sandwiches, a dessert table, and warm drinks. Label allergens.

### How many guests should I invite to a Halloween party?

Choose a number your space can hold with costumes. Kids parties are often 8 to 20, and adult parties 30 to 80.

### What are good Halloween party games?

Costume contests, pumpkin decorating, trivia, a haunted house, scavenger hunts and a photo booth. For kids, a treasure hunt and a costume parade.

### How long should a Halloween party last?

Two hours for kids and three hours for adults is typical.

### How do I keep a Halloween party warm?

Use a tent with sidewalls, heaters, blankets and hot drinks. Keep the food station near the warmth.

### Do I need to rent anything for a Halloween party?

Many hosts rent tables, chairs, linens, a tent and heaters. Plan seats for about half of guests at a casual party.
