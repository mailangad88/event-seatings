**Quick answer**

A good corporate picnic mixes 4 to 6 low-skill games (cornhole, giant Jenga, bocce, ring toss, tug of war), one team competition and a quiet zone with seating and shade. Plan about one game station per 30 to 40 employees, rotate people through in 15 to 20 minute blocks and keep a covered backup for rain.

The company picnic has one job: get people who rarely talk to each other to laugh together. Games are the tool, but a badly planned game area becomes a few enthusiasts in the center and everyone else holding a burger on the edges. This guide picks games that include everyone, explains the space each one needs and shows how to organize them for a Chicago-area crowd.

## Choose by participation, not by flash

The best games share three traits: simple rules, short rounds and no skill barrier. A game that takes 30 seconds to learn and 3 minutes to play keeps lines moving.

### Low-skill, anyone can join

1. **Cornhole.** Doubles teams, familiar to most Midwest crowds.
2. **Giant Jenga.** Always draws a crowd, good for mixed groups.
3. **Giant Connect 4.** Fast head-to-head games.
4. **Ring toss.** Easy for kids and families.
5. **Bocce.** Gentle, social, works for all ages.
6. **Lawn bowling or kubb.** A step up in strategy.

### Team and competition games

7. **Tug of war.** Needs a rope and a flat lawn; keep teams equal size.
8. **Three-legged or sack races.** Good for family picnics.
9. **Water balloon toss.** Hot-day relief; plan for cleanup.
10. **Trivia or scavenger hunt.** Mixed teams, a clipboard and a prize.
11. **Relay races.** Cross-department teams.

### Inclusive and low-motion

12. **Trivia or bingo tables.** Works for people who prefer sitting.
13. **Card and board games** at picnic tables.
14. **Photo scavenger hunt** using phones.
15. **Karaoke or music corner** with a mic.

## Sizing the game area

For 100 employees, plan 3 to 4 stations plus one team event. For 200, plan 6 to 8 stations. Space them at least 6 feet apart, and keep the sports lanes away from picnic tables and food lines.

## What to rent

Beyond the games, a picnic needs infrastructure.

- **Tables and chairs.** Picnic seating for 60 to 70 percent of staff at once, since many people will be playing. See [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).
- **Tents or umbrellas.** Shade for the food area and the quiet zone. See [market umbrellas for shade](/blog/market-umbrellas-for-shade).
- **A check-in table or signage.** A sign-up for team events helps.
- **Restrooms.** Parks and fields often have limited facilities. See [how many portable restrooms per guests](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests).
- **Trash and recycling stations.**
- **Cooling.** Ice, water coolers and misters. See [misting and shade for summer events](/blog/misting-and-shade-for-summer-events).

## Running the day

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Welcome</strong> and quick rules, 10 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Lunch</strong> and open play, 60 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Team competition</strong> with mixed departments, 30 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Open stations and dessert,</strong> 45 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Awards and thanks,</strong> 10 minutes.</li>
</ol>

Name a point person for each station and give them a one-page rules card. Pick a prize that is easy to hand out, like a trophy or an extra half-day off, so winners feel the effect.

## Chicago-area notes

Public parks, forest preserves and park district shelters have their own rules on reservations, alcohol, amplified sound and equipment, so check with the specific park district or forest preserve before you book. Late spring to early fall is the main window, and July is hot (the normal high at O'Hare is about 84 degrees F), so schedule active games in the morning and shade-based games in the afternoon. Indoor backup, such as a pavilion or a nearby banquet hall, protects the day if storms arrive.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Offer something for everyone. Put at least half your stations on level ground with seating nearby, and include a seated game. Provide water, snacks that suit common dietary needs and quiet spots. Ask employees in advance about mobility needs, and place at least one shaded area close to the parking and restrooms.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the best games for a corporate picnic?

Cornhole, giant Jenga, bocce and ring toss are reliable. Add one team event like tug of war or a scavenger hunt, and a seated option for people who prefer to watch.

### How many game stations do I need?

About one per 30 to 40 employees. A 100-person picnic does well with 3 or 4 stations plus a team event.

### What if it rains?

Have a covered backup: a pavilion, tent or indoor space. Many games, like trivia, giant Jenga and cornhole, can move under cover.

### How do I keep games inclusive?

Choose low-skill games, offer seated options and avoid forced competition. Mix departments in teams so people meet new coworkers.

### Do I need an event host?

For 100+ employees, yes. A host explains rules, runs team games and keeps the schedule.

### Can I serve alcohol at a park picnic?

Rules vary by park district and forest preserve, and many restrict alcohol. Check with the specific permitting office before you plan a bar.
