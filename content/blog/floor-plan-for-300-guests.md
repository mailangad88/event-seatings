**Quick answer**

A 300-guest seated event with dancing needs about 6,000 to 7,500 square feet: 30 rounds of 10, a 30 x 30 foot dance floor, a stage, four bars and 5-foot service aisles. Split the tables into two blocks on either side of a central dance zone so no table is more than about 40 feet from the floor.

</div>

Three hundred guests is the size of a gala, a large cultural celebration, a charity fundraiser or a big wedding. Many Chicago ballrooms are built for this number, but a tent or a banquet hall requires careful planning to avoid a room that feels crowded. The main idea at this size is to split the room into zones rather than one long grid.

That plan is efficient: 19 square feet per guest, which works for a dinner where the dance floor is shared with the aisle. For a roomier feel, use 7,000 square feet.

## Why split the room

A long single grid leaves the back tables far from the stage and the dance floor. Splitting into two blocks cuts the farthest distance in half. It also gives you two main service lanes down each side of the floor and a visual focus on the stage. The downside is that the dance floor needs to be wide enough that the two blocks do not feel disconnected.

## Cocktail hour for 300

A cocktail hour for 300 guests requires 6 to 8 square feet each, or 1,800 to 2,400 square feet standing, and bars at each end. If the ballroom is being set while guests drink, cocktail hour must be in a different room. See [cocktail reception layout and standing ratios](/blog/cocktail-reception-layout-standing-ratio).

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Hold guests outside the ballroom.</strong> Cocktail hour in the foyer or pre-function space.</li>
<li><strong>Set the room.</strong> Tables, linens, place settings, centerpieces, lighting.</li>
<li><strong>Open doors at a set time.</strong> Table cards or a clear seating display at the entrance.</li>
<li><strong>Seat in waves.</strong> Call tables by number so the doors do not jam.</li>
<li><strong>Serve dinner.</strong> Start with the head table or the stage-facing rows.</li>

## A tent for 300

A 300-seat tent dinner generally needs a 60 x 120 or larger structure, or two tents joined, with dance floor, bars and prep area. This is where a clearspan structure comes in. See our [clearspan tent guide](/blog/clearspan-structure-tent-rental) and the [tent size calculator](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need). ## Staffing and flow

For 300 guests, coordinate with your caterer on the number of servers and the serving plan. A buffet for 300 should have four lines or stations. Our [buffet line flow guide](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) covers timing. Plan restrooms, too: 300 guests need many more than a typical venue's default; confirm with your venue or a restroom trailer provider.

## A sample run of show for 300

Large events succeed on the schedule, not the table count. A typical wedding with 300 guests might run a ceremony, a 60-minute cocktail hour, 20 minutes to seat the room, 75 minutes of dinner and toasts, then dancing. Put those blocks next to your floor plan and check that each one has a space. The cocktail hour needs its own room or a patio, the ceremony needs seating that can be moved or flipped, and the dinner needs the full ballroom set before doors open.

If the same room hosts everything, a crew needs about 60 to 90 minutes to flip the space from ceremony chairs to dinner rounds. Ask your venue and rental company how long they need, and build that into the timeline. A 300-guest flip is easier when the chairs are stackable and the aisle is wide enough for carts. Our guide to [ceremony vs reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs) covers which chairs move and which stay.

## Accessibility

Place wheelchair-accessible spaces at several tables in different parts of the room, not only at the back, and keep a clear 36-inch route to them. See [ADA accessible event seating and pathways](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways).

## Chairs at 300

Three hundred chairs is a logistics project: stacking, loading, unloading and setting take a crew and several hours. Set the delivery time and ask how the company handles setup and breakdown, as covered in [chair setup and breakdown](/blog/chair-setup-and-breakdown-who-does-what). ## Frequently asked questions

### How big a room do I need for 300 guests?

About 6,000 to 7,500 square feet for a seated dinner with dancing. Under 5,500 square feet gets crowded.

### How many tables do I need for 300 guests?

Thirty 72-inch rounds of 10. Add small tables for registration, cake, gifts and guest book.

### How far should the farthest table be from the stage?

Try to keep it under about 40 feet. Splitting tables into two blocks keeps everyone close.

### How many bars for 300 guests?

Four bars is a common target, or three plus a service bar for beer and wine.

### Can I do a buffet for 300 guests?

Yes, with at least four lines or stations. Staggered table release keeps waits under 20 minutes.

### How many chairs for 300 guests?

300 for guests plus 20 to 30 spares for vendors, head tables and late changes.
