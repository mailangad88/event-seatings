**Quick answer**

Set tables by layer, not table by table. With a crew of 4 to 6 people, one person lays cloths, then others walk the room placing chargers, plates, flatware, glasses and napkins in a fixed order. A 100-guest setup of about 12 tables typically takes 60 to 120 minutes depending on crew size and complexity. Stage everything by table first, mark the first seat and check one table before doing the rest.

</div>

The difference between a calm setup and a frantic one is the plan, not the speed. A crew that moves in a loop with a fixed order of tasks finishes sooner than a crew where everyone does everything. This guide is for hosts, planners and volunteers setting rental dinnerware for a wedding, gala or fundraiser in Chicago or the suburbs. The timings here are planning estimates and depend on your crew, so test them on your first few tables.

## Crew roles

<div class="table-wrap">

| Role | Task | People for 100 guests |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | Marks seat 1, checks table 1, solves problems | 1 |
| Linen | Cloths and runners | 1 |
| Plates | Chargers, plates, bread plates | 1 to 2 |
| Flatware | Forks, knives, spoons | 1 |
| Glasses | Water and wine glasses | 1 |
| Finishers | Napkins, menus, centerpieces, numbers | 1 to 2 |

</div>

For fewer people, merge roles: one person can do flatware and napkins. For a larger crew, split the room into zones by table number.

## Before the crew arrives

Stage everything by table. Use carts, crates or sheet pans with labels. Count first: one table's worth of plates, glasses and flatware on one cart is a quick way to avoid missing pieces. Review [delivery and packing crates](/blog/dinnerware-delivery-and-packing-crates) for how rentals arrive. Check the floor plan, table numbers and the first seat on each table. See [place setting spacing](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements) for the measurements to use.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Lay out tables</strong> and chairs per the floor plan.</li>
<li><strong>Lay cloths</strong> and smooth seams.</li>
<li><strong>Mark seat 1</strong> on every table.</li>
<li><strong>Set one table</strong> and have the lead approve it.</li>
<li><strong>Roll out layers</strong> across the room.</li>
</ol>

## Timing: a planning estimate

For a 100-guest, 12-table dinner with a crew of 5, an experienced team often finishes within about 90 minutes after tables and chairs are in place. A first-time crew should plan for more. Test the speed on your first two tables and scale: if one table takes 8 minutes, 12 tables takes about 96 minutes of work split across the crew. Add buffer for hiccups and the final check.

## Tricks that save time

- **Use a measuring stick** or a seat template for plate placement. A 24-inch strip with marks ensures even spacing.
- **Carry in sets**: one tray with 8 plates and 8 chargers for a table.
- **Hold glasses by the base** and carry them in racks to avoid fingerprints.
- **Wear cotton gloves** for flatware to prevent smudges.
- **Check from the end of the table**: a squinted view reveals crooked settings.

## Chairs and tables first

The room must be ready before the layers begin. Tables go in first, then chairs, then linens. A crew can set chairs quickly if they are stacked near the tables; see [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need). For the final walk-through, count seats against the head count.

## Safety and breakage

Plates are heavy and glasses are delicate. Do not carry more than a crew member can safely hold, and use carts for crates. Keep an empty crate nearby for broken pieces, and note them for the return. See [rental breakage and loss fees](/blog/rental-breakage-and-loss-fees-explained).

## Who sets the table?

At a catered wedding, the caterer or venue often sets tables according to your floor plan. At a DIY or volunteer event, your own crew does it. Ask the caterer what they cover before you recruit helpers. Hiring a setup crew is separate from renting dishes, and rates vary, so we do not state any here.

## Teardown in reverse

Teardown runs in reverse order of the layers: decor, napkins, glasses, flatware, plates, cloths. Stack by piece type and crate them. See [do you wash rental dishes](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-dishes).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to set 12 tables?

With a crew of about 5, many teams finish in roughly 60 to 120 minutes once tables and chairs are placed. A first-time crew should add buffer.

### How many people do I need to set tables?

Four to six for 100 guests. One person per layer works well.

### What order do I set a table?

Cloth, chargers and plates, flatware, glasses, napkins, then decor and numbers.

### Should I set one table first?

Yes. Set one table, check spacing and look, and use it as a model for the rest.

### How do I keep settings even?

Use a seat template or measured strip, and mark seat 1 on every table. Check from the end of the table.

### Who is responsible for setup, the caterer or me?

It depends on your contract. Ask the caterer and the venue what they cover.
