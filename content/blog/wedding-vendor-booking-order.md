**Quick answer**

Book the venue first, since it sets your date and rules. Next book the vendors who take one wedding per day and sell out earliest: photographer, caterer if the venue does not provide one, band or DJ, and officiant. Then book the florist, rentals, cake, hair and makeup, and transportation. Finish with details such as stationery, favors and the cake.

</div>

It is tempting to start with the fun parts, like flowers and decor, but the order you book vendors changes your options. Vendors with limited calendars sell out first. Vendors whose work depends on your venue should wait until the venue is final. A simple order keeps you from paying deposits that you later regret.

## Tier one: venue and date

Everything depends on this. Once the venue is booked, ask about their preferred vendors, restrictions and rules for outside vendors. Some venues require outside vendors to provide insurance certificates, others require specific caterers or bartenders. Do not sign with vendors until you know the rules. See [how to choose a wedding venue in Chicago](/blog/how-to-choose-a-wedding-venue-chicago).

## Tier two: vendors that sell out first

Photographers, bands and DJs and caterers are limited by time, not supply. A single photographer can only work one wedding per day, and the best ones fill their calendar a year out. Book them as soon as your venue and date are set. If your venue has a required caterer, you can skip this step. An officiant also belongs here, especially for a religious ceremony or a specific person you want.

If you want a planner or coordinator, add them to this tier. They can help you negotiate contracts and sequence the rest. Our guide to [wedding planner vs day-of coordinator](/blog/wedding-planner-vs-day-of-coordinator) compares options.

## Tier three: design and supporting vendors

Florist, cake, hair and makeup, videographer, transportation and rentals come next. Rentals are often easier to book than people, but large orders, tents and specialty furniture can be limited on peak weekends. Ask for a quote early and place the order about four to six weeks before the wedding. Read the [complete wedding rental checklist](/blog/complete-wedding-rental-checklist) to avoid missing items.

Book hair and makeup as early as the wedding party is set, since they travel and may need multiple artists. Transportation can be booked later, but shuttles for guests should be reserved before the invitations go out.

## Tier four: stationery, favors and finishing touches

Invitations, programs, signage, favors and welcome bags come last because they depend on the final guest list and styling. Order invitations about four weeks before mailing, and others after the RSVP count is final. Keep a running list of the small things that appear late: place cards, a guest book, a cake knife, a toast flute. Keep your list in a [vendor tracker](/downloads/vendor-tracker) so nothing falls through.

## Lead times by vendor

Use these as starting windows, and move earlier for popular dates.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Vendor | Book about | Key question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Venue | 10 to 14 months out | Is my date available, and what is included? |
| Photographer | 9 to 12 months | How many hours, and when are photos delivered? |
| Caterer | 9 to 12 months | What is the service charge and final-count date? |
| Band or DJ | 8 to 12 months | What equipment do you bring, and how long is the set? |
| Officiant | 6 to 9 months | What is the ceremony length and rehearsal plan? |
| Florist | 6 to 9 months | Is delivery and setup included? |
| Rentals | 4 to 6 months, order 4 to 6 weeks out | What are delivery and pickup windows? |
| Cake | 4 to 6 months | Is delivery and a cutting fee included? |

</div>

Always get a written contract that lists the date, time, services, price and cancellation policy.

## How to book without overcommitting

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Set budget and date.</strong> You cannot negotiate without them.</li>
<li><strong>Book the venue.</strong> Then confirm its vendor rules.</li>
<li><strong>Request quotes from three vendors per category.</strong> Compare on the same package.</li>
<li><strong>Book the limited-calendar vendors.</strong> Photo, music, food, officiant.</li>
<li><strong>Order the rest in tiers.</strong> Flowers, rentals, paper, details.</li>
</ol>

## Avoiding double bookings and conflicts

Before you sign any contract, check that the date, start time and location are right, and that the vendor knows the venue rules. If two vendors need the same thing, such as power or a corner of the room, tell your venue early. Keep one shared calendar of arrival and breakdown times, and give the final version to everyone a week before. Many problems on the day are not mistakes but misunderstandings, and a shared schedule prevents most of them.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area vendor calendars fill up around peak wedding weekends, especially summer Saturdays and early fall. Downtown venues may require approved vendor lists, freight elevator reservations and insurance certificates, so ask before booking outside vendors. Suburban venues are often more flexible but may have load-in windows. For tents and public parks, permits can take time, so start the process early; check with your village or park district. Delivery windows for rentals can be tight in dense neighborhoods and downtown, so share building access details with every vendor. If you want to compare your options, ask each vendor for a written quote that lists the date, hours, services, fees and cancellation terms in the same format, then record all of them in one tracker. Side-by-side comparisons make it much easier to choose confidently, and they give you a record if a detail is later disputed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I book first for my wedding?

The venue and date, followed by the photographer, caterer, music and officiant. These are the vendors most likely to sell out.

### When should I hire a wedding photographer?

Many couples book nine to twelve months out, and earlier for peak weekends. Photographers can only work one wedding per day, so their calendars fill quickly.

### How early do I need to book a wedding caterer?

If your venue does not provide catering, book a caterer as soon as the date is set, usually nine to twelve months ahead. Ask about final-count deadlines.

### When should I order wedding rentals?

Request quotes early, then order about four to six weeks before the wedding. For tents, specialty furniture and peak weekends, reserve sooner.

### Do I need to book a DJ or band before a florist?

Usually yes. Musicians have limited availability, while florists adjust to your style and venue. Book the music first.

### Should I hire a planner before other vendors?

If you want one, yes. A planner can help negotiate contracts and recommend vendors. See [how much to tip wedding vendors](/blog/wedding-vendor-tipping-guide) as you finalize your budget.

### What should be in a vendor contract?

The date, times, services, price, deposit and payment schedule, cancellation policy, and any fees. Do not rely on email promises.
