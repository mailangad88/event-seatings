**Quick answer**

Charger plates are worth it for formal, plated dinners where you want a layered, photo-ready table and the budget allows one more item per guest. They are not worth it for buffets, casual parties or tight budgets, because chargers are decorative: nobody eats from them. A 12 to 13-inch charger protects the linen, anchors the setting and adds color, but it can be dropped without hurting the meal.

</div>

A charger is the big plate that sits under the dinner plate. It is removed before the main course or stays under it, depending on the caterer. Couples planning a wedding in a Chicago hotel ballroom or a suburban banquet hall often ask whether the look justifies another line on the rental order. The honest answer depends on the room, the linen and the style you want. This guide weighs it with no pitch, because chargers are optional.

## What a charger actually does

Chargers have three jobs. They frame the place setting by adding a ring of color or metal around the plate. They protect the tablecloth from sauce and drips between courses. They give servers a place to set the first course without bare linen showing. None of that changes the food.

## When chargers earn their place

- **Formal plated dinners** in ballrooms, where the table is the main décor.
- **Dark or patterned linens** that you want to frame the plate against.
- **Photos.** From the sides and above, a charger gives the table a finished look.
- **Small centerpieces.** If flowers are low, the charger fills the space with color.
- **Tables that sit empty before dinner.** Chargers make a table look set even before the food arrives.

## When to skip them

- **Buffets**, where guests take their own plates and the charger just gets in the way.
- **Family-style dinners**, where table space is needed for shared dishes. See [family-style serving ware](/blog/family-style-serving-ware).
- **Crowded tables**, such as 10 at a 60-inch round, where a 13-inch charger takes inches from every guest. See [place setting spacing](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements).
- **Tight budgets.** Instead of chargers, put the money toward a better chair or more tables.

## Material and finish

Chargers come in glass, metal, acrylic, beaded and woven styles. Gold and silver suit formal evenings, and natural rattan or wood suits garden and barn events. A matching color to your napkin makes a clean look. Our guide to [matching dinnerware to linens](/blog/matching-dinnerware-to-linens) goes into color pairing. Ask the rental company for a sample so you see the finish under your event's lighting.

## Cost drivers

We do not publish prices for dinnerware, but chargers are rented per piece, so the cost scales with guest count. Finish matters: simple styles cost less than beaded or specialty ones. Delivery, setup and return fees also add up, so request a [quote](/quote) with and without chargers to see the difference.

## What happens to them during dinner

At many weddings, servers remove the charger after the salad course and bring the dinner plate. At others, the charger stays under the dinner plate until dessert. Ask your caterer which they prefer. A charger that stays costs more in servers' attention than one that goes, because it adds weight and height under the plate. This also affects how many servers you need; our [crew table-setting guide](/blog/how-to-set-tables-fast-with-a-crew) covers labor.

## A middle path

If the budget is too tight for chargers at every seat, use them at the head table and sweetheart table only. You still get a focal point in photos. You can also use chargers at the first guest tables seen when guests enter. A table with a gold-rim plate already has a ring of metal, as covered in [gold-rim vs white china](/blog/gold-rim-vs-white-china), and may not need a charger at all.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the point of a charger plate?

A charger plate frames the setting, protects the tablecloth and gives servers a base for the first course. It is decorative and not meant to hold food. Many formal dinners use them for the look.

### Do you eat off a charger plate?

No. A charger stays empty. The server places a salad or dinner plate on top of it, and the charger may be removed before the main course.

### Are chargers necessary for a wedding?

No. They are optional. Many beautiful weddings use none. They help most at formal plated dinners with dark linens.

### How big is a charger plate?

A charger is usually 12 to 13 inches across, compared with 10 to 11 inches for a dinner plate. Check the specific pattern's size before you confirm the table layout.

### How many chargers should I order?

Order one per seat, plus a small cushion. Count the head table and any sweetheart table in the total.

### Can I use chargers at a buffet dinner?

You can, but it is rarely worth it. Guests pick up a plate at the buffet, so the charger sits empty or gets removed. Save the money.
