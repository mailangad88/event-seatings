**Quick answer**

A wedding rental budget has about ten categories: ceremony and reception chairs, tables, linens, dinnerware, flatware, glassware, bar equipment, tent and flooring, lighting and power, and delivery plus setup. The count in each is set by your guest count, and the price per piece is set by style, quantity and venue access. Chair estimates run about $5 to $7 for garden folding and $8 to $11 for cross-back; ask for written quotes on everything else.

</div>

Most couples set a total wedding budget and then discover rentals are scattered through it: some in the venue fee, some in the caterer's quote, some from a rental company. This guide shows how to list every category, find the biggest drivers and make a budget you can compare quote to quote. We won't invent percentages or prices for categories we don't publish, because they vary by company and season.

## Start with counts, not prices

Build your quantity first, then compare. A table that says 150 chairs, 19 rounds, 25 cloths, 160 dinner plates and so on is something you can send to three vendors. Our [reception rentals by guest count](/blog/wedding-reception-rentals-by-guest-count) guide gives starting counts.

## The biggest drivers

**1. Guest count.** Every guest adds a chair, a place setting, glasses and linens. Cutting 10 guests saves more than a downgrade on any one item.

**2. Style.** Premium chairs, specialty linens and gold flatware cost more than basic pieces. Our chair estimates show the spread: garden folding $5 to $7, cross-back $8 to $11, ghost $10 to $14, velvet $18 to $25 and Louis $20 to $30. See [wedding chair rental cost](/blog/wedding-chair-rental-cost).

**3. Number of setups.** Renting separate ceremony and reception chairs means two sets and two setups. A flip costs less. See [ceremony vs reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs).

**4. Site and access.** A tent on a lawn needs a crew, a permit and flooring. A ballroom with an elevator needs time. Stairs and long carries add labor.

**5. Date and season.** Peak Saturdays in summer and fall are in highest demand.

## Where couples overspend (and save)

- **Overspending:** upgrading every item instead of the ones in photos; renting extra tables nobody uses; forgetting what the venue or caterer already includes.
- **Saving without regret:** keep premium chairs for the ceremony and head table and use classic ones elsewhere; use lap-length linens on cocktail tables; use paper cocktail napkins and cloth dinner napkins.
- **Where not to cut:** delivery windows, enough spares, and a rain plan.

## Read the quote like a pro

Look for: the delivery and pickup windows, who sets up and who tears down, damage and loss fees, minimum orders, overtime charges, tax, payment schedule, cancellation and changes policy and insurance requirements from the venue. A quote that is cheaper per chair but adds a separate setup charge may cost more.

## A worked example of how to compare

Suppose two vendors quote the same 120-guest list. One shows a low price per chair and charges delivery, setup, pickup and a linen cleaning fee as separate lines. The other shows a higher price per piece but includes delivery, setup and pickup. The right comparison is the all-in total on the same quantities, plus the policies behind it: when do they arrive, how long do they stay and what happens if it rains. Put the two quotes side by side in a spreadsheet with one row per category and a column for each vendor. The gaps usually show up in the support items, like extra tables and spare linens, that one quote forgot.

When you find a gap, ask the vendor to add it in writing instead of assuming. Rental contracts are literal, and the pieces that are not on the order are not on the truck.

## Chicago factors

Downtown venues and high-rises often require certificates of insurance and loading dock reservations, and some charge for freight elevators. Suburban banquet halls may include tables and chairs. Tents in the suburbs usually involve a municipal permit. Ask your venue for its written rules before you request quotes.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I budget for wedding rentals?

It depends on your guest count, venue and style, so we don't give a single number. Build the quantity list, get itemized quotes and compare. Chair estimates range from $5 to $30 per chair depending on style.

### What is included in wedding rentals?

Typically chairs, tables, linens, dinnerware, flatware, glassware, bar equipment, tents, lighting and a dance floor. The venue or caterer may include some.

### Is it cheaper to flip ceremony chairs?

Usually yes, because you rent one set and pay one delivery. It requires crew time during cocktail hour.

### Do rental quotes include delivery?

Not always. Ask whether delivery, setup and pickup are included or separate, and whether stairs or long carries add charges.

### What are common hidden rental fees?

Delivery to difficult sites, after-hours setup, damage and loss fees, cleaning fees and minimum orders. Ask for them in writing.

### Should I rent from one company?

One company simplifies delivery and coordination, but compare itemized quotes. Our planning call helps you build the full list.
