**Quick answer**

The hard part of a surprise party is not the rental list, it is hiding the setup. Choose a venue or a location the guest of honor doesn't visit, schedule delivery and setup for the same day just a few hours before, and keep a 30 to 45 minute buffer between finishing setup and the arrival. Tell your rental company the party is a surprise so the truck, labels and calls don't give it away.

</div>

A surprise party puts two clocks on the day: the guest of honor's schedule and the delivery truck's. If they cross, the surprise is over before it starts. The solution is a plan that moves setup out of sight and gives every vendor the same message.

## Pick a location that stays quiet

The best surprise venues are places the guest of honor won't walk past. A friend's house, a banquet room, a restaurant's private room or a relative's backyard all work. If the party is at their own home, you need a decoy plan: get them out of the house for at least 3 to 4 hours.

## Tell your vendors it's a surprise

Rental companies call the main contact on the order, often by phone or text. Give them your number only, not the guest of honor's. Ask for delivery without a sign-off from the home, or have a trusted friend receive the truck. Ask whether the truck is marked, and plan around it: park it in the alley or a side street if you can. If you're at a venue, tell the manager the party is a surprise so staff don't greet the guest of honor by name.

## What to rent, shaped by the reveal

The reveal is a few seconds. Everything else is a regular party, so rent the same things you would for any gathering of that size and style, with a few tweaks.

- **Seating that is out of sight:** if the party is at their home, set up chairs and tables outdoors or in a back room, not in view from the front door.
- **A clear line from the door:** leave an open path so the group can face the entrance. A table in the middle of the room blocks the reveal.
- **A standing room first, seated later:** people usually stand and watch for the first 10 minutes. Cocktail tables near the entry are a better start than a full set of dinner tables.
- **Lights:** have a lighting plan so you can switch from dim to full light on reveal.

For counts by guest number, use [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [how many tables](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests).

## Where to hide things for a surprise at home

| Item | Where to hide it | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Chairs and tables | Garage or back patio | Garage the guest uses daily |
| Cake and food | Neighbor's fridge | Smells and sounds |
| Balloons and decor | Trunk of a friend's car | Door opening |
| Rental delivery | Same-day, side entrance | Truck out front |
| Guests' cars | Street two blocks away | Familiar plates |

## Your day-of timeline

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Morning:</strong> confirm delivery window with the rental company and the helper who receives it.</li>
<li><strong>Midday:</strong> set up tables, chairs, linens and food. Keep doors and blinds closed on the sides the guest could see.</li>
<li><strong>Buffer:</strong> walk the space, light candles or string lights, and place name signs.</li>
<li><strong>Guest arrival:</strong> park away, enter through a side door and text a group chat for a heads-up.</li>
<li><strong>Reveal:</strong> a decoy lures the guest of honor in at the planned time, and a lookout texts when they're 5 minutes away.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-specific notes

In the city, street parking and one-way alleys make hiding a truck harder. Ask your rental company for an arrival window of an hour or less and a phone call 15 minutes before arrival. In the suburbs, check driveway width and street parking rules. Many villages restrict overnight street parking, so ask neighbors before you stack cars. In winter, plan a coat area out of the entrance path. Also, if a tent is part of the plan, see [Chicago tent permit rules](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and check with your village.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far ahead should I book rentals for a surprise party?

Book as soon as the date is set, ideally 4 to 6 weeks ahead. Same-day delivery windows are the hardest to get on busy weekends, so ask early.

### Can the rental company deliver without telling the guest of honor?

Yes. Give your own contact information as the main contact, ask for a call before arrival, and name a person who will receive the delivery. Just say so when you book.

### What time should I have everything set up?

At least 45 minutes before the first guest arrives. That leaves room for a late truck or a missing item without panic.

### How many guests should I invite to a surprise party?

Smaller is easier to hide. Under 30 people is simple to keep quiet, while 60 or more needs a coordinator and a plan for parking.

### Where do guests park without giving it away?

A street or lot a couple of blocks away, with guests walking in together or entering through a side door. Ask a neighbor to host a few cars in their driveway.

### What if the guest of honor arrives early?

Have a lookout and a backup decoy. Text a group when they leave their location, and keep the lights low until the signal.
