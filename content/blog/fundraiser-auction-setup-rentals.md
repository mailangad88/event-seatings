**Quick answer**

A silent auction needs about one 6-foot table per 8 to 12 items, set along the walls at least 5 feet from the bar, with a clear path around each table. Add a covered checkout table, a staffed registration desk and a stage or podium for the live auction. Cover tables in floor-length black or neutral cloths so bid sheets and items stand out.

</div>

Auctions raise money when bidders can see, reach and stay near the items. They underperform when tables are crammed together or the bar sits in the middle of the walkway. The layout is simple math plus a few rules about flow. This guide covers a fundraiser in a Chicago-area banquet hall, church hall or school gym, whether you have 30 items or 150.

Items sit in groups: experiences, food and wine, gift baskets, art and sports. Group them so bidders can browse one category at a time. Bid sheets go in front of each item at about waist height. Elevate high-value items on a riser or cake stand so they can be seen from across the room.

Planning ranges. If items are large, such as furniture or a vacation package display, they take more space than baskets.

## Cloths, risers and lighting

Use a black or dark neutral cloth: it makes items pop and hides spills. Floor-length cloths cover boxes stored under the table. Small risers (12 to 18 inches) create layers. Easels hold framed items and signs. Light the tables with a clip lamp or a gentle uplight if the room is dim, since a bidder must be able to read the sheet. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

## Keep the bar away from the bids

People stand at bid sheets while holding drinks, and the best-selling tables are the ones near the entrance. Put the bar on the far side of the room and put the silent auction between the entrance and the bar. That way every guest walks past the items. Close silent bidding before dinner or before the live auction, then bring closed items to checkout.

## Checkout and payment

Checkout tables need chairs, a line space of 10 to 15 feet and power if you use card readers. Assign a volunteer to match each winner's number with the item. Set up the checkout near the exit, behind a rope or stanchion if you have one, so guests pay before they leave with their prize. A small table with a locked cash box handles tickets and donations.

## Raffles and legal notes

Illinois regulates raffles, and many counties and municipalities issue local licenses. We have not researched the rules for every place in the Chicago area, so check with your county clerk or village hall before you sell raffle tickets. Silent auctions generally do not have the same rules, but your venue's policies on selling alcohol and on guests' donations still apply. If you accept card payments, confirm the equipment works on the venue's Wi-Fi.

## Signage and bid sheets

Each item needs a clear description, a starting bid, a bid increment and, if you want, a "buy it now" price. Print numbers large enough to read from 3 feet away and use a black pen on a clipboard that is tied to the table. Check at least once during the night that sheets still have room. A volunteer at each section can answer questions and nudge bidders gently near the close. Announce closing times in advance and close sections in stages to avoid a rush at the checkout table.

## Live auction setup

A live auction needs a stage or riser so bidders can see the auctioneer, a podium, a microphone and a screen to show the item. Place bid paddles on each table. Seat the auctioneer's spotters among the guests, one per 4 to 5 tables. See [panel discussion and stage seating](/blog/panel-discussion-stage-seating) for stage and chair tips.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many tables do I need for a silent auction?

One 6-foot table per 8 to 12 items. A 75-item auction typically needs 7 to 9 tables plus a checkout table.

### Where should I put the silent auction?

Along walls between the entrance and the bar, so every guest walks by the items. Keep the aisles open.

### Do I need a license to hold a raffle in Illinois?

Illinois regulates raffles, and local licensing often applies. Confirm with your county clerk or village before selling tickets.

### What color tablecloths work for auction tables?

Black or dark neutral. It reduces glare, hides stains and makes items easier to see.

### How do I handle checkout?

Set a separate table near the exit with a line space, staffed by volunteers, with a card reader and cash box.

### How should I display expensive items?

On a riser at the end of a table or on an easel, with a printed description and a bid sheet nearby. Keep valuable small items in a locked case if the venue has one.
