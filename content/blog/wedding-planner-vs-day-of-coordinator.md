**Quick answer**

A full wedding planner helps from the start with budget, vendors, design and logistics. A partial planner steps in for specific tasks or a stretch of the process. A day-of coordinator takes over about a month out, confirms vendors, builds the schedule and runs the wedding day. Choose based on how much time you have and how much you want to hand off.

Couples often discover the need for help in the last month, when the details pile up and nobody is in charge of the schedule. The names vary, and so do services, which is why hiring someone starts with understanding what you actually need. The question is not which title sounds best but who will do which tasks.

## What a full wedding planner does

A full planner is involved from the early days: setting the budget, recommending venues and vendors, negotiating contracts, designing the look, managing the timeline and running the wedding weekend. It is the most hands-off option and often the most expensive, but it can save time and prevent costly mistakes. It suits couples with busy schedules, large or multi-day weddings or complex logistics, such as cultural ceremonies or travel. See [wedding planning timeline 12 months](/blog/wedding-planning-timeline-12-months) for how much work the early stages involve.

## What a partial planner does

A partial planner fills specific gaps. You might hire one to help with vendor selection, design or the final three months. Packages vary, so ask for a list of tasks. Partial planning suits couples who enjoy the creative parts but want help with logistics, or who started alone and realized that they need guidance. It costs less than full planning, but the boundaries matter. Write down what is included, what is not and who handles each vendor.

## What a day-of coordinator does

Despite the name, a day-of coordinator usually starts a month or more before. They meet you, review contracts, confirm vendor arrival times, build the day's timeline, run the rehearsal and manage the wedding day so that you and your family can enjoy it. They do not typically design the wedding or book vendors. They are a good fit when you have planned most details but want someone to execute them. See the [wedding day timeline template](/blog/wedding-day-timeline-template) to see what the plan looks like.

## Do you need one at all?

Not every wedding needs a professional. A small wedding at a venue with an on-site coordinator may be fine with a friend as helper. But if your wedding has multiple vendors, a ceremony and reception in different places, rentals, tents or out-of-town guests, an experienced person on the day pays off. Many venues provide a venue coordinator, but that person is focused on the venue, not your whole day. Ask what their role includes.

If you do not hire anyone, assign a trusted person to hold the timeline and vendor contacts so you are not fielding questions in your dress.

## How to choose

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>List your tasks.</strong> Mark what you want to do and what you want to hand off.</li>
<li><strong>Set a budget.</strong> Include planner fees in your overall plan.</li>
<li><strong>Interview two or three people.</strong> Ask about experience with your kind of wedding.</li>
<li><strong>Ask for a written scope.</strong> Hours, tasks, backup and who attends.</li>
<li><strong>Check the fit.</strong> You will spend a lot of time with this person.</li>
</ol>

## Questions to ask before you hire

Interview at least two people and ask the same questions each time. How many weddings do you handle on the same weekend? Who will actually be at my wedding, you or an assistant? What is included in your fee, and what costs extra? How many meetings, calls and venue visits are part of the package? How do you handle a vendor who does not show up? Can I speak with a couple who used you recently? A good planner answers directly and puts the answers in writing.

Also ask about their working style. Some planners prefer to make decisions and hand you options, while others want every choice approved. Pick the style that matches how involved you want to be. Finally, ask how they work with your rental company and caterer, because smooth hand-offs on delivery and setup are a sign of an organized professional.

## Chicago-area notes

In the Chicago area, ask planners about their experience with your venue type, such as downtown high-rises, lofts, suburban halls or park sites, since rules on load-in, insurance and parking differ. If you plan a tent wedding, a coordinator who is familiar with permits and rental logistics can be useful. Many venues have in-house coordinators, so clarify who handles vendors and the ceremony. Planners also help with winter and shoulder-season weather plans. Ask whether they will coordinate your rental delivery and pickup times, and share your rental provider's contact with them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a wedding planner and a day-of coordinator?

A planner helps with choices and logistics from the start, while a day-of coordinator steps in a month or two out to confirm details and run the wedding day.

### Is a day-of coordinator worth it?

For many couples, yes. They manage vendors, timing and problems so you can enjoy the day. They are most helpful when you have multiple vendors or locations.

### When should I hire a wedding planner?

Ideally soon after you set a date and budget, often nine to twelve months out. A day-of coordinator is usually hired a few months ahead.

### Does my venue coordinator replace a planner?

Not usually. A venue coordinator manages the venue's tasks, but may not manage outside vendors, your schedule or personal details. Ask what is included.

### What is a partial wedding planner?

A planner who covers part of the process, such as vendor selection, design or final-month logistics. The scope should be defined in the contract.

### How much does a wedding planner cost?

Prices vary widely by market, scope and wedding size. Get quotes from several people and compare scope, not just the number.

### Can a friend be my day-of coordinator?

Yes, if they are organized and not a guest. Give them the timeline and vendor contacts, and make sure they are not also in the wedding party.
