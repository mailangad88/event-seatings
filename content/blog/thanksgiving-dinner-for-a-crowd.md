**Quick answer**

For a Thanksgiving crowd, plan about 1 to 1.5 pounds of whole turkey per person, a seat for every guest, and a table setup that fits your room. Cook some dishes ahead, use a second oven or caterer for the turkey, set a firm serving time, and rent tables, chairs, linens and serving ware at least three weeks early.

Hosting Thanksgiving for 8 is a recipe. Hosting for 20 or 30 is a logistics problem. The oven can only hold one bird, the dining table seats ten, the fridge is full by Tuesday and the dishes pile up before dessert. The fix is to plan the schedule, the seating and the equipment together, and to lean on rentals and helpers.

## Count the guests and the space

Start with a firm headcount, including kids, and a plan for where everyone sits. A standard dining table seats 8 to 10. For 20, add a second table or rent long tables. Plan 20 to 24 inches of table width per person so no one is crowded. If space is tight, use a buffet for the food so the tables hold only plates and drinks.

## Turkey and food amounts

A whole turkey needs about 1 to 1.5 pounds per person, which includes bones and leftovers. A 20-pound bird feeds about 12 to 16 people. For 20 to 30 guests, cook two turkeys, or one turkey and a ham or turkey breast. For sides, plan about half a cup to one cup of each dish per person and expect guests to try most of them. A rough guide: 3 to 4 sides, 1 or 2 vegetables, rolls, gravy, cranberry sauce, and 2 or 3 desserts. See [how much food per person](/blog/how-much-food-per-person-for-a-party). Stay safe with raw poultry: thaw in the fridge, cook to 165 F in the thickest part and chill leftovers within two hours.

## Build a cooking timeline

A clear schedule keeps the day calm.

**Monday to Tuesday:** thaw the turkey, shop, clean the fridge and set up the table.

**Wednesday:** bake pies, make cranberry sauce, prep vegetables, set the table and chill drinks.

**Thursday morning:** roast the turkey, make stuffing and casseroles, and chill drinks.

**An hour before dinner:** rest the turkey, reheat sides, make gravy and set out appetizers.

**Dinner:** carve at the counter, serve buffet-style or family-style, and sit down together.

Use the oven in sequence, not all at once. A roaster, slow cooker and warming trays free up oven space. See [food safety at home parties](/blog/food-safety-at-home-parties) and [keeping food hot](/blog/keeping-food-hot-outdoors).

## Seating plan and the kids table

Seat grandparents near the kitchen and restrooms, and seat chatty guests across from each other. A kids table works well for children 4 and older, with their own plates and simple food. Place a trusted teen or adult nearby. Use place cards, since they save the confusion of finding a seat. See [kids tables and chairs for events](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events) and [seating chart for blended families](/blog/seating-chart-divorced-parents-blended-families).

## Buffet or family style

For 20 or more, a buffet saves table space and reduces passing. Place the buffet where guests can circulate in a loop, with plates at the start and silverware at the end. See [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) and [dinnerware sizes: buffet, plated, family style](/blog/dinnerware-sizes-buffet-plated-family-style). Family style looks warm but needs bigger tables.

## Delegate

Assign each guest a dish, a drink or a task. Ask a friend to handle drinks and another to handle dessert. Have a plan for dishes and leftovers, including containers to send food home.

## Table setting and decor

A simple look works: autumn linens, candles, small pumpkins and gourds. Keep the center of the table low enough for people to see each other. See [linen colors by season](/blog/linen-colors-by-season).

## Chicago-area notes

Thanksgiving in the Chicago area often comes with early snow, cold and wind, and it is also one of the busiest travel days. Arrange parking and coat space, because 20 coats take up a lot of room. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago). If you use a rental truck for tables and chairs, ask about delivery times before the holiday, as schedules can be limited. In condos and two-flats, coordinate with neighbors on parking. See [planning a November event in Chicago](/blog/planning-a-november-event-chicago). Check road conditions for guests traveling from the suburbs.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much turkey do I need for 20 people?

About 22 to 30 pounds of raw turkey, which usually means two birds. The rule of thumb is 1 to 1.5 pounds per person.

### How do I cook Thanksgiving dinner for a large crowd?

Plan a written schedule, use a roaster and slow cookers to free up oven space, cook some dishes ahead and ask guests to bring dishes.

### How far ahead should I thaw a turkey?

In the fridge, allow about one day per 4 to 5 pounds. A 20-pound bird needs about 4 to 5 days.

### How do I seat 20 people for Thanksgiving?

Combine tables or rent long tables, allow 20 to 24 inches per person, and use a kids table and place cards.

### Should I serve buffet or family style for a crowd?

Buffet saves table space and moves faster for 20 or more. Family style is cozy but needs bigger tables.

### What should I rent for a large Thanksgiving?

Tables, chairs, linens, plates, silverware, glassware and chafing dishes are common. Order three weeks ahead.

### How many side dishes do I need for Thanksgiving?

Three or four sides plus vegetables, rolls, gravy and cranberry sauce. Plan half a cup to one cup of each per person.
