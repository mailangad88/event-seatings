**Quick answer**

A chuppah or mandap is a four-post canopy structure, typically 6 to 10 feet wide and 8 to 10 feet tall, with fabric and florals on top. Confirm customs and required size with your officiant or family leader, then ask the venue about fire, anchoring, ceiling height and load-in time.

A chuppah is the canopy used in a Jewish wedding ceremony, and a mandap is the covered platform used in many Hindu and other South Asian weddings. Both are structures that define the ceremony space, and both are a large part of the ceremony photos. This guide is about the decor and rental side: size, setup, materials and logistics for a Chicago-area ceremony. The meaning, rules and customs belong to your family, rabbi, priest or pandit, so ask them first about what is required.

## Structure first, decor second

Both structures usually stand on four posts with a fabric or floral top. A chuppah is often open on all sides and may be freestanding or held by four people. A mandap often has four pillars, steps or a raised platform, and room inside for the couple, officiant and family members. Customs vary by community, family and region, so confirm what your officiant expects before you rent anything.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Feature | Chuppah | Mandap |
|---|---|---|
| Posts | Four, freestanding or held | Four pillars, often fixed |
| Footprint | Often 6 x 6 ft or larger | Often 8 x 8 ft to 10 x 10 ft or larger |
| Top | Fabric, tallit or floral | Fabric, florals, carved top |
| Platform | Rare | Often raised |
| Rental | Often a frame plus drape | Frame, pillars, platform, seating |

</div>

Footprints above are planning ranges; the exact size depends on the number of people who will stand or sit inside, so ask the community leader and the rental company.

## Sizing for the people inside

Count who will be inside the structure: couple, officiant, parents, witnesses, perhaps a small table for the ritual items. A chuppah that is too small leaves family out of the frame. A mandap needs room for a fire pit or ritual seating in some ceremonies, and that changes safety and venue rules.

## Decor options

- **Fabric drape** in sheer white, ivory or a color that fits the theme.
- **Florals** on the posts and top, supplied by the florist.
- **Greenery and candles** (battery candles when flames are not allowed).
- **Lighting** with warm string lights for evening ceremonies.
- **Seating inside** for the couple, such as a pair of [royal throne chairs](/chairs/royal-throne) or velvet chairs.

## Venue and safety questions

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Is a fire or incense allowed?</strong> Some ceremonies use a ritual fire. Ask the venue about fire rules, ventilation and insurance.</li>
<li><strong>What is the ceiling height?</strong> Mandaps and canopies can be tall.</li>
<li><strong>Can the structure be anchored?</strong> Outdoors, use weights.</li>
<li><strong>What is the load-in time?</strong> Platforms and pillars are heavy.</li>
<li><strong>How will the family move around it?</strong> Leave space for the processions.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago planning notes

The Chicago area has many places with experience hosting both ceremony types, from banquet halls to temples to hotels. Do not assume that a venue allows a ritual fire, a raised platform or heavy pillars. Ask early and in writing. Confirm any requirements with your community leader. Winter weddings need a covered entry and a dry path for guests.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Renting a structure before confirming customs and sizes with the officiant.
- Forgetting the ritual table and its power or fire needs.
- Choosing a structure taller than the ceiling.
- Skipping the weights outdoors.


## Frequently asked questions

### How big should a chuppah be?

Often about 6 x 6 feet or larger, depending on how many people stand inside. Ask your rabbi or officiant about customs.

### How big is a mandap?

Often 8 x 8 to 10 x 10 feet or larger, depending on the ceremony and whether a platform and ritual items are included.

### Can a chuppah or mandap be used outdoors?

Yes with anchoring and a wind plan. Check ground, wind and the venue's rules.

### Who decorates a chuppah or mandap?

Usually your florist or decorator adds fabric and flowers, while the rental company supplies the frame. Confirm what is included.

### Can you have a ritual fire indoors?

Only if the venue allows it. Ask about fire rules and ventilation, and talk to your officiant.

### Can I reuse the structure at the reception?

Often yes behind the head table or stage if it is easy to move. Ask the rental company about crew and timing.
