## Awards nights and team dinners

Sports banquets and company events add a few rules to the layout.

1. **Sight lines.** Seat the audience so no one has their back to the stage. At rounds, leave one side of each table empty or open.
2. **Podium and screen.** A screen needs space and darkness. Put tables beyond 8 to 10 feet from it.
3. **Award table.** Reserve a table for trophies, with a floor-length cloth.
4. **Photo spot.** A simple backdrop near the entrance keeps photos from crowding the dining area.
5. **Sound.** A speaker at the front with a monitor on the sides fills the room without being loud.

For more on stage and sound planning, see our guides on [dance floor size](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count) and [lighting](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events).

## Outdoor and forest preserve options

Hoffman Estates has forest preserves and parks nearby. Each site has its own rules and often requires a permit to reserve a shelter. Cook County Forest Preserves manage their picnic groves and require reservations; the village and park district manage others. Ask for capacity, tables provided, parking, alcohol rules and weather policy. For a backyard tent, the Village of Hoffman Estates publishes a tent permit procedure, so check with the building department before booking. Our [tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) lists the questions to ask.

## Comfort over a long evening

Banquets can last four hours or more. Comfort shows up in the chair seat, the room temperature and the walk to the restroom. Pick a padded or molded seat for long programs, and let guests know the schedule. If speeches are long, offer water at every table. Check that older guests are seated near an exit, not in the middle of the room.

## Registration, programs and sound

Banquets with programs need a front door that works. Put a registration table just inside the entrance, with name tags in alphabetical order and a table list on an easel. Plan one person per 50 guests at check-in so the line never reaches the sidewalk, especially on a cold Chicago-area night when people arrive in coats. A coat rack or a staffed closet near the door keeps tables clear.

For the program itself, keep speeches short and schedule them between courses, not during the meal. Test the microphone and projector before guests arrive, and have a backup. If the hall's sound system echoes, add soft elements: floor-length linens, draping or fabric panels absorb noise. Place the dance floor or open area away from the head table so conversation is still possible during the meal.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big a room do I need for 100 guests at rounds?

Roughly 1,200 to 1,500 square feet for tables alone, plus space for a head table, buffet, bar and dance floor. Ask the hall for its seated capacity.

### Should I rent chairs if the hall has them?

If you like the hall's chairs, no. Upgrade only when the style matters, such as for a head table or an upscale dinner.

### How many tables does an awards night need?

Guest tables plus a head table, an awards table, a registration table and a gift or raffle table.

### Do I need a permit for a backyard tent in Hoffman Estates?

Possibly. The village publishes tent permit information, so contact its building department before you reserve a tent.

### What chair is most comfortable for a long dinner?

A molded or padded seat, such as the shell chair, is a good pick. Ask about cushions for other chairs.

### Can the rental company set up the room?

Setup is part of your quote, so ask when you request it. Fees depend on the size of the job and access.
