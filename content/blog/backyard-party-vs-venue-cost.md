**Quick answer**

A backyard party is not automatically cheaper than a venue. A venue often bundles the room, tables, chairs, restrooms and staff, while a backyard needs you to rent or provide each of those. For small or casual parties the backyard often costs less. For larger or formal parties the totals can be close. Price both with the same guest count and compare the full total.

</div>

Everyone assumes the backyard is the budget choice, and then the rental quote arrives. Tent, tables, chairs, linens, restrooms, lighting, generator, delivery and a rain plan add up quickly. A venue can look expensive until you notice it includes the furniture, the staff and the bathrooms. The honest answer comes from building both budgets line by line.

## What each option usually includes

A backyard gives you the space free, but little else. You will likely need a tent or canopy, tables and chairs, linens, serving equipment, extra restrooms for bigger crowds, lighting, trash handling, and cleanup. Our [backyard event space size guide](/blog/backyard-event-space-size-guide-chicago) helps you decide if your yard can hold the crowd.

A venue usually includes the room, climate control, basic furniture, restrooms, parking and sometimes staff. You may still pay for catering, decor, security, insurance and overtime. Ask for a written list of inclusions before you compare.

## Rough Chicago-area cost ranges

These are broad, rough estimates for the Chicago area and vary a lot by season, day of week, guest count and style. Treat them as starting points and get several quotes.

## How to compare the two fairly

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Fix the guest count and date.</strong> Use the same numbers for both budgets.</li>
<li><strong>List every need.</strong> Seating, shade, restrooms, power, parking, staff, cleanup.</li>
<li><strong>Get a venue quote with inclusions.</strong> Ask for what is and is not included.</li>
<li><strong>Get a rental quote for the yard.</strong> Include delivery, setup, pickup and a tent if the weather is a risk.</li>
<li><strong>Add the hidden costs.</strong> Permits, insurance, extra trash service, rain plan.</li>
<li><strong>Compare totals and effort.</strong> Decide whether the savings are worth the work.</li>
</ol>

## The costs people forget

At home, the forgotten items are restrooms, lighting, power for a caterer, lawn repair after a tent, parking help and a plan for rain; see [tent and grass damage](/blog/tent-and-grass-damage) and [restroom trailers](/blog/restroom-trailer-when-you-need-one). At a venue, the forgotten items are overtime, service charges, corkage, decor limits and the cost of rentals if the venue does not supply enough. Our guide [how to save money on event rentals](/blog/how-to-save-money-on-event-rentals) offers tips for both paths.

## A worked way to compare

Create two columns, one for the backyard and one for the venue, and list every item from restrooms to cleanup. Fill in real quotes, not guesses, and give a dollar value to your own time if you are doing setup. Then add a ten percent contingency to each column. Many hosts find that the decision becomes obvious once the columns are side by side, and a few discover that the cheaper option is the one with fewer hidden pieces.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago backyards are often small, and city lots may not fit a tent, a caterer's prep space and a crowd. Check our [two-flat and greystone backyard guide](/blog/chicago-two-flat-greystone-backyard-party) for tips. Tents can need permits in the city and in many suburbs; see [Chicago tent permit](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and [suburban tent permits](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs), and check with your own village. Neighbors, noise rules and street parking also matter. Venues in suburbs may have lower rates on weekdays, but the drive may matter to guests.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a backyard party cheaper than renting a venue?

Often for small, casual parties, yes. For larger or formal parties, the cost of a tent, rentals, restrooms and cleanup can bring the totals close to a venue. Build both budgets and compare.

### What hidden costs come with a backyard party?

Restrooms, lighting, power, trash service, lawn repair, parking help and a rain plan are common. Delivery and pickup fees for rentals also add up.

### What is included in a venue rental?

It varies. Many include the room, tables, chairs and restrooms, while some leave catering, decor and staff to you. Always ask for a written list.

### How many guests can fit in a backyard?

As a rule of thumb, plan about 10 to 15 square feet per seated guest, more with a dance floor or buffet. Our [square feet per guest guide](/blog/square-feet-per-guest-by-event-style) has details.

### Do I need a tent for a backyard party?

If the weather could ruin the event or there is no shade, a tent is smart. See [what size tent do I need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

### How far ahead should I order backyard rentals?

Four to eight weeks is common, and earlier for peak months or large tents. Ask your rental company about their calendar.

### Which option is less work?

A venue usually, because staff handle more of the setup and cleanup. A backyard gives you more control and more tasks.
