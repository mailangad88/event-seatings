**Quick answer**

Put the main bar where guests pass it after the entry but not in the entry path itself, at least 15 to 20 feet from the dance floor speakers and away from the dining tables, with 4 feet of queue space in front and a service door or power source behind it. For 100 or more guests, use two bars: one in the cocktail area and one near the dance floor.

</div>

The bar is a magnet. Wherever you place it, guests will gather, queue and linger. A bar in the wrong spot creates a bottleneck at the door, blocks the path to the restrooms or fills the dance floor with people holding drinks. A few minutes with a sketch of your Chicago banquet hall, loft or tent saves all of that.

**A. Corner bar.** One bar in a back corner, away from the door. Good for small crowds under 75 and for rooms with a service door behind the corner. Guests pass the dining area on the way, so sound and lines can intrude.

**B. Two bars.** One in the cocktail area and one at the edge of the dance floor. Best for 100 guests or more. It splits the line and keeps people from crossing the whole room.

**C. Wall bar.** One long bar along a side wall with a wide queue lane in front. This suits a long banquet hall and a plated dinner where most guests go to the bar at the same time.

## Rules for placement

<div class="table-wrap">

| Rule | Why | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| Away from the entry | Avoid a door jam | At least 15 to 20 ft inside the room |
| Away from speakers | Guests need to order | Keep out of speaker line of fire |
| Queue space | Lines form at peak | 4 ft clear in front, 6 ft at 150+ guests |
| Service access | Bartenders restock | Near a service door or aisle |
| Power and water | Ice machines, lights | Check outlets; most bars need none |
| Not in the sun | Ice melts | Shade for outdoor bars |
| Away from the dining tables | Noise and traffic | At least 8 to 10 ft |

</div>

These distances are rules of thumb, not codes. A fire marshal or venue manager may have different clearance requirements, so ask your venue about aisle widths and exits.

## Count the space

A bar of 8 feet needs about 8 feet by 12 feet of floor for the bar, the bartender zone and the queue, plus 3 feet behind for staff. Two bars need about double. See [bar setup for 100 guests](/blog/bar-setup-for-100-guests) for dimensions, and [mobile bar vs back bar](/blog/mobile-bar-vs-back-bar-rental) for how deep each piece is. If you also have a coffee, water or mocktail station, place those away from the main line: see [water station ideas](/blog/water-station-infused-water-dispenser).

<li><strong>Mark the entry, exits, restrooms and service door</strong> on your floor plan.</li>
<li><strong>Place the dance floor and tables</strong> first.</li>

<li><strong>Add a second bar</strong> if you have 100 guests or more.</li>

## Common mistakes

- **Bar next to the DJ.** Guests cannot hear the bartender, and the bartender cannot hear a drink order.
- **Bar by the door.** Guests arrive, stop and block the entry.
- **One long line across a dance floor.** Guests crossing the floor with drinks causes spills.
- **No place to set glasses.** Add cocktail tables near each bar. See [how many glasses per guest](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest).
- **No sight line to the restrooms.** People wander, and lines tangle.

## Chicago room types

A hotel ballroom usually has a built-in service corridor, so a bar near that door is easy. A loft or gallery in a former warehouse may have no kitchen, so the bar needs ice, water and glass storage nearby. In a barn or tent in the suburbs, place the bar near an entrance to the tent for deliveries but away from the walkway. Always ask your venue where outlets, doors and drains are. Weather matters outdoors; a bar under a canopy beats one in the sun, and our [Layouts and Floor Plans](/blog/topic/layouts-and-floor-plans) section covers site basics.

## Seating near the bar

Put a few tall tables and some chairs near the bar for guests who want to linger, but keep them out of the queue zone. A comfortable [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) or a [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) in a small lounge area makes a good anchor. For how many chairs to plan in total, see [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should the bar go at a wedding?

In the cocktail area, away from the entry, speakers and dining tables, with 4 feet of queue space and a service door nearby.

### How far should the bar be from the dance floor?

About 15 to 20 feet if speakers face the floor. A second bar can sit at the edge of the dance floor if you plan a queue lane.

### Do I need one bar or two?

One bar works for under 75 guests. Use two bars at 100 guests or more, especially if the cocktail hour is short.

### How much space does a bar take?

An 8-foot bar needs about 8 by 12 feet including a queue, plus 3 feet behind for bartenders.

### Should the bar be visible from the entrance?

Visible, but not in the entry path. A bar visible from the door signals the party but should sit 15 to 20 feet inside.

### Can the bar go outdoors?

Yes, with shade, a level surface and a source of ice. Check venue and park rules about alcohol.
