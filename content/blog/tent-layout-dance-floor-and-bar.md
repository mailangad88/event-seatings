**Quick answer**

For 100 seated guests with dancing, use a 30 x 60 ft tent (1,800 sq ft): about 10 round tables of 10, a 16 x 16 ft dance floor in the middle, the bar near an entrance, and the DJ at one end. Keep main aisles at least 5 feet wide and put the bar away from the dance floor and the doors so lines do not block traffic. Place the buffet or food station on a side that is near the caterer's entrance.

</div>

Tents force a layout decision that a banquet hall makes for you. There is no fixed bar, no stage and no built-in exit, so everything you add must fit in a rectangle. A good tent layout puts the dance floor at the heart, the bar on the edge and the food where the caterer can reach it. This guide shows a plan and the numbers behind it.

## The numbers to plan with

| Element | Rule of thumb | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dance floor | 3 to 4.5 sq ft per dancer, for a third to half your guests | 100 guests: about 16 x 16 ft |
| Round table (60 in) | About 8 to 10 sq ft of table plus chairs; allow 10 to 12 sq ft per guest overall | Chair backs need 36 in clear |
| Main aisle | 5 ft | Wider near the bar and exits |
| Bar | About 8 x 12 ft with queue space | One bartender per 50 guests is common; ask your caterer |
| DJ | About 6 x 10 ft | Needs power |
| Buffet | 10 ft clear lane for the queue | Access from both sides |

## Where each piece goes

**Dance floor.** Center it so the dancers are surrounded by guests and the DJ can see the whole tent. If the floor sits at one end, the far tables feel left out. Dance floors need a level base. See [tent flooring and subfloors](/blog/tent-flooring-and-subfloors).

**Bar.** Put it near an entrance, so guests get a drink on the way in, and away from the dance floor so queues do not block dancers. A second bar by the dance floor helps for 150 or more guests.

**DJ or band.** Place them on a short end, facing the dance floor, and not next to a table of grandparents. Keep speakers off the walls so sound does not rattle the sidewalls.

**Food.** Buffets and stations need to be near the caterer's door or prep tent. Plan how the caterer's path avoids guests.

**Head table or sweetheart table.** Put it where everyone sees it. See [sweetheart table chair ideas](/blog/sweetheart-table-chair-ideas).

## Keep exits and aisles clear

Fire codes ask for clear exits and aisles in tents. Northwestern University's tent safety guidance, for example, asks for aisles at least 36 inches wide with exits kept clear at all times. Chicago's permit process asks for egress details on the floor plan for tents over 600 square feet. Do not place a bar, a photo booth or a buffet in front of a door. See [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).

## Cocktail hour and ceremony

If your ceremony and cocktail hour are in the same tent, flip the room: ceremony chairs first, then crews turn it into the dinner plan. That needs a crew and time. Many hosts hold cocktails outside the tent instead. See [ceremony vs reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs).

## Seating that fits the plan

Chairs set the feel and the footprint. A slim chair like a [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) or [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) keeps aisles open. Chairs with arms need more room, so allow extra space for [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining). To size the tent for your own plan, read [what size tent you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should the dance floor go in a tent?

In the center, so guests surround it and the DJ sees the whole room. An end-of-tent dance floor works for small tents. It needs a level base.

### How big should the dance floor be?

Plan 3 to 4.5 square feet per dancer and assume a third to half your guests dance at once. For 100 guests, a 16 x 16 foot floor is common.

### Where should the bar go in a tent?

Near an entrance and away from the dance floor. That keeps lines out of traffic and lets guests get drinks as they arrive.

### How much space do I need between tables?

Allow at least 5 feet for main aisles and about 36 inches between chair backs. More is better near the bar and exits.

### Can the bar and kitchen be inside the same tent?

Bar yes, cooking no. Many codes keep cooking and open flame away from guest tents. Use a separate prep tent and ask your village.

### How do I plan for 150 guests with dancing?

Plan about 2,250 square feet at 15 sq ft per guest, so a 30 x 60 tent is tight and a 40 x 60 (2,400 sq ft) is comfortable. Add a second bar.
