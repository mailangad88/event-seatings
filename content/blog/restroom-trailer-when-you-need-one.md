**Quick answer**

You need a restroom trailer when your venue has no indoor restrooms or too few of them for your guest count, and you want guests in formal clothing to have running water, mirrors and climate control. For a short, casual event, standard portable units can be enough. Trailers need a flat, firm pad, truck access and usually power and a water source, or a self-contained setup, so confirm all of it with the vendor.

</div>

Weddings at barns, farms, vineyards and backyards, plus galas in tents, share a problem: the venue is beautiful but its bathrooms are not built for 150 guests in formal wear. A restroom trailer is the upgrade that makes an outdoor event feel like an indoor one. It is also an item that needs space, hookups and planning, so decide early. This guide explains when a trailer is worth it and what to check.

## Trailer, standard unit or indoor restrooms?

<div class="table-wrap">

| Option | Best for | Typical features | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venue indoor restrooms | Any event with enough fixtures | Real plumbing, full size | Count fixtures vs guests |
| Standard portable units | Casual, short, outdoor events | Basic stall, hand sanitizer | Heat, smell, small space |
| Deluxe portable units | Mid-size events | Sink, flushing, lighting | Limited space |
| Restroom trailer | Weddings, galas, VIP crowds | Multiple stalls, sinks, mirrors, A/C and heat | Footprint, hookups, access |

</div>

The details vary by vendor, so ask what each option includes.

## When a trailer makes sense

1. **The venue has no restrooms or too few.** Ask the venue how many toilets it has and how that compares to the guest count. See [how many portable restrooms per guests](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests).
2. **The dress code is formal.** Guests in gowns and suits need room to move and a mirror.
3. **The event is long.** Over 4 hours, ratios increase and standard units get hot and unpleasant.
4. **You expect alcohol.** Guests visit restrooms more often. Industry guidance adds 15 to 20 percent more units when alcohol is served.
5. **The weather is extreme.** A trailer with A/C or heat is better in a Chicago July heat wave or a cool October night.
6. **Accessibility matters.** Many trailers include an ADA-accessible unit or ramp. Read about [ADA restroom considerations](/blog/ada-restroom-considerations-for-events).
7. **You want a better guest experience.** A calm, clean restroom is part of the event feel.

## When standard units are fine

A short daytime picnic, a field day or a work-site event may do well with a few standard portable units. They are lighter, cheaper to place and need less ground prep. They also fit in smaller spaces, such as the edge of a parking lot.

## Questions for the vendor

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Dimensions and pad:</strong> exact size, leveling needs, ground type.</li>
<li><strong>Hookups:</strong> power draw and water, or self-contained tanks.</li>
<li><strong>Capacity:</strong> number of stalls and urinals, and ADA features.</li>
<li><strong>Service:</strong> attendant, restocking and mid-event pumping if needed.</li>
<li><strong>Insurance and permits</strong> the vendor carries.</li>
</ol>

Ask whether the vendor supplies an attendant, since keeping supplies stocked during a wedding makes a visible difference.

## Chicago and suburb permit notes

Temporary structures and sanitation facilities may be regulated by your village, county health department or park district, and rules differ across Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake and Will counties. Do not assume a permit is or is not required. Call your village or municipal office and the county health department early, and ask your venue what they require. If the event is on public land, the permitting office will tell you.

## Placement tips

- Keep the trailer within a short, lit walk of the tent, but out of the sightline of the main tables and ceremony.
- Place it downwind of dining if possible.
- Avoid low spots that flood after rain.
- Add a path with firm footing. Gravel or boards beat bare grass in heels.
- Provide signs at the tent and string lights on the path. Our guide to [lighting and power](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power) covers how to light a path.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a restroom trailer for a wedding?

Only if the venue lacks enough indoor restrooms. A trailer is common for tented outdoor weddings, barns and farms where formal guests need comfort. Ask the venue how many fixtures it has.

### How much does a restroom trailer cost?

Prices vary by size, season, distance and services, so request quotes from local vendors. We do not publish pricing for restroom trailers.

### How much space does a restroom trailer need?

It depends on the model, but plan for a flat pad, a clear truck route and extra room for steps and a walkway. Ask for exact dimensions in writing.

### Does a restroom trailer need electricity and water?

Many need power for lights, heat and A/C, and water via a hose or onboard tank. Some models are self-contained. Confirm with the vendor.

### Can a restroom trailer be placed on grass?

Often yes if the ground is dry and firm, but soft turf can sink or be damaged. The vendor may require mats or a gravel pad.

### How many guests can a restroom trailer serve?

It depends on the number of stalls. Use the ratio guidance in our post on [portable restroom counts](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests) and ask the vendor how many stalls the trailer has.
