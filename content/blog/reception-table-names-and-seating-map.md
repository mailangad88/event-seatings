**Quick answer**

Name tables with a theme guests recognize (streets, L stops, places you have traveled, songs, flowers) and keep every name to one or two words. Print a seating chart at least 24 x 36 inches near the entrance, sorted alphabetically by guest, and put the name on a stand 12 to 18 inches above each tabletop. Always include a number or letter on the sign so guests can find the table at a glance.

</div>

Table names add personality to a reception, and the seating chart is the first thing guests see. A good one saves 10 minutes of confusion at the door. A bad one leaves guests wandering with a cocktail. For weddings in Chicago, theme ideas come easily because the city is full of names with meaning.

## Theme ideas

Choose names your guests recognize without explaining. A name that needs a note will confuse people. Stick to the same theme for all tables.

## Seating chart formats

- **Alphabetical list.** Fastest to read. Best for 100 or more guests.
- **Mirror or board with names.** Pretty, but harder to read.
- **Escort cards.** Guests pick up a card with the table name. Easiest for guests, takes more setup.
- **Table list.** Names grouped by table. Better for small weddings.
- **Digital screen.** Needs power and a screen. Check with your venue.

## Readability rules

- Use a sans-serif or simple script font at 1 inch tall for names on the chart.
- Use high contrast: dark text on light or the reverse.
- Keep table names to 1 or 2 words.
- Pair each name with a number, such as "Pilsen (7)."
- Put the sign at the same height at every table. See [table numbers and placement](/blog/table-numbers-and-placement).

## Seating strategy

A seating chart is also a puzzle. Seat people who know each other together, mix a few new faces at each table and keep rivals apart. Seat older guests away from speakers and close to restrooms. Kids go near parents. Seat vendors where they can see the room. See [how many tables for your guest count](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) to avoid half-empty tables.

## Making it work for cultural events

Many Chicago celebrations seat families together by relationship rather than by name. Ask elders who they want to sit near, and give families a table label that fits your event. Check our [cultural celebrations guides](/blog/topic/cultural-celebrations).

## Day-of printing checklist

- A master list for the caterer and the venue.
- Two copies of the chart in case one is damaged.
- Extra blank cards for late changes.
- Tape and small tools for last-minute fixes.

## Seating map examples for different room sizes

**50 guests.** One board with table names and guests listed under each, at 24 x 36 inches. Six tables are easy to scan.

**100 guests.** Alphabetical list on a 30 x 40 inch board, with each guest's table name and number. Add a small floor map in the corner showing where each table sits.

**150 or more guests.** Two boards, split into A to L and M to Z, each at 30 x 40 inches. Hire a helper to greet guests at the chart if the line grows.

## Adding a floor map

A simple map helps guests find their way. Draw the room as a rectangle with the entrance, the head table, the dance floor and the tables, then print it small on the chart or on a card. Mark restrooms and the bar. Guests in a large Chicago ballroom appreciate a visual reference, especially at hotels where rooms have columns and sections. Keep it clean: fewer than 12 words on the map.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Table names that look alike, such as Lincoln Park and Lincoln Square.
- A chart so decorative that the names are hard to read.
- Putting the chart in the doorway so the line blocks guests entering.
- Last-minute changes that are not copied to the master list.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are good names for reception tables?

Chicago neighborhoods, L lines, places you have traveled, songs or flowers. Pick a theme guests know.

### Should I use names or numbers?

Either works. Pair a name with a number so guests can find the table easily.

### How big should a seating chart be?

At least 24 x 36 inches for 100 guests, and larger for more. Make sure the text is readable from 3 feet away.

### Where should a seating chart go?

Near the entrance, with room for a line. Avoid a narrow hallway.

### Do I need escort cards and a seating chart?

You need one or the other. Many couples use both.

### Can guests choose their own seats?

Yes, for small or casual events. Large weddings work better with assigned seating.
