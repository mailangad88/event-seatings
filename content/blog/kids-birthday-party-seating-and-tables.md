**Quick answer**

Seat kids at long tables so you can reach every child, and seat the adults at a separate round table or a row of chairs within sight. Plan one 6-foot table for every 6 to 8 kids aged 5 and up, and one seat for every kid at cake time, even if they roam during activities. For 20 kids, you will usually want 3 long tables, 20 child chairs or standard chairs on cushions, and 8 to 10 adult chairs.

</div>

A kids party runs in bursts: chaos, sit-down, sugar, chaos. Seating only has to work for about 30 to 40 minutes of that, at cake and food time, but it has to work fast. The layout should let one adult manage the whole group, and let the other adults sit without being in the way.

## Match the table to the age

Rental tables are 30 inches tall. That is fine for kids around 6 and older, but younger kids may be more comfortable at a low table. Ask when you request a quote what kid-size sets are available. If you can't get a low table, use a standard one and seat the youngest on cushions or booster seats.

## Count for a party of any size

- **Kid tables:** divide kids by 7 and round up.
- **Kid chairs:** one per child at cake time.
- **Adult chairs:** half the parents who stay, plus grandparents. Many drop-off parties need almost none.
- **Cake and gift tables:** one 6-foot table each, or one table with a corner for each.
- **Food:** one 6 or 8-foot table. Pizza boxes stack, so a single table often works.

The 24 inches of elbow room is a common event planning rule of thumb for adults, and kids need slightly less. A 6-foot table can seat 6 to 8 children comfortably.

## Setup details that save you at the party

1. Put name cards or colored placemats at each seat so you can seat friends together and split up pairs who are trouble together.
2. Cover tables in disposable paper rolls, or rented cloths with a clear vinyl overlay for easy cleanup. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for the right sizes.
3. Keep spare napkins at every table. A stack of 10 per child is not an exaggeration. See [how many napkins per guest](/blog/how-many-napkins-per-guest).
4. Put water or juice on the tables before kids sit down, so no one wanders for a cup.
5. Place the cake table at the head of the long tables so every kid has a view.

## Indoor or outdoor in Chicago

In summer a backyard is the easiest, but the shade matters. Tables in full sun get hot and sticky fast. A tent or canopy over the eating area also saves cake from a surprise shower. In spring and fall a basement or party room might beat the backyard, and for winter birthdays, you may be using a party room at a park district facility, which often has its own tables and chairs. Check what's included before you rent more. For bigger parties at home, [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) is a good starting point.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many kids can sit at a 6-foot table?

Six to eight children aged 5 to 8 fit comfortably at a 6-foot banquet table, with 3 or 4 on each side. Older kids need a little more elbow room, so use 6 for ages 9 and up.

### Do kids need special chairs at a birthday party?

Not usually. Kids aged 5 and up can use standard folding chairs, ideally with a cushion for the shortest ones. Younger kids may do better with child-size chairs or a high chair.

### How many adult chairs should I rent?

Plan seats for about half of the adults who will stay, plus any grandparents. At a drop-off party, you may need only a handful. At a party where parents stay, plan for a seat for each adult.

### Should kids sit at round tables or long tables?

Long tables are easier to supervise and serve. Round tables work for older kids who like to talk, but they are harder for one adult to reach. Pick long tables for kids under 8.

### How much space do I need for a kids party?

Plan about 10 square feet per seated child and an extra open space for games. A 20 x 30-foot tent handles about 30 kids at tables, with a lawn for play around it.

### What time should the kids sit down to eat?

About 60 to 90 minutes into the party, once the first activity has burned off energy. Do cake right after the meal, in the same seats, so kids aren't moving twice.
