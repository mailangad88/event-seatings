**Quick answer**

Plan a two-hour party with a loose script: 15 minutes of arrival and free play, 30 to 40 minutes of structured games, 20 minutes for food, 10 to 15 minutes for cake and singing, and a short wrap-up with favors. Anyone who has hosted a room of six-year-olds knows the problem: too much empty time and the energy goes sideways, too many rules and everyone melts down. A simple timeline gives you structure without making the party feel like school. The right games for the age group do the rest.

## A sample two-hour timeline

Adjust the times to your start. The proportions matter more than the clock.

**0:00 to 0:15, arrival and free play.** Have an easy activity ready at the door, such as coloring, stickers or a bin of toys. Kids arrive at different times, and this keeps the first ones busy. Set out name tags if guests are different ages or from different groups.

**0:15 to 0:50, structured games.** Run two or three games of 10 to 15 minutes each. Start with a high-energy one, then a calmer one, then a team or group game.

**0:50 to 1:15, food.** Seat kids at tables with a plate. Use a simple menu such as pizza, fruit and veggies. Keep a table plan so adults can supervise easily. See [kids birthday party seating and tables](/blog/kids-birthday-party-seating-and-tables).

**1:15 to 1:30, cake and singing.** Have the candles, lighter, knife and plates ready. Gather everyone in one spot for photos.

**1:30 to 2:00, free play, craft or piñata.** A craft or piñata keeps the energy going until pickup. Hand out favors as kids leave.

Keep a few extra minutes at the end for parents who arrive early or late.

## Games by age group

**Ages 2 to 4:** keep it short and simple. Bubbles, a sensory table, a parade with instruments, ring toss with soft rings, or a small ball pit. Adults should stay close, and parents often stay for the party.

**Ages 5 to 7:** classic games work. Freeze dance, musical chairs, treasure hunts, red light green light, a pass-the-parcel game, sack races and a piñata.

**Ages 8 to 10:** kids want challenges and teams. Scavenger hunts, relay races, water balloon toss in summer, a mini obstacle course, trivia and capture the flag.

**Ages 11 and up:** movie nights, game tournaments, sports, DIY crafts and photo challenges. Older kids care about being able to talk and hang out, so build in time for that.

For more game ideas, see [carnival games for parties](/blog/carnival-games-for-parties) and [lawn games for weddings](/blog/lawn-games-for-weddings), which also work well for a kids' party.

## Games, space and helpers by age

<div class="table-wrap">

| Age group | Best game types | Space needed | Helpers worth having |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 to 4 | Bubbles, parade, sensory play | A small open area | 1 adult per 2 to 3 kids |
| 5 to 7 | Freeze dance, hunts, piñata | A yard or large room | 1 adult per 5 to 6 kids |
| 8 to 10 | Relays, scavenger hunts, teams | A yard or park | 1 adult per 8 kids |
| 11 and up | Movies, tournaments, crafts | A basement or living room | 1 or 2 adults nearby |

</div>

These are common guidelines. Always follow any rules from a venue, and keep a parent or adult in charge of any activity involving water or climbing.

## Space, tables and seating

A table that works for adults often does not work for children. Kids' tables and chairs sit lower, and a round table of 8 gets crowded. Plan one 6-foot table per 6 to 8 kids, or use kids' tables and chairs. See [kids tables and chairs for events](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events) and [dinnerware for kids at events](/blog/dinnerware-for-kids-at-events).

Plan an adult seating area as well. Parents often stay for younger children, and they need a place to sit and drink coffee. A few chairs near the activity area help. Count one adult chair for every two children under five.

## Bad weather backups

Have an indoor plan for every outdoor game. If a tent is in the budget, it helps with sun and light rain. If the party moves indoors, a basement, garage or living room can handle most games with the furniture moved back. See [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent) and [garage party layout ideas](/blog/garage-party-layout-ideas).

## Safety and allergies

Ask about food allergies on the invitation. Keep nut-free options on hand. Do not leave kids unsupervised near pools, grills or bounce houses. If you rent a bounce house, read [bounce house rental safety and space](/blog/bounce-house-rental-safety-and-space) first.

## Favors and goodie bags

Keep favors small and useful. A few good items beat a bag of plastic. Hand them out at the door so they do not get lost during games.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area weather can change the plan fast, so keep an indoor backup. In winter, plan for coats and boots at the door and a space where kids can run, such as a park district gym, a library meeting room or a play space. In summer, shade and water matter, so use umbrellas or a tent, and offer sunscreen. Many park districts rent pavilions, indoor rooms and gyms for kids' parties, and rules vary by district, so check with your village or park district for capacity, food rules and bounce house policies. If you live in a two-flat or condo building, tell neighbors if kids will be outside, and keep deliveries off narrow alleys. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a kids birthday party be?

Two hours is typical for ages 3 to 8, and two to three hours for older kids. Shorter parties work better for toddlers, often 90 minutes. Put both the start and end times on the invitation.

### What is a good kids birthday party schedule?

Start with 15 minutes of arrival and free play, then 30 to 40 minutes of games, 20 to 25 minutes for food, 10 to 15 minutes for cake, and the rest for a craft or free play. Adjust the order to match your guests.

### How many games do I need for a kids birthday party?

Two or three structured games plus free play is usually enough for a two-hour party. Add a quiet backup in case kids lose interest or the weather turns.

### What games work for a mixed-age birthday party?

Choose games where skill does not matter much, such as freeze dance, bubbles, a piñata or a scavenger hunt in teams that mix ages. Pair younger kids with older helpers.

### How many adults do I need at a kids party?

A common rule is one adult for every five or six young children, with more for toddlers or water play. Parents of younger kids often stay, which helps.

### What should I serve at a kids birthday party?

Keep it simple: pizza, fruit, veggies, juice and cake. Ask about allergies and offer a nut-free option. Plan about two plates per child to cover seconds and cake.

### Where can I host a kids birthday party in Chicago?

Options include your home, a park district room, a play space, a library meeting room or a backyard with a tent. Check with your local park district for rules and rental dates.
