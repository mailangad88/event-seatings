**Quick answer**

A 10 x 10 pop-up canopy covers 100 square feet and shelters about 8 to 10 people at a time, which is enough for a food table, a kids' area or a few seated guests. A rented frame tent starts at about 400 square feet and is built for dining, weather and permits. Use pop-ups for small, short, casual gatherings under 25 guests. Rent a tent for anything larger, any formal event or any event where wind and rain are a risk.

</div>

Pop-up canopies are cheap, fast and easy to find, and they work well at the right job. They are also lightweight, so they become kites in wind. The mistake is buying five of them to make a big tent. This guide shows where each fits.

## When a pop-up is the right choice

- A buffet or drink table.
- A kids' craft area at a larger party.
- A shaded spot for a DJ or a gift table.
- Backyard parties under about 20 guests.
- A short event with a calm forecast.

## When to rent a tent

- More than about 25 guests, or any seated dinner.
- Weddings, graduations and formal events.
- Any event where rain or heat is a real risk.
- Events needing sidewalls, a floor, heat, lighting or a stage.
- Hard surfaces where proper ballast is needed.

## The wind problem

Pop-ups are light, and in a gust they can lift and fly. Weights on each leg are essential, and the canopy needs to come down when the wind rises. A rented tent has heavier anchors and a planned wind trigger. Read [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety) for the questions to ask. If you use a pop-up, hold each leg with a weight rated for the canopy and take it down for gusts. Never rely on the tent staying up on its own.

## Permits and rules

A 10 x 10 pop-up is 100 square feet, below the 400 square feet where Chicago, Oak Park and Winnetka begin requiring permits. That means no permit in most cases, but codes on fire safety and anchoring can still apply. The rules grow if you put several side by side: Chicago counts tents less than 12 feet apart together. A row of four pop-ups can reach 400 square feet and trigger the permit. See [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and [suburban tent permits](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs).

Check your HOA or park district rules too. Many parks restrict canopies. See [HOA and neighbors](/blog/hoa-and-neighbors-tent-party).

## Cost thinking

We do not publish tent prices. A pop-up costs less to buy or rent than a rented tent, but you do the setup, you handle the weights and you replace it when it fails. A rented tent includes delivery, installation, anchoring and takedown. Compare the total, not the sticker. For chairs, our published estimates are on the [chairs page](/chairs).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people fit under a 10 x 10 canopy?

About 8 people standing close, or 4 to 6 with a table and chairs. For a seated dinner, plan 12 square feet per guest.

### Is a pop-up tent good enough for a party?

For a small, casual party in calm weather, yes. For bigger or formal events, a rented frame tent is safer and holds more.

### Do I need a permit for a pop-up canopy?

Usually not, because 100 square feet is below common permit lines. Canopies placed close together can add up, and public spaces have their own rules.

### Can I join pop-ups together to make a bigger tent?

It is possible but not recommended. Joints leak, wind catches them and area adds up for permit rules. Rent one tent instead.

### Are pop-ups safe in wind?

Not in strong wind. They need weights and should come down when gusts begin. Rented tents have heavier anchoring.

### Can I use a pop-up on a driveway?

Yes, with weights on every leg. See [tent on a driveway](/blog/tent-on-driveway-or-parking-lot) for hard-surface tips.
