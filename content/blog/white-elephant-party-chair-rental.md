A white elephant exchange is one of the easiest holiday parties to plan, but it has one seating requirement that rows and head tables don't solve: everyone needs to see the gift pile and everyone else's reaction at the same time. That's a circle, not rows, and most homes and offices don't have 15 or 20 matching chairs that can form one.

## How to lay out a white elephant party

1. **Set every chair in one connected circle**, not two rows or a horseshoe split by furniture, since the game depends on everyone seeing the same gifts and the same reactions at once.
2. **Put the gift pile in the center**, within easy reach from any seat so whoever's turn it is doesn't have to walk far.
3. **Leave one chair slightly closer to the pile for whoever is calling numbers**, if the host wants a clear spot to run the draw from.
4. **Keep a side table nearby for drinks and food**, away from the circle itself, so nobody's elbow knocks a cup while unwrapping.

## How many chairs to rent

Count by confirmed guests, not by an estimate from last year's party:

- **Get a real RSVP count before ordering**, since a white elephant list often grows once people hear "bring a $20 gift and come."
- **Add a couple of extra chairs at the edge of the circle** for late arrivals, without breaking the circle shape for everyone already seated.
- **Don't round down to save on delivery**, since a chair short means someone sits on an arm of a couch outside the circle and can't fully take part in the game.

## Why rows and sofas don't work for this game

- **A couch and a few chairs around it breaks the circle**, leaving some guests facing sideways or with their back half-turned to the action.
- **Rows facing a "front" work for a presentation, not a game everyone plays**, since white elephant has no front: the turn moves around the group, and every seat needs an equal view.
- **Mixed seating heights (a low couch next to a dining chair) make the gift pile hard to see for whoever's lower**, so matching chair heights around the whole circle keeps sightlines even.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a white elephant party?
Plan one seat per confirmed guest. Get a real RSVP count before ordering, since white elephant lists tend to grow once people hear about the gift swap, and add a couple of spares for late arrivals.

### How should seating be set up for a white elephant exchange?
Set one connected circle of chairs around a central gift pile, so every seat has an equal view of the draw and the gifts. Avoid splitting the group between a couch and separate chairs, which breaks the circle and the sightlines.

### Can we just use our couch and dining chairs?
You can, but mixed seating heights and a couch that only faces one direction usually break the circle shape the game needs. Matching chairs set in one full circle keep every seat's view and turn equal.

### What's a good chair for a white elephant party at home?
A comfortable, stackable chair like a mid-century shell or a teak-look garden folding chair works well, since both are easy to arrange in a circle and easy to clear away once the party's over.
