**Quick answer**

Theater seating fits the most people (about 6 to 8 square feet each) but gives no writing surface. Classroom seating adds tables and needs about 10 to 15 square feet per person. Banquet rounds suit meals and group talk at about 10 to 12 square feet each, and U-shape suits discussion for 15 to 30 people. Choose the style by what attendees must do, not by how many you can squeeze in.

</div>

Choosing a conference seating style sounds like a small detail. It decides how many people fit, whether they can take notes, whether they can see each other, and how many chairs and tables you rent. Chicago hotels and suburban conference centers publish capacity charts for each room in these exact styles, which is useful, but their numbers assume the maximum. This guide helps you pick the right style and then count realistically.

| Style | Space per person (rule of thumb) | Writing surface | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theater | 6 to 8 sq ft | No | Keynotes, announcements, presentations |
| Classroom | 10 to 15 sq ft | Yes, 18 or 24 inch tables | Training, workshops with laptops |
| Banquet (rounds) | 10 to 12 sq ft | Shared round | Meals, awards, group exercises |
| U-shape | 20 to 30 sq ft | Yes | Discussions of 15 to 30, facilitated meetings |
| Boardroom / hollow square | 20 to 30 sq ft | Yes | Small decision-making groups |
| Cabaret (half-rounds) | 12 to 15 sq ft | Shared half-round | Workshops with a facing screen |

</div>

These figures are common industry rules of thumb. Your room's capacity chart or fire marshal limit is the final word, so always check posted occupancy.

## How to pick

Ask what the attendee does for most of the day.

- **Listen only, under 90 minutes:** theater. It is the most efficient use of space. See [how many chairs fit in theater seating](/blog/how-many-chairs-fit-theater-seating).
- **Take notes or use laptops:** classroom. Schoolroom tables seat two per 6-foot table and three per 8-foot table, with chairs facing forward only.
- **Eat, then talk:** banquet. Rounds of 8 or 10 build conversation. Turn chairs so nobody has their back to the screen.
- **Decide something together:** U-shape or boardroom. Everyone sees everyone, and the facilitator can walk inside the U.

## Mixed days

Many conferences combine a general session, breakouts and a lunch. A common plan is theater in the main room for the morning, rounds for lunch and classroom in breakouts. If the same room flips, ask your venue how long a turn takes and who does it. Renting extra chairs for the breakout rooms can be cheaper than paying a venue to reset one room three times.

## Chair choice matters in long sessions

An eight-hour day on a thin chair wears people out. For a conference, choose padded seating for the main room and consider [shell chairs](/chairs/shell) or [bentwood chairs](/chairs/bentwood) for networking areas. Folding chairs work for overflow. Folding chairs stack fast if you need quick resets.

## Chicago-specific notes

Downtown hotels often have columns, chandeliers and fixed stages that cut the real capacity. In winter, add a coat rack zone at the entrance. If your event is in a suburban office park, verify whether outside rentals can be delivered to the suite and where the loading entrance is. Ask the venue for its written rental policy early.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which conference seating style fits the most people?

Theater, at about 6 to 8 square feet per person. It has no tables, so it is best for keynotes and short presentations.

### How much space per person do I need for classroom seating?

Plan 10 to 15 square feet per person as a rule of thumb. Add space for the presenter's area and aisles.

### What is the difference between banquet and cabaret seating?

Banquet seats guests all the way around a round table. Cabaret seats them around two-thirds of the round so no one has their back to the stage.

### What is U-shape seating used for?

Discussions and training for about 15 to 30 people. Tables form three sides of a U and everyone faces the open end.

### How many chairs should I order beyond the headcount?

About 5% extra for spares, speakers and last-minute attendees. For public events, ask your venue about overflow rules.

### Who decides the maximum capacity of a room?

The venue and local fire code. Ask the venue for its posted occupancy and capacity chart before you plan layouts.
