Renting for the first time feels like learning a new language. You are asked for a "delivery window," a "damage waiver" and whether you want "will call." Then you hear that linens come back "dirty" and that is fine. This guide walks you through it in order for a Chicago-area party, from a first headcount to the last item on the truck.

## Step 1: Pick the date and a real guest count

Use your confirmed or most likely number, then add a few extra seats. Rentals are billed by the item, so a guess that is 20 too high costs real money. A guess that is 20 too low leaves guests standing. [How many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [how many tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) give the numbers. Most companies check the date first, so have a backup date if you can.

## Step 2: Build the list

Work from the guest's point of view. Where do they sit, eat, drink and put their coat? A short list:

- **Seating:** chairs, plus a few extra.
- **Tables:** guest tables, food, drinks, gifts and cake.
- **Linens:** a cloth for every table, and napkins.
- **Tableware:** plates, forks, glasses. Ask who is cleaning and who is serving.
- **Weather:** a tent, heaters or fans, depending on the season.
- **Extras:** lighting, a dance floor, games, a trash plan.

The [rental order calculator](/downloads/rental-order-calculator) turns a guest count into a list. The [backyard party kit](/downloads/backyard-party-planning-kit) is useful if you are hosting at home.

## Rental words, in plain English

<div class="table-wrap">

| Term | What it means | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Will call | You pick up and return the items yourself | Do I need a truck? What time are they open? |
| Drop-off or delivery | The company brings it to you | What is the window? Who sets up? |
| Delivery window | A range, like 8 am to noon, when the truck comes | Can you call me 30 minutes ahead? |
| Pickup window | When the truck comes back after the party | Is that the same day or next morning? |
| Damage waiver | A fee that covers normal accidental damage | What is excluded? |
| Dirty return | Items go back without washing, in many cases | What must I scrape, rinse or bag? |
| Setup and breakdown | The crew places and takes down items | Is it included or extra? |
| Deposit | A payment that holds your date | Is it refundable if I cancel? |

</div>

## What most hosts find out too late

- **A delivery window is not an arrival time.** The truck might come at the start or the end of a 4 hour window. Make sure someone can unlock the door and sign the whole time, or ask for a narrower window. It may cost more.
- **"Dirty return" has limits.** Many companies take plates and linens back without washing, but expect you to scrape food, remove candle wax and bag wet linens separately. Read [what condition items must come back in](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning).
- **Will call can be a trap for large orders.** A table set for 40 will not fit in a hatchback. Chicago street parking and alley garages make loading harder than it looks. Check the size and weight of what you are hauling before you save on delivery.
- **Measure first.** Doors, gangways, stairs and elevators set what can go in. A 6 foot table will not turn a corner in a narrow two-flat stairwell.

## Step 4: Book and pay

Once you accept, the company usually asks for a deposit to hold the date. Look at the payment schedule, the cancellation terms and any weather policy. [How to read a rental contract](/blog/how-to-read-an-event-rental-contract) walks through each section. Don't sign until the quantities, dates and times match what you agreed.

## Step 5: Delivery and the count

Have a person on site. Count items before the crew leaves, and note any problems on the sheet. See [what to expect on delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect), and [what to do if the order is wrong](/blog/wrong-or-short-rental-order-what-to-do).

## Step 6: Pickup and return

Stack and bag items as the company asks. Be home during the pickup window. A missed pickup can mean a second trip fee.

## A short checklist before you book

- Do you have the date, the address and the access details, like stairs and elevators?
- Did you give a realistic guest count, with spares?
- Are delivery and pickup times written down?
- Do you know who sets up and who breaks down?
- Did you decide on the waiver after reading the exclusions?
- Do you have a person on site for delivery and pickup?

## Frequently asked questions

### What does will call mean for party rentals?

Will call means you pick up the items at the rental company and bring them back yourself. It can save the delivery fee, but you need a vehicle large enough for the order and time to load and return. It is usually best for small orders.

### What is a delivery window?

A delivery window is the range of time when the truck may arrive, such as 9 am to 1 pm. You need to be there the whole window unless the company agrees to call ahead. Ask for the narrowest window you can get, especially on a busy weekend.

### Do I need to wash rental dishes and linens?

Often no. Many companies take plates and linens back dirty, but they want food scraped off and linens bagged separately. Rinsing may be asked for glassware. Check your contract, because rules vary by company and item.

### How far ahead should I book rentals?

Book as soon as your date and guest count are firm. Many weekends fill up in the busy seasons. See [when to book event chair rentals in Chicago](/blog/when-to-book-event-chair-rentals-chicago) for the timing.

### Do I need a damage waiver?

You don't have to, but it is worth considering for large parties, kids and outdoor events. It covers accidental damage as the contract defines it, and it is not insurance. [Read the details before you decide](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained).

### Can I rent for a small party of 20?

Yes. Many companies have minimum orders or delivery fees, so a small order costs more per item. For a small party, ask about will call or bundling everything into one delivery.
