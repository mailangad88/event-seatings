**Quick answer**

Setup and breakdown fees pay a crew to place rented items in your room according to a layout, then collect and load them afterward. Delivery alone usually means items arrive at a door or a dock. Ask whether setup is to your floor plan, how long the crew stays, and whether breakdown has a time cutoff, so you do not pay twice or end up with a pile of chairs in a hallway.

</div>

There is a big difference between "delivered" and "set." Delivery gets 150 chairs to your venue. Setup puts them in rows, spaces the tables and finishes the room. For a Chicago banquet hall or a suburban tent, that second step can take a small crew a couple of hours, and the labor shows up as a line item. Knowing what you are buying avoids the most common budget surprise.

## What setup usually includes

- Unloading the truck and carrying items to the room.
- Placing chairs and tables according to your layout.
- Spacing and aligning rows or rounds.
- Placing linens, if ordered.
- Removing packaging and staging empty crates out of sight.

Setup does **not** automatically include decor, centerpieces, place cards or fine-tuning your table design. Those are separate jobs. For a clear layout, see our guide to [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests).

## What breakdown usually includes

- Collecting chairs, tables and linens at the end of the event.
- Stacking, loading and counting items.
- Taking crates and packaging back.

Breakdown often assumes items are **ready for pickup**: chairs stacked or at a known spot, linens bagged, dishes scraped and racked as required. If the crew has to gather chairs from across a venue or a lawn, expect that to take longer and cost more. Details in [returning rentals](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning).

## Give the crew a plan

A crew can only set what it understands. Provide:

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>A simple layout</strong> with table numbers, seat counts and aisle widths.</li>
<li><strong>The access notes</strong>: dock, elevator, parking, point of contact.</li>
<li><strong>The setup deadline</strong> and when guests, caterer and florist arrive.</li>
<li><strong>Breakdown instructions</strong>: where to stage items and what must be removed.</li>
<li><strong>A phone number</strong> that answers on event day.</li>
</ol>

Even a hand-drawn sketch beats nothing. Our [seating call](/book) will help you build one.

## When to skip setup

Setup is not always necessary. If you have a team of helpers and a simple room, a delivered order that you place yourself can save money. This works best for backyard parties or small gatherings. For weddings and larger events in Chicago, where timelines are tight and guests are dressed up, setup tends to be worth the cost. You can weigh it in [full service vs will-call](/blog/full-service-vs-will-call-event-rentals).

## How setup affects your timeline

Build a schedule that gives the crew time to finish before the first vendor or guest arrives. A common planning approach is to lock rental setup before florals, lighting and catering staging, because those teams need the chairs and tables in place. Shorten the window and everything downstream compresses.

## What can raise setup costs

- Stairs, long carries or multiple rooms.
- Complex layouts, like angled rows or many table types.
- Tight windows or setups that overlap with other vendors.
- Last-minute changes on the day.
- Outdoor setups on soft ground or in the rain.

Sharing these early keeps the quote honest. If you want a compare-it-yourself view, read [event rental delivery fees explained](/blog/event-rental-delivery-fees-explained).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a setup fee for event rentals?

It is a labor charge for placing rented items in your room according to a layout. It is usually separate from delivery, which only gets the items to the venue.

### Is setup included in the rental price?

Sometimes, but not always. Ask whether setup and breakdown are included in the quoted rate or listed as separate lines.

### What does breakdown include?

Collecting, stacking and loading the items, usually when they are staged and ready. If the crew has to gather items from several areas, it can take longer and may cost more.

### Can I set up the chairs myself?

Yes, if the company offers delivery without setup and you have helpers. It is common for backyard events. For larger or formal events, a crew saves time and reduces damage.

### Does the crew follow my floor plan?

A good one will, if you give them a clear layout. Send a sketch with table numbers and aisle widths before the event.

### Who pays if setup takes longer than planned?

It depends on the contract. Delays caused by the venue or last-minute changes may be billed, so confirm access and timing ahead.
