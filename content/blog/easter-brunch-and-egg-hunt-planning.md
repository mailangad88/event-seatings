The ham is in the oven, the eggs are hidden, and then the rain begins. A good plan puts the egg hunt, the meal and the weather backup in a sequence that works whatever the sky does.

## Pick the schedule

Two common schedules work, and the weather can decide for you.

**Hunt first, brunch after.** Kids arrive, hunt eggs while adults mingle with coffee, then everyone sits down to eat. Good when it is mild, because kids burn energy first.

**Brunch first, hunt after.** Adults eat while kids settle, then the hunt happens as a break before dessert. Better in cool weather, because you can pick the warmest part of the afternoon.

Either way, keep the whole event to three or four hours. A typical flow: arrive at 11:00, hunt at 11:30, sit down at 12:15, dessert at 1:30.

## Plan the egg hunt

Plan 12 to 15 eggs per child as a starting point, fewer for toddlers and more for older kids who tend to search harder. Divide the yard into zones by age so toddlers are not trampled: a plain, visible area for under 4, a mixed area for ages 5 to 8, and a harder one for older kids. Use a few golden or prize eggs with something extra. Count how many eggs you hid, so you can find them all after. Plastic eggs are easy; hard-boiled eggs should stay out of the sun and be eaten within a few hours of cooling. Keep a basket for each child and a few spares.

Allergies matter. Use non-food items such as stickers or small toys in some eggs, and keep nut-free candy options. A "no stepping on eggs" rule helps.

## The brunch menu

A buffet is easiest for a crowd. Classic Easter dishes include ham, quiche, egg casseroles, roasted vegetables, fruit, pastries and deviled eggs, plus a spring dessert. ## Seating, tables and layout

Adults usually want to sit and talk while kids run around, so set up the table where adults can watch the yard. Use long tables for a family-style meal or rounds for easier conversation. Keep the buffet away from the hunt area so kids do not run through the food line. Provide a kids' table with simple food. See [backyard party layout plan](/blog/backyard-party-layout-plan).

## Decor

Pastel linens, spring flowers, simple centerpieces and a photo spot are enough. A bunny photo backdrop or a flower wall adds charm. See [linen colors by season](/blog/linen-colors-by-season) and [flower wall rental guide](/blog/flower-wall-rental-guide). Hang a "Happy Easter" sign and set out baskets for kids.

## Weather plan

Chicago-area Easter weather can run from sunny and mild to cold, windy and wet, sometimes all in one afternoon. Have two plans: a tent with sidewalls and a heater for a yard brunch, and an indoor hunt where eggs are hidden in rooms and on stairs. See [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent), [tent sidewalls](/blog/tent-sidewalls-clear-window-solid) and [heating a tent in fall](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall) (the same ideas work in spring).

## Keeping food safe

Hot food stays hot and cold food stays cold. Do not leave eggs, dairy or meats out for more than two hours. See [keeping food cold at outdoor events](/blog/keeping-food-cold-at-outdoor-events).

## Traditions

Many families add a blessing, a church service before brunch or an Easter basket exchange. Plan the brunch around those. Ask the oldest relatives what they want to keep.

## Chicago-area notes

April weather in the Chicago area is unpredictable. Plan a tent or indoor backup and warm layers. Mud is common after rain, so use walkways, mats or flooring if needed. See [tent flooring and subfloors](/blog/tent-flooring-and-subfloors) and [chairs on grass](/blog/chairs-on-grass-sand-gravel). Many park districts and forest preserves hold community egg hunts, and some allow private pavilion rentals in spring, so check with your village or park district for dates and rules. If you host in a city yard or a two-flat, plan where kids can safely hunt, and keep deliveries out of the alley. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many eggs do I need per child for an Easter egg hunt?

A common starting point is 12 to 15 per child, fewer for toddlers and more for older kids. Count the eggs you hide so you can find them all.

### When should I hold the egg hunt, before or after brunch?

Before brunch works when the weather is mild, because kids burn off energy. After brunch is better when it is cold, since you can pick the warmest time.

### What should I serve for Easter brunch?

Ham, quiche, egg casseroles, fruit, pastries, roasted vegetables, deviled eggs and a spring dessert. Add a mimosa or juice bar.

### How long should an Easter brunch last?

Three to four hours with the egg hunt, meal and dessert.

### What if it rains on Easter?

Move the hunt indoors or under a tent with sidewalls. Hide eggs in rooms and on stairs, and keep the food inside.

### How do I keep an egg hunt fair for different ages?

Divide the yard into zones by age, use separate baskets and color-code eggs by age group.

### What do I need to rent for an Easter brunch?

Many hosts rent tables, chairs, linens, a tent and heater. Plan a seat for every guest at a sit-down brunch.
