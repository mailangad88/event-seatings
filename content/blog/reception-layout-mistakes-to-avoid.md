**Quick answer**

The biggest reception layout mistakes are aisles under 4 feet, a bar or buffet that blocks a walkway, a DJ too close to tables, no space for staff, forgetting the cake and gift tables, and trusting a venue's maximum capacity. Draw the room to scale, keep the clearances below and ask for the capacity with your layout.

</div>

A reception floor plan fails in predictable ways. Most are not about style but about inches: a table too close, a line with nowhere to stand, a speaker too near a grandmother. After you have a plan, walk through it as a guest, a server and a person in a wheelchair. If you find a bottleneck on paper, it will be worse in person. Here are 12 mistakes that we see in plans for Chicago-area ballrooms, banquet halls and tents, with a fix for each.

## The 12 mistakes

**1. Aisles under 4 feet.** Servers carrying plates cannot pass behind chairs. Leave 4 feet between chair backs, 5 if possible. Check against [round table sizes](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch).

**2. A bar next to the door.** The bar line backs into the entrance. Move it to a far wall and leave 6 feet of clear floor in front. See [bar placement floor plan](/blog/bar-placement-floor-plan).

**3. A buffet that crosses the dance floor.** Lines and dancers collide. Put the buffet on a wall away from the floor. See [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

**4. A DJ beside a table.** Speakers within 10 feet make conversation impossible. See [DJ and band placement](/blog/dj-and-band-placement-floor-plan).

**5. A dance floor that is too small.** About 40 percent of guests dance at once. See [dance floor size by guest count](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count).

**6. Forgetting the small tables.** Cake, gifts, guest book, escort cards, DJ and desserts add 5 to 8 tables to a 150-guest plan.

**7. Seats with a view of the kitchen door.** Do not put the head table or sweetheart table where guests see trays and trash. Place them away from the service door.

**8. No staging area for staff.** Caterers need space to plate and hold food. Ask what they need.

**9. Trusting the posted capacity.** A venue's maximum may be for standing guests. Ask for the number with tables, a dance floor and a band.

**10. A lopsided room.** All tables on one side and the dance floor on the other leaves guests at the far tables far from the action. Center the floor when you can; see [dance floor placement](/blog/dance-floor-placement-reception-layout).

**11. Ignoring accessibility.** A 36-inch path and a wheelchair space at several tables are the minimum. See [ADA accessible event seating](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways).

**12. Planning to the last seat.** A layout with zero spare seats has no room for a late guest. Keep 5 to 10 percent spare chairs, not at the tables but in a corner.

## Checking your plan

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Draw to scale.</strong> Use graph paper or a free planner, 1 square = 1 foot.</li>
<li><strong>Add clearances.</strong> Draw the 4, 5, 6 and 10-foot zones around aisles, bars and speakers.</li>
<li><strong>Trace three paths.</strong> A guest to the bar, a server from the kitchen, a wheelchair to the restroom.</li>
<li><strong>Count the extras.</strong> Cake, gifts, guest book, DJ, bar, buffet.</li>
<li><strong>Share the plan.</strong> Venue, caterer, DJ and rental company see the same drawing.</li>
</ol>

## Chairs that help the layout

Narrow chairs save inches. A slim [wishbone chair](/chairs/wishbone) or [bentwood chair](/chairs/bentwood) takes less room than a wide throne-style seat, and a stackable [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is easiest to move. Compare sizes on our [chairs page](/chairs) before you finalize spacing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common reception layout mistake?

Aisles that are too narrow. Keep at least 4 feet between chair backs and 5 where servers carry trays.

### How far should the DJ be from the tables?

At least 10 feet, with speakers aimed at the dance floor.

### How much space should I leave in front of the bar?

About 6 feet of clear floor so the line does not block a walkway.

### Where should the cake table go?

Near the dance floor or the head table, visible to all guests and away from the kitchen traffic and direct sun.

### Should I trust my venue's capacity number?

Only if it is for your layout. Ask for the capacity with rounds, a dance floor and a band, and confirm in writing.

### How do I check my floor plan?

Draw it to scale, add the clearances and trace routes for a guest, a server and a wheelchair user.
