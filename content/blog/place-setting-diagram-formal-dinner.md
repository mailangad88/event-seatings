A place setting is the most repeated unit at your event. If you get the layout right once, your rental company or banquet staff can copy it 100 times. If you get it wrong, servers improvise and every seat looks a little different. Here is the standard layout used at formal dinners in Chicago hotels and banquet halls, and how to simplify it.

## The rules behind the diagram

1. **Forks left, knives and spoons right.** The only exception is an oyster fork, which goes on the right.
2. **Blades face the plate.** A knife is never placed with its blade pointing out.
3. **Outside in.** Guests start with the outermost utensil and work toward the plate.
4. **Glasses above the knife.** Water goblet directly above the knife tip, wine glasses to its right, in the order you pour.
5. **Bread plate upper left**, butter knife laid across it, handle to the right.
6. **Dessert utensils** either above the plate or brought with the dessert course.
7. **Napkin** on the plate, folded in the center, or to the left of the forks.

## How many pieces per course

| Meal | Left of plate | Right of plate | Above plate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casual (one entree) | Fork | Knife, spoon (if soup) | Water glass |
| Standard wedding (salad, entree) | Salad fork, dinner fork | Knife, soup spoon (if soup) | Water, wine, dessert fork |
| Formal (3 or more courses) | Salad fork, fish fork, dinner fork | Knife, fish knife, soup spoon | Water, 2 wine, dessert fork and spoon |

A good rule of thumb: never set more than three pieces of flatware on either side. If your menu needs more, bring the extra utensil with the course. See [how many forks, knives and spoons per guest](/blog/how-many-forks-knives-spoons-per-guest) for the rental counts.

## Dressing the setting for the room

**Charger.** A decorative plate that sits under the dinner plate. It is removed before dessert. It also anchors the setting and adds color.

**Menu card.** Lay it on the plate or prop it against the water goblet.

**Place card.** Above the dessert spoon, at the top of the setting.

**Spacing.** Plan about 24 inches of table width per guest. On a 60-inch round, that is 8 guests. On a 72-inch round, it is 10. Our guide to [tablecloth sizes](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) and [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) connect to this.

## Simplify without looking cheap

Not every event needs the full formal setting. Three easy ways to scale back:

- **Set only what you will use.** If dessert is served later, don't set the dessert fork now.
- **Use one wine glass.** Pour red and white into the same glass or skip the second pour.
- **Roll the flatware in the napkin.** A rolled bundle at each seat saves time at buffet or family-style meals, and it works well outdoors where wind blows loose utensils.

For a gold look, see the [gold flatware trend](/blog/gold-flatware-wedding-trend) for how to mix metallics.

## A few Chicago-specific notes

Many banquet halls and hotels in Chicago set tables for you, so ask your venue's coordinator for their standard setting. If your setting differs, give them a sketch like the one above so staff can copy it. At tent and barn weddings, caterers often work from your rental list, so say whether you want rolled napkins or a full setting. For outdoor events, wind is the problem. Weigh the napkin down with the flatware or tuck it into the glass.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which side does the fork go on?

Forks go on the left of the plate. Knives and spoons go on the right, with the knife blade facing the plate. The exception is an oyster fork, which sits on the right with the spoons.

### Where does the water glass go?

Place it directly above the tip of the dinner knife. Wine glasses go to the right of the water glass, in the order they will be poured.

### Where does the bread plate go?

It goes at the upper left, above the forks. The butter knife rests across the plate with the blade facing in and the handle to the right.

### How many pieces of flatware should be on the table?

No more than three on each side at the start. If the menu has more courses, bring the extra utensil with that course. A standard wedding dinner uses a salad fork, dinner fork, knife and sometimes a soup spoon.

### Where does the dessert fork go?

Place the dessert fork and spoon horizontally above the plate, with the fork handle pointing left and the spoon handle pointing right. Or bring them out with the dessert.

### Does a charger plate count as a dinner plate?

No. A charger is a decorative base plate that is removed before food is served or after the first course. It does not hold food.
