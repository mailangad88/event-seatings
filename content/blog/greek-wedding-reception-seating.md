**Quick answer**

Greek Orthodox weddings are usually held in a church, with the crowning (stefana) and a procession around the altar table, followed by a reception with large family tables and plenty of circle dancing. Plan rounds of 8 to 10, a head table for the couple and koumbaros or koumbara (the sponsor of the crowns), a dance floor large enough for a line or circle of 30 or more dancers and a sweets or favors table. Customs vary by family and parish, so confirm details with your priest.

</div>

Chicago has a long-established Greek community, with parishes in the city and suburbs that host weddings every year. The ceremony takes place in the church, so rentals matter mostly for the reception. A Greek reception is a family party, often with a big guest list, loud music and a full dance floor, so the layout needs to leave room for movement and for relatives to sit together.

## What a Greek reception asks of the room

Reception traditions differ by family, but common elements include:

- **Large, multi-generation guest lists** with tables grouped by family.
- **A head table or family table** for the couple, the koumbaros or koumbara and parents.
- **Circle and line dances**, where guests join hands and move around the floor. Plates, glasses or flowers may be involved in some customs, but venue rules vary, so check first.
- **A sweets and favors table**, often with koufeta (sugared almonds) or other favors.
- **Live music** that needs a stage and power.

Ask your parish or family which customs you plan to keep. Do not assume plate breaking is allowed, because many venues prohibit it. Ask the venue first.

## The ceremony and the church

Greek Orthodox ceremonies take place in the church, so the church provides its own furniture and sets its own rules. The couple usually stands before the altar table, so little rental is needed. Ask your parish what the church provides. If the ceremony is outdoors or at a non-church venue, ask your priest what is required and whether it is allowed.

If you want a family sitting area near the front for a ceremony outside the church, a small row of [Louis Medallion chairs](/chairs/louis-medallion) ($20 to $30 estimated) or [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) ($18 to $25) works for elders.

## Chicago notes

- Chicago-area Greek parishes and banquet halls often host receptions with full dance floors. Ask your venue about decibel limits and end time.
- Check venue policies on candles, flowers and floor damage. Many halls limit or ban plate breaking and open flame.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do the koumbaros and koumbara sit?

Usually at the head table beside the couple. They are the sponsors of the crowns and hold an honored role. Ask your family and priest what is customary.

### How big should the dance floor be?

Plan about 5 square feet per dancer. For 60 dancers, that is about 300 square feet. Greek receptions often fill the floor, so choose the larger size.

### Can we break plates at a Greek wedding reception?

Many venues forbid it for safety and cleaning reasons. Some families use flowers or paper plates instead. Always ask your venue and caterer before planning it.

### Do I need a stage for live music?

If you have a live band, yes. A stage of 12 x 16 feet is a common starting size, but ask your band for its requirements. See [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events).

### How should I seat grandparents?

Near the head table, away from loudspeakers and with a short path to the restroom. Choose chairs with arms.

### Can I rent chairs for a church ceremony?

Churches usually have their own pews or chairs. Ask the parish before you rent anything.
