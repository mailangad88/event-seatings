If you live in a Chicago condo or apartment building, the clubhouse or party room is often the easiest place to host a birthday, baby shower, or holiday party. You already know the space, your guests don't have to travel far, and you skip the cost of a separate venue. But these rooms come with their own rules, and getting the furniture right takes a little planning.

## Book the room through management first

Most buildings treat the clubhouse or party room like a shared asset, not a free-for-all. Check with your management office or HOA before you tell anyone about the date. You'll usually need to:

- Reserve the room on a calendar, sometimes months out for a popular Saturday
- Pay a deposit, which may be refunded if the room is left clean
- Sign a short agreement about hours, noise, and cleanup

## Insurance and building requirements

Many buildings ask for a certificate of insurance before they let anyone bring in outside furniture, especially if the room has nice flooring or is shared with other residents. If your building requires one, ask whoever delivers your chairs and tables for their certificate of insurance and pass it along to management ahead of time. Don't assume your own renters or homeowners policy covers it.

## Getting furniture through the building

This is where a lot of condo parties run into trouble. The chairs and tables have to get from a truck outside, through a lobby or loading dock, into an elevator, and down a hallway to the room. Narrow elevator cabs and tight hallway turns rule out some furniture.

Before you place an order, measure the elevator cab's width, depth, and door opening, plus the narrowest hallway turn. A folding [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) or [wishbone chair](/chairs/wishbone) breaks down flat and moves through tight spaces easily, while a long banquet table might not fit around a corner. If your building has a freight elevator, ask the front desk if you can reserve a window for it, since that usually means a bigger cab and fewer interruptions from other residents trying to use the elevator at the same time.

## Guest counts and noise limits

Amenity rooms are shared spaces, so most buildings cap how many people can be in the room at once and set a time when music and noise need to stop. Ask management for the exact numbers before you send invitations, not after. If your group is larger than the room allows, you may need to look at a different kind of space. Our guide on [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) can help you figure out seating once you know your real guest count, and our [backyard event space size guide](/blog/backyard-event-space-size-guide-chicago) has useful math on fitting people into a room even though it's written for yards.

## Choosing furniture for the room

Clubhouse rooms often come with their own tables and chairs, but they may not match your event or there may not be enough. Renting [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) or [bentwood chairs](/chairs/bentwood) can dress up a plain room for a baby shower or holiday party without needing to redecorate. Check the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) if the room's tables are a different size than you expect, since clubhouse tables vary a lot from building to building.

## Parking for your delivery

Street parking near a condo or apartment building can be tight, especially downtown or in dense neighborhoods. If your building has a loading dock, reserve a time slot. If it doesn't, check the city's rules on loading zones before your delivery truck arrives. Our [delivery and parking guide](/blog/chicago-event-delivery-parking-loading-zones) covers what to expect.

## Winter deliveries

If your party falls in the colder months, ask management about salt and ice on the sidewalk or ramp leading to your building's entrance. A delivery crew moving chairs and tables on an icy ramp is a real hazard. See our [winter delivery guide](/blog/winter-event-rental-delivery-snow-and-ice) for more on planning around snow.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need my building's permission to rent furniture for the clubhouse?

Yes, almost always. Most buildings require you to reserve the room through management and may ask for a certificate of insurance from your rental provider before anything is delivered.

### Will my furniture fit in the elevator?

It depends on your building. Measure the elevator cab and hallway turns before ordering, and ask for folding or stackable chairs if your building only has a standard passenger elevator.

### How many guests can I invite to a clubhouse party?

Check with your management office for the room's posted occupancy limit. It varies by building and is usually listed in your reservation agreement.

### Does the building provide chairs and tables?

Some do, but the style or quantity may not match your event. Renting extra or different furniture is common for condo and apartment parties.

### Who needs to provide a certificate of insurance?

If your building requires one, it comes from whoever delivers and sets up your furniture, not from you personally. Ask your rental provider for it ahead of time and send it to management.

### What if my building has strict noise hours?

Plan your party to wrap up before the cutoff time listed in your building's rules, and let your guests know the end time in your invitation.
