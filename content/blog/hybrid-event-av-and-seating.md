**Quick answer**

A hybrid event needs two audiences served at once. Seat in-room guests where the camera sees the stage clearly, place a screen facing the speakers so remote guests are visible, and use a mixing board or dedicated audio feed so remote attendees hear everyone. Keep the first row 8 to 10 feet from the camera, light faces evenly, and hire or assign one person to monitor the online audience.

</div>

Hybrid events fail in familiar ways: remote viewers hear a muffled room, in-room guests stare at the back of a camera operator, and the people on screen are invisible to the stage. These are layout problems more than technology problems. This guide is about the physical setup: where people sit, where cameras and screens go and how to give both audiences the same experience. It is general planning advice; your AV provider will advise on specific equipment.

## Seating that works with a camera

- **Seat guests in front of the camera.** Cameras see a rear view of the audience, so place guests in the frame's center rows if you want applause or reactions to appear in the video.
- **Keep the aisle out of the shot.** A center aisle makes the audience look sparse on video.
- **Avoid mirrors and bright windows** behind the speaker.
- **Allow a camera riser** at the back, 4 x 4 feet is typical, and a cable path away from guests.
- **Mark a no-sit zone** around the camera and mixer so no one blocks the lens.

For theater spacing see [how many chairs fit in theater seating](/blog/how-many-chairs-fit-theater-seating).

## Sound: the part that fails first

In-room sound and remote sound are different problems. The room needs enough volume without echo. Remote guests need a direct feed from the mixer, not a microphone picking up the room. Use a lavalier or handheld mic for each speaker and a mic for audience questions that feeds the stream. Ask your AV provider about echo cancellation and a mix-minus setup that prevents remote guests from hearing themselves. Do a test with someone outside the room before the event.

## Lighting

Cameras need even light on faces, not just on the stage. A mix of front light and a soft back light looks natural. Avoid putting the speaker in front of a bright window. Dim the audience lights just enough to keep the screen readable but not so low that the camera loses detail. For stage lighting, see [corporate stage lighting basics](/blog/corporate-stage-lighting-basics).

## The remote host

Assign one person to watch the chat, read questions to the moderator and welcome remote attendees. Give them a seat near the stage with a laptop and headphones. A shared document with the schedule, links and Q&A rules keeps the team aligned. Tell the audience in the room that questions from remote guests will be read out.

## Networking in a hybrid world

Remote guests miss the hallway. Plan a post-session breakout room or chat, and use a screen in the lobby where in-room guests can say hello. A bar or lounge near a camera can host a remote guest on a tablet. Keep the format simple, because complex networking features go unused.

## Chicago note

Hotels and conference centers downtown often have in-house AV and may require you to use it or pay a fee for outside gear. Ask early about load-in rules, internet bandwidth and power. In winter, snow can affect remote speakers' travel and internet; have a backup plan such as a recorded segment. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is a hybrid event?

An event that serves in-person and online audiences at the same time. Both audiences watch the same program.

### Where should the camera go?

At the back or the side, with a clear view of the speakers and at least 8 to 10 feet from the first row.

### How do I let remote guests ask questions?

Assign a host to monitor chat and read questions to the moderator. Show the questions on a screen too.

### Should remote guests be visible in the room?

Yes, on a screen beside the stage, so speakers and the audience can see them.

### What causes poor audio on hybrid events?

Using a room microphone for the stream instead of a direct feed. Ask your AV provider to feed the mixer directly.

### Do I need extra seating for the AV team?

Yes. A small table for the mixer and a chair for the host, behind or beside the audience.
