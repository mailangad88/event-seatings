**Quick answer**

For a cocktail hour, rent seating for roughly 25 to 35 percent of your guests, grouped into conversation sets of one sofa, two chairs and a low table, and keep the rest of the room open for standing. Add a few highboy cocktail tables near the bar so people have somewhere to set a drink. Place lounge sets along the edges of the room, never in the path from the door to the bar.

</div>

Cocktail hour is the one part of the evening where guests move around, and it is also where a room tends to feel either alive or awkward. Lounge furniture solves a real problem: older relatives, guests in heels and anyone who just arrived from a Chicago commute want a place to land. This guide covers what to rent, how much, and how to arrange it so the space still flows.

## How much seating does cocktail hour need?

People at a cocktail hour stand more than you think, and a room full of chairs makes it feel like a waiting room. A common planner rule of thumb is to provide seats for about a quarter to a third of the guest list during cocktails, and let the rest mingle. Add more if your crowd skews older, if the hour runs past 60 minutes, or if the party is outdoors on a hot day.

## The pieces of a lounge set

A set is a small conversation group. Mix heights and shapes so it looks collected rather than bought as a bundle.

- **Sofa or settee.** The anchor. A two-seat settee fits most spaces; a three-seat sofa suits a larger room. See our guide to [velvet sofas and settees](/blog/velvet-sofas-and-settees-for-events).
- **Two accent chairs.** Armchairs, barrel chairs or [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) turned toward the sofa.
- **Low table.** A coffee table about 16 to 18 inches tall for drinks and small plates.
- **Poufs and ottomans.** Flexible extra seats that tuck under a table until needed.
- **Side table or cocktail table.** One per set, so nobody balances a glass on a knee.
- **Rug.** Defines the set and softens a hard floor, but check it is securely taped to avoid trips.

## Where to put lounge sets

Placement matters more than quantity.

1. **Away from the entrance.** Guests stop and look when they walk in. Let them see the bar and the room first.
2. **Along walls and corners.** This keeps the middle clear for traffic and uses space that is otherwise empty.
3. **Within sight of the bar, not in its line.** Close enough that guests feel connected, far enough that the bar queue does not wrap through the sofas.
4. **Near a view or focal point.** A fireplace, a window or a floral wall gives the set a reason to exist, and it photographs well.
5. **Not blocking exits.** Keep every door and exit sign clear.

For help scaling this, see [lounge layout by guest count](/blog/lounge-layout-by-guest-count).

## Choosing a style that fits the room

Match the furniture to the mood, not just the color scheme.

- **Black-tie ballroom:** tufted velvet settees, gold or acrylic side tables.
- **Industrial loft or warehouse space:** leather sofas, wood-and-metal tables, large rugs.
- **Barn or farm venue:** wicker, rattan and weathered wood, plus a few poufs.
- **Garden or tent:** weatherproof pieces or ones the rental company can protect. See [outdoor lounge rentals in Chicago](/blog/outdoor-lounge-rentals-chicago).
- **Corporate reception:** clean-lined sofas in neutral shades and plenty of highboys.

Match your dining chairs where you can. A [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) or [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) used around a lounge table ties the cocktail space to the dinner room.

## Chicago details that affect lounge plans

Winter and shoulder-season events are common here, so cocktail hour often starts with a coat line. Plan [coat racks](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago) before the lounge, and keep the lounge away from the door draft. Many downtown buildings route deliveries through service elevators with time windows, so confirm sofa dimensions against door widths and elevator size with the venue. Hotels and historic spaces often restrict what can be placed on carpeted or marble floors, so ask about floor protection.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Too many sofas.** They eat floor area and make guests sit rather than mingle.
- **No surface for drinks.** Every seat needs a table within reach.
- **Furniture facing the wall.** Turn sets inward toward each other, not toward a blank wall.
- **Forgetting the move.** If the room flips from cocktails to dinner, plan who moves the lounge furniture and when. Ask the venue about turnover time.
- **Wrong scale.** An oversized sectional in a small room blocks the bar and looks cramped.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many lounge sets do I need for 100 guests?

Plan on 4 to 5 sets. At 25 to 35 percent seated, 100 guests means about 25 to 35 seats, and a set seats 6 to 8. Add a few highboy tables for standing guests.

### Do I need lounge furniture if there are already chairs at dinner tables?

Not always. If cocktails happen in the same room as dinner, guests can sit at their tables. A separate lounge space is worth it when cocktails are in a different room, foyer or outdoor area.

### Can lounge furniture go outside?

Yes, with care. Ask the rental company what is rated for outdoor use, protect fabric from damp grass and rain, and keep a plan for weather. Chicago evenings can cool quickly even in summer.

### How far should lounge sets be from the bar?

Close enough to see it, far enough that the line does not run through the seating. A common range is 10 to 15 feet, adjusted to your room.

### What goes on a lounge table besides drinks?

A small votive or low arrangement, a sign for the signature cocktails and a stack of napkins. Keep it low so guests can see each other across the table.

### Should I rent poufs or extra chairs?

Poufs are flexible and take little space, but they are low and hard for some guests to get out of. Mix them with regular-height chairs so there is an easy option for everyone.
