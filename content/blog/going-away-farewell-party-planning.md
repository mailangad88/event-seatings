A farewell party has a built-in deadline: the guest of honor leaves on a date that will not move. That makes the planning short and the stakes surprisingly high, since people want to say goodbye well. The best farewells are warm, personal and not overbuilt. Gather the right people, make space for stories, and send the guest of honor off with something to remember.

## Who is it for, and who hosts

A going away party might mark a coworker's last day, a friend moving away, a family member shipping out or a student heading to college. Know who the guest of honor is and what they would enjoy. Some people love being the center of attention, and some prefer a quiet dinner. Ask a close friend or the guest's partner before you plan. The host might be a group of friends, a manager, a family member or a team. Decide who covers costs. At work, a collection envelope or a shared page works well, and it should be optional.

## Pick a date

Hold the party about a week before the departure, when everyone is still in town but the guest of honor has time to pack. A last-day party at the office works for coworkers. Avoid the night before, when people are busy. If the person is moving a long way, consider a weekend gathering. A casual party earlier in the month plus a quiet final dinner is also common.

## Choose a format

**Office gathering.** Cake, snacks and cards in a conference room or break room. Fast and simple, about an hour.

**Backyard or patio party.** Relaxed, with a grill or a taco bar. Good for 20 to 50 guests.

**Restaurant or bar.** Easy and no cleanup. Reserve a section or private room.

**Potluck.** Everyone brings a dish. Low cost and personal.

**Surprise party.** Needs a trusted co-planner and a good cover story. See [surprise party planning rentals](/blog/surprise-party-planning-rentals).

**Virtual party.** Useful if guests are far away. Combine with a shipped gift.

Food amounts depend on timing. For a snack-only party, plan 6 to 8 bites per guest. For a meal, see [how much food per person](/blog/how-much-food-per-person-for-a-party).

## Make it personal

The best part of a farewell is the stories. Ask guests to write a short memory or advice on a card. Collect photos for a slideshow or a collage. Build a playlist of songs from the guest's favorite eras. A "memory jar" lets people drop in notes. Keep the program to a few short toasts of about two minutes each. See [guest book alternatives](/blog/guest-book-alternatives-for-weddings).

## Gifts

A group gift is usually better than many small ones. Pick something useful for the new place, such as a gift card, a framed photo or a travel item. For work parties, collect small contributions with no pressure. Keep a card for everyone to sign.

## Food and drinks

Keep it simple. A taco bar, sandwich platters, pizza or a potluck all work. Add cake or the guest's favorite dessert. Plan non-alcoholic drinks and water. See [food station ideas and equipment](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment).

## Seating and layout

Keep seating flexible. Seats for about half of guests, plus a few tables for food and cards. Place the guest of honor where everyone can reach them. Arrange the memory board near the entrance so guests can add a note on the way in. See [backyard party layout plan](/blog/backyard-party-layout-plan).

## Timeline

Plan in this order: confirm the date, tell the guest list, collect money, order food, make the card, set up, host, and send photos afterward.

## A short program that feels warm

You do not need a long program. Gather everyone about halfway through the party, thank the guest of honor and share two or three stories. Let someone read the card aloud and present the gift. Keep it short, so the guest has time to talk with everyone. If there is a slideshow, aim for five minutes and a handful of photos. End with a group photo, and send it to the guest of honor the next day with a note.

## After the party

Offer to help the guest of honor with last-minute tasks like packing, moving boxes or a final lunch. Share contact info so people can stay in touch. If the party was at the office, send a thank-you note to the team and clear the room. If you collected money, report the total and what was spent.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area farewells often happen in offices, restaurant private rooms, backyards or park district spaces. If you reserve a restaurant, ask about minimums, deposit and time limits. For an office party, check with your building about booking the conference room and bringing in food. Summer backyard parties are popular, so plan shade and bug control. See [mosquitoes and bugs at outdoor events](/blog/mosquitoes-and-bugs-outdoor-events). Winter parties benefit from a coat rack and a clear entry. Consider ride-share and transit details for guests who cannot drive, and tell neighbors in advance of any party at home.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a going away party?

Pick a date about a week before the departure, choose a format, invite guests, collect for a gift, order food and plan a few short toasts. Two to four weeks is enough time.

### What should I do for a farewell party at work?

A simple gathering in a break room or conference room with cake, snacks, a card and a few kind words works well. Keep it to an hour.

### How long should a going away party last?

Two hours is plenty for most farewells. An office party can be an hour.

### What food should I serve at a going away party?

Choose something easy and familiar, such as pizza, tacos, sandwiches or a potluck, plus cake. Include non-alcoholic drinks.

### What do you give someone at a going away party?

A group card, a framed photo, a gift card or a small useful item for the new place. Group gifts are usually better than many small ones.

### Should a going away party be a surprise?

Only if the guest of honor likes surprises. Check with someone close to them first.

### How many guests should I invite to a going away party?

Invite people who matter to the guest of honor. 15 to 60 is common, depending on the setting.
