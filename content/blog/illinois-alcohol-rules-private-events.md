**Quick answer**

Illinois does not have one rule for every private party. Check four things in order: your venue's alcohol policy, your city or village rules, whether the person pouring needs a license or BASSET training, and whether you need host liquor liability insurance. Many Chicago-area venues require a licensed, insured bartender or caterer, and some parks only allow alcohol at certain permitted venues. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

</div>

Hosts often assume that if a party is private, the rules are loose. In Illinois they are not that simple. Some venues forbid outside alcohol, some cities require a license for any sale, and parks and forest preserves have their own rules. We are an event rental planner, not a lawyer, so treat this as a checklist of who to ask. Confirm every point with the source listed.

## 1. The venue's policy

This is the most important. Hotels and banquet halls usually hold their own liquor license and require you to buy alcohol from them or use their bartenders. Event spaces such as lofts and galleries may allow outside alcohol but require licensed, insured bartenders. Park and forest preserve sites often have the strictest rules. For example, the Chicago Park District's published FAQ says alcohol service is allowed at its Special Event Venues but not at commemorative events, that the host cannot supply the alcohol and that it must be purchased through and served by a caterer on the Park District's list, with at least $1 million in host liquor liability coverage. Rules change, so ask the Park District or your venue for the current policy.

## 2. City, village and county rules

Chicago and each suburb set their own liquor rules. Whether a one-day event needs a license, whether you can sell tickets that include drinks and whether alcohol can be served in a public place depend on the municipality. A good first call is to your village hall, or to the City of Chicago's business licensing office if you are in the city. If you sell drinks or tickets that include alcohol, a license question almost always applies, so ask before you advertise.

## 3. Who is pouring

The Illinois Liquor Control Commission requires BASSET training, an approved responsible-service course, for people who serve alcohol in on-premises establishments, and course providers state that the certificate is valid for 3 years. Whether that rule applies to a private party that does not sell alcohol depends on the venue, the city and the contract, and the answer is often "the venue requires it anyway." The safe approach is to use a bar company or caterer that holds the license and employs BASSET-trained staff. You can verify their status with the Illinois Liquor Control Commission.

## 4. Insurance

Venues commonly require a certificate of insurance for events that serve alcohol, often naming the venue as additional insured. Host liquor liability coverage is a one-day policy sold by event insurers. Ask your venue for the exact limits it requires, then get a quote. A licensed bar company often carries its own coverage, which can satisfy the requirement.

## General responsibilities of any host

Illinois law sets a minimum age of 21 for alcohol, and hosts who serve minors or visibly intoxicated guests can face serious consequences. Even when a bartender does the pouring, plan to:

- Check IDs for anyone who looks under 30.
- Stop service to anyone who is visibly intoxicated.
- Offer food and non-alcoholic drinks. See [mocktail bar ideas](/blog/mocktail-bar-ideas).
- Plan rides home. Mention ride share and hotel blocks in your invitations.
- End the bar at least 30 minutes before the event ends.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a license to serve alcohol at a private party in Illinois?

It depends on whether you sell alcohol and on your city or village. A private party where drinks are free may not need a license, but a venue may require licensed bartenders. Ask your venue and your village hall.

### What is BASSET training?

BASSET is the Illinois Liquor Control Commission's alcohol server training program. It teaches responsible service, ID checks and when to refuse service. Providers state the certificate is valid for 3 years.

### Can I bring my own alcohol to a venue?

Only if the venue allows it. Many Chicago-area venues require you to buy from them or use an approved caterer, and some charge a corkage fee. Read your contract before you buy.

### Can alcohol be served in a Chicago park?

Chicago Park District rules limit alcohol to certain permitted venues and vendors, with insurance. Check directly with the Park District for your site, since rules vary by park and permit type.

### Do I need event liquor insurance?

Many venues require it. A licensed bar company may already carry coverage, so ask what they provide and what the venue requires.

### Who is responsible if a guest gets hurt after drinking?

Illinois law can hold people who provide alcohol responsible in some situations, and the details depend on who served and to whom. Talk to an attorney or your insurer about your situation.
