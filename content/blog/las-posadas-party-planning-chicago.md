**Quick answer**

A posada has three parts: a short outdoor procession with songs, a piñata, and a long meal with tamales and ponche. In Chicago, plan the walk for 10 to 20 minutes, have heat and seats waiting for older guests, and put the food tables indoors so people go from cold to warm. Count one chair for every guest who is not a child, and set two or three tables for food, drinks and the piñata treats.

</div>

Las Posadas are nine nights, Dec 16 to 24, that remember Mary and Joseph's search for a place to stay. In Mexican and Mexican-American neighborhoods in Chicago, such as Pilsen and Little Village, and in suburbs with large Mexican communities, families, churches and neighbors take turns hosting. Each night has a procession, songs at the door, a piñata and food. Customs vary by region and by family, so use what fits your night. This guide covers the setup for a host who has the whole thing at home, in a hall or in a church parking lot, in a Chicago December.

## The shape of the night

A posada flows in a certain order. Knowing it helps you plan space.

1. **Gather and sing.** Guests meet outside and carry candles or lanterns. Many families sing a traditional song, with some guests inside answering.
2. **Come inside.** The door opens after the song. Coats, hats and strollers need a place right away.
3. **Prayer or reading, then the party.** Some families keep this short for children.
4. **Piñata.** Kids go first, in age groups, and adults follow.
5. **Food.** Tamales, ponche and sweets. This is the longest part.

## The outdoor part, in a Chicago winter

In Chicago, Dec 16 to 24 can be cold and dark early. Sunset is around 4:20 to 4:30 pm, so a procession at 6 or 7 pm is in full dark. Keep the walk short, and light it well.

- **Route.** A sidewalk, a gangway and a front stoop is plenty. A route around the block means a long wait for older guests. Check with your village, park district or building about processions, street use and sidewalks, and shovel and salt the path.
- **Lights.** Battery-powered lanterns and candles with covers are easier in wind than open flames. See [lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events). Open flames and wind off Lake Michigan do not mix well.
- **Heaters.** If guests wait outside, a patio heater near the stoop helps. Propane heaters need fuel, which may not come with the rental, and they need clear space on all sides. Check rules for your location before using one. See [patio heaters, how many per guests](/blog/patio-heaters-how-many-per-guests) and [heating rentals](/rentals/heating-cooling).

## The indoor party

Plan for two zones, food and play. The piñata takes space. It needs a clear area of at least 10 to 12 feet across, a strong hook or a rope over a beam, and a ceiling that is high enough for swinging. Many families hang the piñata outside in good weather. In winter, a garage, hall or open basement is safer. Check ceilings and light fixtures before the party.

These are rules of thumb. Not everyone sits at once, because children run around and adults chat while standing. Always provide seats for older relatives, pregnant guests and anyone who cannot stand through a long night. Chairs with arms near the heat and the food are best. See [comfortable chairs for older guests](/blog/comfortable-chairs-for-older-guests) and [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

### Food: tamales, ponche and the rest

- **Ponche** is a hot fruit punch, often made with fruit, cane and spices. A large urn or a stockpot on a burner keeps it hot. Provide mugs, ladles and a spot for the cups. See [coffee urns](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party) and [hot drink stations](/blog/hot-cocoa-bar-winter-event).
- **Tamales** are often served by the dozen. A rule of thumb is 2 to 3 per adult at a party with other food. Hold them in a covered warming tray or a roaster. See [tamale-making setup](/blog/tamalada-tamale-making-party-setup).
- **Extras** like buñuelos, fruit and bolo bags for children.

For 40 guests, a 6-foot food table with a skirt is enough for tamales and sides, and a second table for drinks. See [buffet layouts](/blog/buffet-table-layouts).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Nine nights means nine hosts.** If you host just one night, tell your rental company which date. Dec 24 is a busy day for deliveries, and many companies close early. Ask for delivery Dec 22 or 23 if you host on Christmas Eve.
- **Doors and stairs.** Chicago two-flats and bungalows have narrow gangways. Measure before you order 8-foot tables.
- **Coats pile up fast.** A coat rack saves a bedroom. See [coat racks](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### When are Las Posadas?

Las Posadas run for nine nights, from Dec 16 to Dec 24. Each night is hosted by a different family, church or group. Christmas Eve is the last posada, often followed by midnight Mass for many families.

### What do you need to host a posada?

You need a place to walk and sing, a room for a meal, a piñata and food. Plan chairs for older guests, a warm place for coats, and enough light outside. Check with your village or building if you will use the street or a shared space.

### How many chairs do I need for a posada?

About one chair for every adult guest, and a few extras. Children usually run around and do not need seats. Chairs with arms for older relatives matter most.

### Can I use a heater outside for a posada?

Often yes, but check the rules for your location, and know that propane heaters need fuel and clear space around them. Keep them away from the door and from flammable decor. Indoor space is a safer choice for a long party.

### How much ponche and how many tamales per guest?

A rule of thumb is a 6 to 8 ounce mug of ponche per guest, with a refill, and 2 to 3 tamales per adult if there is other food. Adjust for your family and menu.

### Where do you hang the piñata in winter?

Indoors in a room with a high ceiling, or in a garage or hall. You need about 10 to 12 feet of clear space and a strong hook or beam. Check what is above the swing area, such as lights and fans.
