**Quick answer**

Help out-of-town guests with three things: a hotel plan, a ride plan and a welcome. Reserve a room block at one or two hotels near the event, share transit or shuttle details, and leave a simple welcome bag with water, snacks, a schedule and a local map. Keep bags light, label them, and deliver them to the front desk the day before guests arrive.

</div>

Guests who travel to your event spend money, time and energy to be there. They also arrive tired, hungry and unsure where to go. A good hotel plan and a small welcome bag solve most of that, and they cost far less than a lavish gift. The trick is organizing it: who books rooms, who stuffs bags, who delivers them and what the schedule says.

## Hotel logistics first

Pick hotels close to the venue, with a range of prices, and ask about a room block, a discounted group rate and a cutoff date. Confirm cancellation terms, parking costs, breakfast and shuttle availability. Tell guests how to book, give the group name and share the deadline. Track bookings in your guest list; see [guest list and RSVP tracker](/downloads/guest-list-rsvp-tracker).

Do not guarantee rooms you cannot pay for. A block usually lets guests book at a rate without a prepayment, but contracts differ, so read the terms and ask the hotel to explain any attrition clause.

## Planning table by guest count

These are general starting points. Adjust for your event and your hotel's terms.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Out-of-town guests | Hotel plan | Bag plan | Ride plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 15 | One hotel, no formal block | Simple bag or room note | Share rideshare tips |
| 15 to 40 | One to two hotels, block of 10 to 20 rooms | Bag per room | Map and transit or shuttle guide |
| 40 to 100 | Two hotels, tiered rates | Bag per room, delivered by front desk | Shuttle to venue is worth pricing |
| 100+ | Multiple hotels, event coordinator | Bags assembled by a helper team | Shuttle loops and a volunteer lead |

</div>

Rough planning guide only. Always confirm terms with your hotel in writing.

## Put it together in six steps

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Choose hotels.</strong> Compare distance, price and parking.</li>
<li><strong>Reserve a block.</strong> Confirm rate, deadline and cancellation terms.</li>
<li><strong>Send booking info.</strong> Include the group name, link or phone number and cutoff date.</li>
<li><strong>Plan rides.</strong> Share transit, rideshare or shuttle info.</li>
<li><strong>Assemble bags.</strong> Gather items, label bags and add a schedule.</li>
<li><strong>Deliver.</strong> Drop bags at the front desk the day before and confirm the hotel will hand them out.</li>
</ol>

## Making bags personal without overspending

A short handwritten note matters more than an item. Add a local touch, such as a snack from a Chicago bakery or a postcard map, and avoid anything that spills or melts. If you serve alcohol, check rules and the guests' ages. Consider dietary needs and allergies when picking snacks. For a shortlist of event gifts, you can also build in a favor at the event itself; see [guest book alternatives for weddings](/blog/guest-book-alternatives-for-weddings) for ideas to personalize.

If you have a schedule with several events, such as a rehearsal dinner, a welcome drink and a brunch, put them in one clear schedule; see [rehearsal dinner rentals](/blog/rehearsal-dinner-rentals) and [day after wedding brunch rentals](/blog/day-after-wedding-brunch-rentals).

## Budget and timing for bags

Order or collect items a few weeks ahead, since shipping and shortages can slow you down. Assemble bags in one sitting with a few helpers and keep a count of how many you need by room, not by guest. Bring a few extras for late arrivals. If you are shipping bags to the hotel, confirm the hotel accepts packages and when, and label each box with your event name and date.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago hotels vary by neighborhood, with downtown rooms close to transit and suburban hotels often offering free parking. Event weekends such as the marathon, large conventions and festivals can sell out rooms; see [planning around Chicago marathon weekend](/blog/planning-around-chicago-marathon-weekend) and [Lollapalooza weekend](/blog/planning-around-lollapalooza-weekend), and book early. Guests from other cities may be new to Chicago weather, so a note on layers is helpful, especially in spring and fall. For rides, share a clear plan; see [guest parking and transit plan](/blog/guest-parking-and-transit-plan-chicago). If you ask a hotel to hold bags, ask about any handling fees.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I put in a welcome bag?

Water, a snack, a schedule, a map and a thank-you note cover most needs. Add small comforts like mints or a pain reliever if you wish.

### How much should I spend on welcome bags?

Many hosts choose a modest amount per bag, and a few dollars to roughly fifteen is a common range. A thoughtful note matters more than the price.

### How many hotel rooms should I block?

Start with a number you expect to fill, such as 10 to 15 rooms, and ask the hotel about adjusting. Read the terms on attrition.

### Who delivers the bags?

Ask the hotel front desk to hold and distribute them, or have a helper hand them out at check-in. Confirm any fees.

### Do I need a shuttle?

Maybe. If the hotel is far from the venue or parking is hard, a shuttle can help. Compare price and rideshare options.

### When should I send hotel info?

As soon as you send invitations, or even with a save the date. Rooms near popular dates go quickly.

### Should I include alcohol in bags?

Check local rules and your guests' ages. Many hosts skip it and offer drinks at the event.
