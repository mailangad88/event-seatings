## 1. Long tables and family-style dining

Long tables feel social and photograph well. They need 8-foot banquet tables, longer cloths or runners and more careful sight lines for toasts. They do not save chairs, but they change how you count tables and how guests pass. See [long tables vs round tables](/blog/long-tables-vs-round-tables-wedding) and [farm tables for weddings](/blog/farm-tables-for-weddings). Check your venue's aisle width: a long table leaves less room behind chairs than a round.

## 2. Mixed seating, on purpose

Instead of one chair for the whole room, couples are using two styles: a cross-back or wishbone at the guest tables and a velvet chair or bench at the head table. The key is "on purpose": pick a shared finish or color so it reads as a decision. See [mixing chair styles](/blog/mixing-chair-styles-at-reception) and [head table chair ideas](/blog/sweetheart-table-chair-ideas).

## 3. Smaller guest lists, better pieces

Many couples are choosing smaller guest lists and spending the savings per guest. At 60 or 80 guests, a [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) at an estimated $18 to $25 costs a small total difference over a basic chair and shows in every photo. See [micro wedding rentals](/blog/micro-wedding-rentals).

## 4. Lounge areas that stay all night

Sofas, settees and low tables give guests a place to sit away from the dance floor. They also work for cocktail hour and the after-party. See [lounge furniture for cocktail hour](/blog/lounge-furniture-for-cocktail-hour) and [after-party rentals](/blog/wedding-after-party-late-night-rentals).

## 5. Indoor-outdoor receptions

Couples love a ceremony outside and a dinner under cover. The rental side is a tent, flooring, sidewalls, power and heat. In Chicago, plan for temperature swings from afternoon to night. See [tented wedding rentals](/blog/tented-wedding-rentals) and [outdoor ceremony rentals](/blog/outdoor-wedding-ceremony-rentals).

## 6. Warm metals and textured tables

Gold flatware, brass candle holders, textured linens and linen napkins in earth tones are common. They need accurate counts of flatware and napkins. See [gold flatware](/blog/gold-flatware-wedding-trend) and [overlays and layered linens](/blog/overlays-and-layered-linens).

## How to use a trend without regretting it

Ask three questions before you rent for a trend. Will it still look good in photos in ten years? Does it work with the room you actually have? And does it make the day better for guests, not just for the camera? A long table that cuts off every conversation across the room fails the second test, and a lounge that crowds the dance floor fails the third. Look for the version of the trend that is simple enough to change if the venue says no.

It also helps to see the trend in person. Ask a rental company to set a sample table, or look at a similar room on a site visit. A chair that looked right on a screen can feel different when you sit in it for an hour.

## What is not changing

Guests still want a comfortable seat, a table with room for plates, food that arrives on time and a bathroom that works. No trend fixes a room that is too small, a layout without aisles or a crew that is short on hands. Treat trends as a layer on top of the basics: right counts, right sizes, right schedule.

## Chicago-specific angles

Chicago couples are getting married in lofts, ballrooms, restaurants, museums, gardens and suburban lawns, each with different rules. The city's weather swings make flexible plans a trend of their own. Choose chairs that work indoors and outdoors, plan heaters and fans, and make sure your venue and permits support a tent. ## Frequently asked questions

### What are the biggest wedding trends for 2027?

Long tables, mixed seating, smaller guest lists with higher quality pieces, lounge areas, indoor-outdoor receptions and warm metals are the looks planners are discussing. Not every couple will use all of them.

### Are round tables out of style?

No. Rounds are still the most efficient layout for guest counts above about 100. Many couples mix rounds with long tables.

### What chair is trending for weddings?

Wood cross-back chairs, wishbone chairs and velvet dining chairs are popular for a warm, modern look. Clear ghost chairs stay popular for a light feel.

### How do I follow a trend on a budget?

Pick one visible element, such as the head table chair or the linens, and keep the rest simple.

### Do trends change how many chairs I need?

Not much. You still need one chair per guest and spares. Lounge areas add seating beyond the count.

### Should I plan a tent in Chicago?

If your outdoor plan depends on weather, yes or have an indoor backup. Check permits and book early.
