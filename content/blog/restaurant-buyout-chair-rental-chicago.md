A restaurant buyout is one of the easiest ways to host in Chicago. The food, the bar, the staff and the room come as a package. Seating is the one piece couples and planners often ask about, because a restaurant's chairs are built for a dinner service, not always for an event.

## When the house chairs are enough

If your guests will sit down for dinner at the restaurant's usual tables, the house chairs are almost always the right call. They fit the tables, the staff know how to set them, and there's nothing to deliver. That covers most rehearsal dinners, birthday dinners and small holiday parties.

## When it makes sense to rent

There are four common reasons:

1. **You need more seats than the restaurant owns.** A buyout often seats guests at long tables, and that layout can need more chairs than the restaurant keeps on hand, especially if you also want seats at the bar or in a lounge.
2. **You're having a ceremony in the room.** Ceremony rows are a separate set of chairs unless your plan is to flip the same chairs from rows to tables during cocktail hour.
3. **You want a different look.** Restaurant chairs are chosen for durability. If they clash with your décor or you want a specific style in your photos, swapping them out changes the whole room.
4. **You're adding a lounge.** Cocktail hour in a restaurant often happens around the bar, and a few lounge pieces give older guests somewhere to sit.

The hard part of a buyout is timing. If the restaurant serves lunch the day of your event, the window to clear its chairs and set yours can be short. Plan these with the restaurant in writing:

- **Where the house chairs go.** Some restaurants have storage space. Others need the rental company to take their chairs away and bring them back at the end of the night.
- **When you can load in.** Ask for the exact time the room is yours, which door to use, and whether there's an alley or a loading zone. Street parking downtown and in busy neighborhoods can make deliveries slow.
- **Who sets the chairs.** Restaurant staff, the rental company or your planner. Make sure it's one of them, not all three guessing.
- **Insurance.** Many Chicago venues ask vendors for a certificate of insurance before load-in. Ask early so it isn't a day-of surprise.

## Chairs that suit a restaurant room

Restaurant rooms tend to be warmer and more intimate than ballrooms, so chairs with wood, cane and woven seats look at home.

- **[Bentwood bistro](/chairs/bentwood):** the Parisian café chair. A natural fit for brasseries, wine bars and candlelit long tables.
- **[Wishbone](/chairs/wishbone):** clean Scandinavian lines for modern restaurants and West Loop-style lofts.
- **[Cane-back](/chairs/cane-back):** a softer, vintage feel for Italian trattorias and garden rooms.

Keep in mind that none of these three stack, so they need more storage space during the swap than stacking chairs do. If space is tight, tell your rental company up front.

## How many chairs to rent

Count only the seats the restaurant can't cover:

| Seats | How to count |
|---|---|
| Dinner | Guests minus the house chairs that match your layout (or all guests if you're swapping the look) |
| Ceremony | All guests, unless chairs will flip from ceremony rows to dinner |
| Lounge or bar | A handful of seats, older guests first |
| Buffer | A few extras for late RSVPs |

## Frequently asked questions

### Do restaurants provide chairs for a buyout?
Most do. A restaurant buyout usually includes the restaurant's own tables and chairs. You'd rent extra chairs only if you need more seats than it has, want a ceremony in the same room, or want a different style.

### Can I bring my own rental chairs into a restaurant?
Often yes, but ask first. Confirm where the restaurant's own chairs will be stored, what time you can load in, which door to use, and whether the rental company needs to provide a certificate of insurance.

### How long does a chair swap at a restaurant take?
It depends on the number of chairs, the access and the crew. The bigger constraint is usually the restaurant's schedule. If it serves lunch that day, the window between the last service and your guests arriving may be only a few hours, so plan it in writing.

### What chairs look best for a restaurant wedding or rehearsal dinner?
Wood and woven chairs like bentwood bistro, wishbone and cane-back suit most restaurant rooms. Pick one that matches the restaurant's style so the room looks planned rather than rearranged.
