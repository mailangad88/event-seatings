A house or condo party in Chicago means chairs on hardwood, tables in a living room and a crowd that scoots back. Floors in older greystones, bungalows and two-flats are often original hardwood, and a few hundred pounds of furniture moves around a lot in one night. A few cheap steps save the floor.

## Felt pads and glides

Felt pads and plastic glides spread the weight and slide instead of scratching. They work best on furniture you own. Rental chairs are different: companies usually do not want stickers or adhesive on their items, and sticky residue can mean a cleaning charge. Ask first. If pads are not allowed, protect the floor instead of the chair:

- Lay a **runner or rug** along the paths and under the dining area.
- Use **area rugs** with a grip pad under each table.
- Add **cardboard or a thin tarp** under a bar, buffet or heavy table where it sits.
- Keep **chair seating on rugs** where guests will scoot most.

A chair on a runner moves on the runner, not the floor.

## Moving heavy pieces

<div class="table-wrap">

| Piece | How to move it |
|---|---|
| Folding table | Two people, carried, legs folded |
| Round table | On edge, rolled slowly, or carried flat |
| Stack of chairs | A few at a time, not dragged |
| Heater, cooler, bar | Cart with soft wheels, or a blanket under it |
| Furniture you are clearing out | Slide on blankets or furniture sliders, not bare feet |

</div>

Anything that has to move across the floor can go on a blanket or a furniture slider. Clear the room before the crew arrives so they are not weaving around your couch. See [setup and breakdown](/blog/setup-and-breakdown-fees-event-rentals) for what a crew does and does not do.

## What most hosts find out too late

**Scratches usually belong to the person who owns the floor.** Rental companies cover item damage through the waiver, not your floor. See [damage waivers](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained). If you rent, your lease and security deposit are what count, and a landlord or condo board may have rules about delivery and floor protection. Ask before the date.

**Wet shoes are a bigger enemy than chairs in winter.** Slush, salt and grit grind into hardwood. Put mats at the door and a shoe area, and see [winter delivery](/blog/winter-event-rental-delivery-snow-and-ice) for the delivery side.

**A dolly or cart can leave marks too.** Ask the crew to lay a runner where carts roll, or to carry over the floor.

## Who pays for damage

- **A scuffed rental chair:** the rental company, or you, depending on your waiver.
- **A scratched floor in your own home:** you, unless a guest or contractor caused it and you can get them to pay.
- **A scratched floor in a rental unit:** you, through your security deposit or lease terms.
- **A scratched venue floor:** you, under the venue contract.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I put felt pads on rental chairs?

Ask the company first. Many do not allow adhesive on their items. If they say no, use a runner or rug under the chairs instead.

### How do I protect hardwood floors from folding chairs?

Lay rugs or runners where chairs are used, lift chairs instead of dragging them, and keep grit off the floor with mats at the door. Felt pads work if the company allows them.

### Will rental tables scratch my floor?

They can, especially when dragged. Carry tables, set them on a rug or runner and use a cover under heavy ones like a bar.

### Who pays if a rental chair scratches my floor?

In most cases you do, since your floor is not part of the rental. A waiver usually covers the item and not your property. Check your lease or homeowner terms.

### Is carpet safe for tables and chairs?

Carpet handles feet well, but legs can leave dents and spills can stain. Put boards or pads under heavy items and blot spills right away.

### Should I move my furniture out for a party?

If you can, yes. A clear room is faster to set up and safer for floors. Store pieces in a bedroom or garage.
