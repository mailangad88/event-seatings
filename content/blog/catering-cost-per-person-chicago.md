## What goes into a per-person price

The base is the menu: ingredients, preparation and portions. On top of that sit staff (cooks, servers, bartenders), rentals, delivery, setup, cleanup, service charges, taxes and sometimes a gratuity. Dietary needs, such as vegetarian, halal, kosher or allergen-safe meals, can change both menu and pricing.

Some caterers quote all-inclusive per-person prices, and others quote food only, leaving staff and rentals separate. Before you compare, find out which structure you are looking at. For help matching food amounts to your crowd, see [how many chafing dishes for a buffet](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet) and [plated vs buffet vs family style equipment](/blog/plated-vs-buffet-vs-family-style-equipment).

## Rough Chicago-area estimates by style

These are broad, rough estimates for food service only, before tax, service charge, alcohol and rentals, and they vary widely by caterer, menu and season. Use them to sense-check, then request quotes.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Service style | Rough range per guest | Typically pushes cost up | Typically brings cost down |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drop-off casual | $15 to $40 | Premium proteins, many dishes | Simple menu, fewer items |
| Buffet with staff | $30 to $90 | Many entrees, carving stations | Two entrees, seasonal sides |
| Stations or family style | $40 to $110 | Chef-attended stations, rentals | Fewer stations, shared platters |
| Plated dinner | $60 to $150+ | Courses, specialty cuts, more servers | One entree choice, fewer courses |
| Cocktail reception | $30 to $100 | Heavy passed apps, long event | Fewer items, stationary food |

</div>

Rough estimates only. They are not quotes and do not include alcohol, tax, service charge or tip.

## How to compare caterer quotes

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Share one brief.</strong> Give every caterer the same date, guest count, location, service style and dietary needs.</li>
<li><strong>Ask for an itemized proposal.</strong> Food, staff, rentals, delivery, service charge and tax on separate lines.</li>
<li><strong>Clarify what is included.</strong> Plates, glassware, linens, tables, chafing dishes, cleanup.</li>
<li><strong>Check kitchen access.</strong> Does your venue allow outside caterers, and what prep space is available?</li>
<li><strong>Ask about the headcount deadline.</strong> Know when numbers lock; see [final headcount deadline](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals).</li>
<li><strong>Taste and decide.</strong> Compare totals and menus, not only per-person numbers.</li>
</ol>

## Ways to lower the bill without cutting quality

Limit the menu to a few well-done dishes instead of many average ones. Choose a buffet or stations over plated service. Serve lunch or brunch instead of dinner; see [brunch party rentals](/blog/brunch-party-rentals-for-20-to-50). Trim the bar to beer, wine and a signature drink; see [beer and wine only bar setup](/blog/beer-and-wine-only-bar-setup). Ask whether renting your own plates and linens costs less than the caterer's rentals; see [working with a caterer and rental company](/blog/working-with-caterer-and-rental-company). Finally, an accurate headcount prevents paying for food nobody eats.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area caterers often price differently for city and suburban events. City events can involve limited kitchen access, loading restrictions and parking, which may raise staff time. Suburban events may add travel. Winter menus and holiday dates are in high demand, so book early; see [Chicago winter holiday party season booking](/blog/chicago-winter-holiday-party-season-booking). If you serve alcohol, review [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events) and ask your caterer what licensing they carry. Food safety matters outdoors; see [keeping food hot outdoors](/blog/keeping-food-hot-outdoors) and [keeping food cold at outdoor events](/blog/keeping-food-cold-at-outdoor-events).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does catering cost per person in Chicago?

It depends on style and menu. Casual drop-off food costs the least, while plated multi-course dinners cost the most. Many Chicago-area events fall somewhere between $20 and $150 or more per guest before tax and service charge, so get quotes for your specific plan.

### Is buffet cheaper than plated?

Often yes, because buffets usually need fewer servers and simpler plating. But a lavish buffet with many entrees can cost more than a simple plated meal. Compare menus, not only styles.

### What is a service charge on a catering bill?

It is a percentage added to cover staffing and operations. It may or may not be a gratuity for servers. Ask exactly what it covers and whether tip is extra.

### Does the quote include plates and silverware?

Not always. Some caterers include rentals, and some leave them to you. Our [rental order calculator](/downloads/rental-order-calculator) helps you count what you need.

### How can I reduce catering costs?

Simplify the menu, choose a less formal service style, trim the bar and confirm an accurate headcount. Ask your caterer for options at several price points.

### How far ahead should I book a caterer?

Many people book several months ahead for weddings and peak seasons, and a few weeks for small parties. Popular caterers fill up for holiday dates.

### How much food do I need per guest?

A common rule is to plan for slightly more than you think for appetizers and sides, and let your caterer advise on portions. For buffets, caterers often plan two entrees for mid-size crowds. Ask what they recommend.
