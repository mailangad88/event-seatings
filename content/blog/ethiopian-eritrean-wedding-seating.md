Chicago is home to Ethiopian and Eritrean communities, and weddings are often large events with hundreds of guests and many traditions. A wedding may include a church service or a separate religious ceremony, a procession, a feast and long dance sets with live or recorded music. The details differ by region, religion and family, so this guide focuses on the seating and room plan that serve those traditions.

## What shapes the layout

Common features include:

- **Large guest lists.** Families often invite wide networks of relatives and friends.
- **A decorated head area.** The couple often sits on a stage or at a head table with thrones, flowers and drapery.
- **Shared dishes.** Many families serve food on large platters for the table to share. This needs table space and sometimes a lazy Susan or a central stand.
- **Coffee ceremony.** A coffee ceremony may take place in a corner, with a low stool, a tray and small cups. Ask the family what they need.
- **Dancing.** A lively dance floor with a DJ or live band.

## Coffee and dessert corners

Many families host a coffee service. It needs a small table, a stool or chair for the server, a tray, a place for charcoal or a safe heat source if used and good ventilation. Check with your venue about open flame, incense and smoke, which can trigger alarms. For a coffee station, see [coffee and tea service rental](/blog/coffee-and-tea-service-rental), and for a sweets table see the [dessert table equipment checklist](/blog/dessert-table-equipment-checklist).

## Chicago notes

- Ask your venue about incense, smoke and open-flame rules before planning a coffee ceremony.
- Banquet halls in the city and suburbs often cap guest counts at rounds. Confirm early.
- Winter weddings need a coat plan. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many guests sit at a table?

Eight to ten is common. A 72-inch round seats 10 and leaves room for shared platters.

### Do I need a stage?

Many couples use a stage so everyone can see them. If the hall has one, you can decorate it. If not, rent a riser.

### Can I do a coffee ceremony at the venue?

Often yes, but ask about open flame, charcoal and smoke. Many venues have rules.

### How big should the dance floor be?

About 4 to 5 square feet per dancing guest. For 300 guests, a 28 x 28-foot floor is a good starting point.

### What chairs are best for long events?

Padded stackable chairs for guests and thrones for the couple. Add armchairs for elders.

### How early should I book?

As soon as your date and hall are set. Peak summer dates fill quickly. See when to book.
