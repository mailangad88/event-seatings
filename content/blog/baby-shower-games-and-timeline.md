**Quick answer**

A baby shower works best at about two to three hours: 30 minutes of arrival and mingling, 45 minutes of food, two or three short games totaling about 20 minutes, 30 to 40 minutes of gift opening, and 10 minutes for cake and photos. Choose games that are optional, easy to explain and do not embarrass the guest of honor.

Baby shower games have a reputation: some guests love them and some quietly dread them. The trick is to keep them short, optional and well spaced. A good timeline lets games fill natural gaps, like the moment after lunch, without taking over the party. Here is a simple schedule and a list of games that tend to work.

## A two-hour timeline

Adjust the times to your party, but keep the order.

**0:00 to 0:30, arrival and mingling.** Greet guests, point them to the food and drinks, and set up an easy icebreaker such as a guess-the-baby-photo wall or an advice card table. Guests find their seats.

**0:30 to 1:15, lunch or brunch.** Serve food while guests sit and talk. A buffet or family-style meal keeps things relaxed. Keep the host free to mingle.

**1:15 to 1:35, games.** Play two or three short games. Hand out small prizes. Keep it light and let anyone opt out.

**1:35 to 2:10, gifts.** Seat the guest of honor in a central spot. A helper writes down gifts and who gave them. Keep comments short so the gift opening does not drag on.

**2:10 to 2:30, cake and photos.** Cut the cake, take group photos and hand out favors.

If the shower will run longer, add a longer mingling block rather than more games.

## Games that tend to work

**Guess the baby food.** Taste or smell jars and guess the flavor. Silly and quick.

**Baby shower bingo.** Guests fill squares with likely gifts and mark them as they are opened. It runs during gifts, so no extra time.

**Guess the mom's belly size.** Measured with ribbon or yarn. Skip it if the parent-to-be would not enjoy it, and ask first.

**Advice cards or wishes for baby.** Quiet and sentimental. Guests write a note while they eat.

**Who knows mom best.** A short trivia round. Keep questions friendly.

**Nursery rhyme or song challenge.** Teams fill in lyrics.

**Diaper raffle.** Guests bring a pack of diapers for a raffle ticket.

**Baby photo match.** Guests match photos of the guests to the people.

Avoid games that involve food that looks like a diaper, embarrassing questions or physical contact. Ask the parent-to-be what they would find fun. For more party-game ideas see [carnival games for parties](/blog/carnival-games-for-parties).

## Pick games by group size

<div class="table-wrap">

| Group size | Best game types | Seating | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 15 | Whole-group trivia, advice cards | One or two tables | Easy to include everyone |
| 15 to 30 | Bingo, taste tests, photo match | Rounds of 6 to 8 | Table teams work well |
| 30 to 50 | Table competitions, raffle | Rounds of 8 | Use a microphone for rules |
| 50 or more | Bingo, raffle, team games | Rounds of 8 | Keep to two games |

</div>

Group size changes how you run games. For a bigger shower, use table-based games and a microphone so everyone can hear the rules.

## Gift opening without the drag

Gift opening can drag. A few ideas keep it moving: have a helper hand over gifts and note who gave each one, ask the guest of honor to read cards silently and say thank you aloud, and open the biggest or most meaningful gifts first. Some parents prefer to open gifts later in private, which is also fine. Provide a gift table near the entrance and an easy way to take gifts to the car. See [card box and gift table ideas](/blog/card-box-and-gift-table-ideas).

## Plan seats so games work

Table games work when tables of 6 to 8 sit together, with pencils and cards on each table. Make sure every guest has a clear view of the guest of honor. See [baby shower rentals and seating](/blog/baby-shower-rentals-and-seating) and [bridal shower table setup](/blog/bridal-shower-table-setup) for layouts that also work for baby showers.

## Prizes and favors

Prizes can be small: a candle, a coffee gift card, a plant or a snack. Favors should be useful and affordable, such as seed packets or a cookie. Keep them in one place so you can hand them out without searching.

## Make it inclusive

Co-ed showers, family showers and workplace showers each have a different feel. Ask the parent-to-be what they prefer. Keep games gender-neutral and sensitive to different family situations. See [gender reveal party setup](/blog/gender-reveal-party-setup) if the reveal is part of the shower.

## Supplies checklist

Pens and pencils, printed game cards, a timer, prizes, a gift list notebook, tape and scissors, a trash bag, a camera, a basket for cards, and a microphone for larger groups.

## Order of operations

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area showers are often held in a home, a restaurant private room or a community room, and each has its own rules. Some restaurants limit decor, so ask before you hang a balloon garland. In summer, an outdoor shower under a tent or canopy needs shade, fans and a rain plan. See [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent). In winter, a coat rack and a clear entry help. Check with your village or park district if you want to reserve a pavilion or room, as policies on food, decor and capacity vary. Parking can be tight in city neighborhoods, so share directions.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many games should I play at a baby shower?

Two or three short games are plenty. Make them optional and keep each under ten minutes. Too many games can feel like homework.

### What are some good baby shower games?

Popular games include baby shower bingo, guess the baby food, advice cards, who knows mom best, a diaper raffle and baby photo match. Ask the parent-to-be what they like.

### How long should a baby shower last?

Two to three hours is typical, including food, a few games, gifts and cake.

### What is a good baby shower schedule?

Mingle for 30 minutes, eat for 45, play games for 20, open gifts for 30 to 40, then cake and photos. Shift the order if guests need to leave early.

### When do you open gifts at a baby shower?

Usually after the meal and games, when guests are settled. Some families open gifts after the party in private. Choose what is comfortable for the parent-to-be.

### How do I keep a baby shower from being boring?

Keep the food good, the schedule short and the games optional. Add one hands-on activity, such as a station for decorating onesies or writing wishes.

### Do I need to rent anything for baby shower games?

Not usually, but tables and chairs matter. Seat guests at rounds of 6 to 8 so table games are easy, and provide a microphone for groups over 30.
