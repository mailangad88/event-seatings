**Quick answer**

A modern minimalist wedding relies on a few well-chosen pieces: a single chair style with clean lines (wishbone, shell or ghost), long tables or round tables with simple neutral linens, plain white or matte tableware, one metal finish and room to breathe. Rent fewer items, in a limited palette, and let the architecture and food do the work. Plan about 12 square feet per guest to keep the room airy.

</div>

Minimalism is not the same as cheap or empty. It is a decision to remove everything that doesn't earn its place, which makes the pieces that remain more visible. For rentals, that means paying attention to line, finish and spacing. Chicago has a strong architecture backdrop for this look: lofts, galleries, museums and modern venues in the West Loop, River North and the suburbs.

## The minimalist rule of three

Limit the room to three variables: one chair, one linen color and one metal. Everything else repeats.

## Chairs

Pick a chair with a clear silhouette and no ornament. [Wishbone chairs](/chairs/wishbone) (estimated $14 to $18) bring a curved Danish look; [mid-century shell chairs](/chairs/shell) ($9 to $12) suit terracotta, sage or black; clear [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) ($10 to $14) vanish into the room. Black or natural oak wishbones work with white linens and concrete floors. Avoid mixing three styles; if you want contrast, use one accent chair at the head table. See [wishbone chairs for a modern wedding](/blog/wishbone-chairs-modern-wedding).

## Tables

Long tables fit the minimalist look and reduce visual clutter. A single 8-foot banquet table seats 8 to 10, so 100 guests need 10 to 12 tables; arrange them in two or three long rows. See [long tables vs round tables](/blog/long-tables-vs-round-tables-wedding) and [banquet table sizes](/blog/banquet-table-sizes-6-ft-vs-8-ft). Leave 6 feet between rows so servers can pass.

## Linens

Choose solid linen in white, ivory, oatmeal, stone or charcoal. Skip patterns. Options:

- Floor-length cloths on each table.
- Runner only on a wood or stone table.
- Napkins in the same color or one contrast.

See [table runner sizes](/blog/table-runner-sizes-and-styles) and [linen colors by season](/blog/linen-colors-by-season).

## Tableware and glassware

Plain white or matte china with no pattern, simple flatware in one finish (matte black or brushed gold are popular) and clear stemmed or stemless glasses. Skip chargers or use a single flat one. The goal is restraint without losing function. See [gold-rim vs white china](/blog/gold-rim-vs-white-china) and [gold flatware](/blog/gold-flatware-wedding-trend).

## Decor by subtraction

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Start with the room.</strong> What is already beautiful?</li>
<li><strong>Remove</strong> anything that competes: extra linens, many candles, signage clutter.</li>
<li><strong>Repeat one element</strong> down the table: a single stem, a candle, a bowl.</li>
<li><strong>Keep sight lines open.</strong> Low or very tall centerpieces, nothing at eye level.</li>
<li><strong>Edit last:</strong> take one thing away before the guests arrive.</li>
</ol>

## Where minimalism goes wrong

The most common failure is not too many pieces, it is too many small decisions that each look slightly different. Three white plates that are not the same white, two chair finishes that almost match, a runner that is a shade too warm: in a restrained room, these stand out. Ask your rental company to confirm that every piece in a category comes from the same lot, and view samples together in daylight and in the evening lighting.

The second failure is leaving the room feeling unfinished. Minimalism still needs warmth. A single piece of greenery, a good candle, a textured napkin or a wood chair gives the room life without clutter. And guests still need basics: a visible bar, a coat spot, clear signs to the restrooms. Edit decor, not hospitality.

## Lighting

Warm dimmable light, a few pendants, candles in simple holders. Avoid colored uplights. For tents, use white fabric and a few strings; see [tent lighting ideas](/blog/tent-lighting-ideas).

## Chicago notes

Loft and gallery venues often have concrete floors and limited ceiling attachment points. Ask what you can hang or tape, load-in hours and elevator access. Sound can bounce off hard surfaces, so add linens or soft furnishings to absorb it, which makes conversation easier. Winter coat check needs a clean spot out of the main view.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a minimalist wedding?

A wedding with a limited palette, few decor pieces and a clean layout that lets the venue and food stand out. Rentals are fewer but chosen with care.

### Is a minimalist wedding cheaper?

It can be, because you rent fewer items. But quality pieces cost the same, so the savings come from skipping extras, not downgrading.

### What chairs fit a minimalist wedding?

Wishbone, shell and clear ghost chairs have clean lines. Pick one style and use it throughout.

### What linens suit a minimalist look?

Solid neutrals in white, ivory, oatmeal or charcoal. Runners on wood tables are popular.

### How many tables do I need?

Count guests divided by 8 to 10 per 8-foot banquet. Use rounds if the room is irregular.

### Can I do minimalist in a tent?

Yes. Use white tent walls, flooring, simple lighting and the same neutral table settings.
