**Quick answer**

A common industry rule of thumb is 1 portable restroom for every 50 guests at an event up to 4 hours long. Add 15 to 20 percent more units if alcohol is served, and add more for longer events. Include at least 1 ADA-accessible unit, and plan about 1 accessible unit for every 20 standard units. For 100 guests with alcohol, that means 3 units, and for 200 guests, 5 or 6.

</div>

Too few restrooms is the complaint guests remember. A line of 15 people in the heat, or in a Chicago October drizzle, can sour an otherwise good event. Too many is a waste. This guide gives you a ratio you can defend, a chart for common crowd sizes and a few adjustments that matter.

## The basic ratio

According to equipment rental providers like United Rentals, a standard planning ratio is at least one portable toilet per 50 guests for events up to 4 hours. For longer or multi-day events, the number goes up, and providers advise talking to the vendor about service. If alcohol is served, many suggest adding 10 to 20 percent more units. OSHA's separate rules for workers, such as one toilet for 1 to 20 workers, apply to job sites, not guests at events.

## What changes the number

**Alcohol.** More drinks mean more visits. Add 15 to 20 percent as a baseline.

**Event length.** After 4 hours, guests visit again. Ask your vendor about extra units or servicing.

**Weather.** Hot days mean more water, and cold days mean guests stay inside tents and visit all at once.

**Guest mix.** Many women, children or older guests raise the load. Lines are often longer for women's facilities, so favor more units overall.

**Peak times.** Restrooms get busy right after the ceremony, after dinner and at the end of the night. Plan for peaks, not the average.

**Indoor fixtures.** If the venue has indoor restrooms, count their stalls and urinals before renting. A venue with 6 fixtures for 100 guests may need only a few extras. See [restroom trailer: when you need one](/blog/restroom-trailer-when-you-need-one).

## A worked example

Say you are hosting a 120-guest fundraiser for a nonprofit in a rented tent behind a community center, with a cash bar, running 5 hours. The base ratio gives 120 divided by 50, or 2.4 units. Because alcohol is served, add about 15 to 20 percent, which brings you to roughly 2.8 units. Because the event runs past 4 hours, add a cushion. Round up to 4 units, one of them accessible, and ask the center how many indoor fixtures guests may use. If the center has two indoor restrooms with 3 stalls each that guests can reach, you might rent fewer units, but only if the building is open to guests and the route is accessible.

## Placement and extras

- Place units close enough to be found but away from dining, food and the ceremony.
- Provide a lit path for evening events.
- Add hand-washing stations and sanitizer.
- Ask for service during longer events, to restock and clean.
- Mark ADA units clearly and place them on a firm, level route. See [ADA restroom considerations](/blog/ada-restroom-considerations-for-events).
- Sign every unit with directions.

## Chicago permit note

Requirements for temporary toilets at public events vary by city, village, county and park district, and festival-scale events face stricter rules than private parties. If you are hosting on public land or closing a street, the permitting office will tell you what sanitation they require. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many portable toilets do I need for 100 guests?

Two for a dry event under 4 hours, and 3 if alcohol is served. Include one accessible unit in the count.

### How many porta potties for 200 people?

Four for a short, dry event, and 5 or 6 with alcohol or a longer program. Round up, because lines grow fast.

### How many do I need for a wedding?

Weddings run long and serve alcohol, so use the higher numbers. For 150 guests, plan 4 units, plus any indoor fixtures.

### Do I need an ADA portable restroom?

For public events, include at least one, and about 1 for every 20 units is a common practice. Ask your vendor and municipality.

### How often should units be serviced?

For multi-day events, daily servicing is common. For one-day events, ask your vendor about mid-event service for long programs.

### Do kids count as guests?

Yes. Children use restrooms as often as adults and often need help, so count them.
