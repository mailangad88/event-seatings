Plan 2 to 6 extra seats close to the couple for sponsors and parents, a pair of kneeling cushions or two chairs for the lazo ceremony if your family includes it, and a dance floor sized for a room that dances all night. Mexican and Mexican-American weddings in the Chicago area often bring together several generations, long guest lists and sponsors who help the couple with the ceremony, the reception or both. The seating plan has to honor those relationships. It also has to work for a room that is often full of dancing, so you want clear paths between tables and a dance floor close to the family.

## Who the padrinos are and why they affect seating

Padrinos and madrinas (godparents or sponsors) are people the couple chooses to honor. Families use the title differently. Some couples have one pair of padrinos, others have several, each sponsoring something specific such as the rings, the bouquet, the cake, the music or the lazo. Because each sponsor is a guest of honor, they usually sit with the couple's families rather than at a general table.

Ask each family:

- How many sponsors do we have, and are they couples or individuals?
- Do sponsors sit at the head table, at a table beside it or with their own families?
- Who needs to be near the dance floor, such as grandparents or the parents of the couple?

## The lazo and arras: what to plan for

Many Catholic Mexican ceremonies include a lazo (a cord or rosary draped around the couple) and the arras (13 coins), and sponsors often present them. Whether the ceremony happens at a church or on the reception site, the physical needs are small but easy to forget:

- **Two kneeling cushions or two chairs** placed in front of the altar or ceremony area. Church kneelers can serve if the ceremony is in a church; confirm what the church provides.
- **A small table** for the lazo, arras and unity candle, if used, with a cloth.
- **Chairs for sponsors** in the first row, ideally with a clear line of sight.
- **Clear space** for photographers and a ring bearer or coin bearer.

If the ceremony is at a church, ask what the church provides before renting anything. Many churches have their own rules about outside items.

## How many seats for a boda

Mexican wedding guest lists often run large. Plan table counts first and adjust. Rounds of 8 or 10 both work, and 72-inch rounds make 10-seat tables comfortable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do padrinos sit at a Mexican wedding?

There is no single rule. Many couples seat padrinos at a table beside the head table or at the head table itself. Others seat them with their own families. Ask your parents and padrinos what feels right and make sure sponsors have a prominent spot.

### How many padrinos can a couple have?

There is no fixed number. Some couples choose one pair, others have a dozen sponsors, each for a different part of the wedding. Plan seating for each sponsor and their spouse or partner.

### Do I need special chairs for the lazo ceremony?

Usually just two chairs or kneeling cushions for the couple and chairs for sponsors nearby. If the ceremony is in a church, the church often provides kneelers. Ask before you rent.

### How much space do I need for a lively dance floor?

A common rule of thumb is 4 to 5 square feet per dancing guest. For 150 guests, plan about 20 x 20 feet and lean larger if you expect most of the room to dance.

### Can I rent chairs for a Chicago backyard or tent boda?

Yes. For backyard and tent weddings, rent chairs, tables, linens and a dance floor together so they arrive in one delivery. Check village rules for tents. Our [tent permit guide](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs) explains what to confirm.

### How early should I plan seating?

Start once your venue and guest estimate are set, and finalize the padrinos list a few weeks before. The earlier you decide who sits where, the easier it is to size tables correctly.
