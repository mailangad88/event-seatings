**Quick answer**

Choose white for a crisp, modern, high-contrast look and ivory for a warm, romantic, softer one. Ivory flatters wood chairs, gold accents, candlelight and warm flowers. White suits clear chairs, silver, bright daylight tents and bold color palettes. Never mix the two on the same table or in the same sightline, because the difference looks like a mistake, not a choice.

</div>

Ivory versus white is the most common linen question, and it is easy to get wrong because the difference is small on a swatch and obvious across a room. Pairing the wrong shade with your china, flowers, chairs or the bride's dress can make a clean look appear dingy. Here is how to decide for a Chicago-area wedding or event.

White is a clear, bright, slightly cool tone. Ivory is white with a touch of cream or yellow. Rental companies name their shades differently, and "ivory" at one supplier can match "cream" or "antique white" at another. Always ask to compare the actual cloth.

If you are using clear [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost), white linens look crisp. If you are using wood [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) or [gold infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity), ivory warms the room.

## The mix-up to avoid

Two shades of off-white in one view always reads as an error. The most common pairings that fail:

- White tablecloth and ivory napkins on the same table
- White house linens at the cake table and ivory guest tables
- White chair sashes against ivory cloths

If you need contrast, make it deliberate: use a deeper color for napkins, such as champagne or gray, instead of nearly-matching shades. See [cloth napkin sizes and colors](/blog/cloth-napkin-sizes-and-colors).

## What the venue and the season do

Chicago venues vary hugely. A white-walled gallery or hotel ballroom with white linens can read cold. A historic hall with wood paneling and warm bulbs makes ivory glow. Summer tents often have bright, cool light that turns ivory slightly gray; winter ballrooms with candles flatter ivory.

## A note on photos

Photographers handle white and ivory differently. White can overexpose and lose texture in bright light; ivory keeps detail and reads warmer in color correction. If your gown is bright white, an ivory table can make it look cleaner by contrast. Read [tablecloth colors for photos](/blog/tablecloth-colors-for-photos) for more.

## Care and condition

White shows stains and dinginess faster, while ivory hides small marks. If your menu has red wine, tomato sauce or oil, ivory is more forgiving. Rental companies clean linens between uses, but marks sometimes remain; ask how they inspect and what their policy is. We explain typical rules in [linen stain and damage policies](/blog/linen-stain-and-damage-policies).

## Quick decision tree

- **Modern venue, clear chairs, bright daylight:** white.
- **Warm venue, wood or gold chairs, evening:** ivory.
- **Ivory gown:** ivory or champagne.
- **Not sure:** ivory, because it is more forgiving of light, stains and varied whites.

## Test it before you commit

Because the difference is subtle, the most reliable way to decide is a side-by-side test. Ask your supplier for a small swatch or a photo of each cloth laid on a table, then compare them next to the actual items they will sit beside: a plate, a napkin, your invitation suite and a printed photo of the flowers. Look at them in two kinds of light, a window and a warm lamp.

If you are choosing for a ballroom, ask the venue what bulbs they use. Warm white bulbs around 2700K make ivory glow and white look slightly cream. Cooler bulbs make white look clinical and ivory look flat. If your lighting designer is adding uplighting in a color, the color will tint both shades, so view them under that light too.

## Mixing cloth, napkin and chair

Ivory and white are not rivals at every layer, as long as there is a clear hierarchy. A common arrangement that works: ivory tablecloths, a deeper champagne or gold napkin, and wood or gold chairs. A white and silver variant: white cloths, gray or navy napkins, and clear or white chairs. The rule is that near-matches fight and clear differences work.

Also check the less obvious white items. Candles, menu cards, place cards, table numbers and the cake all have their own shade of white. They are small enough that a slight difference does not matter, but a big linen mismatch beside them can. If your stationery is a warm cream, ivory linens will look intentional; if it is bright white, go with white.

## For photos and gowns

Your photographer will edit white balance for the day, and they handle either shade. If a bride's gown is bright white, the table should not be pure white right beside it in portraits taken at the head table. Ivory gives a gentle step down. If the gown is ivory, the opposite applies: white linens make the gown look warm, which many people like. Ask your photographer which they prefer for the lighting in your venue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ivory or white better for tablecloths?

Neither is better; it depends on the room and the rest of the table. White is crisp and modern, and ivory is warmer and softer. Pick one based on chairs, china, flowers and lighting.

### Can I mix ivory and white at a wedding?

It is risky. Two near whites look like a mismatch. If you want variety, use a clearly different color for accents instead.

### What color linen looks best in photos?

Both photograph well when well lit. Ivory is more forgiving under strong flash, and white can blow out highlights. Ask your photographer.

### Do ivory linens look yellow?

In cool light they look closer to cream, and in warm candlelight they look golden. Check a swatch under the venue's real lights.

### Which shade hides stains better?

Ivory hides small marks better than white. Dark colors hide them most.

### Do white linens go with a gold chair?

Yes, though ivory usually blends warmer with gold. White gives a crisp, high-contrast look that some couples prefer.
