**Quick answer**

A baptism or christening party is usually a daytime lunch or brunch after the church service, with 30 to 100 guests, many of them older relatives and young children. Plan one chair per guest, rounds of 8 or 10 or long family tables, a cake and dessert table, a gift and card table, a few high chairs and a spot for the baby to rest. Soft colors and floor-length linens are common. Book rentals as soon as you set the church date, because spring Saturdays fill quickly.

</div>

Baptisms and christenings take place in many Christian traditions, and the celebration afterward ranges from cake at home to a banquet-hall luncheon. In Chicago and the suburbs, families often host in a parish hall, a restaurant private room, a banquet hall or a backyard. Whatever the setting, the guest list usually spans babies to great-grandparents, so comfort and flexibility matter more than style.

## What this party needs

- **Seats for everyone at once.** The meal follows the service, so most guests sit together. Plan one chair per guest.
- **High chairs and a baby station.** Parents need a safe place for babies. See [high chairs and baby station at events](/blog/high-chairs-and-baby-station-at-events).
- **A kids table** for toddlers and older children, near their parents.
- **A cake and dessert table.** The cake is often the center of attention.
- **A gift and card table**, or a card box. See [card box and gift table ideas](/blog/card-box-and-gift-table-ideas).
- **A photo area**, often with a backdrop or simple balloon display.

Count children as guests. A child under 5 may share a parent's chair, but plan chairs for everyone to be safe. For a church hall or restaurant, ask what is included. Many parish halls have tables and chairs.

## Linens and colors

White, ivory, soft blue, soft pink and sage are common baptism colors, but any palette works. A 72-inch round table with a floor-length cloth needs a 132-inch round, and a 60-inch round needs a 120-inch round. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart). Keep patterned linens simple, because the cake and flowers will carry the decor.

## The cake and dessert table

A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth fits a cake, a cake stand, plates and napkins. Place it away from direct sun and the entrance. See [cake stand sizes](/blog/cake-stand-sizes-for-wedding-cakes) and [dessert and cake plates](/blog/dessert-and-cake-plates-how-many). Plan 1 dessert plate and 1 fork per guest, plus a few extra.

## Timing the day around naps and church

Most baptism parties follow the service, so the schedule is tight. A common pattern is church at 11 a.m. and lunch by 1 p.m., which leaves little time to arrange tables if rentals arrive late. Ask the rental company to deliver and set up before the service starts, and ask a friend or relative to be on site to receive the delivery. Babies often nap in the early afternoon, so a quiet corner with a portable crib or a stroller parking area helps. Keep the cake cutting early so tired toddlers do not miss it, and plan the toast before the food gets cold.

Think through the flow once before the day: guests arrive, find a seat, eat, see the cake, take photos and say goodbye. If the entrance, the cake table and the photo corner are all in one spot, the line backs up. Spread them out and mark each with a small sign.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many guests come to a baptism party?

Many are small, 30 to 60 people, but it varies. Some families invite everyone from the parish. Count your confirmed list, including children.

### How many chairs do I need?

One for each guest, including children, plus a few extras. Add high chairs for babies.

### What kind of tables work best?

Rounds of 8 to 10 encourage conversation, and long tables suit family-style meals. Pick the table shape that fits your room.

### Do I need a cake table?

Most parties have one. A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth is plenty for a cake and plates.

### Can I host at home?

Yes, if your space fits the guest list. Rent folding chairs and tables, and measure doorways and walkways first.

### Where do I put the gifts?

On a small table near the entrance, with a card box. Keep it where the host can keep an eye on it.
