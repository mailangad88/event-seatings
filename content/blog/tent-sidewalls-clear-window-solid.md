**Quick answer**

Solid white sidewalls give the most privacy, shade and wind protection and cost the least. Cathedral window sidewalls add see-through arches and a formal look. Clear sidewalls show the view and keep light in the tent, but they trap heat, show condensation and work best for evenings and cool weather. For most Chicago-area events, use solid sidewalls on the windy and public sides and clear or window panels where you want the view.

</div>

Sidewalls are the part of a tent rental that people add last and wish they had planned first. They decide whether the tent feels like a room or like a roof on stilts, and they change what the weather does to your guests. In the Chicago area, where one event can see sun, wind off the lake and a thunderstorm in an afternoon, the right sidewall mix matters. This guide compares the three common types and gives a plan by season.

## Solid white sidewalls

**Best for:** privacy, shade, a backdrop for projection or lighting, wind protection, or a catering side you do not want guests to see.

- Block sun and give true shade in July and August.
- Cut drafts and rain spray in spring and fall.
- Are the standard and usually the cheapest.
- Look plain, so many hosts add draping or flowers. See [tent decor, draping and florals](/blog/tent-decor-draping-and-florals).

## Cathedral window sidewalls

**Best for:** weddings and galas where you want a formal, architectural look with some of the view.

- Arched clear vinyl windows set in white panels.
- Show the garden or lake without exposing the whole tent.
- Often priced higher than solid panels. Ask your company for the difference per panel.
- Windows still hold heat and fog up when the tent is full.

## Clear sidewalls

**Best for:** evening parties in spring and fall, tents with a great view, and using the tent as a glass pavilion at night.

- Show string lights and chandeliers glowing from outside.
- Keep rain and wind out while keeping the view.
- Trap heat in summer and can feel like a greenhouse. See [cooling a tent in summer](/blog/cooling-a-tent-in-summer).
- Collect condensation inside when it is cool and humid outside.
- Show every puddle, trash can and port-a-potty beyond the tent. Check what is behind each side.

## Wind and sidewalls

A tent with all sidewalls closed acts like a bigger sail than one with open sides, so it needs a heavier anchoring plan. Your tent company may tell you to open or drop the walls if the wind rises. Ask in advance what wind speed triggers that. See [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety) and [stakes vs ballast](/blog/tent-anchoring-stakes-vs-ballast).

## Doors, entrances and service sides

Plan where guests enter, where the caterer enters and where the bar, DJ and restrooms sit. A few panels are doors. Exit paths must remain clear for code, so do not block a door with a decor wall or a buffet table. Chicago's permit process asks for floor plans with egress details for tents over 600 square feet. See [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).

## Seating and the walls

Walls change how chairs feel. Clear panels at night make a white [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) nearly vanish, which suits a modern look. A solid white tent loves warm wood from a [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back). See also [tent layout with dance floor and bar](/blog/tent-layout-dance-floor-and-bar).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are clear tent sidewalls worth it?

They are worth it for evening events in cool weather and for tents with a view. They are not worth it for hot afternoons because they trap heat. Many hosts use clear panels on only one or two sides.

### Do I need sidewalls on my tent?

Not always. Summer parties often do better with open sides for airflow. Add sidewalls for rain, wind, privacy or cool weather, and ask for a plan to drop or roll them quickly.

### What are cathedral window sidewalls?

They are tent walls with arched clear windows set into white vinyl. They give a formal look and a partial view. They typically cost more than solid white walls.

### Do clear sidewalls keep a tent warm?

They block wind and trap some warmth, but a tent still needs a heat source in cold weather. Check the rules on heaters with your village first, since many restrict gas heaters.

### Can I leave some sides open?

Yes. Mixed setups are common: open sides on breezy days and closed sides at night. Make sure panels can be raised or lowered without moving tables.

### Do sidewalls make a tent more likely to blow over?

Closed sidewalls catch more wind, so the tent needs stronger anchoring and a lower threshold for dropping the walls. Ask the company for its wind plan.
