**Quick answer**

A rule of thumb is one bartender for every 40 to 50 guests at a full bar and one for every 75 guests at a beer and wine bar. For 100 guests plan 2 bartenders and 1 or 2 bar stations; for 200 guests plan 4 bartenders and 2 stations. Add one more if cocktail hour is short or you serve signature cocktails, because made-to-order drinks take longer than pouring wine or beer.

</div>

Too few bartenders turns cocktail hour into a line, and too many costs money for people who stand around. The right number depends on what you serve and how fast guests arrive. Planners in Chicago use the same set of numbers; they are rules of thumb, so confirm with your bar company or caterer, who knows your crowd.

## What drives the number

**Drink type.** A bartender can pour beer or wine faster than they can mix a cocktail. A shaken or built drink takes longer.

**Arrival pattern.** If 100 guests all arrive at once and head to the bar, the first 20 minutes is a rush. If guests trickle in over an hour, the load is lighter.

**Length of cocktail hour.** The longer it is, the more drinks per guest, and the more bartenders you need for the same line time.

**Number of stations.** Two bartenders at one bar get in each other's way. Two bars with one bartender each are often faster.

These are planning ranges, not rules from any licensing body. If your event is 6 hours with a fully open bar and heavy drinkers, use the top of the range.

## One bar or two?

A single long bar lets two or three bartenders work side by side. It is simple to stock, simple to staff and easier to monitor. The downside is one long line. Two bars halve the line but need duplicate bottles, ice and glassware. Place the second bar where guests naturally gather, such as the cocktail area or near the dance floor. See [bar setup for 100 guests](/blog/bar-setup-for-100-guests) for a layout and [bar placement in your floor plan](/blog/bar-placement-floor-plan) for how to position them.

## Do you need a barback?

A barback restocks ice, glasses and bottles so the bartender never leaves the bar. For 100 guests or fewer, a barback is optional. For 150 or more, one barback per 2 or 3 bartenders prevents a stalled line. Some bar companies include them.

## Servers and other staff

Bartenders are only part of the picture.

- **Glass collection.** One person to clear empty glasses from cocktail tables keeps the supply flowing.
- **Wine service at tables.** Servers pour at dinner; a bartender is not needed at the table.
- **A bar captain** on larger events, to manage inventory and cash-out.
- **Security and ID checks.** Some venues require this; ask.

## Illinois and venue rules

Servers and bartenders at licensed venues in Illinois are required to complete state-approved alcohol training called BASSET. The requirements and who must hold a license can differ by city and by whether you are hiring a bar service or serving yourself. Read [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events) and ask your venue or bar company for current requirements.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many bartenders for 100 guests?

Two to three for a full bar, one to two for beer and wine only. Plan two if guests will arrive over a long window and three if they arrive at once.

### How many bartenders for 150 guests?

Three to four for a full bar, two for beer and wine. Add a barback to keep the bars stocked.

### How many bartenders for 200 guests?

Four to five for a full bar, three for beer and wine. Two or three bar stations spread the line.

### Is one bar enough for 100 guests?

It can be if the bar is 8 feet long, you have two bartenders and guests arrive over a long period. Two bars are better when cocktail hour is short.

### Do I need a barback?

Not under 100 guests. At 150 guests or more, one barback for every 2 or 3 bartenders helps. They carry ice, bottles and glasses so the bartender never leaves the line.

### Do signature cocktails need more bartenders?

Yes, add one. Shaken or built drinks take longer than a pour. A short menu helps; see our signature cocktail station guide.

### Do bartenders need a license in Illinois?

Servers are generally required to complete state-approved BASSET training, but requirements vary. Confirm with the Illinois Liquor Control Commission, your city and your bar company.
