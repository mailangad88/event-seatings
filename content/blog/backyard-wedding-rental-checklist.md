**Quick answer**

A backyard wedding needs everything a venue normally provides: chairs, tables, linens, dinnerware, a tent or rain plan, lighting, power, restrooms and a catering prep area. Measure the yard first. Plan roughly 10 to 12 square feet per seated guest for dinner, add space for the bar, dance floor and caterer, and check your village for tent and event rules before you order.

</div>

A backyard wedding feels personal and costs less in venue fees, but you become the venue. In the Chicago suburbs that means thinking about lawn slope, neighbors, parking on a residential street and a June thunderstorm. This checklist starts with the yard, because the yard decides the rest.

## Step 1: measure and sketch the yard

Walk the space with a tape measure and mark the sprinkler heads, trees, slopes and where the power is. Then place the big pieces on paper.

## Step 2: choose the structure

If you can't count on weather, rent a tent. A tent also gives you a ceiling to hang lights from and a defined dining room. See [what size tent do I need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) and [frame vs pole tents](/blog/frame-tent-vs-pole-tent). Most Chicago-area municipalities require a permit for larger tents; ask your village building department, and see our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) for the city rules. Call JULIE (811) before anything is staked into the ground.

## Step 3: the checklist

**Seating**
- Ceremony chairs: one per guest. Lightweight garden folding chairs are easy on lawns; wood [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) look more finished.
- Reception chairs: one per guest, plus 2 to 5 spares.
- Chair ground protection if the lawn is soft. See chairs on grass.

**Tables and linens**
- Guest tables (60-inch rounds seat 8, 72-inch seat 10), plus cake, gift, guest book, bar and buffet tables.
- Floor-length cloths and napkins. Use the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

**Food and drink**
- Dinnerware, flatware, glassware, coffee service. Ask your caterer what they bring.
- Bar tables, ice tubs, trash and recycling bins.
- A prep tent or covered table for the caterer.

**Comfort and safety**
- Portable restrooms. The usual guideline varies by guest count and hours, so ask your restroom provider.
- Lighting along paths, steps and the restrooms.
- Fans, heaters or blankets depending on the month.
- Coat racks and a dry spot for bags.

## Power and lighting

Backyards usually have two or three outlets, and one circuit can't run a band, a caterer's warmer and string lights at once. Add up the watts, or ask a rental company about a generator. Keep cords off walkways and use covers where guests walk. Our guide to [backyard party lighting](/blog/backyard-party-lighting-ideas) covers layouts, and [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events) show spacing.

## The caterer's needs

A backyard caterer needs a place to stage food that is out of sight and out of the way: a covered table, running water if possible, and a clear route to the tent. Ask what equipment they bring and what they expect you to rent, such as prep tables, chafing dishes, ice bins and trash bins. A short call between your caterer and your rental planner before you finalize the order avoids doubled or missing items.

## Neighbors, parking and noise

Tell neighbors early. Check your village for rules on amplified music, event hours and street parking, and ask whether a temporary event permit applies. Assign someone to wave cars into spots, and plan a shuttle or valet for larger lists.

## Ground, grass and drainage

Lawns in the Chicago suburbs can stay soft well into June after a wet spring, and heavy tables and a dance floor will leave marks. Walk the yard after a rain and note where water pools. Ask your tent company about flooring and ground protection, and keep chair legs off the soft spots by placing them on walkway boards or a flat subfloor. If the yard slopes, a level surface for the dance floor matters more than the view, because tables and chairs rock on slopes and guests notice.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do I need for a backyard wedding?

A seated dinner needs roughly 10 to 12 square feet per guest, so 100 guests need around 1,000 to 1,200 square feet for tables alone. Add space for a bar, dance floor, caterer and restrooms. Measure the yard before you pick a guest count.

### Do I need a tent for a backyard wedding?

Not always, but you need a weather plan. A tent protects against rain and sun and gives you a place to hang lights. If you skip it, line up an indoor fallback you can set up within hours.

### Do I need a permit for a backyard wedding in the Chicago suburbs?

It depends on the village. Tents above a certain size, amplified music and large gatherings can all trigger rules. Call your village hall or building department and ask before you book.

### How many chairs do I need?

One for each guest at the ceremony and one at dinner, plus a few spares. If the ceremony is on the same lawn, chairs can be flipped to the tent. See our ceremony to reception flip guide.

### Can rental trucks reach my backyard?

Often not directly. Measure the gate width, and plan for crews to carry items through side yards. Tell your rental company about gates, stairs, hills and fences when you quote.

### What do backyard weddings forget most?

Restrooms, power, a caterer prep area and a rain plan. Add lighting for paths and steps, too, since guests will be walking on grass in the dark.
