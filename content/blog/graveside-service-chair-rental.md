If you're planning a graveside service, you're doing it on short notice and at a hard time. Seating is a small part of the day, but it's one families often ask about: who brings the chairs, are there enough, and will Grandma have somewhere to sit. Here's how it usually works, so you can ask the right questions once and move on.

## Who usually provides the chairs

For most graveside or committal services, the funeral home arranges the setup at the grave with the cemetery. That often includes:

- A small tent or canopy over the grave
- A row or two of chairs for the closest family
- A device to lower the casket, and green carpet or grass mats around the grave

What's included varies by funeral home and cemetery. Ask your funeral director three things: how many chairs come with the service, whether there's a tent, and whether they can add more chairs if you need them. Often the simplest answer is to have the funeral home add chairs, since they already work with the cemetery.

Families usually add chairs when:

1. **The family is large** and the included chairs only cover a spouse and children.
2. **Many guests are older** or have trouble standing on uneven ground.
3. **The service is longer** than a short committal, with military honors, several readings or music.
4. **A gathering follows nearby**, such as a reception at home or in a hall, and you'd like one order for both.

- **Close family in front**, facing the casket or urn, under the tent if there is one.
- **Extra seats at the ends of rows** for older guests, so they don't have to squeeze past anyone.
- **Other guests stand or sit behind** the family rows.
- **Keep chairs off neighboring graves** and markers. The cemetery staff will tell you where chairs can go.
- **Leave a firm path** from where cars park to the seats, especially after rain.

## What to ask the cemetery

Cemeteries have their own rules, and they differ a lot. Before you book outside rentals, ask the cemetery office or your funeral director:

- Can an outside rental company deliver chairs, and do they need to be approved first?
- When can chairs be set up and when must they be picked up?
- Where can a delivery vehicle park, and how far is the walk to the grave?
- Is there a rule about tents, stakes or anything placed on the grass?

If anything is unclear, let the funeral director handle it. They work with the cemetery staff and can usually sort it out with one phone call.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the funeral home provide chairs for a graveside service?
Often, yes. Many funeral homes and cemeteries set up a small tent and a row or two of chairs for the closest family. The number varies, so ask your funeral director how many chairs are included and whether they can add more.

### How many chairs do I need for a graveside service?
Plan seats for close family first, such as a spouse, children and parents, then add seats for older relatives or anyone who can't stand for long. Most other guests usually stand behind the family rows during a short committal service.

### Can I bring rental chairs to a cemetery?
Sometimes. Many cemeteries limit which outside vendors can set up and when. Ask the cemetery office or your funeral director before you book, and confirm where a delivery vehicle can park and where chairs may be placed.

### What chairs are best for a graveside service?
Light folding chairs are the most common choice because they're easy to carry across grass and set level on uneven ground. A wood folding chair looks a little warmer than plain white or metal.
