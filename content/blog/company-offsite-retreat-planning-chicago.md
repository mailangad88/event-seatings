**Quick answer**

Plan a company offsite by starting with one clear outcome, then choosing a location that suits it. Book six to ten weeks ahead, split the day into working sessions and breaks, and mix furniture setups so people can both focus and talk. Budget for food, space, materials and a facilitator if you want one. Send the agenda and logistics a week ahead so people arrive ready.

</div>

Most offsites fail the same way: a good room, good lunch and a vague reason for being there. People spend the day in presentations, leave with a to-do list nobody owns, and conclude that they could have done it over video. A retreat earns its cost when the purpose is specific, the agenda protects time for real conversation, and the space helps the group do the work.

These are planning habits, not formulas. A leadership team of eight and a 60-person department need very different spaces. Use the numbers to start a conversation with your venue.

## Decide what the offsite is for

Write the outcome as a sentence you could test: "By 4 p.m., we will have agreed on next year's three priorities," or "Our two merged teams will know each other's work and have a shared working agreement." If the best you can write is "build culture," keep going until it is concrete. The purpose decides who attends, how long it runs and whether you need a facilitator.

Then pick the format. A working offsite uses a table and whiteboard setup with long blocks of discussion. A bonding offsite leans on activities and meals. Many teams blend the two, with a morning of work and an afternoon of something shared. See our [team building event ideas for Chicago](/blog/team-building-event-ideas-chicago) for activities that suit different group sizes.

## Choose the right space

A retreat does not have to be a resort. Chicago-area teams use conference rooms, loft spaces, restaurants with private rooms, park district facilities, museum rooms and large homes. Judge the space on natural light, quiet, wall space for flip charts, restroom access, parking, and whether you can control food and break timing.

Think through furniture. A group that needs to write and discuss wants tables, not rows of chairs. Our [board retreat seating and setup guide](/blog/board-retreat-seating-and-setup) shows how to arrange a boardroom, U shape or cabaret style. If the space comes bare, rental tables, chairs and linens can turn it into a working room, and a soft seating corner can make breaks feel different from sessions.

## Build an agenda that breathes

Start later than a normal workday and finish earlier than people expect. Put the hardest topic after a coffee break, not after lunch. Keep sessions to about 90 minutes at most, with 15 minute breaks. Include at least one block with no assigned content, where people choose what to discuss.

Food shapes energy. A heavy lunch followed by a presentation is a recipe for yawns. Light lunch, plenty of water and coffee, and a mid-afternoon snack work better. Collect dietary needs when you send the invitation, and see our guide to [dietary restrictions planning](/blog/dietary-restrictions-planning-for-events). Finally, assign someone to capture decisions and owners during the day, so the output does not disappear.

## Setup options by group size

Furniture choices depend on how many people you have and what they will be doing. These are planning guidelines.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Group size | Best layout | What to rent or confirm |
|---|---|---|
| 6 to 12 | One boardroom-style table | Chairs, a few tables for notes and snacks |
| 13 to 25 | U shape or cabaret rounds | Tables, chairs, flip chart easels |
| 26 to 50 | Cabaret rounds with a front area | Rounds, linens, a lectern or screen |
| 51 to 100 | Rounds plus breakout rooms | Tables, chairs, a registration table, lounge seating |

</div>

Keep aisles wide enough for movement. Plan about 6 to 8 feet between tables if people will walk through often, and check ADA pathways.

## How to plan an offsite

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Define the outcome.</strong> Write one sentence about what changes because of the day.</li>
<li><strong>Set the budget and date.</strong> Check calendars and avoid quarter end.</li>
<li><strong>Pick a space.</strong> Tour it at the same hour you will use it.</li>
<li><strong>Draft the agenda.</strong> Mix focus time, breaks and a social moment.</li>
<li><strong>Order food and rentals.</strong> Collect dietary needs and count furniture by layout.</li>
<li><strong>Send logistics.</strong> Share address, parking, dress and agenda a week ahead.</li>
<li><strong>Capture decisions.</strong> Assign a note-taker and send the summary within two days.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-area notes

Teams in Chicago can choose between downtown rooms with transit access and suburban spots with free parking. If most people take the train, pick a location close to a station and say so in the invite. Winter offsites need a plan for coats and snow. Summer offsites open up park pavilions and outdoor spaces, but check with the park district about permits and reservations, and see the [Chicago Park District event permit guide](/blog/chicago-park-district-event-permit). For outdoor team days, our [team building outdoor event rentals](/blog/team-building-outdoor-event-rentals) guide covers tents, tables and shade. Weather in spring and fall swings quickly, so keep an indoor backup.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a company offsite?

Define one clear outcome, pick a location that fits, set a budget, draft an agenda with breaks and a social element, and arrange food and furniture. Send logistics a week ahead.

### How far in advance should I plan an offsite?

Six to ten weeks is typical for a one-day offsite. Bigger groups, travel or multi-day retreats need more time.

### What should be on an offsite agenda?

A mix of focused working blocks, breaks, a meal, and a short block for open conversation. End with decisions, owners and next steps.

### How long should a team offsite be?

Many offsites are one day. Two days works when you need travel or deep strategic work. Shorter focused half days can also be effective.

### Where can I host an offsite in Chicago?

Options include conference rooms, lofts, private rooms at restaurants, park district facilities, museum spaces and rented event halls. Choose based on light, quiet, parking and furniture.

### What furniture setup is best for an offsite?

For working sessions, tables with chairs in a U shape or cabaret rounds work well. Add a soft seating area for breaks if space allows.

### How do I make an offsite worthwhile?

Keep the goal specific, protect time for discussion, and capture decisions with owners and dates. Follow up within two days.
