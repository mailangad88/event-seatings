Lombard is known as the Lilac Village, and its spring festival is the biggest thing on the local calendar. Between the lilacs, a compact downtown with a Metra station and quiet residential blocks, it is a good place for a spring wedding, a graduation party or a summer picnic. Timing is the main puzzle.

## Lilacia Park and Lilac Time

Lilacia Park is a landmark garden managed by the Lombard Park District, and Lilac Time is the festival that celebrates it every spring. The village issues a proclamation each year, and the park fills with visitors during the festival. If you want the garden for photos or a ceremony, ask the district what is allowed and avoid festival weekends. Lilacs bloom in May, so that is when the park is most crowded and most beautiful.

## Space types in Lombard

- **Lilacia Park.** A garden destination, with rules shaped by public use. Ask the district what private events it allows.
- **Park shelters and fields.** For picnics, reunions and graduations. Reserve through the district.
- **Downtown Lombard.** Streets and sidewalks are village matters.
- **Banquet halls, restaurants and churches.** Indoor space for receptions.
- **Private yards.** Many Lombard lots are modest in size, so measure.

## Why the calendar matters more than the permit

In Lombard the biggest conflict is not paperwork; it is competing with Lilac Time. Traffic, parking and photography crowds peak around the festival. If your event is public or near downtown, check the village calendar first. If it is private, tell guests to expect traffic, and plan parking.

## Seasonal notes

May is the most popular month and the busiest. June brings long evenings, with sunset about 8:30 pm near the solstice. Fall is calm and good for outdoor events. In winter, the village's holiday events bring crowds to the downtown core, so check the calendar.

## A spring-event checklist for Lombard

Spring in Lombard is lovely and unpredictable. Build a checklist that handles all of it.

1. **Pick a date outside Lilac Time weekends** unless you want the crowds.
2. **Have a weather plan.** May temperatures swing widely and showers are common. A tent with sidewalls, or an indoor backup, protects the day. See [tent sidewalls: clear, window, solid](/blog/tent-sidewalls-clear-window-solid).
3. **Check parking.** Ask the village what is allowed on residential streets and tell guests.
4. **Order extra napkins and glasses.** Spring events see more mingling and more breakage. Our [how many glasses per guest](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest) guide gives counts.
5. **Plan the photos.** If the garden is part of the day, schedule portraits for early or late light, and keep guests moving between sites.

Lombard is close enough to Chicago that many couples marry here and hold the reception elsewhere. If you split a day across two venues, plan a transport window for chairs and décor.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is Lilac Time in Lombard?

In May, when the lilacs bloom. The village issues a proclamation each year and the festival is organized around Lilacia Park. Check the Lilac Time website for exact dates.

### Who manages Lilacia Park?

The Lombard Park District. Ask it about private events, photography and garden use.

### Should I avoid Lilac Time for my event?

If your event is near downtown or the park, yes. Traffic and parking tighten. A date a week before or after is easier.

### Do I need a permit for a street event in Lombard?

Yes, the Village of Lombard handles streets and public property. Ask the village for current forms and timing.

### Do you deliver in Lombard?

### What color linens suit a Lombard spring event?
