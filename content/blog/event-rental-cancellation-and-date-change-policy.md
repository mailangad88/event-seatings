**Quick answer**

Cancellation policies for event rentals usually get stricter as the event gets closer, with a window where changes are free, a window where a fee applies, and a final window where the deposit or payment is not returned. A date change is often easier than a cancellation if the company has inventory. Ask for the exact cutoff dates in writing before you pay.

</div>

Plans change. A venue closes, a guest list shrinks, a grandparent cannot travel. A good cancellation policy is not about expecting the worst; it is about knowing, before you pay, exactly what a change would cost. In Chicago, where popular dates book up fast, the policy also tells you how seriously the company holds your slot.

We are not going to quote cutoffs, because they vary. What you should ask is:

1. How long after booking can I cancel for a full refund?
2. When does a fee start, and how is it calculated?
3. When does it become non-refundable?
4. Is the deposit treated differently from the balance?

## Cancellation vs date change

|---|---|---|

| Best for | Event is not happening | Event is only delayed |
| Ask about | Refund vs credit | Is the deposit transferable? |

A date change is usually friendlier than a cancellation, but only if the items are free on the new date. Peak weekends in Chicago can be tight; see [peak season and booking early](/blog/peak-season-event-rentals-chicago). Ask whether the policy allows one free change, and whether the price on the new date stays the same.

## Reduced orders

Cutting the quantity is the most common change of all. A smaller headcount means fewer chairs, linens and place settings. Many contracts allow a reduction up to a deadline, then lock. Learn more in [final headcount deadlines](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals).

## Weather and force majeure

Weather or an emergency may be covered by separate language. Policies vary a lot, so read the clause and ask how a forced venue closure, or a severe weather alert, would be handled. We cover this in [rain clauses and weather language](/blog/rain-clause-event-rental-contract).

## Credits vs refunds

Some companies offer a credit toward a future event instead of a refund. A credit can be fine if you plan to rebook, but check:

- Does it expire?
- Can it be used on any date, or only certain ones?
- Is it transferable to someone else?
- Does it apply to the deposit only, or to a balance already paid?

## Cancel in writing

Always notify the company in writing, by email or whatever method the contract states. A phone call is not enough. Include your event date, your name, your order number and what you are asking for. Keep their reply.

## Protect yourself before it happens

- Choose a vendor whose policy you can read before you pay.
- Pay a smaller deposit first if the company allows it. See [deposits explained](/blog/rental-deposits-explained).
- Consider event insurance for large events; [event insurance basics](/blog/event-insurance-basics-for-private-events) explains what it does and does not cover.
- Keep records of every change.

## What a fair policy looks like

A fair policy gives you a real window to change, a reasonable fee that reflects the company's lost opportunity, and a final cutoff where the date is held for you. It is clear, in the contract and not just on a website, and the company answers questions about it without hedging. Our checklist of [red flags](/blog/event-rental-red-flags) covers policies that are too vague.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I cancel an event rental and get my deposit back?

Sometimes. It depends on how close you are to the event and the company's terms. Many policies refund early cancellations and become stricter later.

### What is the difference between cancelling and rescheduling?

Cancelling ends the order. Rescheduling moves it to a new date, which is usually easier on the budget if the company has inventory available.

### Can I reduce my order after I book?

Often, up to a deadline. After that, the quantities are locked. Ask for the exact cutoff and any limits on how much you can reduce.

### What if the venue cancels my event?

Check the contract's force majeure and venue-related terms. Many companies work with clients in this case, but the policy and your documentation matter.

### Do rental companies offer credits instead of refunds?

Some do. A credit can help if you plan to rebook, but confirm its expiration and the dates it can be used.

### How should I notify a rental company of a cancellation?

In writing, by the method stated in the contract. Include your event date, name and request, and keep their reply.
