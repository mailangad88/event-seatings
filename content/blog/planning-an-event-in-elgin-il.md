Elgin is a Fox River city with a big downtown, a historic park and a long event history. It is close to St. Charles, so deliveries are easy. It is also large, so the key is knowing who owns your space and how many guests it holds.

## City parks, not a park district

Elgin's parks are run by the City of Elgin Parks and Recreation Department, which also rents the Lords Park Pavilion. Listings advertise the pavilion as an event space that can hold up to about 250 people with Victorian-style details, so check current capacity and fees with the city. Lords Park is a historic park, with a zoo-era history and a lagoon, and Wing Park is another city park with a pool.

## Seasonal notes

Spring and summer weekends fill the pavilion, so book early. Fall is the most reliable. In winter, plan an indoor space and watch the weather for deliveries.

## Historic spaces need extra care

Historic buildings often have rules about floors, candles and fixtures. Ask what is allowed before you plan decor. Protect floors with felt pads on chair feet if you use wooden or stone floors. Check candle and fire rules, and read [candles at venues fire code rules](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules). If you are planning from Chicago, ask the city for a floor plan with exact dimensions.

## Planning a wedding in a historic space

Historic venues come with beauty and constraints. Use this order.

1. **Ask for the floor plan** with exact dimensions and the location of doors, stairs and columns.
2. **Confirm capacity** for seated dinner, not just standing receptions. Seated dinners need about 10 to 12 square feet per guest for tables and aisles, as a rule of thumb.
3. **Check load-in** routes. Historic buildings may have steps, narrow doors and limited elevators.
4. **Ask about décor limits.** Tape, nails, candles and open flames are often restricted.
5. **Plan a rain route** for guests moving between a park ceremony and the pavilion.

Elgin is close to St. Charles, so a site visit is easy for us once we are operating, and we are happy to talk through your plan on a call before then. The more you know about the room before you order, the fewer changes you make late.

## Parking and arrival

Large events in Elgin parks and historic spaces depend on parking. Ask the city where guests can park and whether a shuttle is needed. Put signs at the entrance and tell guests where to enter. Plan a drop-off point for older guests, and keep accessible routes clear of cables and rental deliveries.
