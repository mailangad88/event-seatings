**Quick answer**

Plan about 1 cup of coffee per guest for a dinner, 2 for a morning event, and 1 to 2 cups of hot tea for every 4 guests. Rent a 100-cup urn for 100 guests, or smaller urns spread across stations, plus cups, saucers, spoons, cream and sugar. Put coffee at the dessert course, not before, so it stays hot.

</div>

Coffee is the most forgotten line item on a rental order. At a wedding, funeral reception, corporate breakfast or fundraiser, it is also the item guests notice most when it's missing. Here's a plan you can use in an office, a banquet hall or a community hall in Chicago.

## How much to plan

The standard rule of thumb among caterers is about one cup per guest at a dinner and 1.5 to 2 cups per guest at a morning event. A "cup" in an urn is 5 to 6 ounces, smaller than a mug, so a 100-cup urn serves roughly 100 pours at that size.

Large urns need a dedicated circuit. A busy hotel ballroom often has them; a loft or gallery may not. Ask your venue about outlets and, if you use extension cords, rent a heavy-duty, cord-safe one. See the [Lighting and Power](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power) section for power planning.

## Tea service

Offer 4 to 6 teas: black, decaf, green, mint or herbal, and a chamomile. Keep hot water in an airpot or urn and tea bags in a basket. Include lemon slices, honey and milk. Plan on 1 tea drinker for every 3 to 4 coffee drinkers, then adjust for age and event type.

## Station layout

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Urn at one end</strong> near an outlet, with a drip tray.</li>
<li><strong>Cups and saucers</strong> next, then spoons.</li>
<li><strong>Cream, sugar and sweetener</strong> in the middle.</li>
<li><strong>Tea basket and hot water</strong> at the other end.</li>
<li><strong>Trash and used cup bin</strong> at the exit of the station.</li>
</ol>

Place the station out of the main traffic path, near the dessert table. A line forms during dessert, so allow room for 6 to 8 people to stand without blocking tables. A banquet table with a floor-length linen hides cords; see the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) to pick the right size.

## Late-night and winter

A late-night coffee station is a favorite after a long reception and doubles as a send-off. See [late-night coffee bar](/blog/late-night-coffee-bar). In a Chicago winter, hot drinks make a welcome entrance too; read [hot cocoa bar for winter](/blog/hot-cocoa-bar-winter-event).

## Self-serve or served?

Self-serve stations are cheaper and let guests choose when to drink. Served coffee, poured at the table by staff during dessert, is more formal and keeps lines short. For a plated dinner, many caterers offer both: tableside coffee for the head table and a self-serve station for everyone else. Ask your caterer if coffee service is included before renting urns.

## Cups: porcelain, paper or mugs

Porcelain cups and saucers look formal and keep drinks hot, but they require washing and hauling. Paper cups are cheaper, with a lid for outdoor or walk-around events. Mugs fit casual, rustic or office events. Make sure your cup count includes some for tea. For a coffee cart or a very large crowd, paper is practical. Match the choice to your plates and glassware from the [dinnerware](/blog/topic/dinnerware) section.

## Seating by the coffee

If coffee is part of a reception area, add a few seats nearby so older guests can sit while they drink. Light [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) are an easy choice for a lounge corner.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much coffee do I need per person?

About one 6-ounce cup per guest at a dinner and 1.5 to 2 cups per guest at a morning event. A 100-cup urn covers 100 guests at a dinner.

### How big a coffee urn do I need for 100 people?

A 100-cup urn for a dinner. For a morning event, plan two 100-cup urns or one urn that you refill.

### How many tea bags do I need?

Plan 1 tea drinker for every 3 to 4 coffee drinkers. For 100 guests, 25 to 35 tea bags in 4 to 6 flavors is a reasonable starting point.

### Do coffee urns need electricity?

Most do. Ask your venue about nearby outlets. If power is limited, use insulated airpots filled by your caterer.

### Should I serve coffee before or after dinner?

After. Coffee served with dessert stays hot and avoids waste. Have decaf available too.

### How many cups and saucers do I need?

One per guest plus 20 percent. Add more if the event includes a late-night coffee bar.
