**Quick answer**

For a board retreat of 8 to 20 people, use a hollow square or U-shape so everyone sees each other, with 24 to 30 square feet per person. For 20 to 40, mix a main session in cabaret or classroom style with breakout tables. Choose padded chairs, plan a working lunch at rounds, and leave a wall for flip charts and a screen.

</div>

A board retreat is a working day with high stakes: strategy, budgets, hard conversations. The room should not get in the way. Tight rows, stiff chairs and a screen that half the group can't see turn a good agenda into a slog. The right setup is mostly about choosing a layout that supports conversation, comfortable seating for a long day, and breakouts that do not require moving the whole room. This guide is for retreats in a Chicago-area conference center, hotel meeting room, lodge or office.

## Chairs for an eight-hour day

Retreat attendees sit for long stretches. Choose chairs with supportive backs, arms if possible and a firm seat. [Velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) are comfortable and look upscale. [Bentwood chairs](/chairs/bentwood) or [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) work for a lodge or barn-style venue. Avoid folding chairs for the main session. Add a few lounge chairs or a sofa for breaks; see [lounge furniture for cocktail hour](/blog/lounge-furniture-for-cocktail-hour). For the decision between chair styles, see [corporate gala seating beyond the banquet chair](/blog/corporate-gala-seating-beyond-banquet-chair).

## Walls, screens and flip charts

Decide where the screen and flip charts go before you place tables. A good retreat room has one long wall free for sticky notes, flip chart sheets or a whiteboard, and a screen visible to everyone without craning. Place the screen at the open end of the U, or at a corner of the hollow square. Leave 6 feet between the screen and the first seats. Bring an easel for each breakout group.

## Working lunch and breaks

Lunch at the same table as the session saves time, but it ruins the table. A better plan is to set a side area for food with rounds of 6 to 8 so people can talk in different groups. Use a buffet for speed and keep coffee, water and snacks in the back so people can refill without walking through the discussion. See [how many coffee urns for a party](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party) for beverage counts.

## Retreat at a Chicago-area venue

Offsites in forest preserves, lodges and conference centers outside the city can have limited furniture. Ask the venue what it supplies, especially chairs for a 12-hour day. Winter retreats need a coat area; summer retreats may use an outdoor terrace for breaks. If the venue has weak Wi-Fi, ask about a hotspot or a wired connection, and plan power strips on tables.

## Agenda-driven setup

Set the room for the hardest conversation of the day, not the easiest. If the morning is a presentation and the afternoon is a debate, start in cabaret and switch to a U-shape after lunch only if the venue can turn the room fast. Otherwise pick the layout that works for discussion and project the slides to the open end. Tables with a 30-inch depth give room for a laptop and a notebook without crowding; two-person-per-table spacing of 6 feet is generous. Place water and a bowl of snacks in the center of each table so people do not leave the discussion to refill. Supply pens, name tents and a printed agenda at each seat, and keep a spare chair or two at the back for late arrivals or an assistant.

## Confidentiality and comfort

Board materials often include sensitive information. Choose a room with a door that closes and a layout that keeps screens private. Do not leave materials on tables during breaks. Check that you can control temperature and lighting, since people get cold or sleepy after lunch.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best seating layout for a board retreat?

A hollow square or U-shape for 8 to 20 people. They let everyone see each other and still allow a screen or flip chart.

### How much space per person do I need?

About 24 to 30 square feet per person for a U or hollow square. Cabaret needs less, around 15 to 20 square feet.

### What chairs are best for an all-day meeting?

Padded or supportive chairs with backs and, if possible, arms. Avoid folding chairs for the main room.

### Do I need breakout rooms?

For groups over 12, yes. A few tables with easels in corners are enough if separate rooms are not available.

### Should lunch be in the meeting room?

Better in a separate area. It keeps the meeting table clean and gives a mental break.

### How do I keep the retreat private?

Use a room with a door, keep materials covered or removed during breaks and ask the venue about staff access.
