**Quick answer**

Choose folding tables when you need many tables, easy delivery and floor-length linens that hide them. Choose heavy wood or farm tables when the table itself is the decor, you have a low guest count and the venue can handle the weight. Folding tables are lighter and usually cost less to rent and deliver; wood tables look better bare but need more crew and floor protection. Ask for a quote with both.

</div>

Both table types do the same job. They hold dinner. But the choice shapes your look, your budget and your setup day. A banquet hall full of folding tables with floor-length cloths looks classic. A barn with wood farm tables looks warm and relaxed. Here is how to choose.

## When folding tables win

- **Large guest lists.** Folding tables fit in trucks and in storage.
- **Tight timelines.** Setup crews move them fast.
- **Tents and temporary venues.** Light tables are easy on soft ground.
- **Matching a color theme.** Linens let you change the look.
- **Limited budget.** Spend on chairs and linens instead.

## When wood tables win

- **Barns and rustic venues.** The wood matches the building.
- **Intimate weddings.** Under 50 guests, wood tables show off.
- **Photo-focused events.** A wood table with a runner photographs well.
- **Long-table dinners.** Farm tables are wide and seat more comfortably.

## The hidden differences

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Weight</strong> Wood tables can weigh well over 100 lbs. Ask the rental company.</li>
<li><strong>Access</strong> Stairs, elevators and narrow doors may decide the choice.</li>
<li><strong>Floors</strong> Wood legs scratch. Ask for protective feet or pads.</li>
<li><strong>Weather</strong> Wood tables outdoors need a sealed finish.</li>
<li><strong>Setup</strong> Wood tables need more people, so allow more time.</li>
</ol>

## Mixing the two

Use farm tables for the head table and folding rounds for guests. The wood table stands out, and you save on weight and cost. See [how to mix round and long tables](/blog/mix-round-and-long-tables).

## Chicago venue notes

Many Chicago and suburban venues provide folding tables and charge a fee for outside tables. Ask your venue about their table inventory before you rent. Some venues limit what can go through service elevators or across wood floors, and some require rental companies to carry insurance. Get those rules in writing.

## A decision tree you can follow in a minute

Answer these in order and stop at the first yes.

1. Is the guest count over 100? Choose folding tables with floor-length linens.
2. Is the venue a barn, farm or loft with a rustic look? Choose wood or farm tables.
3. Are stairs, elevators or narrow doors a problem? Choose folding tables.
4. Do you want the table to show? Choose wood.
5. Is the floor hardwood or historic? Ask about protective pads before choosing either.

If you land on a mix, use wood for the feature table and folding for the rest.

## What to ask the rental company

- Exact dimensions and weight of each table.
- Whether the legs fold and how the table stores.
- The finish of wood tables and whether it is sealed against spills.
- Delivery and setup crew size, and whether the quote includes carrying to the room.
- A damage policy, because stains and scratches on wood can be charged to you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are folding tables or wood tables better for weddings?

Folding tables work for most weddings because linens cover them. Wood tables suit barn and rustic weddings.

### Are wood farm tables more expensive?

Usually. They are heavier and more specialized, so request a quote with both.

### Do wood tables need tablecloths?

No. Many use a runner or bare wood.

### Can heavy tables go on a wood floor?

Yes with pads or protective feet. Ask the rental company.

### How many people carry a wood table?

Often 2 to 4. Ask for the weight and your venue's rules.

### Can I mix folding and wood tables?

Yes. Use wood for the head table and folding for the rest.
