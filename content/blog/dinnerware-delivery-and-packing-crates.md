**Quick answer**

Rental dinnerware usually arrives in stackable crates sorted by piece, such as plates in one, bowls in another and flatware in bins. Plan staging space near the kitchen, a clear path from the truck, and 10 minutes to count at delivery. A 100-guest plated dinner is typically many crates of plates, so tell your venue about load-in access, stairs, elevators and parking before delivery day.

</div>

The delivery truck is the first real moment of a rental order. Crates come off, someone signs a sheet, and a dozen things happen at once: where do the crates go, who counts, who is allowed on the loading dock? In Chicago, where venues are in high-rises, old buildings and on streets with limited curb space, a few details decide whether delivery takes 20 minutes or two hours. ## What the crates look like

|---|---|---|
| Plates | Stacked in crates with dividers | Heavy, lift with two people |
| Bowls and cups | Racks or divided crates | Keep upright |
| Glassware | Racks with dividers | Do not stack wet |
| Flatware | Bins or sleeves | Count at pickup |
| Linens | Bags or boxes | Keep dry |
| Platters | Padded or wrapped | Flat storage |

</div>

Details vary by company, so ask how the order will be packed. If a venue has a narrow service door, it is worth knowing crate dimensions in advance.

## Questions to answer before delivery

- Where is the loading entrance, and what are the hours?
- Is there a freight elevator that needs a reservation?
- How far is the walk from the truck to the staging room?
- Can the truck park, or is there a time limit on the curb?
- Who will receive and sign?
- Is there a room with a lock for staging?

Chicago high-rises and many historic buildings have strict load-in rules. Always ask the venue manager, and send the answer to your rental company. Our [venue rules guide](/blog/topic/chicago-planning) covers some of this for chairs.

## Count at delivery

Count crates first, then pieces of each type, using the delivery sheet. Look for chips and cracks and take photos. Note anything missing on the sheet before the driver leaves. This takes about 10 minutes for a 100-guest order. See [rental breakage and loss fees](/blog/rental-breakage-and-loss-fees-explained) for why it matters.

## Staging space

A 100-guest order needs floor space the size of a small room. Crates stack but do not stack safely above shoulder height. Keep them off wet floors and out of the kitchen path. At home events, a garage or dining room works. At a venue, use a back-of-house room. Check that the crates do not block fire exits.

## Delivery timing

Ask for a delivery window that leaves time for setup. For a 100-guest dinner, many planners aim for delivery the day before or the morning of, depending on the venue's rules. A crew can set tables faster with a plan; see [how to set tables fast with a crew](/blog/how-to-set-tables-fast-with-a-crew). Last-minute orders and short windows are tougher; ask early about the delivery window.

## Repacking for pickup

Scrape, stack and crate pieces the same way they arrived. Keep the dividers. Leave crates in the same place, with the delivery sheet on top. See [do you wash rental dishes](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-dishes). If something is broken, set it aside in a labeled box, so it can be counted.

## Winter and weather

Winter delivery in Chicago means icy docks and cold crates. Allow extra time for loading. Glassware that moves from a cold truck into a warm room can fog and sometimes crack, so give it a few minutes to adjust before use.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is rental china delivered?

Usually in stackable crates with dividers, sorted by piece type. Glassware comes in racks and flatware in bins. Ask your rental company how your order will be packed.

### Should I count items when they arrive?

Yes. Count crates and pieces with the driver and note anything missing or damaged before they leave. It protects you from disputed charges later.

### How much space do crates need?

A 100-guest plated order needs roughly the floor area of a small room. Stage it near the kitchen and away from walkways.

### Who unloads the truck?

It depends on the contract. Some companies deliver to the door; others bring crates to a set location. Confirm in your agreement.

### What if the venue has a freight elevator?

Reserve it in advance and tell the rental company the time window. High-rise venues often limit load-in hours.

### How do I repack for pickup?

Scrape, stack and place pieces back in the crates with dividers. Put broken pieces in a separate labeled box.
