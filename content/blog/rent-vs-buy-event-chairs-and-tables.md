**Quick answer**

Rent if you host a few events a year or need more than a handful of chairs, because buying means paying for storage, transport, cleaning and replacement on top of the price. Buy only if you host often, have dry space to store the items, and can move them yourself. To check, divide the purchase cost by the rental rate per event: that is how many events it takes to break even.

</div>

It is a fair question. A stack of folding chairs is not expensive, and a set of tables looks like a one-time purchase. But the sticker price leaves out the things that make renting attractive: someone else stores them, delivers them, replaces broken ones and brings the style you actually want. Here is a straight way to work it out for your own event in Chicago or the suburbs.

**Break-even events = purchase cost ÷ rental rate per event**, plus the yearly cost of owning.

For example, if you were weighing a garden folding chair, our estimate for renting is $5 to $7 per event. Take the price you were quoted to buy, divide by that rental rate, and you have the number of events it takes to break even before storage and upkeep. Because we cannot know what you would pay to buy, plug in your own number.

Add the hours: loading, hauling, setting up, breaking down and storing. If you pay a helper or take time off work, it counts. Chicago basements flood, garages freeze and summer attics are hot, and stored furniture suffers in all of them.

## When buying makes sense

- You host **more than a few large events per year**, such as a community group or a recurring family gathering.
- You have **dry, secure storage** with easy access.
- You have a **vehicle** and helpers.
- You are happy with **basic, durable styles**.
- You need chairs on **short notice** and cannot plan ahead.

For a church hall, a club or a business that hosts frequently, buying folding chairs and tables is often reasonable.

## When renting wins

- **Weddings, quinceañeras and big parties**, where you need 100 or more chairs once.
- **Style matters.** Looks like [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back), [ghost](/chairs/ghost) or [velvet](/chairs/velvet-dining) chairs are rarely worth buying for a single event.
- **No storage.** Chicago condos and apartments do not have room for 150 chairs.
- **Delivery and setup** are part of the value.
- **A one-time event.** Buying leaves you with items to sell or store.

## The hybrid approach

Many hosts do both: own a few basics, like a couple of tables and a dozen folding chairs, and rent the rest for big events. That keeps everyday needs covered while leaving the big numbers to the pros. Compare styles in [Chiavari alternatives](/blog/chiavari-chair-alternatives).

## Quality differences

A chair you buy for a backyard may not be the same grade as a commercial rental chair. Commercial chairs tend to be built for repeated stacking, transport and heavy use. Ask about weight rating and construction when you compare. ## What about tables?

Tables are bulkier than chairs, so storage and hauling matter more. A banquet table is heavy, and round tables are wide. The rental case for tables is stronger than for chairs for many hosts, and linens add another wrinkle because they must be cleaned and pressed. If you own a table, you still need linens to fit it; see [tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it cheaper to rent or buy chairs for an event?

For a single event or a few per year, renting is usually cheaper once you add storage, transport and cleaning. Buying wins when you host often and have space.

### How many events before buying chairs pays off?

Divide the purchase price by the rental rate per event to get a break-even count, then add the yearly cost of storing and maintaining them.

### How much does it cost to rent chairs?

Our estimates range from about $5 to $7 for a garden folding chair to $20 to $30 for a Louis chair, with prices depending on style, quantity and services. A quote gives you exact numbers.

### Should I buy folding chairs for a backyard party?

If you host regularly and have storage, maybe. For a one-time party with 50 or more guests, renting avoids the storage and hauling.

### Do rental chairs look better than ones I can buy?

Often the styles are different. Rental companies offer wedding-focused chairs, like ghost or cross-back, that would be expensive to buy for one event.

### Is it worth buying tables?

Rarely, unless you host frequently. Tables are bulky, heavy and hard to store, and you still need linens for them.
