**Quick answer**

Put the seating chart 10 to 15 feet inside the entrance, off the main path, with an alphabetical list. Mount table numbers 60 to 72 inches high so they read over seated guests, and mark reserved rows or tables with a sign on the chair or table. Use one chart per 100 guests so the line moves.

</div>

A good seating chart lets 150 guests find their seats in under 10 minutes. A bad one creates a traffic jam at the door, with guests trying to read small cards while the bartender waits. The fix is almost always the same: put the display in the right place, make it big enough to read from a few feet back, and mark the tables so that guests can walk straight to them.

## What to put where

| Item | Where | Height or size |
|---|---|---|
| Seating chart or escort board | Side wall 10 to 15 ft inside the entrance | Top of display about 60 to 72 in |
| Escort cards | Table or board next to the chart | Alphabetical, large type |
| Table numbers | Center of each table or on a stand | Top of sign about 60 to 72 in |
| Reserved ceremony rows | Chair ends of the first rows | Small sign or ribbon on the aisle chair |
| Head table or sweetheart sign | Front of the table | Visible from the room |
| Directional signs | Hallways and turns | Eye level, large type |

These heights are rules of thumb; test in the room by standing at the back of the nearest table and looking for the number.

## Types of seating display

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Escort cards.</strong> A card per guest with a table number, arranged alphabetically on a long table.</li>
<li><strong>Seating chart board.</strong> One large board with names under each table, often on an easel or a mirror.</li>
<li><strong>Assigned seats.</strong> Place cards at each setting. Good for 50 guests or fewer.</li>
<li><strong>Signage only.</strong> Table numbers and open seating. Easiest, but no control.</li>
</ol>

For a 150-guest reception, a chart board or escort table is the best mix of speed and style. For ideas on boards and easels, see [signage and easels for events](/blog/signage-and-easels-for-events), [mirror seating chart display guide](/blog/mirror-seating-chart-display-guide) and [reception table names and seating map](/blog/reception-table-names-and-seating-map).

## Ceremony and special seating

At a ceremony, reserve the first rows for family and mark them with small signs or ribbons on the aisle chairs. Keep the signs consistent on both sides, as discussed in [seating chart for divorced parents and blended families](/blog/seating-chart-divorced-parents-blended-families). At a corporate event, reserve front rows for speakers and sponsors and add a second sign for accessible seating; see [ADA accessible event seating and pathways](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways). Here are some tips for reserved seating:

- Use a different color for reserved seats than for general signs.
- Put reserved signs on chairs, not on the floor.
- Tell the ushers who sits where, and give them a list.
- Remove the signs after the ceremony so guests can sit anywhere.

For 200 or more guests, set up two or three alphabetical stations (A to H, I to P, Q to Z) so lines stay short. Place them in different spots of the entrance zone. Pair each with a staff member or friend. At night, light the chart; see [lighting for night event photos](/blog/lighting-for-night-event-photos).

## Chicago specifics

Winter coats matter: if the chart sits next to the coat rack, the entrance clogs. Move them apart; see [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago). At a large Chicago hotel, the pre-function foyer is a good place for the chart, which clears the ballroom doors until the room is ready. For tents, put the chart outside the door under a canopy or awning in case of rain.

## Chairs and tables with signs

Some chairs hold a sign better than others. A back with an open lattice, like the [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back), takes a hanging sign; a solid back such as a [shell chair](/chairs/shell) works with a clip. ## Frequently asked questions

### Where should the seating chart go?

About 10 to 15 feet inside the entrance, against a side wall, so guests can read it without blocking the door.

### How high should table numbers be?

The top of the sign should be around 60 to 72 inches off the floor so they can be seen over seated guests.

### Should I use escort cards or a seating chart?

Escort cards for 50 to 100 guests and a large board for 100 or more. For very large events, use both with alphabetical lists.

### How do I reserve seats at a ceremony?

Place signs or ribbons on the aisle chairs of the reserved rows and tell the ushers who sits where.

### How many seating charts do I need?

One per 100 guests. For 200 or more, set up two or three alphabetical stations.

### Do I need place cards at each seat?

No. Assigned tables are enough for most weddings. Use place cards for formal dinners or when you need specific seats.
