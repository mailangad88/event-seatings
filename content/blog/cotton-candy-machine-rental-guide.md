**Quick answer**

A commercial cotton candy machine spins one cone every 30 to 60 seconds, about 60 to 120 cones an hour with a trained operator. Plan one cone for each guest, then add 25 percent for seconds. A pound of hard sugar floss mix typically makes about 20 to 25 cones, so 100 guests need around 5 to 6 pounds, 125 cones and a dedicated outlet.

</div>

Cotton candy is pure theater. Guests watch it spin and kids line up for it. It is also one of the more fussy machines to rent, because humidity, power and an untrained operator can turn a fun idea into a sticky mess. Here is how to size it, supply it and place it at a Chicago-area party.

Output depends on the machine, the operator and the weather. A small party machine may do 30 to 40 cones an hour, while a commercial model with a skilled operator can do far more. Ask your rental source for the actual rate on the machine you will get.

## Humidity is the enemy

Cotton candy absorbs moisture from the air and collapses. A damp Chicago summer day, with humidity often well above 60 percent, makes floss wilt in minutes and clogs the spinner head. You will see this on lakefront evenings and after storms. To protect it:

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Spin under a tent</strong> or inside an air-conditioned room.</li>
<li><strong>Spin to order,</strong> not ahead.</li>
<li><strong>Bag finished cones</strong> in clear sealed bags if you must make ahead.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the sugar sealed</strong> and use a fresh bag in humid weather.</li>
<li><strong>Wipe the bowl</strong> between flavors to avoid clogs.</li>
</ol>

Cool, dry Chicago fall days are the best weather for it. In winter, indoor events with the heat on work well.

## Placement and layout

Put the machine on a stable table at least 3 feet from walls and tent sides, because hot sugar can stick to fabric. Keep it out of the main buffet line, and near the dessert area or entrance. Place a drop cloth or a mat under it for floor protection, since floss and sugar spatter. A 6-foot table fits the machine, a cone holder and a sign. For food stations that work with it, see [food station ideas](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment), [popcorn machine rental](/blog/popcorn-machine-rental-guide) and [snow cone machine rental](/blog/snow-cone-machine-rental-guide).

## Power and safety

A cotton candy machine uses a motor and a heating element. Many draw 8 to 12 amps, so give it its own circuit and a cord rated for the load. Ask your venue where the outlets are and which breaker feeds them. See our [lighting and power guides](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power). Never use it in the rain or on wet ground. The spinner head is hot, so keep children back from the bowl. Wear food-safe gloves if you serve for a crowd.

## Allergy and labeling

Many floss sugars are only sugar and dye, but some flavors contain additives. Put a sign with the flavor name, and tell guests with dye sensitivities. Do not put the machine near a nut-based snack.

## Chicago event ideas

Cotton candy works at school fairs, church festivals, graduation parties, backyard birthdays and corporate picnics. For weddings, it is popular as a late-night or "candy bar" item. Pair it with fall festivals at a suburb farm or orchard where the cooler weather helps. For a school, park district or church event, ask the property whether outside vendors or machines are allowed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much cotton candy do I need per person?

One cone per guest, plus about 25 percent for seconds. A pound of floss sugar makes roughly 20 to 25 cones, depending on the cone size.

### How long does it take to make a cone of cotton candy?

About 30 to 60 seconds for a trained operator. A small machine may be slower, so ask about output.

### Can I use a cotton candy machine outside?

Yes in shade and low humidity, with a covered outlet. Wind and humidity both ruin the floss, so a tent with sidewalls helps.

### Do I need an operator?

Yes. Someone has to stay with it, spin to order and keep the area clean. Ask your rental company if they provide training or staff.

### Why is my cotton candy flat and sticky?

Humidity or a damp bag of sugar. Use fresh sugar, spin in a cooler room and bag finished cones quickly.
