Power problems rarely show up in the planning meeting. They show up at 7:40 p.m., when the caterer's warmer, the DJ's speakers and the heaters all share a single breaker. A simple checklist, completed a few weeks before, prevents it. This guide gives you the questions, the math and a worksheet.

## The basic math

Electrical load is measured in watts. Circuits are rated in amps.

**Watts = volts × amps.** In the United States, outlets provide 120 volts. A 15-amp circuit provides 1,800 watts at most, and a 20-amp circuit provides 2,400 watts. For loads that run for hours, plan to stay under about 80 percent of the rating, which is 1,440 watts on a 15-amp circuit and 1,920 watts on a 20-amp circuit.

Several outlets in a room may share one breaker. Venues usually know which ones, so ask.

## Ask the venue

1. How many outlets are available, and what circuits are they on?
2. What are the amp ratings and are any GFCI protected?
3. Is there a house electrician? Do outside vendors need to use them?
4. Is a generator allowed? Where can it be placed? See [quiet generator placement](/blog/quiet-generator-placement-events).
5. Are there hours when power is off?
6. Who can I call if a breaker trips?

Get the answers in writing and send them to every vendor.

## Common surprises

- **Everything on one breaker.** Several outlets in a room often share one circuit.
- **Caterer needs more than they said.** Ask again two weeks before.
- **Outlets are far away.** Long cords reduce power; use heavier gauge.
- **The venue turns off power at a set hour.** Ask.
- **Phone charging.** A charging station is a small load, but a long cord run.
- **Overnight loads.** Refrigeration should not be on the same circuit as heaters.

## Chicago notes

Many older Chicago buildings have limited circuits per room, and downtown venues may charge for extra power or require union electricians. Suburban banquet halls vary widely. For a park or forest preserve, ask the district whether outlets are available at your pavilion at all.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much power do I need for a wedding?

It depends on the caterer, DJ, lights and heating. Add watts from each vendor and compare them to the venue's circuits.

### How many watts are in a standard outlet?

A 15-amp, 120-volt circuit provides up to 1,800 watts, and a 20-amp one up to 2,400 watts. Plan for 80 percent of that on steady loads.

### Can I plug the DJ into the same outlet as the caterer?

Avoid it. Heavy caterer loads can trip the breaker and cause hum in audio. Use separate circuits.

### Do I need a generator?

If the venue cannot supply enough outlets or amps, yes. Tents, parks and backyards usually need one.

### What if the breaker trips?

Know where the panel is and who can reset it. Reduce the load before resetting.

### Who should I ask about power?

Start with the venue, then every vendor who brings equipment. Ask for wattage in writing.
