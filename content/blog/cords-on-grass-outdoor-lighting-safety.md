**Quick answer**

Keep cords off walking paths, route them along edges, cover any crossing with a cord ramp, and lift every plug connection off the ground in a weatherproof cover. Use outdoor-rated cords, plug into GFCI protection, and shut off power if the lawn floods. Before driving stakes or poles into a lawn, call 811 so buried utilities are marked.

</div>

A lawn looks like a flat, forgiving surface. It is not. Grass hides cords from guests, holds water against connections and swallows tent stakes that may hit a gas line. Most lawn incidents are small: a guest in heels catches a cord, a connection soaks, a breaker trips mid-dinner. The fixes cost very little if planned ahead.

Walk the lawn with a list of every power need and every guest path: the walkway from parking, the route to restrooms, the path to the bar. Then draw the cord route so it avoids all of them or crosses with a cover. A longer route that stays at the edge is better than a short one through the middle.

## Cord covers and ramps

- **Cord ramps (cable protectors)** are rubber or hard plastic channels that cover cords and make a gentle slope for feet and wheels. Use them at every crossing.
- **Mats and turf protection** help keep covers from sinking in soft ground.
- **Tape is a last resort.** Gaffer tape holds on hard floors but not on wet grass. If you tape, put it on a hard surface.
- **Do not bury cords** in the lawn or under mulch. Buried wiring needs different, rated cable.

For wheelchairs, strollers and walkers, a cover is mandatory. A cord that looks flat to you can stop a wheel. Also see the guidance for [accessible seating](/faq).

## Water and the ground

Wet grass is a conductor and a puddle maker.

- Lift connections 4 to 6 inches off the ground, onto a stake, bucket or plastic box.
- Cover each connection with a weatherproof box.
- Keep power strips off the lawn and inside waterproof enclosures.
- Turn off sprinklers or irrigation for the event. Ask your landscaper about the schedule.
- Use GFCI-protected power; see [extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events).
- If the lawn floods or rain is heavy, shut off power at the source before touching connections.

## Call before you stake

In the United States, 811 is the national call-before-you-dig number. Utilities mark buried lines for free in many places, but you must call a few days ahead, and rules about lead times vary by state. In Illinois, the service is run by JULIE; check their website or call 811 for the current requirement. Tent stakes, light poles and fence anchors all count. Tent companies typically handle this for their own stakes, so ask who is responsible.

## Poles, lights and wind

String lights need poles when trees are not available. On lawns:

- Use poles with heavy bases or sandbags, not just stakes.
- Place poles at least 3 feet inside the edge of walkways.
- Add guy lines only if marked with flagging so guests do not trip.
- Take care with wind. A 10-foot pole with a long string can tip in a gust. See [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events).
- Do not hang lights from tent poles unless the tent company agrees.

## Lighting the lawn

Low path lights prevent falls, but they must be visible, stable and away from the mower line. Keep lanterns on flat bases and not on the grass edge. [Lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events) are good for paths. For a no-cord option, [battery lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting) keeps the lawn clear.

## Cleanup

Pick up every cord, stake and zip tie before leaving. Mowers and children find them later. Check the lawn with a flashlight after dark.

## Chicago notes

City lots and older neighborhoods often have shared fences and shallow utilities, and public parks have their own rules. Ask your park district about electrical use, stakes and generators. In suburbs, check with the village on tent permits, which often involve stakes and electrical inspections.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I run an extension cord across a lawn safely?

Route it along the edge, cover any crossing with a cord ramp, and keep connections raised in weatherproof covers. Use outdoor-rated cord.

### Should I tape cords to grass?

No. Tape does not hold on wet grass. Use ramps and route around paths.

### Do I need to call 811 for tent stakes?

Yes, any digging or staking should be preceded by a call to 811. Ask your tent company who is handling it.

### Can I leave cords out in the rain?

Outdoor cords can handle rain, but connections should be covered and off the ground. Shut off power in heavy rain or flooding.

### How do I protect wheelchair users from cords?

Use cord covers at every crossing, and keep a clear accessible route that is free of cords.

### Are solar lights safe for lawns?

Yes, solar lights have no cords, but they are dim. Use them for paths, not for dining.
