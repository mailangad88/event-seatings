**Quick answer**

Move rentals indoors or into a garage if you can, since linens and cushions should not sit outside. If items must stay outside, stack chairs, tie or weight them down, cover them with a tarp that lets air move, and keep linens and glassware bagged and dry. Tell the company where items will be left. You are usually responsible for them from delivery to pickup.

</div>

A Friday delivery for a Saturday party, or a Sunday night when pickup is Monday morning, means rentals spend a night with you. In Chicago that can mean damp air off the lake, a surprise storm, a neighbor's cat or raccoons that rummage anywhere food was served. A little prep keeps items clean and the count right.

## Dew, rain and wind

- **Dew and damp.** Even a clear night leaves moisture on metal and fabric. Chairs dry in the sun. Linens can mildew or spot if left folded and wet.
- **Rain.** Cover chairs and tables with a tarp or plastic, but let the sides breathe. A tight wrap traps water. Keep linens in their bags and out of puddles.
- **Wind.** Lake-effect gusts can tip a stack of light chairs. Stack them low, lean them against a wall or fence, and strap or weight the stack.
- **Ground.** Put items on pavers, a patio or a pallet, not directly on soggy grass. See [tent and grass damage](/blog/tent-and-grass-damage) for the same ground issues.

## Theft, animals and neighbors

Chairs and tables are bulky and not an easy grab. Glassware, speakers, heaters and tablets are. Keep small items inside, and put anything with value out of sight. Raccoons and other animals go after leftover food and smelly linens, so do not leave napkins or tablecloths with food on them outside. Tell neighbors that a pickup is coming, so a truck at 7:00 a.m. is not a surprise. For shared spaces see [HOA and neighbors](/blog/hoa-and-neighbors-tent-party).

## What most hosts find out too late

**You may be the one responsible.** Many contracts treat the items as yours once they are signed in, which includes weather and theft. Read the terms, and see [damage waivers](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained) for what a waiver usually excludes.

**Wet linens can be a bigger bill than missing ones.** A stain or mildew can mean a cleaning or replacement line. Put dry linens in bags and keep the bags off the ground.

**Tell the company the real spot.** If the crew is to drop at a gate, a garage or a back stoop, write it on the order. If a delivery is left unattended, ask for a photo of where they placed it. The [delivery day guide](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect) covers the handoff.

## A simple overnight routine

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Pick the spot.</strong> Garage, porch or a corner of the yard near a wall.</li>
<li><strong>Stack low and even.</strong> Same size stacks, easy to count.</li>
<li><strong>Cover loosely.</strong> A tarp over the top, held down with weights or bungee cords.</li>
<li><strong>Bag and carry in the soft items.</strong> Linens, napkins, cushions, glass.</li>
<li><strong>Photograph the setup.</strong> A wide shot and a close shot at dusk.</li>
<li><strong>Check in the morning.</strong> Dry anything damp and recount before pickup.</li>
</ol>

December sunset arrives around 4:20 to 4:30 p.m., so winter set-ups happen in the dark. Do the routine earlier in the day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I leave rental chairs outside overnight?

Usually yes, if they are stacked and covered, but a garage is better. Chairs handle outdoor life better than linens. Check your contract for who is responsible.

### What happens if it rains on my rentals?

Chairs and tables dry out and are fine. Linens, cushions and cardboard-packed items can stain or mildew, so keep them inside or bagged. Tell the company if anything was soaked.

### Who is responsible if rentals are stolen overnight?

You are, in most contracts, from delivery to pickup. Ask the company how theft is treated and whether a waiver covers it. It often does not.

### Will raccoons get into my rentals?

They may go after food scraps, so keep food-soiled linens and dishes inside. Chairs and tables are not a draw.

### How should I cover rental chairs?

Use a tarp or plastic sheet over the stack, tied or weighted, with the sides open for air. Do not wrap tightly, since that traps moisture.

### Can the company drop items the night before and leave them?

Often, yes, with a note of where to place them. Ask for a photo and make sure the spot is safe and dry. See [weekend pricing](/blog/weekend-event-rental-pricing-friday-to-monday) for how timing affects cost.
