**Quick answer**

An employee awards night works best with round tables of 8 or 10 facing a stage, with every seat angled toward the podium. Plan a stage of at least 12 x 16 feet for 150 guests, a 5-foot path to the stage from the winners' tables, and 10 to 12 square feet per person. Seat award winners near the front but not all together, and assign tables by team so people sit with friends.

</div>

An awards night asks the room to do two things at once: feed a few hundred people and give 10 or 20 of them a moment on stage. When the layout fails, the winners walk through a crowd, the back tables can't see, and the program drags because the servers are blocked by the aisle. The fix is mostly geometry. This guide covers the seating plan, stage, and rental counts for a Chicago-area ballroom or banquet hall.

Rounds work best when no one sits with their back to the stage. Leave the stage-side seats empty (a crescent) or turn chairs slightly. Put tables close enough that the back row can read the screen, which means the screen should be big or repeated on side monitors.

## Stage and podium

| Guests | Stage size | Podium | Screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 75 | 8 x 12 ft | 1 | One 8 ft screen or projection |
| 150 | 12 x 16 ft | 1 + side table | One or two 10 ft screens |
| 300 | 16 x 24 ft | 1 + two side tables | Two screens |

Stage height is 18 to 24 inches for rooms up to 150; taller for larger rooms. Add stairs on both sides if more than one person walks on at a time, and a ramp or lift for accessibility. Ask your venue what is built in. Many hotel ballrooms have a stage or riser you can book.

## Seat people so the night flows

- **Assign tables by team.** People relax when they sit with friends.
- **Seat winners across tables.** Spread them so cameras can find them without clustering, but put them in the front half.
- **Seat executives where staff can reach them.** Not at a head table facing everyone, unless the culture is formal.
- **Reserve access seats.** Put wheelchair users and guests who need accessible routes near aisles.

## Chairs that last through a program

Awards dinners run two to three hours. A chair with a slim seat gets uncomfortable. [Velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) and [bentwood chairs](/chairs/bentwood) look good in photos and sit well. [Cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) fit a rustic or warm company culture. For large rooms, a standard banquet chair is fine, and an upgraded style at the winners' tables adds polish. See [corporate gala seating beyond the banquet chair](/blog/corporate-gala-seating-beyond-banquet-chair).

Servers should clear tables before the first award. Dim the lights slightly for the stage, but not so much that the room can't see their plates. If you serve alcohol, close the bar for the formal program or shift it to a side room so the line doesn't distract.

## Rental list for an awards night

| Item | Count (150 guests) |
|---|---|
| Round tables, 60 or 72 in | 15 |
| Chairs | 150 + 10 spares |
| Floor-length cloths and napkins | 15 + 3 spares |
| Stage, podium, steps | 1 set, size above |
| Utility tables (registration, awards, bar) | 4 to 6 |
| Centerpieces or table numbers | 15 |

Awards need a table near the stage for trophies or certificates, covered in a black or neutral cloth, with a staff member managing the order. Put a small table with water and tissues behind the stage.

## Accessibility and the walk to the stage

Winners need a step-free route to the stage. A ramp or a lift costs more than steps, so decide early. Place accessible seating near the aisle on the stage side, not in a back corner. Keep chairs at the aisle pulled in tightly so wheelchairs and trays can pass. If you have a visually impaired or hard-of-hearing employee, ask what they need: a front table, a sign interpreter placement or captions on the screen.

## Chicago notes

Many companies hold awards nights in January and February, after year-end. Winter weather affects arrival times, so build 20 to 30 minutes of slack into the schedule and keep the coat check open from the start. Downtown venues will have load-in restrictions; ask the venue manager about dock times and elevator limits. ## Frequently asked questions

### How should I seat award winners?

Spread them across tables in the front half, not all at one table. This way photographers can reach them and the stage path stays short.

### How big should the stage be for an awards night?

About 12 x 16 feet for 150 people. Smaller rooms can use 8 x 12, and larger rooms 16 x 24.

### How many people per table?

8 or 10. Tables of 10 save space; tables of 8 are more comfortable and easier to serve.

### Should dinner come before or after the awards?

Before is best. Plates and servers are distracting during speeches, and people pay more attention when fed.

### How long should an awards program run?

Keep it under 90 minutes to two hours. Past that, attention fades and people leave early.

### What if some employees can't attend in person?

Plan a stream or a recorded segment. See [hybrid event AV and seating](/blog/hybrid-event-av-and-seating) for layout and AV tips.
