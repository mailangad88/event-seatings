**Quick answer**

Most corporate galas are seated at 60-inch or 72-inch rounds, with 8 to 10 guests per table. The chair is the biggest visual upgrade you can make after linens: ghost chairs read modern, velvet reads black-tie, and gold or wood styles sit in between. Pick one style for the room, keep the aisles at least 5 feet wide, and confirm the venue allows outside rentals before you order.

</div>

A gala in a Chicago hotel ballroom or a suburban banquet hall usually arrives with the venue's standard banquet chair. It is comfortable enough and it is forgettable. Your sponsors, honorees and board members will spend three hours at those tables, and the chair is what they sit on, look at and photograph. This guide covers when to upgrade, which styles suit which kind of gala, and how to lay the room out so the upgrade actually shows.

## When the standard chair is enough, and when it isn't

A padded banquet chair is a fine choice for a working lunch or a short awards program. It stops being fine when your event sells tickets at a premium, hosts press or photographers, or needs the room to feel different from last year's. If guests pay for a seat, the seat is part of what they paid for.

## Match the chair to the type of gala

- **Black-tie or donor dinner.** [Velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) in a deep tone, or [gold infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity) with ivory or black linens. Keep everything else restrained.
- **Tech, design or startup gala.** [Ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) keep sightlines open and put the focus on lighting and centerpieces.
- **Warm, community-minded fundraiser.** [Cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) or [bentwood chairs](/chairs/bentwood) feel welcoming and pair with natural linen.
- **Large, budget-aware event.** Standard banquet chairs at the guest tables, with an upgraded style only at the head table or VIP tables. Mixed seating works when you keep the colors related. Our guide to [Chiavari chair alternatives](/blog/chiavari-chair-alternatives) lists more options.

## Lay out the rounds

A 60-inch round seats 8 comfortably and a 72-inch round seats 10. Rule of thumb for banquet seating is about 10 to 12 square feet per guest, which includes the table, the chairs and the walkways. Leave 5 feet between table edges so servers can pass behind seated guests.

Face every chair toward the stage when you can. At rounds, leave one or two seats at the back of each table empty (a "crescent" arrangement) so nobody sits with their back to the program. That drops capacity, so build it into your count early.

Count guests, then speakers, honorees, sponsors' staff and anyone checking in at the door. Chairs for the program stage, registration and silent auction tables are easy to miss. Our [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) guide walks through every zone.

## Chicago details that change the plan

- **Loading docks and elevators.** Downtown hotels and high-rise ballrooms often schedule deliveries in narrow windows. Ask the venue for dock hours, freight elevator rules and whether outside rentals are allowed at all.
- **Cold-weather arrivals.** Winter galas need a coat check and wet-floor planning. A few rental racks at the entrance beat a pile of coats on spare chairs.
- **House chairs.** Some venues include chairs in the room fee. If you replace them, ask who removes and stores the originals, and whether that costs extra. Ask for the venue's written rental policy early.

## Frequently asked questions

### What chairs are best for a corporate gala?

It depends on the tone. Velvet or gold chairs read black-tie, ghost chairs read modern, and wood styles read warm. Choose one style for the room and test it against your linen color before ordering.

### How many guests fit at a round table for a gala?

A 60-inch round seats 8 comfortably and a 72-inch round seats 10. Crowding more guests onto a table makes service slow and the table look cluttered.

### How much space do I need per guest at a banquet?

Plan about 10 to 12 square feet per guest as a rule of thumb. Add space for a stage, dance floor, bar and buffet, which are separate from guest seating.

### Can I mix chair styles at one gala?

Yes, if the colors are related. Use an upgraded chair at the head table or sponsor tables and a simpler chair elsewhere. Keep the chair heights similar so the room looks even.

### Should I replace the venue's chairs?

Only if the venue allows it and the upgrade is visible. Ask who stores the house chairs, what the removal fee is and whether delivery is limited to certain hours.

### How far ahead should I book gala chairs?

Book as soon as the date and venue are confirmed, especially for fall and December events when Chicago ballrooms fill up. We recommend ordering well before the venue's final headcount is due.
