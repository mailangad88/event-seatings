**Quick answer**

A raw space usually has walls, a floor and a roof, and almost nothing else. Ask about occupancy limit, restrooms, power, heat and cooling, loading, lighting, noise limits and insurance. Then rent everything that makes it an event: tables, chairs, linens, dinnerware, glassware, lighting, restrooms, heating or cooling, dance floor and décor.

</div>

Warehouses, lofts and raw spaces are popular in Chicago for a reason: they are open, flexible and photogenic. They are also the venue type where the rental list is longest, because the room supplies almost nothing. Planning a raw space event is closer to building a restaurant for a night than to booking a ballroom.

## Build the room from zero

Start with a floor plan. A raw space gives you lots of square footage with no built-in focal point, so you create zones: arrival and coats, bar, dining, dance and lounge. Keep aisles wide, and put the loudest zone far from quiet spaces. Cover concrete with rugs or a dance floor, and soften hard walls with drape. See [ceiling draping](/blog/ceiling-draping-for-weddings-and-banquet-halls), [pipe and drape](/blog/pipe-and-drape-uses-and-sizes) and [banquet hall room transformation ideas](/blog/banquet-hall-room-transformation-ideas).

## Restrooms and comfort

If the space lacks enough restrooms, rent them. See [how many portable restrooms per guests](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests) and [restroom trailers](/blog/restroom-trailer-when-you-need-one). For comfort, ask about heat in winter and cooling in summer; portable options exist, but they require power and clearance. Read [cooling a tent in summer](/blog/cooling-a-tent-in-summer) for ideas that apply to any large space.

## Load-in and noise

Raw spaces often sit in industrial areas with truck access, which helps. But parking can be hard, and neighbors may be sensitive to late-night noise. Ask the venue about curfew, sound limits and the loading dock. Share the dock height and door width with your rental company.

## Lighting and atmosphere

Raw spaces are dark in the wrong places. Overhead fixtures may be industrial, bright or missing, and daylight fades through high windows. Layer light from the ground up: string lights over the dining area, uplights on walls, table lamps or lanterns on tables and pin spots on key focal points. Warm color temperatures soften concrete and steel. See our [guide to lighting color temperature](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events) and [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events).

Plan the power distribution too. Run cords along walls, cover them where people walk and avoid daisy-chaining heavy loads. A caterer with warming equipment, a DJ and a coffee station can overload a single circuit, so ask the venue which panels feed which outlets.

## Temperature

Warehouses can be freezing in winter and hot in summer. Ask whether heat or cooling exists, how it is controlled and whether it is included in the rental. Portable heating and cooling need space, power and sometimes fuel, and some venues restrict them. Test the temperature on a similar day before you book if you can.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent for a warehouse event?

Everything a finished venue would give you: tables, chairs, linens, glassware, lighting, restrooms, heat or cooling, a dance floor and decor.

### How do I find the occupancy limit of a warehouse venue?

Ask the venue for it in writing and check with the city or village if you are unsure.

### Do warehouse venues have enough restrooms?

Many do not. Ask for the count and rent more if needed.

### Is a warehouse venue cheaper?

The room may cost less, but rentals, power, restrooms and decor can make the total comparable. Ask for a full quote.

### Can I use candles in a warehouse?

Often restricted. Ask the venue and the fire code authority.

### How do I make a warehouse feel warm?

Linens, rugs, drapes, warm light and soft seating.

### How should I handle power?

Get the available amps and outlet locations from the venue, and add up your load.
