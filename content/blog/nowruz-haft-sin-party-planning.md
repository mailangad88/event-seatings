**Quick answer**

Nowruz is the Persian New Year and begins at the spring equinox, usually March 20 or 21. The centerpiece is the haft-sin table, set with seven items that start with the letter S in Persian. Families gather at the moment of the equinox, share a meal that often includes sabzi polo ba mahi (herb rice with fish), and visit relatives for about two weeks. The 13th day, Sizdah Bedar, is spent outdoors with a picnic.

</div>

Nowruz means "new day." It is a spring holiday celebrated by Persian families and by many people from Afghanistan, Central Asia, the Caucasus, Kurdish communities and other regions. In Chicago, families gather in homes, community halls and banquet rooms, often in neighborhoods like Albany Park and across the suburbs. Traditions vary from family to family, so use this guide as a starting point and follow your own.

## The date and the moment

Nowruz begins at the exact moment of the spring equinox, which falls on March 20 or 21. The minute changes each year, so check this year's time on a Persian calendar. Many families gather before that time, sit at the haft-sin table and wait for the moment, called the tahvil. Then they exchange greetings, gifts and money (often as new bills) and eat.

The seven S items usually are:

- **Sabzeh:** wheat or lentil sprouts, grown in a dish for about two weeks before Nowruz, for rebirth.
- **Samanu:** a sweet pudding made from wheat germ.
- **Senjed:** dried oleaster fruit.
- **Sir:** garlic.
- **Sib:** apple.
- **Somaq:** sumac berries.
- **Serkeh:** vinegar.

Other items often sit beside them, such as a mirror, candles, painted eggs, hyacinth, coins (sekkeh) and a book such as the Quran or poetry by Hafez or the Shahnameh. Families differ on what they include, so follow your family's way.

## Where the table goes

The haft-sin table is a display, not a place to eat. Put it where guests will see it as they enter, such as a living room wall or a console table. Cover it in a cloth and leave room around it for photos. A rented banquet table works well if you do not have one. See our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) to match a cloth to the table. If you use candles, keep them clear of the sabzeh and any fabric, and check any building rules. ## The family meal

Many families serve **sabzi polo ba mahi** on the evening of Nowruz or the next day. It is rice mixed with fresh herbs, served with fried white fish. Other dishes may include kuku sabzi (an herb frittata), reshteh polo (noodle rice), dolma and sweets like baklava or cookies. Menus vary by region and by family.

For a crowd, plan serving space and heat. Rice and fish need to stay hot, so consider chafing dishes and a long buffet table. See [how many chafing dishes you need](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet).

## Sizdah Bedar: the picnic on day 13

On the 13th day of the new year, in early April, many families go outdoors for Sizdah Bedar. They picnic in a park, play games and toss the sabzeh into running water to let go of bad luck. Single people may tie knots in grass blades as a wish. In Chicago, the weather in early April is unpredictable.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Pick a spot.</strong> A park district pavilion or a forest preserve grove. Check permits and rules with your village or park district.</li>
<li><strong>Plan for cold.</strong> Heaters, blankets and a tent help if it is windy or raining.</li>
<li><strong>Set tables and seats.</strong> Picnic tables or folding tables with chairs.</li>

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Start the sabzeh early.** It takes about two weeks to sprout. This is not a rental tip, but it is the one thing hosts forget until too late.
- **Book early for the equinox weekend.** Late March is busy, and rental companies may fill up on weekends. Check the date as soon as you know it.
- **Plan the table for photos.** Guests will want to take pictures at the haft-sin table, so leave space and light.
- **Measure doors and stairs.** Banquet tables and chair carts need room.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is Nowruz in 2027?

Nowruz begins at the spring equinox on March 20 or 21. The exact time changes each year, so check a Persian calendar for the minute.

### What are the seven S items on a haft-sin table?

Sabzeh (sprouts), samanu (sweet pudding), senjed (dried oleaster), sir (garlic), sib (apple), somaq (sumac) and serkeh (vinegar). Many families also add a mirror, candles, painted eggs and hyacinth.

### What do you eat on Nowruz?

Many families serve sabzi polo ba mahi, which is herb rice with fish, and kuku sabzi. Sweets and tea are also common. Menus vary by region and family.

### What is Sizdah Bedar?

It is the 13th day of Nowruz, when families spend the day outdoors with a picnic and games. People toss sabzeh into water and tie knots in grass as wishes.

### How long does Nowruz last?

The holiday lasts about 13 days. Families visit each other during that time, and the final day is Sizdah Bedar.

### How many tables do I need for a Nowruz party?

Plan one table for the haft-sin, one or two for food, and enough dining tables for your guests. Use about 24 inches per person at the table as a rule of thumb.
