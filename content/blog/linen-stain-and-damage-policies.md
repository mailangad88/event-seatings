**Quick answer**

Most linen rental agreements separate normal wear and food spills (usually covered) from permanent damage such as burns, cuts, candle wax, makeup, ink and mildew (often charged at a replacement rate). Read the damage section before you sign, ask what counts as "normal," and photograph linens at delivery and return. Policies differ by company, so confirm yours in writing.

</div>

Nobody plans for a candle to tip onto a tablecloth, but at a seated dinner with 150 guests, something always happens. The question is who pays and how much. This guide explains how linen damage policies usually work so you can read your contract with the right questions in mind. It is general information, not a legal reading of any contract.

### Usually normal wear
Crumbs, grease from food, water marks, light wine spills and standard soiling that commercial laundering can remove.

### Often the gray area
Candle wax, lipstick or makeup, ink, glitter and confetti. Some suppliers absorb a small number; others charge per item.

### Usually chargeable
Burns, cuts, tears, permanent dye transfer, paint, mildew from damp storage, and missing items.

## What to look for in the contract

<div class="table-wrap">

| Clause | What to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Definition of damage | What counts as normal wear? | Sets what is billed |
| Replacement rates | Is there a stated rate per item? | Avoids surprise totals |
| Candle policy | Are candles allowed on linens? | Wax is a common charge |
| Glitter and confetti | Allowed or fee? | Hard to remove from fabric |
| Missing items | What is the rate? | Lost napkins add up |
| Damage window | When must damage be reported? | Timing of billing |
| Deposit | Is there one and when is it returned? | Cash flow and risk |

</div>

Do not rely on a verbal assurance. If something is important to you, get it in the written agreement.

## How to avoid charges

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Read the policy</strong> before you sign, not after the event.</li>
<li><strong>Check at delivery:</strong> count and look for existing marks, and photograph them.</li>
<li><strong>Limit risk items:</strong> use candle holders with cups, skip glitter, and put a mat under hot items.</li>
<li><strong>Brief the team:</strong> tell your caterer and venue to avoid markers and pens on linens.</li>
<li><strong>Return correctly:</strong> follow the steps in our guide on [washing rental linens](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-linens).</li>

## Common causes and fixes

- **Candles.** Use votives in glass and stable holders, and keep open flames away from fabric. Many venues also have their own candle rules, so ask.
- **Wine and sauces.** Pale linens show more. Choose darker napkins or tablecloths if the menu is heavy on red sauce.
- **Makeup.** Napkins often carry lipstick. Order a few spares and make sure they are not mixed with other items.
- **Ice buckets and sweating glasses.** Condensation leaves rings and can make mildew in wet bags.
- **Markers.** Escort cards signed on tablecloths are a common surprise.

## Who is responsible on the night?

The person who signs the rental agreement is almost always responsible, even if the damage was done by a guest, a caterer or a venue crew. If you suspect another vendor caused a problem, take a photo and note the time. Some caterers and venues carry insurance or have their own terms, so ask who covers what. Some events also need event insurance; check your venue's requirements.

## Insurance and deposits

Ask whether your rental company takes a deposit, whether it covers damage, and how damage is billed. Event liability insurance sometimes covers rented property, but terms vary, so ask your provider. We cannot advise on coverage, but your venue can usually tell you what it requires.

## Budget for it

## A Chicago-area checklist for the contract

Event rental contracts are written by each company, so there is no single Chicago standard. These are the questions to bring to any supplier you consider for a Chicago or suburban event, whether it is a downtown hotel ballroom or a backyard tent.

1. Do you charge for normal food and wine stains, or only for permanent damage?
2. What is the replacement rate for each type of linen, such as tablecloths, napkins, runners and overlays?
3. Do you restrict open flame or wax on your linens, and do you charge for wax?
4. If the venue or caterer damages a linen, do you bill me or them?
5. How do you check linens when you pick them up, and how soon do you tell me about damage?
6. Is there a late return fee if the event runs long or pickup is delayed?
7. Do you offer a damage waiver, and what does it cover?

Write the answers in your notes and ask for the policy in the final agreement. A verbal answer does not help if the person you spoke with is not the person who processes your return.

## Red flags

- No written damage policy at all
- A policy that bills any visible mark as damage, including ordinary food stains
- Replacement rates that are not listed anywhere
- No count at pickup, so there is no record of what came back

A good supplier will explain their process without defensiveness. If they cannot, consider the risk when you compare quotes.

## Special cases

**Sequin and velvet** are often handled under stricter terms because they cost more to clean and replace. Ask about them separately. **Lace and delicate overlays** can snag on rough table edges or chairs. **Dark linens** can transfer dye to light ones if bagged wet together. Keep dark and light separate for the return, and never seal damp items together.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I pay if a guest spills wine on a rental tablecloth?

Usually not for ordinary spills. Most suppliers expect wine and food stains and treat them in laundering. Permanent damage, like burns, is more likely to be billed.

### What happens if linens are burned by a candle?

Burns are almost always chargeable at a replacement rate. Use candle holders with cups and keep flames away from fabric to avoid it.

### Can I be charged for missing napkins?

Yes. Missing items are usually billed at the replacement rate in your contract. Count before you bag and report missing items yourself.

### Is candle wax considered damage?

It depends on the supplier. Some treat small amounts as normal wear and others charge per item. Ask before you plan candles on tables.

### Should I photograph linens at delivery?

Yes. Photos of the stack and any existing marks give you a record. Do the same at return.

### Does event insurance cover rental damage?

Sometimes. Coverage varies by policy, so read yours or ask your provider. Your venue may require insurance regardless.
