**Quick answer**

A good mocktail bar offers 3 drinks (one citrus, one fruity, one herbal or spiced), served in the same glasses as your cocktails, with real garnishes. Plan about 1 mocktail per non-drinking guest per hour and assume 15 to 25 percent of guests will choose them. Set the station beside the main bar, not at the end of the room, so it feels part of the party.

Not everyone drinks: designated drivers, pregnant guests, people in recovery, kids and plenty of others. A mocktail bar gives them a drink with the same care as the cocktails. It also eases the load on the main bar. Many couples in the Chicago area now add one to weddings and corporate events.

## The three-drink formula

Pick three drinks with different flavors so every guest finds one.

These are classic mixes, so ask your bartender or caterer to refine amounts. Batching the base in a dispenser saves time, as in a [signature cocktail station](/blog/signature-cocktail-station-ideas).

## How many mocktails

As a rule of thumb, assume 15 to 25 percent of guests will choose a mocktail at any one time, and that they will drink about 1 per hour. For 100 guests over 4 hours, that is roughly 60 to 100 servings. Add water and soda as a fallback, and see the [water station guide](/blog/water-station-infused-water-dispenser).

## What to rent

- **A 6-foot table or bar** next to the main bar. See [mobile bar vs back bar](/blog/mobile-bar-vs-back-bar-rental).
- **Glass dispensers** for batched bases.
- **Glassware:** highballs, rocks glasses or coupes. See [glassware types](/blog/wedding-glassware-types-explained).
- **Ice bins, tongs and garnish dishes.**
- **Bar tools,** napkins and a trash bin; use the [bar tools checklist](/blog/bar-tools-and-cocktail-napkins-checklist).
- **Menu signs** with ingredients, since many guests ask about allergies and sugar.

## Make it feel like part of the party

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Use the same glassware</strong> as your cocktail bar.</li>
<li><strong>Name each drink</strong> and print it on the menu.</li>
<li><strong>Garnish properly:</strong> herbs, citrus, berries, a skewer.</li>
<li><strong>Place the station</strong> next to the main bar.</li>
<li><strong>Train the bartender</strong> to offer it by name.</li>
</ol>

Do not label non-alcoholic drinks as "the sober option" or put them in a different corner. A guest who is driving, pregnant or in recovery should be able to order without comment. A bartender should also keep mocktail ingredients separate from alcohol (separate tongs and garnish), which matters for guests who avoid alcohol entirely.

## Setting the menu sign

A small framed sign does a lot of work. List each drink by name with three or four ingredients, mark which are low sugar and which contain nuts or dairy, and say plainly that every drink is alcohol free. Guests who do not drink often skip the bar because they are unsure what is on offer or do not want to ask. A sign removes that friction, and it gives guests a conversation piece. If your wedding has a theme or a color palette, name the drinks to match, such as a name for the couple's first date or a favorite place in Chicago.

## Allergens and dietary notes

Post ingredients for common allergens: nuts in syrups, dairy in creamers, citrus. Offer low-sugar choices such as sparkling water with fruit. Check cider and ginger beer labels for alcohol content, since some craft products are not alcohol free.

## Mocktails and the rules

If your bar is regulated by your venue, non-alcoholic drinks are usually not restricted, but the bar location still is. Read [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events) for the alcohol side. At events with minors, a mocktail bar is also the safest place for under-21 guests to order from; see [kids' glassware and toasts](/blog/kids-glassware-and-toasts).

## Seating the station

Place two or three seats nearby for guests who want to linger. A light [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) or a clear [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) fits most bar areas.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a mocktail bar?

A mocktail bar is a station serving non-alcoholic cocktails with the same glassware and garnishes as an alcohol bar. It gives non-drinkers a special drink.

### How many mocktails should I offer?

Three is enough. Offer a citrus, a fruity and an herbal option, and keep water and soda available.

### How many guests will order mocktails?

A rule of thumb is 15 to 25 percent at any time, more for events with many drivers, families or daytime crowds.

### Can bartenders make mocktails?

Yes. Ask your bar company to include them in the menu and quote them, because some charge for a dedicated staffer.

### What glasses do I use for mocktails?

Use highballs for fizzy drinks and rocks or coupe glasses for short ones. Using the same glasses as cocktails makes everyone feel included.

### Can I make mocktails ahead of time?

Yes. Batch the base and keep it cold, then add soda and garnish at the bar so the drink stays fizzy and fresh.
