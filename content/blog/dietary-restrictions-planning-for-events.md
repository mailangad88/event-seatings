**Quick answer**

Ask about dietary needs on the RSVP with a short list of options and a free-text box, then give the caterer a count and a list of severe allergies at least two weeks out. Label every dish, use separate serving utensils, and mark special meals at the table. Plan a few dishes that suit many diets, and have one person responsible for answering food questions on the day.

Everyone has seen the sad plate: a vegetarian guest with a pile of plain sides, or a person with a nut allergy asking staff what is in each dish. Dietary needs are a matter of comfort and, sometimes, safety. The good news is that a simple process handles most of it, and it doesn't require a custom menu for every guest.

## Dietary planning rules of thumb

<div><strong>2 weeks</strong><span>Send the final list to the caterer this far out</span></div>

This is practical planning, not medical advice. For severe allergies, talk directly with the guest and the caterer, and follow your caterer's food safety procedures.

## Ask the right way

On your invitation or registration form, add a short question: "Do you have any dietary needs or allergies we should know about?" Offer checkboxes for common ones such as vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, nut allergy, shellfish allergy, halal and kosher, plus a free-text box for details. Make it clear that the information goes to the caterer.

Follow up personally with guests who list severe allergies. A short note like "We have you down for a peanut allergy; our caterer will prepare a safe meal and a staff member will check with you at the table" builds trust. Keep a clean list with names, needs and table numbers. A tool like the [Guest List and RSVP Tracker](/downloads/guest-list-rsvp-tracker) keeps these notes next to each name.

## Work with your caterer

Share the counts and details early, and ask how the kitchen handles cross-contact. Ask whether the caterer can offer a menu that works for many guests, such as grilled proteins, vegetable dishes, rice and salads with dressing on the side. For plated dinners, ask how special meals will be marked and delivered, and whether servers know who gets them.

For buffets, the layout matters. Place vegetarian and allergen-friendly dishes first, use separate serving utensils for each dish and keep labels in front. Our [buffet line flow guide](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) explains how to avoid bottlenecks when several dishes need their own utensils and labels. The [corporate event planning checklist](/blog/corporate-event-planning-checklist) shows where menu decisions fit in the larger plan. If you want to simplify, a family-style or station format gives flexibility.

## Labels, tables and day-of tips

Print simple labels with the dish name and the main allergens, such as milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat and soy. Use different colored cards for vegetarian, vegan or gluten-free if that helps. For plated meals, use small cards at the place setting or a color-coded dot on the place card; our [seating chart planner](/downloads/seating-chart-planner) makes it easy to note meals by seat.

Brief servers and volunteers on the special meals before service. Designate one person, the caterer's lead or your event lead, to answer food questions. Keep a few extra safe meals in the kitchen for surprises. If you serve alcohol, offer non-alcoholic options and label them too.

## Common needs and what to plan

This table is a general guide and not a medical reference. Ask the guest and the caterer about specifics.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Need | Planning note | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetarian | Offer a main dish, not just sides | Hidden meat stock or gelatin |
| Vegan | Plant-based main and dessert | Dairy, eggs, honey |
| Gluten-free | Separate prep and utensils | Sauces, croutons, fryer oil |
| Nut allergy | Talk to the guest and kitchen | Desserts, sauces, garnishes |
| Dairy-free | Dedicated dishes or substitutions | Butter, cream, cheese |
| Halal or kosher | Confirm certified sources | Shared equipment, preparation rules |

</div>

When guests have religious needs, ask them directly what is acceptable, and ask the caterer about certification and preparation.

Chicago's food scene gives you many caterers with experience in dietary needs, but ask each about their allergen process and whether they can prepare kosher, halal or other specialized meals, which may require certified providers. For events at hotels or event halls, ask whether outside caterers are allowed and how the kitchen handles allergies. ## Frequently asked questions

### How do I ask guests about dietary restrictions?

Add a short question to the RSVP with checkboxes for common needs and a text box for details. Follow up personally for severe allergies.

### When should I give the caterer the dietary list?

About two weeks before the event is a common deadline, with an update a few days before. Check your caterer's requirements.

### How do I label food at a buffet?

Use cards in front of each dish with the name and main allergens. Place friendly dishes first and use separate serving utensils.

### How do I handle severe allergies?

Talk to the guest and the caterer directly, ask about cross-contact practices and make sure staff know who has the allergy.

### How many vegetarian meals should I plan for?

Use your RSVP counts and add a small buffer. Buffets and family-style meals with several plant-based dishes handle uncertainty well.

### What if a guest shows up with a need I didn't know about?

Keep a few extra safe meals available and ask the caterer how they handle last-minute requests. Be calm and apologetic.

### Do I need to offer non-alcoholic drinks?

Yes. Offer water, soft drinks and a non-alcoholic option that feels special, such as a mocktail or sparkling juice.
