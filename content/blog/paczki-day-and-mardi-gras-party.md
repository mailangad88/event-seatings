In Chicago, Fat Tuesday means two things at once. For Polish families and neighbors, it is Paczki Day, when people line up for filled doughnuts before Lent. For many others, it is Mardi Gras, with beads, music and a king cake. You can host both at the same party. The key is that Tuesday is a work night, so you plan around the clock.

## What each tradition is

**Paczki.** A paczek (the plural is paczki) is a rich, round doughnut filled with jam or cream. Traditional fillings include prune, rose hip, custard and plum, and many bakeries also make fruit and cream versions. Poland has its own Fat Thursday, the Thursday before Ash Wednesday, while many Polish Americans, including those in the Chicago area, mark Fat Tuesday. Traditions vary by family, so ask your guests how they celebrate.

**Mardi Gras.** French for Fat Tuesday, it is the last day before Lent in many Christian traditions. The colors are purple, green and gold. A king cake is a ring-shaped cake, and a small figure is hidden in it. Whoever finds it may get to host the next party or buy the next cake, depending on the group.

## The paczki tasting table

A tasting table is the center of the party. Order from a local bakery ahead of time, since many sell out on Fat Tuesday. Ask for a variety of fillings and cut each in half so guests can try more than one.

- **Setup.** A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth. Use tiered cake stands or trays to give height.
- **Labels.** Small cards with the filling name for each. Some guests have allergies, so ask about nuts, dairy and eggs before the party.
- **Plates and napkins.** Paczki are sticky. Provide small plates, plenty of napkins and a trash bin nearby. See [dessert table equipment](/blog/dessert-table-equipment-checklist).
- **Drinks.** Hot coffee and tea go well with them. See [coffee and tea service rental](/blog/coffee-and-tea-service-rental).

For a Polish crowd, ask what the family makes for the day. Our guide to a [Polish wedding reception in Chicago](/blog/polish-wedding-reception-chicago) lists foods that many Polish families serve at gatherings.

## The king cake and Mardi Gras decor

King cake is easy to find in bakeries around Chicago in the weeks before Fat Tuesday, or you can make one. Put a small figure in the cake yourself if the bakery does not, and tell guests so no one chokes on it. Decorate with purple, green and gold tablecloths, runners and beads. Use a color theme in the linens to carry it across the room: see [linen colors by season](/blog/linen-colors-by-season).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Weeknight deliveries are tight.** Rental companies deliver and pick up on set windows, and a Tuesday party means a pickup the same night or the next morning. Ask which it is.
- **Order paczki early.** Bakeries in Polish neighborhoods like Avondale and Jefferson Park can have long lines and sold-out items on the day. Place orders as soon as you can.
- **Winter parking is hard.** Snow takes up street spots, so tell guests where to park. See [guest parking and transit tips](/blog/guest-parking-and-transit-plan-chicago).
- **Count chairs and glasses.** A mix of coffee cups, wine glasses and water cups adds up.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is Paczki Day in 2027?

In Chicago, Paczki Day falls on Fat Tuesday, February 9, 2027. In Poland, the matching tradition is Fat Thursday, which comes earlier in the same week.

### What is a paczek?

A paczek is a rich, filled doughnut, usually with jam or cream and dusted with sugar or glaze. The plural is paczki. Prune, rose hip and custard are common fillings.

### How many paczki should I buy per guest?

Plan on 1 to 2 per person if you cut them in half for tasting. Order a few extra, since people take them home.

### What is a king cake?

A king cake is a ring-shaped Mardi Gras cake with a small figure hidden inside. The person who finds it often hosts the next party. It is covered in purple, green and gold sugar.

### How do I host a party on a weeknight?

Start around 6 pm, serve food right away and end by 9 pm. Keep the setup simple so guests who come from work can sit or stand and eat. Plan a quick cleanup.

### Can I combine Paczki Day and Mardi Gras?

Yes. Many Chicago hosts serve paczki and a king cake at the same table, and use Mardi Gras colors. Mention the traditions to guests so everyone knows what they are eating.
