## How to lay out the room

1. **Put check-in at the entrance**, with its own table and chairs for the staff running it, so the flow into the room is clear from the door.
2. **Line up employer booths along the walls or in rows**, with enough space behind each table for staff to stand and greet people before they sit.
3. **Give each booth one candidate chair facing the recruiters**, not a shared bench, so conversations stay one-on-one.
4. **Set a waiting row off to the side**, not directly behind any single booth, so people in line aren't listening in on the conversation ahead of them.

## How many chairs to rent

Count chairs by role, not by total attendance, since most candidates are standing or walking the room at any given moment:

- **2 to 3 chairs per booth.** Two for recruiter staff, one for the candidate. A popular employer with two reps interviewing at once may want 2 candidate chairs instead of 1.
- **A handful at check-in**, enough for however many staff are running registration.
- **A waiting row sized to your busiest hour**, not your total headcount. 10 to 15 chairs covers most campus and community career fairs; a large hiring event with dozens of employers may want more spread across the room instead of one long line.

## If you're hosting at a hotel, college or workforce center

Many hotels, college career centers and local workforce development offices in the Chicago area already own folding chairs and tables for this kind of event, since hiring fairs are a regular booking for them. Before you rent more, ask:

- How many chairs and tables are included for your expected number of employer booths?
- Is the room set already, or will your team need to arrange booths and the waiting row yourselves?
- Can outside rental furniture come in alongside the venue's own, and is there a load-in window before doors open?

Renting makes the most sense when you're running the event somewhere without built-in seating, such as an office lobby, community center gym or outdoor plaza, or when the venue's chairs don't match the number of booths you've booked.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a career fair?
Plan 2 to 3 chairs per employer booth, enough for recruiter staff and one candidate, plus a small check-in area and a waiting row near the booths. Count by role rather than total attendance, since most people are walking the room rather than seated at once.

### How should seating be set up at a job fair?
Put check-in at the entrance, line employer booths along the walls or in rows with a candidate chair facing each one, and set a waiting row off to the side so people in line aren't crowding the booth ahead of them.

### Does the venue provide chairs for a career fair?
Often yes. Hotels, college career centers and workforce development offices frequently own folding chairs and tables for hiring events. Ask what's included for your number of booths before you book rentals.

### What chairs work best for a career or job fair?
A chair that looks professional and stacks away quickly works best, such as a mid-century shell chair for booths or a folding chair for a waiting row you may need to add to or remove on short notice.
