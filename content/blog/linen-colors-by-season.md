**Quick answer**

Pick linens that fit the light and the mood of the month. Spring suits blush, sage and soft white. Summer suits crisp white, sky blue, coral and greens. Fall suits rust, burgundy, deep green and champagne. Winter suits ivory, navy, black, silver and emerald. Keep a neutral base such as ivory or white in every palette, and add season color through napkins, runners and florals.

Chicago has four real seasons, and linen colors that look right in a May garden can look wrong in a January ballroom. Light changes, flowers change, and guests dress differently. A seasonal approach also saves money: in-season flowers and warm or cool linens pull a room together with less decor. This guide gives a palette for each season and how to build it into the table.

## Spring (April to May)

Spring in the Chicago area is cool, often rainy, and the light is soft. Pastels work, but avoid anything that looks washed out under gray skies. **Blush, sage and soft white** are the usual trio, with champagne napkins for warmth. Lamour or matte polyester photographs best in flat light.

## Summer (June to August)

Bright light and long days favor crisp whites, sky blue, coral and green. Tent interiors can look harsh at midday, so avoid shiny fabrics in direct sun. Lighter linens also feel cooler, and light colors show spills, so keep wine away from white napkins or have spares. For tent events, see our [outdoor event chairs guide](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues).

## Fall (September to November)

Fall is peak wedding season around Chicago, and warm colors rule: rust, burgundy, deep green, gold and mustard. Velvet runners and rust napkins on ivory cloths are classic. See [Chicago fall wedding linen colors](/blog/chicago-fall-wedding-linen-colors) for more specific combinations.

## Winter (December to March)

Winter events use rich, cool or metallic tones: ivory, navy, emerald, silver and black. Heavy fabrics such as velvet and sequin feel appropriate. Candlelight loves metallic and dark linens. Our [winter wedding seating guide](/blog/winter-wedding-seating) covers layouts for the same season.

## Pairing season color with the venue

The season sets the mood, but the building decides how color reads. Chicago-area events happen in very different rooms: glass-walled lakefront spaces full of cool daylight, historic halls with dark wood, hotel ballrooms with warm chandeliers, and white tents on suburban lawns. A rust napkin that glows in a barn looks muddy in a dim ballroom. Walk the room at the hour your event will happen, or ask your venue for photos of that time of day, before you lock a palette.

- **Glass and daylight rooms.** Lean on color that holds up in strong light: deeper greens, navy, and saturated accents. Pale blush can wash out.
- **Dark wood or brick.** Ivory and champagne bring light back to the table; very dark linens disappear.
- **Neutral ballrooms.** These are the easiest. Almost any seasonal accent works against beige carpet and cream walls.
- **Tents.** White tent walls bounce light, so rich colors look truer than pastels.

## Using flowers to carry the season

In-season flowers do much of the work, and linens can stay quiet. Spring tulips and ranunculus pair with blush and sage. Summer garden flowers pair with white and green. Fall dahlias and foliage pair with rust and burgundy, and winter greenery and white blooms pair with navy, emerald and silver. Ask your florist what is in season in the Midwest for your date, then pick napkins and runners that echo one flower color, not all of them.

## When to stay neutral all year

If your event spans seasons, or you are unsure of the room, a white or ivory base with two small accents is the safest plan. Accents can change by event without replacing the whole linen order: a pack of napkins, a few runners and a head table cloth are enough to shift the feel from summer to fall.

## Frequently asked questions

### What linen colors are best for a summer wedding?

White, sky blue, coral, and green. They feel cool, work with bright flowers and stay fresh under outdoor light.

### What colors work for a fall wedding in Chicago?

Rust, burgundy, deep green and gold on an ivory base. Velvet and linen textures add the warmth.

### Should I choose linen colors based on the season or the venue?

Both. The venue sets the base (wood, stone, carpet) and the season sets the mood. If the room is dark, favor lighter linens; if it is bright, deeper colors hold up.

### Do white linens work all year?

Yes. White or ivory suits every season and gives flowers and chairs the spotlight. Season shows up through napkins, runners and decor.

### How many colors should I use?

Two or three. One neutral, one main accent, and one metallic or secondary tone.

### Can I use dark linens in summer?

Yes, in evening tents or ballrooms. Navy, black and emerald look elegant in summer when the room is dim.
