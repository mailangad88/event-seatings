**Quick answer**

For a party in a Chicago condo or high-rise, call building management first and ask four things: whether you must reserve the freight elevator and loading dock, what hours deliveries are allowed, whether vendors must send a certificate of insurance (COI) naming the building, and how much a party room or unit can hold. Rules vary by building, so get the answers in writing before you order any rentals.

</div>

A 40th-floor party has a view that no banquet hall can match, and a delivery path that no banquet hall has to deal with. Chairs and tables do not ride the passenger elevator in most Chicago high-rises. They go through a loading dock, onto a freight elevator and down a hall. If any link in that chain is not booked, the delivery waits on the curb. This guide shows what to ask and in what order.

## Every building sets its own rules

We cannot give you a citywide rule for high-rise deliveries, because there is not one. Rules come from the building's management company, the condo association and sometimes the lease. What is common is that buildings reserve the freight elevator for moves and deliveries, limit hours, ask vendors for proof of insurance and sometimes require a deposit. The only reliable source is your property manager.

## The five questions to ask management

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Freight elevator.</strong> Is a reservation required, how long is a slot, and is there a fee or deposit?</li>
<li><strong>Delivery hours.</strong> Which days and times may vendors enter? Weekend limits are common.</li>
<li><strong>Loading dock or curb.</strong> Where does the truck stop, how tall a vehicle fits, and who opens the dock?</li>
<li><strong>Insurance.</strong> Does the building want a COI, and what language and limits does it need?</li>
<li><strong>Capacity.</strong> What is the maximum number of guests for the unit or the party room?</li>
</ol>

## Understanding the COI

A certificate of insurance is a one-page document from a vendor's insurer proving it carries coverage. Buildings often want it to name the association or management company as an additional party. Ask management for the exact wording, then pass it to your rental company. A rental company that has not seen your building's requirements cannot guess them, and the certificate takes time to issue. Request it at least a week before the event, and do not wait for delivery day.

## Hallway, door and elevator measurements

Chairs fold or stack, but tables do not shrink. Measure before you order:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Where to measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Freight elevator door width and height | Round tabletops and 8-foot banquet tables are the tightest fit |
| Hallway width and corners | Carts need turning room |
| Unit entry door | Often narrower than the lobby doors |
| Party room doorway | Check for a step or threshold |
| Elevator weight limit | Ask management; do not guess |

</div>

Standard banquet tables are 30 inches tall and either 72 or 96 inches long, and round tables typically come in 48, 60 and 72-inch diameters. If a door is tight, ask for folding-leg tables or a smaller diameter. Our [round table size guide](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch) shows how much each size seats.

## Plan the setup window

High-rise setups run long because every trip is a round trip: dock, elevator, hall, unit. A rule of thumb is to ask for a longer setup window than you would for a ground-floor party, and to book the freight elevator for both delivery and pickup. Pickup is where people forget. If the elevator is only reserved for the morning, a late-night party can leave rentals stuck on the floor.

## Furniture that suits a small high-rise space

Condo living rooms are tight, so the plan is mostly cocktail seating. Light chairs that two people can carry are easier on a freight elevator and in a narrow hall. The [wishbone chair](/chairs/wishbone) and [bentwood chair](/chairs/bentwood) fit a modern interior. For lounge seating, see our [lounge layout by guest count](/blog/lounge-layout-by-guest-count). Plan chair counts with the [how many chairs guide](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need), and remember that standing guests need less than seated ones.

## Noise, balconies and neighbors

Condo rules often cover noise and balcony use. Check quiet hours, whether open-flame candles or grills are banned, and whether the balcony has a guest limit. Give guests clear instructions on where to enter and which elevator to take, so they do not wander to the freight door.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to reserve the freight elevator for a party delivery?

Most Chicago high-rises require it for deliveries and moves, but each building decides. Ask management for the booking process, the time slot length and whether there is a fee. Book both delivery and pickup.

### What is a COI and why does a high-rise ask for one?

A COI is a certificate of insurance showing a vendor's coverage. High-rises ask for it to protect the building if a vendor damages property or someone is hurt. Ask management for the exact wording and give it to your vendors early.

### Can a rental company deliver to the lobby only?

Sometimes, but many buildings require vendors to use the loading dock and freight elevator. Whether a lobby handoff is allowed is your building's call. Ask before you confirm a delivery plan.

### How many guests can I have in a condo party?

That depends on the unit size, the association rules and fire code. Ask management for the limit for your unit or party room rather than relying on a general number. Allow room for seating, pathways and a bar.

### What hours can vendors deliver to a Chicago high-rise?

It varies by building, and weekends are often more restricted. Ask for the allowed hours in writing and schedule setup to finish well before guests arrive.

### Do I need a deposit for the freight elevator?

Some buildings charge one, and some do not. Ask management what they require and when it is refunded. Do not assume your landlord or board waives it.
