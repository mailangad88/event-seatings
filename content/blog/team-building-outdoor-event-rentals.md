**Quick answer**

A team building day runs on stations: a check-in, a shaded base camp, activity areas, a lunch zone and a rest area. For 60 people, plan one 20 x 30 foot tent, 30 to 40 chairs, 6 to 8 tables and shade near every activity. Choose a site with restrooms and a wet-weather option, and give every group a home base where they can sit and refuel.

</div>

Outdoor team building days look simple: a park, some games, lunch. The ones that go wrong have no shade, no place to sit between activities and a food line that eats an hour. The ones that go right are organized into stations with a home base for each team. Here is how to plan the rental list for a Chicago-area forest preserve, park district shelter or corporate campus.

## Activities and gear

Most activity providers bring their own props. Ask them what they need from you: tables, chairs, canopies or power. Typical needs include 6-foot tables for puzzle tasks, a few chairs for judges, a canopy for supplies and a mic for the facilitator. Keep supplies off the grass with a table or a tarp.

## Lunch

Choose a lunch format that moves quickly: boxed lunches, a taco bar or a sandwich buffet with two sides. A 6-foot buffet table with access on both sides serves about twice as fast as one side; see [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed). Seat teams together at 8-foot tables so conversation continues. Place drinks in a separate station and use real cups where possible to reduce waste.

## Heat, rain and bugs

Summer in Chicago brings heat, humidity and sudden thunderstorms. Your plan should have shade over every sitting area, water at every station, and a clear plan to move to a shelter if lightning approaches. Fans and misters make a difference in July; see [misting and shade for summer events](/blog/misting-and-shade-for-summer-events). Provide sunscreen, bug spray and first aid. A weather call by a named person with a known cutoff time avoids last-minute confusion.

## Site and permit questions

Parks, forest preserves and corporate campuses each have different rules for tents, vehicles, grills and alcohol. We haven't verified rules for specific sites, so ask the site manager about permits, load-in routes, stakes and generators. Ask whether a certificate of insurance is required; see [corporate event COI and vendor requirements](/blog/corporate-event-coi-and-vendor-requirements).

## Facilitators, judges and the sound problem

Outdoor groups spread out and voices get lost in wind. If a facilitator gives instructions to 60 or more people, plan a portable speaker and a wireless mic, and ask the park about noise rules. A table with a cloth near the start line holds scoreboards, prizes and sign-in sheets. Judges need a chair, a clipboard and shade, so count them in your chairs. Photographers and the event lead should wear bright shirts so teams can find them. Keep a small group of spare chairs and a folding table in a vehicle or at base camp for the unexpected, such as an extra station or an injured participant who needs to sit.

## Inclusion

Choose activities that everyone can join: seated options, low-mobility roles and non-competitive tasks. Provide a chair at each station for anyone who needs it. Check dietary needs for lunch. Keep a quiet corner at base camp for anyone who needs a break.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent for outdoor team building?

A tent for base camp and lunch, tables, chairs, canopies at stations and drink gear. Add trash stations and a first-aid table.

### How many chairs for 60 people?

About 30 to 40, since teams move between activities.

### Do I need a tent?

Yes, for shade and for a rain plan. A 20 x 30 foot tent suits about 60 people at tables.

### What if it rains?

Have a tent with sidewalls, an indoor backup or a reschedule date. Decide by a set time the morning of.

### How do I make lunch quick?

Use boxed lunches or a double-sided buffet line. Seat teams together.

### Is a permit needed?

It depends on the site. Ask the park district, forest preserve or campus manager.
