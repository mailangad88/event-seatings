**Quick answer**

Yes, a farm table usually looks best with a runner only, or no cloth at all, because the wood grain is the decoration. Use a runner 14 to 18 inches wide that hangs 6 to 12 inches past each end of an 8-foot table (a 108 to 120-inch runner), and set cloth napkins directly on the wood. A farm table (also called a harvest or feast table) is a long, narrow wooden table, often 8 feet or longer, that seats guests at both sides and sometimes the ends. At a rustic Chicago-area wedding or a long-table dinner it replaces a room full of rounds. Because the wood is the star, the linens are small and chosen with care. This guide covers when to go bare, how to size a runner, and how to avoid the common mistakes.

### Bare wood

The cleanest option. Napkins, plates and glassware sit directly on the table, and candles or greenery run down the center. It works when the table is in good condition and your venue allows food and drink directly on the wood. Ask your rental company how the table is finished, because some are sealed and some are not.

### Runner only

The most popular. A runner protects the center strip, adds color and gives the centerpieces a place to sit, while the wood still shows at the edges. A runner in a natural fabric such as linen-look cotton, burlap or a soft gauze suits the style.

### Full cloth

A full cloth makes sense on a banquet table, not a farm table, but it has uses. Consider it when the wood is scarred or mismatched, when you need a uniform color across several different table styles, or when a tent venue wants a protective layer. If you do, use a simple, slightly textured cloth and add a runner for dimension. Our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) lists banquet sizes, but most farm tables are narrower, so confirm the exact dimensions.

## Runner size for a farm table

Farm tables vary, so measure or ask. A common rental farm table is 8 feet (96 inches) long and 36 to 40 inches wide, wider than a 30-inch banquet table. That extra width lets you use a slightly wider runner.

For very long tables, consider two runners overlapping slightly in the middle, or leave a gap and place a centerpiece to bridge them. See [table runner sizes and styles](/blog/table-runner-sizes-and-styles) for the full guide to width and drop.

## Place setting spacing

A farm table seats guests close together, so plan 24 inches of table length per guest as a minimum for a comfortable dinner. That means an 8-foot table seats 8 with two on each side if you add the ends, or 6 to 8 along the sides. Do not push the number up just to fit more guests; elbow room is the first thing people notice. See [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) for related counts.

## Napkins on a farm table

With no cloth, the napkin is the only fabric each guest sees. Choose a napkin color that contrasts with the wood: ivory or white on dark wood, and deep green, navy, terracotta or charcoal on light wood. Linen or cotton-blend napkins suit the look. Tuck a sprig of greenery or a place card under a simple napkin ring. Our [napkin ring and placement guide](/blog/napkin-rings-and-placement) shows where each piece goes.

## Chairs for farm tables

Farm tables are low and long, so seating style matters. Bench seating is popular but not comfortable for a long dinner and hard to adjust for spacing. Individual chairs let you space guests evenly. A [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) matches the wood look, a [bentwood chair](/chairs/bentwood) gives a softer line, and [rattan garden chairs](/chairs/rattan-garden) suit a boho setup. If you are planning a barn or rustic space, see [barn wedding seating ideas](/blog/barn-wedding-seating-ideas).

## Chicago planning notes

Farm tables are common at barns, vineyards and backyard tents in the western and northern suburbs. Many venues have their own tables, so check whether you are renting them or using theirs. Ask about the table surface, the legs (some have trestle legs that limit seating at the ends), and whether they allow candles directly on the wood. For a tent on grass, make sure the floor is level, because a farm table wobbles more than a banquet table.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need a tablecloth on a farm table?

No. Most farm tables look best with a runner or bare wood, since the grain is part of the style. Use a cloth only to hide damage or unify mixed tables, or if your venue requires a barrier.

### What size runner fits an 8-foot farm table?

A runner 14 to 18 inches wide and 108 to 120 inches long. That leaves 6 to 12 inches of overhang at each end and shows the wood at the sides.

### What napkins go on a bare wood table?

Napkins in a color that contrasts with the wood: ivory or white on dark wood, and deeper colors on light wood. Linen-look or cotton-blend napkins fit the style.

### How many people sit at a farm table?

About 8 for an 8-foot table with guests on both sides, using 24 inches or more per person. Adding the ends can raise it to 10, but check for leg interference.

### Can candles go directly on a farm table?

Sometimes, depending on the venue and the table finish. Use glass holders or trays to protect the wood, and ask your rental company and venue before you plan open flames.

### How do I keep a runner from blowing away?

Weight it with centerpieces, candles in glass holders or a small tuck of fabric tape at the ends. In tents, avoid lightweight satin runners that lift easily.
