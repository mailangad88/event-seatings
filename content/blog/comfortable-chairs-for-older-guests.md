Older guests are often the people the whole event is for: grandparents, parents, mentors. They are also the people most affected by a hard seat, a long walk, a low chair or a crowded table. The good news is that comfort is mostly planning, not budget. You can seat everyone well with a few smart choices on chair type, cushions and layout. This guide covers each, using the chairs available in the Chicago area.

## What makes a chair comfortable for an older guest

Think about the whole sit: getting down, staying and getting up.

- **Cushion.** Pressure builds on a hard seat after an hour or so.
- **Back support.** A back that meets the lower spine, not a decorative hoop.
- **Arms.** Armrests make standing easier. Many event chairs have none; ask.
- **Stability.** No wobble, no rocking. See [how to spot a quality rental chair](/blog/how-to-spot-quality-rental-chair).
- **Seat height.** A very low seat is hard to rise from.
- **Weight.** A chair a guest can slide in and out without lifting is easier. Heavy chairs are harder; very light chairs can tip when leaned on.

## Layout matters as much as the chair

1. **Seat them near the entrance and restrooms.** A shorter walk is the best accommodation.
2. **Keep the route clear.** Leave at least 36 inches around tables, and 48 inches where possible. Avoid cords, thresholds and tent flooring edges.
3. **Choose tables with room.** A 60-inch round with 8 guests is more comfortable than a 72-inch round with 12.
4. **Don't seat anyone in front of a speaker.** Loud bass and a long dinner are a poor combination.
5. **Think about temperature.** Avoid air vents and doors. In a Chicago winter, a draft near the entrance is uncomfortable for everyone.

## Mobility aids

Some guests use a cane, walker or wheelchair. Leave space for the aid beside the chair, and plan for a clear spot at the table for a wheelchair so a guest does not have to be moved. A commonly published planning figure for a wheelchair space is 30 by 48 inches. For guidance on how many accessible seats to plan, see [chair weight capacity and seat sizes](/blog/chair-weight-capacity-and-seat-sizes), and ask your venue about its own accessibility policy.

Provide a chair with arms near the aisle for guests who need help standing. If you do not have arm chairs, ask your rental company what is available.

## Ceremony seating

Ceremonies are shorter, but older guests still need care. Reserve the first rows near the aisle, put a sign on those chairs, and make sure the route from the car or door is clear. If the ceremony is outdoors, avoid grass for the first rows. Our [outdoor event chairs and Chicago weather](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues) guide explains why a firm surface matters.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most comfortable event chair for older guests?

A chair with an included cushion and a supportive back, such as the velvet dining or Louis medallion. A cushioned wood chair is the next best.

### Do older guests need chairs with arms?

Arms help many people stand up, but many event chairs do not have them. If you have guests who need them, tell your rental company early.

### How much space should I leave between tables?

At least 36 inches of clear path, and 48 inches where the room allows. That gives a walker or a server room to pass.

### Are folding chairs okay for older guests?

For a short ceremony, yes. For a long dinner, a cushioned chair is better.

### Where should older guests sit?

Near the entrance, close to restrooms and away from speakers, vents and doors.

### How do I choose a chair for a three-hour dinner?

Pick one with a built-in cushion, or add a cushion to a wood chair. The seat is the first thing guests notice after an hour.

### Can I ask the rental company to hold a few extra comfortable chairs?

Yes. Ask for a few spares in a cushioned style so staff can swap quietly.
