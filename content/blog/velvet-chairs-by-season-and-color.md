Velvet is the one rental chair that changes the temperature of a room. A blush velvet chair says spring brunch; an emerald one says December ballroom. In Chicago, where the seasons feel very different from each other, color choice does a lot of the styling for you. This guide gives a color-by-season map, shows how to place velvet in the room and explains where to save money by using it only for part of the guest list.

## Our four velvet colors

Our [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) has a channel-tufted back, slim gold or black legs and a cushion included. The colors:

- **Emerald:** a deep green with a jewel quality.
- **Blush:** a soft dusty pink.
- **Navy:** a dark blue that reads close to black in low light.
- **Champagne:** a warm beige gold, the most neutral of the four.

## Spring: blush and champagne

March through May in Chicago is often cool and wet, so most spring events are indoors. Blush velvet warms up a gray day. Pair it with ivory linen, peonies or ranunculus and gold or brass flatware. For a more neutral look, champagne does the same job with less pink.

## Summer: champagne and blush

Summer receptions are often late afternoon and evening, sometimes under a floored tent. Champagne reads light and does not compete with greenery. Remember that velvet is an indoor chair in our catalog, so summer outdoor use needs a covered, dry, floored space. Heat and humidity are the real enemies of upholstery.

## Fall: emerald and navy

Fall brings color, milder weather and earlier sunsets. Emerald velvet looks natural beside rust, burgundy and pumpkin, and navy suits a black-tie evening. Fall is also when ballroom availability is most limited, so see [when to book event chair rentals](/blog/topic/budget-and-booking) before you decide.

## Winter: navy and emerald

Winter weddings and galas have the darkest rooms, and velvet is made for them. Navy with silver flatware and white florals, or emerald with evergreen, candles and brass. Our [winter wedding seating](/blog/winter-wedding-seating) guide covers coat checks, heating and room flow.

## Use velvet only where it counts

Velvet is the highest-priced wood-and-upholstery chair in our catalog, with an estimated range of $18 to $25 per chair. Many planners use it only at the sweetheart or head table, or at family tables, and save with a plainer chair for the rest. The result is the same in photos, because those are the tables people look at.

## Matching linens and care

- **Emerald or navy velvet:** ivory, champagne or black linen. Avoid green linens with green velvet; the colors fight.
- **Blush velvet:** white, ivory or soft gray linen.
- **Champagne velvet:** nearly any linen, including sequin overlays.

Velvet shows crumbs, pet hair and spills. Keep the chairs in their wrapping until setup, avoid placing them near the cake cutting, and tell your venue they are upholstered. If something spills, blot rather than rub.

## Frequently asked questions

### What color velvet chair is best for a winter wedding?

Navy and emerald. They look rich in low light, pair with silver, brass and evergreen and hide small marks better than lighter tones.

### How much does it cost to rent velvet chairs in Chicago?

Our estimate is $18 to $25 per chair, with the cushion included. Delivery and setup affect the final quote.

### Can velvet chairs go outside?

No. Our velvet chair is indoor-only in the catalog. Use it in a ballroom, loft or a floored, fully covered tent.

### Are velvet chairs comfortable for a long dinner?

Yes. The cushion is included and the upholstered back is more supportive than a wood slat, which is why many planners choose it for long dinners and older guests.

### Do velvet chairs stack?

No. Plan delivery, storage and floor space for each chair separately.

### Which velvet color is the most neutral?

Champagne. It works with white, ivory, sequin and metallic linens and keeps looking right as your palette changes.

### Can I mix two velvet colors?

Yes, if the tones are in the same family, such as navy and emerald or blush and champagne. See [mixing chair styles at one reception](/blog/mixing-chair-styles-at-reception) for rules that keep it looking intentional.
