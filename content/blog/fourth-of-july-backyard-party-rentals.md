**Quick answer**

A July 4 backyard party runs from afternoon to dark, so plan for heat first and fireworks second. Rent a tent or canopy for shade over the food and main seating, seats for 60 to 70 percent of guests, picnic-style or banquet tables, and coolers and drink tubs. For 50 guests that is about 32 chairs, 5 to 6 tables, and a 20 x 30-foot tent. Sunset in Chicago on July 4 is around 8:30 p.m., so light the yard before then.

</div>

The Fourth is the busiest outdoor day in the Chicago area. Everyone with a yard is hosting, tent and table inventory is thin, and the weather is either 90 degrees and humid or a storm cell sliding in from the west. The best plan uses shade as the core, tables that suit food and plates, and a layout that gets everyone comfortable by dark.

## Heat comes first

July afternoons in Chicago can be hot and humid, and a tent without airflow turns into an oven. Choose a tent with sidewalls you can roll up, set fans where they hit seated guests, and put the drinks in a shaded corner. Our [guide to cooling a tent in summer](/blog/cooling-a-tent-in-summer) covers fans and airflow, and [what size tent you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) helps with the footprint. Anchor tents with weights or stakes for gusts that come before storms. If your tent is over the size your village or the City of Chicago regulates, read [Chicago tent permit rules](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and check with your own village.

### Tables and linens
Rectangular tables are handy for burgers, sides and desserts. Picnic-style long tables work well for family seating. A red, white and blue cloth or runner is classic; a plain white cloth with flag napkins is lower effort. Use the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for the right sizes, and tie or clip cloths because July afternoons get breezy.

## Fireworks and local rules

Rules about fireworks vary by town, and many Chicago-area villages have their own limits. Check your village before you plan to shoot anything off. Most hosts stick with watching a local show from the yard, and many suburbs hold displays around July 4, so look at your own village or park district for dates and viewing spots rather than guessing. If you are viewing a public show, ask neighbors about parking and traffic.

## Food safety in the heat

Hot weather makes food planning a rental question too. Cold dishes should sit on ice, not in direct sun, and a good rule is to keep perishable food out of the heat for no more than about two hours. Rent a second table so you can rotate dishes in from the kitchen instead of leaving everything out all afternoon. Chafing dishes keep burgers and sides hot, and shallow trays over ice keep salads cold. Cover everything with lids or mesh domes against flies.

Plan a shaded spot for the grill person, too. A small canopy or the edge of the main tent works, but keep the grill at a safe distance from the fabric. Read the manufacturer's clearances for your tent and grill, and keep the grill outside the tent on a stable surface.

Finally, think about trash. A holiday party with 50 guests creates a lot of cups, plates and bottles. Set out labeled bins for trash and recycling at both the food table and the viewing area, and ask a helper to empty them before dark.

## Frequently asked questions

### What time is sunset on the Fourth of July in Chicago?

Around 8:30 p.m. Local fireworks usually start shortly after dark, so plan lights and chairs for the viewing area by 8 p.m.

### How many chairs do I need for a July 4 party?

About 60 to 70 percent of guests. For 50 guests, rent about 32 chairs, plus your own patio furniture.

### Do I need a tent in July?

Yes for shade and a rain backup. Afternoon storms come up fast in the Chicago area. A 20 x 30-foot tent covers about 50 guests at tables.

### How do I keep guests cool?

Shade, fans and cold drinks, with a misting setup if you have one. Keep the food out of the sun for safety. See our [misting and shade](/blog/misting-and-shade-for-summer-events) guide.

### When should I book rentals for July 4?

As early as you can. The holiday weekend is popular, and tents and tables go first. Four to six weeks is a minimum.

### Can I use fireworks at home?

Check your village. Rules vary, and many places restrict or ban consumer fireworks. Watching a public show is simpler.
