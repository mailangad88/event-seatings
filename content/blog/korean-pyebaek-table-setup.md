**Quick answer**

Pyebaek is a small, family-only Korean wedding ritual where the couple greets the groom's parents, then the bride's parents, with deep bows and ceremonial food. For the setup, plan a low or standard-height table for the food and gifts, a screen or backdrop, cushions or chairs for the parents, and a clear floor area of about 8 x 10 feet. Because the ritual is short and intimate, many couples hold it in a side room of the venue before the reception.

</div>

Pyebaek (pronounced roughly "pyeh-bek") is a traditional ceremony in which a couple honors their parents and receives their blessing. In many families it follows the main wedding ceremony or takes place before the reception, and only close family attend. Couples in Chicago and the suburbs often do it in a quiet room at the venue, or at a family home. The setup is simple, but a few details can make or break it: the table height, the seating for the parents and the floor space for the bows.

## What the ritual needs

Families practice pyebaek differently, and some skip it or adapt it. In general, the ritual involves a table set with symbolic foods, and the couple performs deep bows to each set of parents while wearing traditional hanbok or formal wear. The parents then offer advice and blessings, and sometimes toss dates and chestnuts for the bride to catch. Because customs differ, ask your parents or an elder what to include. Do not assume a single correct version.

## Table height and seating for parents

Traditional pyebaek tables are low, and the parents sit on cushions on the floor. Many families adapt for comfort, especially for older parents or those with knee or hip issues. The usual choices:

- **Low table with floor cushions** for a traditional look. Check that parents can get down and up easily.
- **Standard 30-inch table with chairs** for comfort, using a floor-length cloth.
- **Cocktail or small round table** for a compact setup in a small room.

Ask each parent which they prefer. For chairs, [Louis Medallion chairs](/chairs/louis-medallion) ($20 to $30) or [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) ($18 to $25) look formal and are comfortable. A pair of [Bentwood Bistro chairs](/chairs/bentwood) works for smaller rooms.

## Floor space and room choice

The bows need a clear area of about 8 x 10 feet, plus room for a photographer and a few family members. Fit it into a side room, a bridal suite or a quiet corner. Ask the venue whether a private space is available and for how long.

<li><strong>Choose table height</strong> with both sets of parents.</li>
<li><strong>Arrange seating:</strong> parents beside the table, family along the walls.</li>
<li><strong>Set the backdrop:</strong> a screen, fabric panel or floral display.</li>
<li><strong>Clear the floor</strong> and protect it for the bows.</li>

## The table items

The foods and items on the table are chosen by the families. A cloth, a few small plates or dishes and a decorative cover are typical. Ask your families what they want. Our [place setting guides](/blog/casual-place-setting-diagram) show how to arrange small plates if you rent them.

## Chicago notes

- Many Chicago hotels and banquet halls have a bridal suite or private room that works for pyebaek. Ask early, because rooms are limited.
- If the ritual happens on the same day as the ceremony, tell your coordinator the exact time so photographers and family are ready.
- For a home pyebaek in the suburbs, check parking and entry for the rental delivery.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is pyebaek?

Pyebaek is a Korean wedding custom in which the couple bows to their parents and relatives and receives advice and blessings, often with symbolic foods on a ceremonial table. Families practice it in different ways. Ask your elders what they want to include.

### Does a pyebaek table have to be low?

No. Traditional tables are low and families sit on cushions, but many couples use a standard-height table and chairs for comfort. Ask your parents which they prefer.

### How much space do we need?

Plan about 8 x 10 feet of clear floor for the couple's bows, plus room for the table, parents and a photographer. A room of about 12 x 14 feet is comfortable.

### Who attends pyebaek?

Usually only close family and sometimes a few friends. It is a private ritual, so many couples hold it in a side room rather than the main ballroom.

### Can I rent items just for pyebaek?

Yes. Small tables, chairs and linens are easy to rent for a short ritual. Tell us the room size and how many people will attend and we will help you plan the setup.

### What do I do if the venue has no private room?

Ask if you can use a corner of the ballroom with a screen or a bridal suite. Some couples hold it before guests arrive.
