**Quick answer**

Ukrainian wedding receptions often feature a decorated bread (korovai), embroidered towels or linens, a head table for the couple and parents, many family tables and a lot of dancing. Plan rounds of 8 to 10, a separate display table for the korovai and bread, a dance floor of about 20 x 20 feet for 150 guests and a sweets table. Customs vary by region and family, and many Chicago families blend them with American traditions, so ask your parents what to keep.

Chicago is home to Ukrainian Village and a long-standing Ukrainian-American community, with churches, cultural centers and banquet halls across the city and suburbs. A Ukrainian wedding reception often follows a church ceremony and runs late into the night. Because the families usually contribute food, symbols and relatives, the layout has to make room for a lot of people and a few special tables.

## Elements that affect the layout

Customs vary, but several features show up often:

- **Korovai.** A large decorated wedding bread, sometimes displayed on a special table with embroidered cloth. It needs a sturdy table and a prominent spot.
- **Rushnyk.** Embroidered ritual towels, used in ceremony and decor. A table or stand may hold them.
- **Parents' welcome.** Parents sometimes greet the couple with bread and salt at the entrance.
- **Long toast sessions.** Guests often make many toasts, so chairs need to pull out easily and the room needs clear sightlines.
- **Dancing.** Traditional and modern music fills the floor, and many families dance together in lines and circles.

Ask your family and your church what to include, and share the plan with your venue early.

A korovai needs a table about 30 to 36 inches wide, with a cloth that matches or complements the embroidered pieces. A 48-inch round with a floor-length cloth works. Check the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for the right size. Keep the display table away from foot traffic so the bread is safe from bumps.

## Long dinners need comfortable chairs

Ukrainian receptions often include multiple courses and long toasts. That makes chair comfort important. [Gold Infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity) ($9 to $13 estimated) stack well for banquet halls, [Louis Medallion chairs](/chairs/louis-medallion) ($20 to $30) suit the head table and [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) ($8 to $11) pair well with embroidered linens and a rustic look. Choose cushions for elders or fill the room with chairs that have padded seats.

## Linens and decor

Embroidery, red, black and white are traditional colors in Ukrainian design, but you can use any palette your family prefers. Neutral linens let patterned items stand out, and a runner with embroidery or a rushnyk draped across the head table adds a cultural note. For runner sizing see [table runner sizes](/blog/table-runner-sizes-and-styles), and for layering see [overlays and layered linens](/blog/overlays-and-layered-linens).

## Church ceremony notes

Many Ukrainian couples marry in a church, and the church provides its own seating or pews. If your ceremony is at a venue, ask your priest what is needed for the crowning and the ritual walk. Ceremony chairs should leave a clear central aisle and space for the wedding party.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a korovai?

A korovai is a decorated wedding bread, often a symbol of the family and community. Families make or buy it and display it at the wedding. Ask your family how it is used in your celebration.

### Do I need a special table for it?

A sturdy table with a cloth in a visible but low-traffic spot works. A 48-inch round or a 6-foot banquet table both fit. Keep it away from the dance floor.

### How many guests sit at a table?

Most Ukrainian receptions seat 8 to 10 at each round. Ten per table fits more guests but leaves less room for platters.

### How big should the dance floor be?

About 5 square feet per dancing guest. For 150 guests, plan about 20 x 20 feet and lean larger if your family dances in a circle.

### What chairs suit a long evening?

Padded, stackable chairs like Gold Infinity chairs, or head table chairs like the Louis Medallion. Consider seat comfort for long dinners.

### Can I rent items for a church hall reception?

Yes. Many church halls have basic tables and chairs. Ask what the hall provides and whether it allows outside rentals.
