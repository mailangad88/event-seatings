**Quick answer**

Plan about 12 square feet per guest for a seated dinner at round tables, and about 15 square feet per guest if you add a dance floor, bar and buffet inside the same tent. That means a 20 x 30 ft tent (600 sq ft) for about 50 seated guests, a 30 x 60 ft tent (1,800 sq ft) for about 150, and a 40 x 60 ft tent (2,400 sq ft) for about 200. Add a separate tent or space for cooking and catering prep.

</div>

Picking a tent from the guest count alone is how people end up with chairs touching the tent walls and servers squeezing sideways between tables. Tent size depends on what happens under the roof: dinner only, dinner plus dancing, or a standing cocktail party. This guide gives you the rule-of-thumb numbers planners use, a chart for the common tent sizes, and a way to adjust for your own plan. These are industry rules of thumb, so confirm the final footprint with your tent company.

## Start with square feet per guest

Tent sizes are quoted in feet (20 x 30) and the area is simply width times length. What you divide that area by depends on how people use the space.

Numbers round down on purpose. A tent that is slightly too big is a nice problem. One that is slightly too small means moving a table out onto wet grass.

## What eats up space besides tables

Guests are the easy part. These are the items that make a 100-person party need a tent built for 150:

- **Dance floor:** Plan about 3 to 4.5 square feet per dancer, and assume only a third to half your guests dance at once. A 16 x 16 floor is a common size for 100 guests. See [how to lay out a tent with a dance floor and bar](/blog/tent-layout-dance-floor-and-bar).
- **Bar:** A single 6-foot bar needs the bar itself plus room for a line. Budget roughly 8 x 12 feet.
- **Buffet:** Buffet lines need access from both sides in tight rooms, and a 10-foot clear lane for the queue.
- **DJ or band:** A DJ table plus speakers takes about 6 x 10 feet. A band needs a stage and more.
- **Cake, gift and guest book tables:** Allow one 6-foot table each.
- **Aisles:** Main aisles of at least 5 feet and 36 inches between chair backs are common planning minimums.

## Do the sum for your own event

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Count guests</strong> and add 5 to 10 percent for late RSVPs and plus-ones.</li>
<li><strong>Pick the style:</strong> 12 sq ft each for seated, 15 for seated with dancing, 7 for cocktail.</li>
<li><strong>Add fixed items:</strong> bar, band, buffet and cake tables from the list above.</li>
<li><strong>Find the area</strong> and pick the next stock size up.</li>
<li><strong>Check the lawn:</strong> the tent also needs room outside for stakes or ballast and for guests to walk in.</li>
</ol>

A worked example: 80 guests, seated, with a dance floor and a bar. 80 x 15 = 1,200 square feet. The next stock sizes up are 30 x 40 (1,200 exactly) or 30 x 45. With fixed items, most planners would pick 30 x 45 or 30 x 60 for comfort.

## Chicago-area notes

Backyards in the city and inner suburbs are often smaller than they look, and a 30 x 60 tent needs far more than 1,800 square feet of yard once you count the working space around it. Read our guide on [how much space you need around a tent](/blog/space-around-a-tent) before you commit. Also check permits early: in the City of Chicago, tents over 400 square feet need a building permit, and many suburbs use a similar threshold. Our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) has the details.

Under the tent, the seating matters as much as the roof. Count chairs at 1 per guest plus a few spares, and choose a style that suits the setting. Our [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) work well on grass, and [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) suit a rustic tent. For help counting, see [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### How big of a tent do I need for 100 guests?

For a seated dinner, plan about 1,200 square feet, which is a 30 x 40 tent. If you add a dance floor, bar and buffet, a 30 x 60 tent (1,800 sq ft) is more comfortable. Tight is possible, but guests notice it.

### What size tent do I need for 50 people?

A 20 x 30 tent (600 sq ft) fits about 50 seated guests at rounds. If you want a dance floor, move up to 20 x 40 or 30 x 30. A 20 x 20 works only for a cocktail-style party of around 50.

### How many square feet per person do I need under a tent?

Use 10 to 12 square feet for a seated dinner at round tables and 14 to 16 with a dance floor. Standing cocktail parties need 6 to 8. These are rules of thumb, so your tent company's recommendation for your exact setup should win.

### Is it better to get a bigger tent?

Usually yes, within your yard and budget. A larger tent gives room for service, a bar and a dance floor, and it handles guest counts that grow. The tradeoff is a higher rental cost, a bigger permit footprint and more yard needed.

### Do I need a bigger tent if I add a dance floor?

Yes, add the dance floor area to the tent. A 16 x 16 floor adds 256 square feet. Dancers also need walking room around it, so most planners add 20 to 25 percent to the base tent size.

### Can I hold the cooking in the same tent as the guests?

Usually no. Codes in many places keep cooking and open flame away from guest tents, and caterers prefer a separate prep tent. Ask your caterer and your village or the City of Chicago before you plan around it.
