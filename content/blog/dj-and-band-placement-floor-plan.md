**Quick answer**

Put the DJ or band at one end of the dance floor, facing the room, at least 8 to 10 feet from the nearest table and away from the bar. A DJ needs about 6 x 4 feet plus power, a four-piece band about 12 x 10 feet and a six-piece about 16 x 12 feet. Ask your entertainer for their exact footprint and power needs.

</div>

The DJ or band sets the mood for the evening, and its position determines how the room sounds. A speaker aimed at a table makes conversation impossible; a DJ tucked behind the bar kills the dance floor. The goal is a spot where the sound goes toward the dance floor, the entertainers can see the room and cables stay out of the walkways.

## Footprints

| Setup | Footprint | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| DJ with two speakers | 6 x 4 ft table plus 2 speaker stands | 1 to 2 outlets |
| DJ with lights and booth | 8 x 6 ft | Tall stands; ceiling clearance |
| Four-piece band | 12 x 10 ft | Drums, bass, guitar, keys, plus vocals |
| Six-piece band | 16 x 12 ft | With horns or extra keys |
| Eight-piece or larger | 20 x 14 ft or more | Often a riser |

These are approximations. Always ask your DJ or band for a technical rider with exact size, speaker positions and power needs, then add it to your floor plan before ordering tables. A band may also need a riser; see [stage and riser sizes for events](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events).

## Rules for placement

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Face the dance floor.</strong> Speakers aim at the floor and away from tables.</li>
<li><strong>Stay 10 feet from tables.</strong> Closer than that, guests cannot talk.</li>
<li><strong>Keep away from the bar.</strong> The bar queue needs quiet and open floor.</li>
<li><strong>Near power.</strong> Plan outlets or a generator; avoid extension cords across aisles.</li>
<li><strong>Visible entrance.</strong> The entertainer should see the doorway for introductions.</li>
</ol>

Also consider the room's shape. In a rectangular ballroom, place the band on a short end so the sound spreads along the long axis. In a tent, put the DJ at the end away from the entrance. Do not point speakers at glass walls or hard surfaces that bounce sound. See [dance floor placement in a reception layout](/blog/dance-floor-placement-reception-layout) and [dance floor size by guest count](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count).

## Sound level and neighbors

The farther the tables are from the speakers, the quieter the room feels, but you also need enough sound for guests at the dance floor. Chicago and suburban venues sometimes have decibel limits or end times for amplified music; ask your venue and, for outdoor events, your village or park district. Keep the toast microphone separate from the DJ if you can; see [sound system for speeches](/blog/sound-system-for-speeches) and [microphone types for events](/blog/microphone-types-for-events).

## Sightlines and the first dance

Think about the moments the room will watch: the grand entrance, the first dance, the toasts and the cake cutting. The entertainer should have a clear view of the door and the head table, and guests should have a clear view of the dance floor from every table. If a band's stage blocks the view, move the first dance to the middle of the floor and set a few chairs along the edge for older guests who want to watch. A DJ table placed against a wall also gives you a place to hang a banner or a sign without cluttering the floor.

Lighting belongs in the same conversation. Uplights and a spotlight need stands, which take space near the stage, and a haze machine needs a clear area. Ask your entertainer which of these they bring and whether they need a separate power run. Our [LED dance floor lighting guide](/blog/led-dance-floor-lighting) explains what each type does.

## Power

A DJ rig needs one or two standard outlets, while a band may need a dedicated circuit or more. Ask your venue where the outlets are and how many amps are on each circuit. In a tent, plan a generator, away from guests and the dance floor. See [event power needs checklist](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist), [generator size for events](/blog/generator-size-for-events) and [outdoor extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events).

## Seating the entertainers

Bands and DJs often need a table for equipment, a few chairs and a meal. Add them to your chair count and your catering count; see [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need). A bar stool or a [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) works for a DJ; a band may bring its own. Do not forget a chair for the emcee or the photographer.

## Cocktail hour music

If you have a live band, consider a smaller group or a solo musician for cocktail hour, placed near the bar or the entrance. Keep the band's gear on stage, and let guests walk past it. If you are flipping the room between phases, see [ceremony to reception room flip layout](/blog/ceremony-to-reception-room-flip-layout). ## Frequently asked questions

### Where should the DJ be placed at a wedding?

At one end of the dance floor, facing the room, with speakers aimed at the floor and at least 8 to 10 feet from the nearest table.

### How much space does a band need?

A four-piece band needs about 12 x 10 feet, a six-piece about 16 x 12 feet. Ask the band for its rider.

### Should the DJ be near the bar?

No. Keep them apart to avoid noise at the bar and crowding on the dance floor.

### Do bands need a stage?

Not always, but a riser helps sightlines for groups of five or more. See our stage and riser sizes guide.

### How much power does a DJ need?

Usually one or two standard outlets. Ask your DJ for the exact requirement.

### Can I put the DJ in the middle of the room?

It is possible in a large room, but it takes space and cables are harder to hide. Most events put the DJ at an end.
