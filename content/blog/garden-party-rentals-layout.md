**Quick answer**

For a garden party, choose light chairs with wide feet that will not sink in soft grass, mix long and round tables, and add shade over the main seating. Plan one chair per guest for a seated tea or lunch, and about 60 percent for a mingling party. For 40 guests that is 24 to 40 chairs, 5 round tables of 8 or 3 long tables, and a 20 x 30-foot tent or canopy for shade.

</div>

A garden party borrows the best of an outdoor wedding and a backyard barbecue: soft light, lots of flowers and a slower pace. It also carries a few risks that an indoor party does not, like soft ground, wasps near the dessert, and a guest in heels walking across a lawn. The right rentals make the lawn work for you.

## Pick the right chair for grass

The most common garden party mistake is a chair with narrow legs. They sink into soft lawn and tip. Look for wider feet, a lighter frame or a chair with a flat base.

- [Rattan garden chairs](/chairs/rattan-garden) have a wide stance and a soft look.
- [Garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding), estimated at $5 to $7, are practical and easy to move.
- [Cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back), $8 to $11, are sturdier but can sink on very soft ground.
- [Ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost), $10 to $14, suit modern gardens and look airy against plants, but the thin legs need firm ground.

On soft ground, use plywood or ground mats beneath chairs. For details, read [chairs on grass, sand and gravel](/blog/chairs-on-grass-sand-gravel).

## Shade, bugs and breeze

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Find the afternoon shadow</strong> before you place the tent. Trees shift the shade by hour.</li>
<li><strong>Use a tent or canopy</strong> at least over the food and half the seating. See [what size tent you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).</li>
<li><strong>Cover food and drinks</strong> with domes, lids or mesh to keep wasps away.</li>
<li><strong>Weigh down linens and menus</strong> with clips or small stones.</li>

## Chicago-area notes

Public gardens in the Chicago area, such as the Morton Arboretum and the Chicago Botanic Garden, have their own event policies, so contact them directly about private events and outside rentals instead of assuming. At a private garden, check with your village about tent size rules and neighbors about noise. Spring and fall bring the most comfortable temperatures, while July and August call for fans and extra shade. Our [rattan chair guide](/blog/rattan-chairs-garden-party) shows more garden styling ideas. For evening light, use [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events).

## Food and drink that suit the setting

Garden parties lean on light, grazing food: finger sandwiches, fruit, cheese, scones and a few warm dishes. Set the tea and dessert table within sight of the seating, with plates stacked on the near side and platters on the far side. Cake stands and risers add height without taking more table space.

Offer one non-alcoholic option that looks as special as the rest, such as a pitcher of lemonade or iced tea with fruit. A beverage dispenser holds more than a pitcher and keeps the table neat; see [how many beverage dispensers you need](/blog/how-many-beverage-dispensers-do-i-need). Put ice in a separate bin, not in the dispenser, so drinks do not get watered down.

Glassware matters outdoors. Glass looks better, but plastic is safer on hard surfaces, near pools and in parks. Pick one or the other for each zone and tell guests where to find them. Finally, consider restrooms: if guests will walk through the house, put down a runner and a sign so they find the way without wandering through bedrooms.

## Frequently asked questions

### What chairs are best for a garden party?

Chairs with a wide base and light frame, like rattan garden or garden folding chairs. Thin-legged chairs can sink on soft lawn.

### How many chairs do I need for a garden party?

One per guest for a seated tea or lunch, and about 60 percent for a mingling party. For 40 guests, that is 24 to 40.

### Do I need a tent for a garden party?

Not always, but shade and rain cover make a big difference. A tent over the tables is the most useful.

### How do I protect the lawn?

Use ground mats under chairs, avoid dragging tables and keep heavy equipment on paths. Ask your rental company about delivery routes.

### What tables work best?

Rounds of 6 or 8 for lunch, long tables for a feast and cocktail tables for mingling. Mix styles for a garden look.

### What if it rains?

Have a tent over the main seating or a plan to move indoors. Tell guests the plan in the invitation.
