## Chairs for grass, gravel and barn floors

Soft ground changes the chair choice.

- **Turf.** Wide-footed wooden chairs, like the [Windsor spindle](/chairs/windsor) and [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back), are the stable choice. Use foot pads if heels sink.
- **Gravel or paver paths.** Keep ceremony aisles on firm ground or add an aisle runner.
- **Barn floors.** Old plank floors can be uneven, and chairs may wobble. Ask for a walk-through.

## Questions to ask a Barrington venue

Country venues vary a lot, so get specifics.

1. **Does the venue include tables and chairs?** Some do, some do not.
2. **Can a tent go up, and who handles permits?** Villages and counties differ.
3. **What power is available?** A DJ, caterer and lights all need outlets.
4. **Where can trucks park?** Long gravel drives can limit large trucks.
5. **What is the rain plan?** Moving 120 guests indoors takes planning.
6. **What are the noise and end times?** Some country properties have quiet rules.

Do not assume a rule from another property applies here. Ask the venue and the local government where the property sits, and note that Barrington touches several counties, so the right office depends on the address.

## Delivery to country properties

Long driveways and soft lawns affect delivery. Ask us to plan with these in mind:

- A delivery window early enough to set up before the caterer.
- A staging area near the tent, not a quarter-mile away.
- A contact who can open gates.
- A plan for pickup if guests stay late.

## Lighting and power on country property

Rural and large-lot properties often have limited outlets, and they may be far from the tent. Ask the owner where the circuits are and how much they can carry, because a DJ, a caterer and string lights together can overload one outlet. If the property cannot supply enough power, a generator may be needed; place it away from guests, and ask a professional about safe cord routing across grass. Lighting is a safety item as well as a mood item. Path lights at the parking area, the restroom route and the tent entrance keep guests safe when the sun sets. In Chicago-area summers, sunset is roughly 8:30 pm near the longest day, and much earlier in fall, so plan lighting for the time of year you pick.

## Staying flexible on the day

Weather can change in an afternoon. Have a decision time written down, such as noon on the day, to choose between the lawn and the tent. Keep a few extra chairs in the truck for guests who arrive unexpectedly, and ask your delivery contact to stay reachable on the day. A small contingency often makes the difference between a calm setup and a rush.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs for a 120-guest Barrington wedding?

About 126 chairs, which is 120 guests plus 5% spares. If the ceremony is on-site, you can often move the same chairs to the reception, as long as you plan the flip.

### Are wooden chairs good on grass?

Yes, wide-footed wood chairs are more stable than slim metal chairs on soft ground. Foot pads help in damp conditions.

### Do I need a tent permit in Barrington?

Possibly, depending on the size and the address. Ask the village or county that covers your property, and read our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) for the types of questions to ask.

### What table size is best for a barn?

Rounds of 60 or 72 inches are common, and long farm tables suit rustic barns. Pick by the room width and the way you want guests to interact.

### Can chairs go on a gravel driveway?

It is possible but not ideal. Use plywood or mats under chair legs for ceremonies on gravel, and ask the property owner.

### How do I plan for rain?

Choose a venue with an indoor backup, or add sidewalls to your tent. Decide the rain call time in advance.
