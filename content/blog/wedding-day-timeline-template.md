**Quick answer**

Build your wedding day timeline backward from the ceremony time. For a 5 p.m. ceremony, plan hair and makeup from about 11 a.m., photos and getting dressed early afternoon, a 20 to 30 minute ceremony, a one-hour cocktail hour, dinner around 6:30, toasts and dances during and after dinner, and cake before the send-off. Add 10 to 15 minute buffers between major moments.

</div>

A wedding day runs on a handful of fixed points: ceremony, dinner, first dance, and the venue's end time. Everything else floats. The couples who feel relaxed on the day usually have a one-page timeline that every vendor has seen, with buffers built in for the things that always run late: hair, family photos and getting guests into the room.

## Start with the fixed points

Write down what cannot move: the ceremony start, the time you must vacate the venue and any vendor limits such as the band's set length or the caterer's service window. Then work backward from the ceremony. Subtract travel time, photos, getting dressed and hair and makeup. Then work forward through cocktail hour, dinner, dances and the end of the night.

If your ceremony and reception share a room, the cocktail hour is also when staff flip the room. See [ceremony to reception flip](/blog/ceremony-to-reception-flip) for how that works and how much time to allow. If they are in different places, add travel time and a small buffer for guests to find parking.

## Morning and afternoon

A full wedding party often takes four to six hours for hair and makeup, so start earlier than you think. Plan a quiet lunch, because people forget to eat. Schedule detail photos while hair is being finished. Schedule a first look if you want one, and group photos before the ceremony so you can join cocktail hour. Allow extra time if the group is large or the locations are separate.

Ask your photographer how long they need for each set of photos, and then add a few extra minutes. Family photos are the most common source of delay, so prepare a written list of groups with names.

## Ceremony, cocktail hour and dinner

Guests should be seated about 15 to 20 minutes before the ceremony starts. Plan a short, calm window between guest arrival and the processional. After the ceremony, cocktail hour usually lasts about an hour, with bar, appetizers and seating for older guests. See [cocktail hour rentals](/blog/cocktail-hour-rentals-wedding) for how to set up. Dinner service varies: plated meals take longer than buffets, and large buffets need time for table-by-table release.

Schedule toasts during or right after the first course so people are attentive but not too hungry. Limit speeches to a few minutes each and to a short list of people.

## Dancing, cake and the send-off

After dinner, the dance floor opens with first dances and then the party. Cut the cake once guests have settled in, usually an hour or two after dinner begins. Plan a bouquet or garter moment only if you want one. A last dance and send-off at the venue's end time closes the evening. Leave 30 to 45 minutes for vendors to pack and for rentals to be gathered. For late nights, see [wedding after-party rentals](/blog/wedding-after-party-late-night-rentals).

Keep a printed copy of the timeline with the photographer, DJ, caterer and coordinator. A shared plan prevents each vendor from running their own clock.

## A full timeline you can copy

Here is a sample schedule for a 5 p.m. ceremony and an 11 p.m. venue end time. Change the times to fit your day.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Time | What happens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 11:00 a.m. | Hair and makeup begin | Order the lunch |
| 1:30 p.m. | Get dressed, detail photos | Photographer arrives |
| 2:30 p.m. | First look and portraits | Before guests arrive |
| 3:30 p.m. | Wedding party and family photos | Use a written group list |
| 4:30 p.m. | Guests seated | Music starts |
| 5:00 p.m. | Ceremony | About 20 to 30 minutes |
| 5:30 p.m. | Cocktail hour, room flip | Photos continue |
| 6:30 p.m. | Grand entrance, first course | Toasts begin |
| 7:30 p.m. | Dinner, first dances | Keep speeches short |
| 8:30 p.m. | Open dancing, cake cut | Bar stays open |
| 10:30 p.m. | Last dance, send-off | Vendors start packing |
| 11:00 p.m. | Venue end | Rentals ready for pickup |

</div>

Add a column for who is responsible, such as coordinator, photographer or DJ, and share the sheet a week before the wedding.

## How to build your own timeline

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>List the fixed points.</strong> Ceremony, dinner, venue end time.</li>
<li><strong>Work backward for getting ready.</strong> Add hair, makeup, photos and travel.</li>
<li><strong>Work forward for the reception.</strong> Cocktail hour, dinner, toasts, dancing.</li>
<li><strong>Add buffers.</strong> 10 to 15 minutes between big moments.</li>
<li><strong>Share the one-page plan.</strong> Give it to every vendor and the wedding party.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago has sunset and traffic realities that shape timelines. In winter, sunset can be before 5 p.m., so outdoor photos need to happen early. In summer, outdoor ceremonies can feel hot in the afternoon and golden at evening. Downtown traffic, construction and weekend events can add travel time, so give the wedding party extra buffer, and plan the transportation between ceremony and reception. Some Chicago-area venues require vendors to load in during limited windows, so ask for the load-in and load-out times before you finalize the schedule. Rental deliveries and pickups also need to fit around your timeline.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a wedding ceremony be?

Many ceremonies run 20 to 30 minutes, and religious ceremonies can be longer. Ask your officiant for an estimate and add time for the processional and recessional.

### How long should cocktail hour be?

About an hour is common. It gives you time to take photos, flip the room if needed, and give guests drinks and snacks before dinner.

### What time should a wedding reception start?

It depends on your ceremony. Many receptions start 30 to 60 minutes after the ceremony ends, with cocktail hour first and dinner after.

### How long does hair and makeup take?

For a full wedding party it can take four to six hours. Ask your stylist for a start time and add a buffer, since late starts push everything back.

### When should I do wedding photos?

Many couples do portraits and group photos before the ceremony, so they can enjoy cocktail hour. Others do a first look. Ask your photographer how long they need.

### How do I handle a late schedule on the day?

Cut or shorten flexible items like cake cutting or extra dances rather than dinner. Make sure a coordinator or trusted person has the timeline.

### Who should get a copy of the wedding timeline?

Share it with your photographer, DJ or band, caterer, venue manager, planner or coordinator and wedding party. Our [run of show guide](/blog/event-run-of-show-how-to-write-one) explains how to format it.
