**Quick answer**

Start a class reunion 9 to 12 months ahead. Form a small committee, find classmates through social media and school records, set a ticket price that covers all costs, and book a venue about 6 to 9 months out. Expect around 25 to 40 percent of your class to attend. Keep the program light, with name tags, music, food and time to talk.

Reunions are hard to plan because you do not know who is coming until the last minute, and you have to collect money from people you have not talked to in decades. Success depends on starting early, using a small committee and keeping the schedule simple. The reward is a night where people who shared a hallway get to catch up.

## Form a small committee

Five or six people can run a reunion. Assign clear roles: chair, treasurer, venue and food, communications, program and decor, and a classmate locator. Meet monthly at first, then every two weeks as the date gets closer. Keep a shared document with the guest list, budget and tasks. The [guest list and RSVP tracker](/downloads/guest-list-rsvp-tracker) works well for this.

## Find your classmates

Start with the names you have. Use social media groups, alumni associations and school records. Ask classmates to help find others. Set up a simple email or group page with updates. Include a short form so people can update their contact info and let you know if they plan to come. Ask for a favorite memory or a photo, and plan to use them in the slideshow.

## Pick a date and a format

Most reunions happen in late summer or fall, when travel is easier and homecoming weekends bring people back. Avoid long holiday weekends. A Saturday evening is usual. Format choices include:

**Dinner and dancing.** A banquet hall or restaurant room with a DJ. Best for 60 or more.

**Casual picnic.** A park pavilion with a cookout and lawn games. Good for a lower ticket price and family-friendly turnout.

**Cocktail party or bar night.** A private room at a bar or brewery. See [brewery and winery event venues](/blog/brewery-and-winery-event-venues-chicago).

**Weekend of events.** A casual Friday meetup, the main dinner on Saturday and a Sunday brunch.

## Set the budget and ticket price

List all costs: venue, food, drinks, DJ, photographer, decor, name tags, printing, and a small cushion. Divide by the number of paying guests. Many committees set the ticket price slightly above the cost per person to cover no-shows. Offer an early-bird price and a deadline. Consider covering costs with a sponsor or a raffle. Keep the money in a separate account and report it openly.

Attendance varies with how long it has been since graduation, how well you reach people, and the date and price. Use these as a starting range, then update as RSVPs come in.

## Venue and layout

Choose a space that allows conversation. Name tags with yearbook photos are a hit. Set up a registration table at the entrance with name tags, the schedule and a table for memorabilia. Leave room for mingling. A good layout has seating for about 60 to 70 percent of guests, lounge areas and a dance floor. See [floor plan for 100 guests](/blog/floor-plan-for-100-guests) and [dance floor size by guest count](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count).

## Food and drink

Appetizers and a buffet or stations work well, because people move around. See [food station ideas and equipment](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment). Add a cash bar or tickets for drinks. Include a non-alcoholic option. Remember the 'Class of' sheet cake or cupcakes.

## Program

Keep the program short: a welcome, a moment of silence for lost classmates, a slideshow and a few awards or fun trivia. Avoid long speeches. Play music from your high school years. Provide a photo wall or a yearbook table.

## Communication

Send updates by email, social media and a printed postcard to those who are offline. Tell people the date early, even before the venue is set. Remind them about the ticket deadline.

## Day-of

Arrive early to set up. Have a team at the registration table. Take a group photo early in the evening, before people leave. Collect leftover supplies and money counts at the end. The [run of show template](/downloads/run-of-show-template) keeps the evening on schedule.

## Chicago-area notes

For Chicago-area reunions, many classmates now live in other states, so pick a date that works for travel, such as homecoming weekend or a summer holiday weekend. Banquet halls, VFW or Legion halls, park district buildings, restaurants and breweries are common choices. Parking is a key issue in city venues, so plan how guests will park and how to reach the venue by transit. For suburban park districts, check with your village or park district for reservation dates, deposits and alcohol rules. Winter reunions need coat space, and summer outdoor reunions need shade and rain plans. See [suburban park district pavilion rentals](/blog/suburban-park-district-pavilion-rentals).

## Frequently asked questions

### How far in advance should I plan a class reunion?

Nine to twelve months is ideal. This gives you time to find classmates, book a venue and sell tickets.

### How much should I charge for a class reunion ticket?

Add up all costs, divide by the expected paying guests, and add a small cushion. Many committees offer an early-bird price. Prices vary by venue and food style, so get quotes first.

### What percentage of a class attends a reunion?

Attendance varies, but many planners expect roughly 25 to 40 percent of the class. Plan for a range and update as tickets sell.

### How do I find my classmates?

Use social media groups, alumni associations, school records and word of mouth. Ask each classmate to name three others.

### What should I do at a class reunion?

Offer name tags, a photo slideshow, music from your school years, food, a short program and time to talk. Take a group photo early.

### How long should a class reunion last?

Three to four hours for a dinner event. For a weekend, add a casual Friday gathering and a Sunday brunch.

### What is a good venue for a class reunion?

A banquet hall, restaurant private room, park pavilion, brewery or school gym can work. Choose by size, price and ease of parking.
