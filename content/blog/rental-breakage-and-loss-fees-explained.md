**Quick answer**

Rental companies commonly charge per piece for items that come back broken, chipped or missing, though the terms differ by company. Some build a small breakage allowance into the price; others bill damage after the return count. To keep the bill low, count pieces at delivery, keep crates covered, set aside broken pieces for the final count and read the contract for what counts as damage. We do not publish dinnerware fees; ask each company for its schedule in writing.

</div>

The first rental invoice often looks fine and the second one, after the return count, has extra lines. Replacement for four plates, two missing forks, one chipped cup. It is not a trick. Rental inventory is expensive and each piece is expected to come back. What you can do is understand the system so the final bill matches what you expected. This guide uses Chicago-area events as examples, but the rules apply anywhere.

## Three kinds of charge

**Breakage.** A piece is broken or cracked beyond use. Most companies charge a replacement amount per piece.

**Loss.** A piece does not come back. Plates left at a venue, flatware thrown out with napkins and glasses taken as souvenirs all count.

**Damage or soil.** A piece is returned chipped, stained, burned or too dirty to wash. This depends on the contract's definition of "normal wear."

## What the contract should say

Read these items before signing:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Term | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Breakage allowance | Is a small percentage included? |
| Replacement price | Is it listed per piece? |
| Damage definition | What counts as normal wear? |
| Count deadline | How long after the event is the count final? |
| Cleaning charge | What is "excessive soil"? |
| Insurance or waiver | Is there an optional damage waiver? |
| Dispute process | How do you challenge a count? |

</div>

If the contract does not answer, send the questions by email so you have it in writing. Reading policy is part of picking a company, and our [china rental guide](/blog/china-rental-guide-what-pieces-exist) covers other ways to compare.

## Count at delivery

When crates arrive, count them with the driver. Note the number of crates and the number of pieces on the sheet, and check for chips and cracks on arrival. Take photos of anything damaged. If you wait until the event day, you cannot prove a piece was already damaged. This only takes ten minutes. Put the count sheet where the caterer can find it.

## Reduce loss on event day

- **Assign one person** to watch the crates.
- **Ask the caterer to scrape** into dedicated bins, not into bags with flatware.
- **Count flatware** at teardown before trash goes out.
- **Cover crates** if guests or kids might wander off with glasses.
- **Keep disposable plates** for late-night snacks that walk onto the dance floor.

See [do you wash rental dishes](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-dishes) for the cleanup flow.

## When to buy a waiver

Some companies offer an optional damage waiver. It may cover accidental breakage but not loss. Whether it is worth it depends on the group. For a large party with drinking, kids or tight venues, the waiver may be a good trade. For a small home dinner, it may not. Ask for the waiver terms in writing, including what it excludes.

## Who is responsible?

The renter signs the contract, so the renter pays. If a venue or caterer handles the dishes, the contract between you and them should say who is responsible for breakage. Do not assume the caterer is covering it. Ask the caterer for the language. Our [catering equipment topic page](/blog/topic/catering-equipment) has other planning guides.

## Dispute a count

If the return count says 12 plates are missing and you counted all of them, ask for the count sheet and the pickup photos. Reasonable companies will compare both. Keep your delivery sheet and photos for at least a few weeks after the event.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who pays for broken rental dishes?

The renter named on the contract pays unless the contract says otherwise. If a caterer or venue handles the dishes, ask them to confirm who is responsible in writing.

### What does a breakage fee cover?

It covers replacing pieces that are cracked, chipped or shattered beyond use. Some companies also charge for stained or burned pieces. Terms differ, so read yours.

### Do rental companies charge for missing flatware?

Yes, commonly, because small pieces are the easiest to lose. Count flatware at teardown and watch the trash.

### Is a damage waiver worth it?

It can be for large, busy parties. Check what it excludes, because most waivers cover accidents but not loss. Ask for the terms before you decide.

### How do I avoid extra charges?

Count at delivery, guard the crates, scrape and stack properly, and count at teardown. Photograph any damage you see.

### Can I dispute a damage charge?

Yes. Ask for the count sheet and photos, and compare them with your own notes. A clear delivery sheet makes disputes quicker.
