A sip and see answers a common problem for new parents: everyone wants to visit, but the parents are tired and the baby is tiny. By gathering visitors in one low-key event, the parents can introduce the baby, accept well wishes and then get back to rest. The planning is lighter than a shower, but the details around health, space and timing matter more.

## What a sip and see is

A sip and see is a casual open house after the baby arrives. Guests "sip" a drink and "see" the baby. Unlike a shower, it is not built around gifts or games. Hosts may be grandparents, friends or the parents themselves. It is often relaxed enough to hold at home or in a small rented room.

## Timing and length

Wait until the parents feel ready, usually two to eight weeks after the birth, and keep the guest list flexible in case plans shift. An afternoon open house of two to three hours lets guests drop in without a strict schedule. Mid-afternoon often works well around a baby's nap schedule. Choose a day that is not cold or rainy if you can, and have a backup plan.

## Health and comfort

Newborns are fragile, so set simple, kind expectations. Ask guests to stay home if they are sick. Put a hand-washing station or sanitizer by the door. Ask for vaccines to be up to date if the parents want that, and let guests know in the invitation. Keep the baby in a calm area, away from loud music, and let the parents decide who holds the baby. A sign at the door is an easy way to share the rules without feeling awkward.

## Who to invite

Keep the list smaller than a shower. Family, close friends and a few neighbors work well. Include kids only if the parents are comfortable. Ask the parents for names and any health concerns.

## Create a quiet room

A bedroom or side room with a comfortable chair, a changing station and a closed door gives the parents a break. Tell guests it is off-limits. Provide a small table with water and snacks for the parents.

## Plan by guest count

<div class="table-wrap">

| Guests | Space | Seats | Tables | Food style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 to 15 | Living room | 8 to 12 | 1 to 2 | Coffee and pastries |
| 15 to 30 | Home plus patio | 12 to 20 | 3 to 4 | Brunch bites, dessert |
| 30 to 50 | Backyard or small hall | 20 to 30 | 5 to 6 | Stations |
| 50 or more | Hall or tented yard | 30 to 40 | 8 or more | Catered, with a quiet room |

</div>

Open house guests come and go, so seats for about half to two thirds of guests is common. Add extra for grandparents.

## Food and drinks

A sip and see needs less food than a shower. Think coffee and tea, juice, a few bites such as pastries, mini sandwiches or fruit, and a cake or dessert. Keep food easy to eat while holding a drink. Have a few allergen-friendly options. See [how much food per person](/blog/how-much-food-per-person-for-a-party) and [coffee and tea service rental](/blog/coffee-and-tea-service-rental).

## Seating and layout

Create small seating groups so guests can chat without crowding the baby. Put chairs near the parents, and keep walkways clear for strollers and car seats. Set up a table for guests to sign a guest book or leave a note. See [guest book alternatives](/blog/guest-book-alternatives-for-weddings) and [high chairs and baby station at events](/blog/high-chairs-and-baby-station-at-events).

## Decor and photos

Keep decor light: a welcome sign, a few flowers and a simple balloon arrangement. A photo spot with natural light is better than a flash. Remind guests that the parents decide which photos can be shared online.

## Gifts

Gifts are optional at a sip and see. Many invitations simply say "no gifts needed." If there is a registry, share it only if asked. Provide a basket for cards.

## Pets, kids and siblings

If the baby has older siblings, make a kid-friendly corner with books and snacks. If the family has pets, decide whether they stay in the party or in another room.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area sip and see parties are usually at home, in a backyard or in a small party room. Spring and fall can be unpredictable, so plan a backup indoors. Winter events need coat space and a way to keep entries dry for strollers and car seats. If you rent a space, ask about quiet rooms and access, and check with your village or park district for rules about decor and food. Parking and stairs matter when guests carry car seats, so choose a venue with easy access. See [community center event rentals in the suburbs](/blog/community-center-event-rentals-chicago-suburbs).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a sip and see party?

It is a relaxed open house where family and friends meet a new baby. Guests sip a drink, see the baby and congratulate the parents. It is usually held a few weeks after the birth.

### When should you have a sip and see?

Two to eight weeks after the birth, once the parents feel ready. Choose a time that fits the baby's nap schedule.

### How long should a sip and see last?

Two to three hours, as an open house. Guests can drop in and leave whenever they want.

### Who hosts a sip and see?

Often grandparents, close friends or the parents themselves. Choose whoever has the time and space, and confirm with the parents.

### Do you bring a gift to a sip and see?

Gifts are optional. Many invitations say no gifts needed. A card, a meal or a small present is welcome.

### What should I serve at a sip and see?

Coffee, tea, juice and light food such as pastries, fruit, mini sandwiches and a dessert. Keep it easy to eat while holding a drink.

### How do I keep the baby safe at a party?

Ask guests to stay home if sick, set up a hand-washing station, keep the baby in a calm area and let the parents decide who holds the baby.
