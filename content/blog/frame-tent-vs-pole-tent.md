**Quick answer**

A frame tent stands on a metal skeleton with no center poles, so the whole floor is open, and it can sit on patios, driveways and parking lots using weights. A pole tent is held up by center poles and tension from stakes, so it needs soft ground, extra yard around the edges and usually works around the poles. Choose a frame tent for most Chicago-area backyards, patios and venues with hard surfaces. Choose a pole tent when you have a large lawn and want the classic peaked look.

</div>

Tent companies talk about "frame" and "pole" tents like everyone already knows the difference. For a host, the difference decides where the tent can go, how many tables fit and whether the grass gets staked. This guide compares the two on the points that affect real planning in Chicago and the suburbs.

## How each one stands up

A **frame tent** is built on a rigid aluminum or steel frame, like a big outdoor box. The roof fabric is stretched across it and held by the frame, not by tension from the ground. Because it is a self-supporting structure, it can be anchored with stakes in soil or with weights (ballast) on pavement.

A **pole tent** hangs fabric from tall center poles and pulls it taut with ropes and stakes around the perimeter. It is a tension structure, so the stakes are what hold it up and the perimeter stakes need soft ground.

## Side-by-side comparison

## Where a frame tent wins

**Hard surfaces.** Many Chicago-area events happen on a driveway, patio, parking lot or rooftop deck where stakes are not an option. Frame tents use weights, so they can sit on concrete or asphalt. Read [stakes vs ballast on concrete](/blog/tent-anchoring-stakes-vs-ballast) for how that works.

**Tight yards.** A city lot or suburban backyard rarely has the 5 to 10 extra feet that a pole tent's stake lines need, as a rule of thumb. Frame tents can fit closer to fences and houses. Ask the tent company for the exact working footprint.

**A clean floor plan.** With no center poles, round tables, a dance floor and a bar all fit without guests sitting behind a pole.

**Sidewalls.** Frame tents take straight sidewalls neatly, which is useful for fall and wet weather. See [clear, window and solid sidewalls](/blog/tent-sidewalls-clear-window-solid).

## Where a pole tent wins

**Large lawns and big guest counts.** A pole tent can look better in a big open field, and the peaks are the shape many people picture for a festival or a wedding in a meadow.

**Atmosphere.** The draped peaks, and high ceilings in the middle, are popular for lighting and chandeliers. Some couples hang a chandelier from a center pole, which is a nice use of the pole.

**Cost.** Pole tents can cost less than frame tents for the same area in some markets, but quotes vary a lot by company, season and labor. Ask for both options in writing instead of assuming.

## What neither one can do

- Replace a permit. Large tents in Chicago and many suburbs need one. See [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).
- Handle high wind without a plan. Both types need a wind action plan. See [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety).
- Be installed on an unmarked lawn. Utilities need to be located before stakes go in. Read the [JULIE utility locate guide](/blog/julie-utility-locate-tent-illinois).

## A quick way to decide

If you answer yes to any of these, choose a frame tent: hard surface, tight yard, guest count under 150, fall or wet-season event, or you want the dance floor and tables fully open. If you have a big lawn, a rural venue and you love the look of peaks, ask for pole tent pricing too. Many planners get quotes on both and compare the footprint, the crew time and the permit steps before deciding.

Whichever you choose, give guests seating that handles grass. [Garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) have a wide footprint, and [rattan garden chairs](/chairs/rattan-garden) suit a relaxed tent look. For sizing, see [what size tent do you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a frame tent and a pole tent?

A frame tent stands on a metal frame and has no center poles. A pole tent uses center poles and stakes with ropes to hold the fabric taut. The frame tent works on more surfaces and gives you an open floor.

### Which tent is better for a backyard wedding?

A frame tent is usually the better fit because it needs less yard around it and keeps the floor open for tables. Pole tents can be great if you have a big lawn and want the peaked look.

### Can a frame tent go on concrete or asphalt?

Yes, with weights or other approved ballast on the legs. Many companies are cautious about asphalt in hot weather. Ask how they anchor on your surface and what weight they use.

### Are pole tents cheaper than frame tents?

Sometimes, but not always. Pricing depends on size, season, labor, sidewalls and accessories. Request quotes for both and compare what each includes.

### Do I still need a permit for a pole tent or a frame tent?

Permit rules depend on tent area and height, not on style. In Chicago, tents over 400 square feet need a permit. Check your village for suburban events.

### How much yard do I need around each type?

Frame tents usually need less. Pole tents need extra room for ropes and stakes. Your tent company can give you the exact working footprint for your size, which is the number to measure against your yard.
