**Quick answer**

Seat each parent with their own partner, siblings and close relatives at separate tables that are the same size and the same distance from the couple. Give both parents equal roles and visible spots. At the ceremony, use the first row on each side. Talk to each parent in advance so nobody learns about the plan from a place card.

</div>

Few parts of event planning cause more quiet stress than the seating chart when parents have divorced. The layout is a rental and floor-plan problem, but the real work is equity: both parents should feel they are honored, and nobody should feel placed at the back. This guide explains the common layouts, how to think about them and a few practical ways to make them work in a Chicago-area ballroom, hall or backyard.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Mirror layout.</strong> Two parent tables of equal size on either side of the couple. Easiest to explain and most visibly fair.</li>
<li><strong>Neutral mix.</strong> Each parent sits at a table of their own family, but neither is next to the head table.</li>
<li><strong>Rotating.</strong> Parents sit at the head table for a short ceremony or toast, then join their own tables.</li>
</ol>

Pick the mirror layout when the relationship is friendly, and the neutral mix when there is tension. If one parent has remarried, include their spouse at the same table. Do not seat a former spouse and a new partner together unless everyone has agreed.

## What to say when you tell each parent

Keep the conversation short and positive. Something like: "We want you both to have a place of honor, so each of you will have your own table near us with your partner and your family." Avoid asking who they would prefer not to see; ask where they would be most comfortable. If a parent pushes for a special arrangement, listen, then offer a version that keeps the two tables equal, such as swapping which table is nearer the dance floor. The goal is a plan both parents can describe the same way.

If one parent is not attending or is not in contact, do not leave an empty table. Ask the couple whether to seat other relatives in that spot, or to honor the parent in another way. A seating chart is a list of names, but it is also the first thing guests read when they walk in, so make it calm and clear.

## Ceremony seating

At the ceremony, parents traditionally sit in the first row on each side of the aisle: the bride's family on one side, the groom's on the other. For blended families, give each parent a seat in the first row and put stepparents in the first or second row beside them, depending on what each parent prefers. Use reserved signs or ribbons on the chair ends, as covered in [reserved seating and signage layout](/blog/reserved-seating-and-signage-layout). See [wedding ceremony floor plan and aisle width](/blog/wedding-ceremony-floor-plan-and-aisle-width) for how many rows to reserve.

## Questions to ask each parent first

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who should sit with you? | Partner, stepparent, siblings, grandparents |
| Is there anyone you do not want to sit next to? | Prevents an awkward table |
| Do you want to be at a table near the couple or near the bar? | Preferences differ |
| Any mobility needs? | Place near an aisle and the restroom |
| Would you like a role in the toast or dances? | Be even-handed |

Ask separately, by phone, and be matter-of-fact. Treat the answers as private.

## Place cards and signs

Use table numbers, not "Mom's side" and "Dad's side" on the signs. Guests see only a number; parents know where they sit. This avoids implying a split. Our [reserved seating and signage guide](/blog/reserved-seating-and-signage-layout) covers easels, boards and escort cards. A seating chart with alphabetical names (not table by table) also reduces comparisons.

## Head table with a blended family

A long head table is hard when you have divorced parents, stepparents, siblings and a wedding party. A sweetheart table avoids the problem because the couple sits alone and parents are at their own tables. If you still want a head table, seat parents on opposite ends with their partners, or use two tables. We compare options in [head table vs sweetheart table layout](/blog/head-table-vs-sweetheart-table-layout).

## Kids and step-siblings

Step-siblings and half-siblings are family but often do not know each other well. Seat children with a parent or at a table of peers, and keep the youngest near the aisle for restroom trips. If you have a kids table, check [kids table placement](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events) so the children are visible from adult tables.

## A quick checklist before you print the chart

- Both parent tables have the same size, chairs and linens.
- Both parents are the same distance from the couple, or both are equally close to the dance floor.
- Each parent has a partner or plus-one named, if applicable.
- Grandparents sit near an aisle and a restroom.
- Anyone with mobility needs has an easy path, as in [ADA accessible event seating](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways).
- Photographers have the list of family groups and who should not be posed together.

## Dances and toasts

The seating chart is also a program chart. Parent dances, toasts and photo sessions raise the same question of fairness. Give both parents a role of similar length. Photographers can take group photos in separate sets to avoid putting people together who prefer not to be.

## If your table plan is tight

If the venue is small, avoid putting divorced parents at adjacent tables. Leave at least one table between them. A larger room allows for the mirror layout. For a 100-guest room, see [floor plan for 100 guests](/blog/floor-plan-for-100-guests). The chairs can be matched across tables with a consistent set, such as the [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) or [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back), so no table appears upgraded.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do divorced parents sit at a wedding reception?

At separate tables of the same size and style, at similar distance from the couple, each with their own partner and family.

### Should divorced parents sit together?

Only if both want to. Otherwise, seat them apart and make sure each has a clearly honored spot.

### Where do stepparents sit at the ceremony?

Beside their spouse in the first or second row, depending on what the parents prefer. Ask them in advance.

### Can divorced parents sit at the head table?

Yes, but it works best with a large head table and a clear arrangement, such as parents at opposite ends. A sweetheart table avoids the issue.

### How do I avoid hurt feelings in the seating chart?

Treat both sides identically in table size, chairs, linens and placement, and tell each parent privately before the invitations go out.

### Where do I seat a parent's new partner?

At the same table as the parent. Include a name card for them just like any guest.
