**Quick answer**

Pick a backdrop that matches your event, your lighting and your guest count. Pipe and drape and step-and-repeat are the easiest, flower walls and balloon walls take longer to set up, and neon needs power. Plan an 8-foot-wide backdrop for groups of 4 to 6, and leave 8 to 10 feet for the photographer.

A good backdrop turns any corner of a room into a photo spot. It also helps your guests post better pictures, which gets your event shared. The choice is large: draped fabric, flower walls, shimmer panels, neon, greenery or a simple painted board. Here is how to choose one that works with your venue, lights and guest count.

## Choosing by event

- **Weddings:** drape plus a flower wall or a neon name sign.
- **Birthdays and quinceaneras:** sequin panels, balloons, neon.
- **Corporate:** step and repeat banners, branded drape. See [corporate photo backdrop guide](/blog/corporate-event-photo-backdrop-guide).
- **Baby showers:** greenery, balloons and soft pastels.
- **Holiday parties:** winter whites, metallics and lights.

For a group of 4 to 6 people, an 8-foot-wide backdrop works. Larger groups or wide dresses want 10 feet or more. Leave 8 to 10 feet between the backdrop and the camera.

## Lighting the backdrop

Photos fail more from bad light than from a bad backdrop. Avoid putting the backdrop under a spotlight or in front of a window. Even light from the side or front works best. A ring light or two softbox lights make faces look better. If you plan a neon sign, ask about power and noise. See [marquee and neon signs](/blog/marquee-letters-and-neon-signs-rental) and our [lighting and power resources](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power).

## Chicago notes

Winter events mean coats. Put the backdrop away from the coat check, and keep the walk short for guests in heels. For outdoor backdrops in spring wind, weight everything and avoid light fabrics.

## Sizing for group shots

A group of six in formal wear is about 8 feet across. A couple with a bridal party of ten needs 12 feet or two backdrops. Guests will crowd in, so a backdrop that fits four will look cramped by the third group. If your guest list is more than 100, consider a second photo corner on the other side of the room so lines stay short. Corporate and nonprofit events often put the backdrop near the check-in table so every guest passes it once.

## Pairing with seating

A velvet [chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) or a pair of [royal thrones](/chairs/royal-throne) in front of a backdrop becomes a seat for portraits. Kids and older guests love a place to sit.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Making the backdrop too small for a group.
- Placing it where flash glares off a window.
- Hanging heavy items from a weak frame.
- Forgetting a fire exit clearance.


## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best photo backdrop for a wedding?

A drape or flower wall with soft front lighting is a safe choice. A neon sign adds personality. Choose based on your venue and style.

### How big should a photo backdrop be?

About 8 feet wide and 8 feet tall for groups of 4 to 6. Go to 10 feet for bigger groups or wide dresses.

### How far should the camera be from the backdrop?

About 8 to 10 feet is a good starting point, with the group standing 3 to 4 feet in front of the backdrop to avoid shadows.

### Can you use a backdrop outside?

Yes, with weights or stakes, and ideally some wind protection. Light fabrics and balloon walls are the first to fail in breezes.

### How do I light a photo backdrop?

Use even light from the front or sides, and avoid windows and overhead spotlights. Ring lights or softboxes help.

### Do I need power for a backdrop?

Only if it has lights or a neon sign. Ask the rental company about power needs and cord covers.
