**Quick answer**

Plated dinners need the most plates per guest (about 3) and the fewest serving pieces. Buffets need about 2 plates per guest plus stacks at the line. Family-style needs 1 to 2 plates per guest plus 1 platter or bowl for every 4 to 6 guests per dish, and a 72-inch round or long table for room. Ask your caterer which service style you have before you build the dinnerware list.

## Plated dinners

A plated dinner uses one plate per course and the most servers. The table holds only the place setting, so a 60-inch round is enough. The count is high but the layout is easy. See our [plates per guest counts](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest) for how to calculate it, and [place setting spacing](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements) for the table.

## Buffets

At a buffet, plates are stacked at the start of the line. Guests need space to walk, so allow at least a few feet in front of the table and extra space for a second line if the guest list is large. Most buffet food lives on chafing dishes and trays from a catering equipment order; our [catering equipment cluster](/blog/topic/catering-equipment) covers those. Guests pick up a plate, then return to a table that has only flatware and napkins. Buffet-style lets you skip chargers and bread plates altogether.

## Family-style

Family-style is the most table-hungry. Every table gets its own platters and bowls, which means a 60-inch round fits fewer settings and less food. Many caterers suggest a 72-inch round for 8 to 10 or a long table. Place plates and platters with intention: platters in the middle, plates at the edge, glasses angled. Read [family-style serving ware](/blog/family-style-serving-ware) for the count of platters per table.

## Mixed service

Many Chicago receptions mix styles: passed appetizers, plated salad, buffet entree and cake cutting. Count each part separately, and see the [appetizer plates guide](/blog/appetizer-plates-cocktail-hour). A mixed service can also reuse plates across courses to save money, so ask your caterer whether salad plates can double as dessert plates.

## A quick way to choose

If your priority is the lowest piece count, choose a buffet. If it is a polished look with the least table clutter, choose plated. If it is a warm, shared feel and you have large tables, choose family-style. Many Chicago-area couples pick based on the room as much as the menu: a long, narrow hall suits buffet lines at the sides, while a square ballroom with 72-inch rounds suits plated or family-style. Ask your caterer how many servers each style needs, because labor, not plates, is often the bigger line on a catering quote.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which service style needs the fewest dishes?

A buffet or casual meal needs the fewest plates, usually one or two per guest. Family-style needs fewer plates but more serving pieces. Plated dinners need the most plates.

### Do I need serving platters for a plated dinner?

No. Servers plate in the kitchen, so you do not need platters on the table. You may still want a serving tray for the bread or cake.

### How much extra table space does family-style need?

Plan on a 72-inch round for 8 to 10 guests, or a long table with extra width. Shared dishes take room that would otherwise hold a centerpiece.

### Can I switch from plated to buffet to save money?

Often yes, because buffets use fewer plates and fewer servers. However, the buffet needs extra tables, chafing dishes and space for a line. Compare both on a quote.

### How many plates do guests need at a buffet?

Plan on 2 per guest: one for dinner and one for dessert. Add extras for second trips. If you have a long cocktail hour, count appetizer plates separately.

### Does the caterer bring plates?

It depends on the contract. Many caterers can supply plates or work with rentals. Ask what is included in the package before ordering.
