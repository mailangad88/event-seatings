**Quick answer**

Add twice the drop you want to the table's size. A standard rental table is 30 inches tall, so a floor-length cloth needs a 30-inch drop on every side: a 60-inch round table takes a 120-inch round cloth, a 72-inch round takes a 132-inch round, a 6-foot banquet table takes a 90 x 132-inch cloth and an 8-foot banquet takes a 90 x 156-inch cloth. For a shorter "lap length" look, use about a 15-inch drop instead.

</div>

Linen orders go wrong in one of two ways: the cloth stops a few inches short and shows the table legs in every photo, or it puddles on the floor where guests catch it with their heels. Both come from the same mistake, which is guessing at a size instead of doing a quick sum. This guide gives you the sum, a chart for the tables you'll see at almost every Chicago-area venue and rental company, and the details people forget, like cocktail tables and napkins.

## The one formula you need

**Cloth size = table size + (drop × 2)**

The "drop" is how far the cloth hangs down from the tabletop edge. You count it twice because it hangs on both sides.

Most rental tables in the US are 30 inches tall, so:

- **Floor length** uses a 30-inch drop. It hides the legs, storage boxes and anything you tuck under the table. It's the standard for weddings, galas and any table that shows up in photos.
- **Lap length** uses roughly a 10 to 15-inch drop. It's cheaper, cooler in summer and easier for guests to sit at, which is why it suits casual dinners, picnics and buffet lines that get refilled from behind.

If a rental company quotes you a size that doesn't match this chart, ask what height their tables are. A few use 29-inch tables, and some sweetheart tables are a different height altogether.

## Round tables: 60 inch or 72 inch?

Most banquet halls around Chicago stock both. A **60-inch round** seats 8 comfortably and leaves room for a centerpiece and bread plates. A **72-inch round** seats 10 and saves you floor space and linens when your guest list is large. It's worth checking your table size before you pick linens, because the 120-inch and 132-inch cloths aren't interchangeable: a 120-inch cloth on a 72-inch table stops 6 inches short of the floor on every side.

## Banquet tables: the two-cloth trick

Floor-length banquet cloths in 90 x 156 inches are common, but not every rental company has them in every color. When yours doesn't, ask for **two cloths**: a plain floor-length base in white or ivory and a lap-length overlay on top in your color. It looks deliberate, and it often costs less than a specialty color in the large size.

For a head table where guests sit on one side only, you only need the front to reach the floor. Many planners use a fitted spandex cover or a pleated skirt on the guest-facing side instead.

## Cocktail tables: the tie that changes everything

Highboy cocktail tables are about 42 inches tall, so a floor-length cloth would need a 144-inch round. That's rarely stocked. The usual answer is a **120-inch round cloth gathered and tied** at the waist with a sash or ribbon, which turns the extra fabric into a skirt shape. Spandex covers fitted to the table are the other option, and they hold up well in breezy outdoor spots like a lakefront terrace.

## Don't forget the extra tables

The guest tables are easy to count. These are the ones that get missed:

- Cake table (often a 48-inch round with a floor-length cloth)
- Gift and card table
- Guest book or welcome table
- Escort card or seating chart table
- DJ or band table (usually a 6-foot banquet)
- Dessert or late-night snack table
- Bar fronts and buffet lines

A 150-guest reception can easily have six to eight of these on top of the guest tables. Count them on your floor plan before you send a linen order.

## Napkin sizes

For dinner service, a **20 x 20-inch** cloth napkin is the standard. It's big enough for a decorative fold and covers a lap properly. Smaller 17 or 18-inch napkins exist but look skimpy on a formal place setting. For cocktail hour, paper cocktail napkins are typical, and many couples have them printed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size tablecloth do I need for a 60-inch round table?

A 120-inch round cloth reaches the floor on a standard 30-inch-tall table. For lap length, use a 90-inch round. Anything between those sizes will look like a mistake, so pick one or the other.

### What size tablecloth fits an 8-foot banquet table to the floor?

A 90 x 156-inch rectangular cloth. The table is 30 x 96 inches, and adding a 30-inch drop on every side gives 90 x 156. A 90 x 132-inch cloth fits a 6-foot table to the floor.

### What tablecloth size works for a 72-inch round table?

A 132-inch round cloth reaches the floor. A 120-inch cloth will stop about 6 inches short on every side, which shows the table legs in photos.

### How do I cover a highboy cocktail table?

Use a 120-inch round cloth tied at the middle with a sash, or a fitted spandex cover. A true floor-length cloth for a 42-inch-tall table would need to be about 144 inches round, which few rental companies stock.

### Should tablecloths touch the floor at a wedding?

Most wedding planners choose floor length for guest tables because it hides the legs and looks finished in photos. A cloth that just kisses the floor is ideal. Avoid cloths that puddle in high-traffic areas, because guests and servers trip on them.

### How many extra tablecloths should I rent?

Order one or two spares beyond your table count. Spills happen during setup, and you'll almost always find a table you forgot, like the gift or guest book table.
