**Quick answer**

Battery lighting lets you place light anywhere without cords. Choose rechargeable fixtures with a rated run time at least 2 hours longer than your event, charge everything fully the day before, and carry spares. Battery uplights, LED candles, lanterns and string lights all work. They are usually dimmer than wired versions, so use more fixtures or place them closer to what you want lit.

</div>

Not every beautiful venue has outlets where you want light: a field, a garden, a park pavilion, a rooftop terrace, or a barn with two sockets. Battery lighting solves it, and it is far better than it was a few years ago. This guide covers what is available, how to plan run times and the mistakes that leave the last hour in the dark.

## Planning run time

Manufacturers list run time at a given brightness, color and condition, so treat the number as the best case. Cold weather, bright settings, saturated colors and wireless control reduce it. A practical approach:

1. Calculate the total time the lights are on, from load-in tests through the last guest.
2. Add 2 hours of margin.
3. Pick fixtures rated longer than that at the brightness you plan to use.
4. Ask the vendor for the run time at that brightness.
5. Charge fully before delivery, and plan swaps for any that run short.

For winter events, batteries lose capacity in the cold, so aim for a bigger margin. See [winter event lighting in Chicago](/blog/winter-event-lighting-chicago-sunset).

## A charging plan

- Charge all rechargeable fixtures 12 to 24 hours before the event, not the same morning.
- Store charged units indoors, in a warm room.
- Label each unit and tally them in and out.
- Bring a power strip and a few chargers for emergencies.
- Check that remote controls have fresh batteries.
- Keep spares of the type of battery your LED candles use.

Disposable batteries are fine for LED candles with a long run time. Rechargeable cells reduce waste and can be more reliable, but only if charged and tested.

## Brightness trade-offs

Battery fixtures are usually less bright than wired ones, so adjust the plan:

- Place battery uplights closer to the wall, about 1 foot, and use more fixtures; see [how many uplights](/blog/how-many-uplights-for-a-wedding).
- Use battery lighting for ambiance and keep wired or generator-powered light for functional areas like the bar and buffet.
- Group LED candles and lanterns so the glow adds up.
- Use reflective surfaces, like mirrors or glass, to spread light.

## Weather and water

Outdoor battery lighting needs a water-resistance rating. Check the manufacturer's rating for rain, and do not leave fixtures in puddles. Snow and cold slow the battery. Keep the vendor's instructions for charging ports, which often are the weak point for moisture.

## Combine battery and wired lighting

A hybrid plan is often best.

- Wired string lights over the dining area; see [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events).
- Battery lanterns on paths; see [lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events).
- A small inverter generator for the DJ, placed far away; see [quiet generator placement](/blog/quiet-generator-placement-events).
- Battery uplights on trees and fences.

This reduces cords on the lawn, as described in [cords on grass](/blog/cords-on-grass-outdoor-lighting-safety), and keeps the load low on the generator.

## Safety

LED battery lighting has no open flame and runs cool, which makes it a good fit for tents and venues that restrict candles. See [candles at venues](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules). Still, keep batteries away from heat and do not charge them under pillows, linens or in enclosed bags.

## Chicago notes

Parks, forest preserves and many rooftops have no outlets, so battery lighting is common. Public sites may restrict stakes, so choose weighted bases. Ask your park district before bringing anything that is staked or hung.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do battery event lights last?

It varies by fixture, brightness and temperature. Pick units rated at least 2 hours longer than your event and charge them fully the day before.

### Are battery uplights as bright as wired ones?

Usually not. Place them closer to the wall and use more of them, or reserve them for accents.

### Can battery lights go outside in the rain?

Only if rated for it. Check the water resistance rating and protect charging ports.

### Do batteries run down faster in cold weather?

Yes, cold reduces capacity. Keep units warm until use and allow extra margin.

### Should I use disposable or rechargeable batteries?

Rechargeable units are more reliable if charged and tested. Disposable batteries are fine for LED candles.

### Can I run all lighting on batteries?

For a small event, yes. For dinners, bars and DJs, wired or generator power is usually needed for the main load.
