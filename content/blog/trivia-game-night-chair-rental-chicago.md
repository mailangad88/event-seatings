## How to lay out the room

1. **Put the host and any screen or scoreboard at the front**, where every table has a clear line of sight.
2. **Give each team its own table**, rather than splitting a team across two tables or seating two teams at one long table. Mixed tables lead to mixed-up answer sheets and arguments about who said what.
3. **Leave an aisle between tables** for answer sheets to be collected and for people to get up without disturbing other teams.
4. **Keep the bar or food station off to the side**, not behind the host, so people can get up between rounds without crossing the sightline.

## How many chairs to rent

Count chairs by team, not only by total guest count:

- **6 to 8 chairs per team** is the comfortable range. Fewer than 6 and a team can feel short-handed; more than 8 and a table gets hard to talk around.
- **Round up, don't crowd.** If you have 50 players and tables that seat 8, that's 7 tables (one with fewer players), not 6 tables packed to 9 each.
- **Add a few extra chairs** for walk-ins, which are common at open trivia nights and fundraisers.

## If you're hosting at a bar or brewery

Many bars, breweries and taprooms in the Chicago area already have seating for a trivia night, since it's a regular part of their calendar. Before you book rental chairs, ask the venue:

- How many of their own tables and chairs are available for your group size?
- Is private event space available, or will you share the room with regular guests?
- Can outside furniture come in, and is there a load-in time?

Renting makes the most sense when your group is larger than the venue's regular setup, when you want every team seated together rather than split across the room, or when you're hosting at an office, banquet hall or other space without built-in seating.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a trivia night?
Plan 6 to 8 chairs per team table rather than counting guests alone. If a team has more players than one table comfortably seats, add another table instead of crowding extra chairs around one.

### How should tables be arranged for a trivia or game night?
Set each team at its own table in rows facing the host and any screen or scoreboard, with an aisle between tables so answer sheets can be collected and people can move without disturbing other teams.

### Does the bar or brewery provide chairs for trivia night?
Often yes, since many venues already run regular trivia nights with their own tables and chairs. Ask what's included for your group size before you book rentals, and rent extra only if your group is larger than their usual setup or needs a private space.

### What chairs work best for a trivia or fundraiser game night?
Light chairs that are easy to carry and set around small tables work well, such as bistro or café-style chairs. For a longer night, a chair with some shape to the seat, like a mid-century shell chair, is more comfortable than a flat folding chair.
