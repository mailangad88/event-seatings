## City-run parks

Yorkville's parks are handled by the United City of Yorkville Parks and Recreation Department. The city publishes a Park Rental and Use Policy and an online Park Reservation Application, so you can read the rules and apply on one site. The riverfront has Bicentennial Riverfront Park, which hosts community events such as Yorkville Riverfest.

## Read the policy first

Because the city posts its Park Rental and Use Policy, you can answer many questions yourself: how many people a shelter holds, whether you can bring tents or inflatables, how alcohol is handled, and what happens if you cancel. Read it before you request a quote, then share the relevant parts with your rental company. For tents, see [what size tent do I need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

## Delivery from St. Charles

Yorkville is south of St. Charles down the Fox River valley, a longer rural drive than Aurora. We plan a longer window and ask for a contact at the site. Riverfront parks may have paths and gates that limit trucks, so ask the city where vehicles may stop.

## Seasonal notes

Riverfront events cluster in summer. Fall is calm. Spring floods can affect river-edge areas, so ask about conditions. Winter events move indoors.

## A Yorkville example

For an 80-guest riverfront picnic, plan ten rounds of 8 and a few banquet tables. ## Questions for the city

Call the city and ask: what is the capacity of the shelter I want, can I reserve it for a private event, what is the deposit, can I serve alcohol with a permit, and is there a deadline for the permit? Keep a note of the answers and the name of the person you spoke with. For travelers from Chicago, confirm parking and restroom access too.

## Rain plans for riverfront events

Riverfront events are lovely and weather-sensitive. Build a plan with three levels.

- **Light rain:** pop-up covers over food and the DJ, towels for chairs, and a few umbrellas.
- **Steady rain:** a tent with sidewalls over the main seating, and a move of tables toward higher ground.
- **Storm or flood watch:** an indoor backup, such as a hall or a church, and a clear message to guests.

Ask the city about shelter roofs and about conditions near the river after heavy rain. Tell your rental company your backup, because it affects what we load. Read [outdoor lounge rentals](/blog/outdoor-lounge-rentals-chicago) for ideas to make wet-weather seating comfortable. Decide on the backup at least 48 hours ahead, and message guests by noon the day before.
