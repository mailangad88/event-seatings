**Quick answer**

Spend first on what guests and photos see most: the base cloths on guest tables and the head table. Spend second on napkins, since each guest holds one. Spend last on accents such as runners, overlays and sashes, and add them where they matter. Save by using one base cloth for most tables, skipping linens on tables nobody sees, and choosing standard colors. We do not publish linen prices, so ask for an itemized quote and compare line by line.

Linens are rarely the largest cost of an event, but they are one of the easiest to get wrong. Money tends to leak into extras that look good on paper and disappear in the room, while the basics, such as the right cloth on the right table, get squeezed. This guide gives a simple way to rank linen spending, so you can allocate whatever your budget is. We do not state prices for linens because they vary by supplier, fabric and date.

### 1. Base cloths
They cover the largest area and set the color of the room. Put money here: the right size, a good fabric and a clean look. Floor-length cloths on guest tables and the head table matter most. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

### 2. Napkins
Each guest touches one. A good napkin in a contrast color lifts a plain table more than almost any other item. See [cloth napkin sizes and colors](/blog/cloth-napkin-sizes-and-colors).

### 3. Accents
Runners, overlays and a special head table cloth. Add them where they show most. Skip them if the budget is tight and let flowers and candles carry the table.

### 4. Extras
Chair covers, sashes, napkin rings and skirting. These are nice, not necessary. Often a better chair is a smarter choice than covers; see [chair sashes and covers](/blog/chair-sashes-and-covers-worth-it).

## Where to save

- **Standard colors.** White, ivory and black are stocked in larger quantities and are usually easier to source than specialty colors.
- **One base fabric.** Use a single polyester or lamour across guest tables, and spend extra only on the head table.
- **Fewer accents.** A runner on every other table or only at the head table can still look planned.
- **Skip hidden tables.** Tables behind a bar or kitchen door do not need premium linens.
- **Accept house linens** at the venue where you can, if the colors work.
- **Right-size orders.** Count tables carefully so you do not pay for unused cloths. See the [linen ordering checklist](/blog/linen-ordering-checklist).

## Where not to save

- **Wrong size cloths.** A cloth that stops short shows the table legs and wastes the rental.
- **Napkin count.** Running out is worse than renting a few extra.
- **Cheap, thin fabric under bright light.** Table seams and marks show through.
- **Skipping the damage policy.** A surprise charge can erase a savings. See [linen stain and damage policies](/blog/linen-stain-and-damage-policies).
- **Last-minute changes.** Late orders can lose color choices or add fees; see [how far ahead to order linens](/blog/how-far-ahead-to-order-linens).

## Linens vs chairs vs other decor

Linens work with chairs, not instead of them. If the budget is limited, compare a better chair with plain linens against cheap chairs with fancy linens. A good chair appears in every photo, and a plain ivory cloth often looks better than a cheap fancy one. Chair estimates on our site run from $5 to 7 for a [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) to $8 to 11 for a [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) and $10 to 14 for a [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost). For the overall picture, see [wedding chair rental cost](/blog/wedding-chair-rental-cost).

## Chicago notes

Delivery, setup and pickup can cost as much as the items themselves at city venues, because of loading docks, elevators and time windows. Ask if delivery and setup are separate lines, and whether the quote includes pickup. Peak dates can have minimums or fewer color choices, so a flexible date may open more options.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I budget for wedding linens?

It depends on your guest count, table count, fabrics and supplier, so we do not publish a number. Get itemized quotes, fund base cloths and napkins first, and add accents with what remains.

### What is the best way to save on linens?

Use one standard base cloth across guest tables, skip accents on hidden tables, and choose stocked colors. Count carefully so you do not rent unneeded pieces.

### Are napkins or tablecloths more important?

Both matter, but cloths set the room and napkins set the place. If you must choose, get good base cloths first and then upgrade napkins.

### Should I rent or buy linens?

For a single event, renting is usually simpler: no cleaning, storage or leftover stock. Buying can make sense for repeat events, but compare total cost and effort.

### Do I need to budget for damage?

Yes, a small reserve. Spills are normal, but burns, tears and missing items can be billed. Read the supplier's policy.

### Are overlays and runners worth the extra cost?

They can be, at the head table or where photos are taken. On every table they add up quickly, so choose where they matter most.
