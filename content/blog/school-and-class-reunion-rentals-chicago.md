A class reunion is a different kind of party to plan than a wedding or a birthday. Nobody sends a save-the-date a year out, half the guest list replies in the last two weeks, and the venue is often a rented hall, a restaurant banquet room or even the old school gym rather than a space built for events. The planning committee usually does this once every five or ten years, so it helps to have a plan that doesn't depend on guessing right on the first try.

## Guest-count uncertainty is normal

Reunions reply late. People wait to see who else is going, travel plans change, and a chunk of the list usually confirms in the final week or two. Rather than treating your early RSVP count as final, set a realistic low and high range and tell us both numbers when you book. We can hold a flexible order and confirm the final chair and table count closer to the date, instead of locking in too early and scrambling to add more later.

## Round tables vs. long tables

Reunions are built around mingling, catching up with people you haven't seen in years, and that usually favors round tables over long banquet rows. Round tables of 8 or 10 let small groups talk without shouting down a long table, and they make it easier for people to move between tables during the night. Long tables work better for a sit-down dinner with a program, or for a smaller, tighter-knit graduating class that wants to sit together as one group. ## Decor you add yourself

Most reunions lean on nostalgia: old photos, yearbook pages, class colors, a slideshow running on a loop. We handle the chairs, tables and linens, and you bring the decor that makes the room feel like your class. If you want a dedicated table for photos or a memory wall, plan it into your table count early so it doesn't get squeezed in at the last minute.

## Timing around the venue's own schedule

A rented hall, restaurant or school gym usually has its own schedule of other bookings before and after yours. Confirm your load-in and load-out window with the venue directly, and pass that window to us so delivery and pickup happen inside it. School gyms in particular may only be available outside school hours or on a weekend, so double check with the school's office on exact access times.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan rentals when I don't know the final guest count?

Give us a low and high estimate when you first book, and we'll hold a flexible order. Confirm the final number one to two weeks before the event so we can adjust the chair and table count without starting the order over.

### Should a class reunion use round or long tables?

Round tables of 8 or 10 work best for mingling, since smaller groups can talk easily and people can move between tables. Long tables suit a sit-down dinner with a program or a smaller class that wants to sit together.

### Does a restaurant banquet room still need rental chairs?

Often not, since most banquet rooms include their own house tables and chairs. You may still want a few extra chairs, a sign-in table or linens, so check with the restaurant on what's included before you order.

### Can rentals be delivered to a school gym?

Yes, as long as the school confirms access hours and an entrance for delivery. School gyms usually have no furniture of their own, so plan on a full chair and table order.

### What decorations do you provide for a reunion?

None. We handle chairs, tables and linens. You bring photos, yearbook pages, class colors and anything else that makes the room feel like your class.

### How far ahead should a reunion committee book rentals?

As soon as the venue and date are set, even before the final guest count is known. Booking early holds your date, and we can firm up the exact count closer to the event.
