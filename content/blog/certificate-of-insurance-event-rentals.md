**Quick answer**

A certificate of insurance (COI) is a one-page document from an insurance company showing that a vendor has liability coverage. Many venues and buildings ask rental vendors for one, often naming the venue as an "additional insured." Ask your venue exactly what it requires, then ask your rental company to send the COI directly to the venue well before the event.

</div>

It is one of the least glamorous parts of planning, and one that can quietly derail a delivery. A Chicago building manager says "we need a COI from every vendor" and you realize you do not know what that is. The good news is that it is a routine document, usually issued in a day or two by the vendor's insurance agent. The key is to ask early.

## What a COI is

A COI is **proof**, not coverage. It is issued by an insurance agent or broker and lists:

- The insured (the vendor) and the insurer.
- The types of coverage, such as general liability.
- The policy period.
- The coverage limits.
- The certificate holder, the person or business receiving the document.
- Sometimes, an "additional insured" line naming your venue.

It does not change the policy. It shows that the policy exists and who it covers.

## Why venues ask for it

Venues want to know that if a vendor's crew causes damage or an injury on the property, there is insurance to respond. That is why they ask for a COI from caterers, florists, DJs and rental companies. Requirements are set by each venue or building, and we cannot list them for you. Ask yours: which vendors, what coverage types, what limits, and whether the venue must be named as additional insured.

## Who provides what

The rental company provides its own COI from its insurer. You, the client, may be asked for event insurance in a separate document. These are different, and venues sometimes want both. Read [event insurance basics](/blog/event-insurance-basics-for-private-events) for the client-side coverage.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until delivery week.** Agents are usually quick, but a venue's review can take days.
- **Name mismatch.** A small difference in a legal name can mean a reissue.
- **Missing additional insured wording.** If the venue requires it, say so.
- **Wrong dates.** The policy period must cover the event date.
- **Assuming the venue's rules are universal.** They are not. Each venue is its own.

## Does a small event need a COI?

A backyard party usually does not. Hotels, event halls, office buildings and some public sites often do. If you rent a park district space or a city location, check with the organization about their vendor and insurance rules. We do not want to guess which Chicago sites require what, so ask directly, and our guide to [Chicago venue rules for chair rentals](/blog/topic/chicago-planning) is a good companion.

## What to ask your rental company

- Can you issue a COI, and how long does it take?
- Is there a fee for additional insured endorsements?
- Can you send it directly to my venue?
- Does your policy cover delivery and setup at the venue?

These belong in your [questions list for rental companies](/blog/questions-to-ask-an-event-rental-company).

## If the venue says no COI, no delivery

Do not wait. Ask the rental company for the document immediately, and ask the venue if they will accept a draft or a pending certificate while the final one is processed. If the paperwork is stuck, call the venue and explain. Most would prefer a conversation to a rejected crew on the day. Also see [what happens on delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a COI for an event?

A certificate of insurance is a document from an insurer showing a vendor's coverage. Venues use it to confirm that vendors working on their property are insured.

### Does my rental company need to provide a COI?

If your venue requires it, yes. Ask your venue for its requirements, then ask your rental company to send the certificate directly to the venue.

### What does "additional insured" mean?

It means the venue is named on the vendor's policy for the covered activity, so it can be protected if a claim arises from the vendor's work. Whether it is required depends on the venue.

### How long does it take to get a COI?

It depends on the insurance agent. Often it takes a day or two, but allow more time in case wording needs to be corrected.

### Is a COI the same as event insurance?

No. A COI proves a vendor's coverage. Event insurance is typically bought by the host to cover the event itself. Some venues ask for both.

### Do I need a COI for a backyard party?

Typically not, but check with your village or any HOA that has rules. Venues, buildings and public sites are the usual places that ask.
