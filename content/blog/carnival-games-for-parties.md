**Quick answer**

Plan one carnival game booth for every 15 to 25 guests, each with about 8 to 10 feet of frontage and 6 feet of depth, plus a clear queue line of 10 feet. For 100 guests that is 5 or 6 games. Arrange them in a U or a loop so lines never cross, put a prize table and a ticket table near the entrance and add shade and seating for adults.

</div>

Carnival games are the easy win at school fundraisers, church festivals, company family days and big kids' birthdays. They work because the rules are short, the prizes are small and the energy is contagious. They fail when there are too few booths, the lines tangle or no one knows how tickets work. This guide gives you the numbers and a layout for a Chicago-area lawn, park or parking lot.

## How many games do you need?

Not every guest plays at once. At a typical carnival, about half the guests are in a game line or cycling between games at any time. Aim for a booth for every 15 to 25 guests, and favor the lower end if most guests are children.

## Games that work and their footprints

- **Ring toss.** About 8 x 10 ft. Easy for small kids.
- **Bean bag toss or cornhole.** About 8 x 20 ft for kids' versions.
- **Duck pond.** A small pool or tub, 6 x 6 ft, with water and supervision.
- **Bowling or milk-bottle toss.** About 8 x 15 ft.
- **Ball drop or basketball.** About 8 x 12 ft.
- **Spin-the-wheel prize game.** 6 x 6 ft, popular, needs a clear queue.
- **Face painting and crafts.** Table plus 2 chairs, with shade.
- **Giant Jenga or Connect 4.** Open play, 10 x 10 ft.

## Tickets and prizes

A ticket system keeps lines moving and makes budgeting easy.

1. **Sell or hand out tickets at the entrance.** One game equals 1 to 3 tickets.
2. **Set a prize table** with tiered prizes: small for 1 to 3 tickets, bigger for more.
3. **Stock prizes at about 3 to 5 per child**, then adjust after the first hour. Avoid choking hazards for children under 3.
4. **Use a cash box or an app** for ticket sales, and assign one person to them.
5. **Sign the rules** at every booth in one sentence.

For fundraisers, pricing is your decision. We do not set prices for games or tickets. Think about the margin after prizes and rentals, and check any rules on raffles or charitable games with your state and village before you sell chances. Games that award cash or valuable prizes can fall under state law, so ask before you plan one.

## Staffing and flow

Each booth needs one volunteer, plus a floater per 4 booths and a person at tickets and prizes. Give volunteers a one-page instruction sheet and a shift schedule, with breaks. Put the most popular games, such as spin the wheel, at the back of the loop so lines do not block the entrance.

## Furniture and rentals

Besides games, you need furniture. A ticket table and a prize table are usually 6-foot rectangular tables. Parents need shaded chairs, and children need a rest spot. Plan seating for 30 to 40 percent of guests at once. See [kids party rentals checklist](/blog/kids-party-rentals-checklist) and [kids tables and chairs](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events). Add [market umbrellas](/blog/market-umbrellas-for-shade) or a tent for shade. Add a hand-washing station near food and the face painting table. For larger events, plan [portable restrooms](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests).

## Chicago planning notes

School lots, park districts and church grounds often require permits or approvals for tents, inflatables and food. Check with your site and village. Weather is the main risk: plan a rain date or a covered backup, such as a gym. Fall carnivals can be chilly, so add a warm drink station.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many carnival games do I need for 100 kids?

Five or six booths for 100 guests, and closer to 8 if most are children. Add open-play games to spread the crowd.

### How much space does a carnival game booth need?

About 8 to 10 feet of frontage and 6 feet of depth, plus about 10 feet for the line.

### How do tickets work at a carnival?

Guests buy or receive tickets at the entrance, spend 1 to 3 per game and trade tickets for prizes. This keeps lines moving.

### What prizes should I buy?

Small, safe items and a few larger ones. Avoid small parts for children under 3.

### How many volunteers do I need?

One per booth, a floater for every 4 booths, and one each for tickets and prizes.

### Do I need a permit for a carnival?

It depends on the location, tents, inflatables and food. Ask your venue and village. Also check rules for raffles.
