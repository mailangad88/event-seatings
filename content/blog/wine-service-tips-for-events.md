**Quick answer**

Pour 5 ounces per glass, which gives 5 glasses from a 750 ml bottle. For a seated dinner plan about half a bottle of wine per guest if wine is the main drink, and chill whites and sparkling to roughly 45 to 50 degrees F and serve reds around 60 to 65 degrees F. Open bottles just before dinner, fill glasses one-third full and keep a bucket of ice for whites and rosé within reach.

Wine looks easy until 100 people sit down at once. Servers need to know how much to pour, when to open and where cold bottles live. Whether you hire a bar company or a caterer's staff, these basics keep service smooth and the wine at the right temperature.

## Pour sizes and bottle math

A standard 750 ml bottle holds about 25 ounces. At a 5 ounce pour you get 5 glasses. A glass of wine at a toast is smaller, around 4 ounces for sparkling.

## How much wine per guest

These are rules of thumb used by caterers and wine shops. Confirm with your supplier, who knows your crowd.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Scenario | Bottles per guest | 100 guests |
|---|---|---|
| Wine is the only alcohol, 4 hours | About 1/2 | 50 bottles |
| Wine with beer and cocktails | About 1/4 | 25 bottles |
| Dinner wine only, 2 pours | About 1/3 | 33 bottles |
| Toast, sparkling | 1/6 | 16 to 17 bottles |

</div>

A case holds 12 bottles. Ask about case discounts and whether the shop accepts returns of unopened bottles. See [beer and wine only bar setup](/blog/beer-and-wine-only-bar-setup) for a combined count.

## Temperature

Serving at the right temperature changes how wine tastes more than the label does.

- **Sparkling:** about 40 to 45 degrees F, very cold.
- **White and rosé:** about 45 to 50 degrees F.
- **Light red:** about 55 degrees F.
- **Full red:** about 60 to 65 degrees F, slightly below room temperature.

At a summer Chicago event, red wine in a warm tent heats quickly, so put reds in a cooler shaded area and bring them out in batches. Whites need ice tubs: see [how much ice for a party](/blog/how-much-ice-for-a-party).

## Opening and pouring order

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Chill whites and sparkling</strong> 3 to 4 hours before service.</li>
<li><strong>Open reds</strong> 15 to 30 minutes before dinner, if your supplier suggests breathing time.</li>
<li><strong>Pre-set glasses</strong> on tables before guests sit.</li>
<li><strong>Pour from the right,</strong> women and elders first if your service style is formal.</li>
<li><strong>Top off</strong> when a glass is down to a third, and stop at each table's request.</li>
</ol>

A server should hold the bottle by the base, wipe the neck between pours and never let the bottle touch the glass. At an open bar guests pour at the bar; at a seated dinner, servers pour at the table, which uses more bottles but reduces lines.

## Corkage and storage

If you bring your own wine, the venue may charge corkage, which is a per-bottle fee for service. Some venues prohibit outside wine. Ask. Keep unopened bottles in a cool place away from direct sun, and ask your caterer where they can be stored. Check the rules in [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events).

## Serving wine with different menus

Heavy, rich dishes pair with fuller reds, and light dishes with crisp whites. For a wedding, offer one white and one red the guests will like, and avoid unusual styles unless your crowd is wine-savvy. If you offer a wine pairing with a plated meal, tell the caterer so the glass count rises: one glass per wine.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a standard glass of wine?

A standard pour is 5 ounces, which is 5 glasses from a 750 ml bottle. Restaurants and caterers sometimes pour 6 ounces.

### How many bottles of wine per person?

About half a bottle per guest if wine is the only alcohol at a 4-hour event, and a quarter bottle if there is also beer or cocktails.

### What temperature should I serve white wine?

Roughly 45 to 50 degrees F. Chill it for 3 to 4 hours, and keep it in an ice bucket during service.

### Should I decant red wine for a wedding?

Not usually. Opening bottles 15 to 30 minutes before dinner is enough for most reds. Ask your wine supplier for advice on special bottles.

### How many wine buckets do I need?

One bucket per 2 to 3 bottles of white, rosé or sparkling at tables, plus tubs at the bar.

### Can I bring my own wine to a venue?

Only if the venue allows it, and often with a corkage fee. Ask before you buy.
