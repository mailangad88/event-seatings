September in Chicago averages a 75.5°F high and a 57.1°F low at O'Hare, with 3.19 inches of rain, one of the lowest totals of the warm months. September is the month Chicago planners quietly love. The humidity breaks, the lake is still warm, the lawns are green and the light turns golden. It is also the month when evenings shorten faster than people expect, and when wedding venues book a year ahead. Here is what to know.

## September weather

NWS 1991-2020 normals for O'Hare list September at a 75.5°F average high, a 57.1°F average low and 3.19 inches of precipitation. That is a drop from August's 82.5°F high and a noticeable drop in rain. The first week often feels like summer, while the last week can be sweater weather by evening.

## Daylight shrinks quickly

Sunset is about 7:26 pm on September 1 and about 7:02 pm on September 15 (calculated for 2027), with the fall equinox bringing roughly 12 hours of daylight around September 23. A reception that starts at 6 pm is in daylight at the start and dark by the time dessert is served. Plan lighting from the beginning: string lights, uplights and candle-free lanterns. ## Wedding season and holiday weekends

Labor Day is Monday, September 6, 2027, so the weekend before it is a holiday weekend. The weekends that follow are some of the busiest of the year for Chicago-area weddings, along with early October. If you want a Saturday, confirm the venue and the rental list at the same time. Read [when to book event chair rentals](/blog/topic/budget-and-booking).

## What to rent for September

September is the easiest month for the rental list because the weather works for you, but it still helps to be intentional.

- **Chairs for the lawn.** The [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) in walnut matches early fall color, and the [bentwood bistro chair](/chairs/bentwood) works for café-style rehearsal dinners. Estimates are $8 to $11 for cross-back and $9 to $12 for bentwood.
- **A tent as insurance.** Storms are still possible. See [what size tent you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).
- **Heaters or wraps for 9 pm.** The average low of 57°F means chilly guests after dark.
- **Lighting.** Plan for dark by 7:30 pm.
- **Bug control.** West Nile cases usually peak in August and September, per Illinois health guidance, so see our [bug guide](/blog/mosquitoes-and-bugs-outdoor-events).

## Layering for guests

September days can start at 60°F and end at 80°F, so guests dress for the warm part of the day. Offer a basket of wraps, serve warm drinks after sunset and plan seating that can move indoors. A tented reception with half the sidewalls down keeps the breeze out without losing the view.

## Permits

Parks and forest preserves are popular in September. The Chicago Park District reviews permits for at least 14 days and requires permits for 50-plus people, tents, alcohol, amplified sound and inflatables. Suburban districts differ. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is September a good month for an outdoor wedding in Chicago?

Yes, it is one of the most popular. Average highs are 75.5°F, rain is lighter than in summer and the light is warm. Book early because popular dates fill fast.

### What time is sunset in Chicago in September?

About 7:26 pm on September 1 and about 7:02 pm on September 15.

### How much rain does Chicago get in September?

About 3.19 inches at O'Hare, lower than July and August.

### Do I need heaters in September in Chicago?

Not always, but the average low is 57.1°F, so by 9 pm guests may want wraps or a heater at the tent edge.

### Do I need a tent in September?

A tent is a wise rain plan and also gives shade at midday.

### Are mosquitoes still a problem in September?

Yes. West Nile cases usually peak in August and September, so plan repellent and fans.

### When is Labor Day in 2027?

Monday, September 6, 2027. The weekend before is a holiday weekend, so rentals and venues book up.

## More ways September fits your plan

If your guest list is large, September is the month where an outdoor cocktail hour and an indoor dinner work especially well: the golden hour outside, the climate control inside, and no need to heat or cool a tent for six hours. Ask your venue how long the room can stay open after the ceremony, and plan the transition so guests are not standing around while tables are flipped. For a smaller backyard dinner, the same logic applies to the patio: start outside, move in when the temperature drops.
