"One per guest" is the right start, but it's not the whole answer. Here's how to count.

## 1. Reception seating

Plan **one chair per confirmed guest**, plus:

- **Vendors who eat with you**, like your photographer, videographer and planner, if they sit at a table
- **A small buffer** for late RSVPs and last-minute plus-ones. Many planners add a few percent.

## 2. Ceremony seating

If your ceremony is **in a different spot** from your reception, you have two choices:

1. **A second set of chairs.** This costs more but needs no rush during cocktail hour.
2. **A "flip."** The same chairs are moved from ceremony to reception during cocktail hour. It costs less, but it needs enough staff and time.

Short ceremonies sometimes seat only part of the guest list, with older guests and family seated and others standing at the back. Talk it through with your venue.

## 3. Cocktail hour and lounge

You don't need a seat for everyone during cocktail hour. Aim for **seating for a portion of your guests**, especially older relatives. A lounge vignette with a few statement chairs adds style too.

## 4. Head table or sweetheart table

This is where a **statement chair** pays off. A velvet, Louis or throne chair for the couple makes the head table stand out in photos.

## Quick worksheet

| Ceremony | Guests (or a flip) |

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for 100 guests?

About 103 to 105 chairs for a seated reception: one per guest plus a few spares. If the ceremony is in a different space and cannot be flipped, add a second set of about 100.

### Do vendors and the wedding party need chairs?

Yes, count them. Photographers, the DJ and planners often eat during dinner, and the wedding party needs head table seats. Add them to the guest count before adding spares.

### How many chairs fit at a 60-inch round table?

Eight fit comfortably and ten fit snugly. Narrow chairs like Chiavari fit ten; wider chairs like cross-back are better at eight.
