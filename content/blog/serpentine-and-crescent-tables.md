**Quick answer**

A serpentine table is a curved S-shaped section that you join to build wavy buffet lines, bars and display tables. A crescent or half-moon table is a curved half circle that seats guests on the outside edge, often used for head tables and lounge groups. Sizes vary by manufacturer, so ask the rental company for exact dimensions and plan seating on the outside of the curve only.

</div>

Straight banquet tables get a little dull for a buffet line or a dessert wall. Curved tables solve that. They are not hard to use, but they are easy to order wrong, because there is no single universal size. This guide explains the shapes, what each one does well and how to map them on your floor plan for a Chicago-area venue.

## Serpentine tables

A serpentine table is a gentle S-curve, usually about 5 to 6 feet long, and made to join. One piece looks like a wave. Two or three chained together make a snake. Rental companies stock them with different widths, so confirm how deep they are, because that decides whether you can fit chafing dishes or only platters.

Good uses:

- **Buffet lines.** Curves slow the line down and let you stagger dishes. See [buffet table layouts](/blog/buffet-table-layouts).
- **Dessert and candy displays.** Joined sections give you several levels of visual interest.
- **Bars.** A curved bar encourages guests to spread out instead of crowding one end.

Skip them for seated guests. Curved tops are awkward for plates and chairs.

## Crescent and half-moon tables

A crescent (or half-moon, half-round) table is a semicircle. Some rental companies stock a half of a 60-inch round, others a narrower curve. Two give you a full circle when pushed together. Crescents work in three ways.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Use | How it works | Seats |
|---|---|---|
| Head table | Place the curve facing the room, guests sit on the outside | 6 to 8 per 60 in half-round, tight |
| Lounge corner | Two crescents face each other with a low table between | 8 to 10 |
| Display | Cake or gift wall in a corner | None |
| Full circle | Join two to make a 60 in round | 8 |

</div>

## How to size a curved layout

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Ask for exact dimensions</strong> length along the curve, depth and radius.</li>
<li><strong>Draw it</strong> Sketch with the correct curve, not a straight line.</li>
<li><strong>Count linens</strong> Curved tables need fitted cloths or skirting; standard rectangles bunch.</li>
<li><strong>Plan the walkway</strong> Leave 4 to 5 feet in front of a buffet for the line.</li>
<li><strong>Test the join</strong> Ask whether sections lock together or only sit side by side.</li>
</ol>

## Linens and skirting

A rectangular cloth will not drape neatly over a curve. Rental companies use fitted spandex covers or pleated skirting with a top cloth. Ask what they stock for curved shapes before you pick colors, and see our guide to [pleated skirting for head tables and buffets](/blog/pleated-table-skirting-head-table-buffet).

## Chicago planning notes

Many Chicago banquet halls own straight tables only. If your hall is serving food from a buffet, ask whether they will let a rental company bring curved pieces and whether their staff will arrange them. Hotels and some event spaces require outside rentals to arrive on a schedule, so build the delivery window with your venue. This is part of what a seating call covers.

## Common mistakes with curved tables

Curved pieces fail in predictable ways, and most are avoidable with one question to the rental company.

- **Ordering by length alone.** Two serpentine sections of the same length can have very different depth. A shallow top fits platters but not a chafing dish with fuel, so ask for the depth in inches before you plan the menu.
- **Forgetting the gap.** Where two curved sections meet there is a seam. Cover it with a runner, a floral piece or a sign.
- **Planning food on both sides.** A serpentine buffet works best with the line on one side and servers behind. Curves make a double-sided line confusing.
- **Ignoring caterer needs.** Ask your caterer where hot boxes, extension cords and sneeze guards will go. Heat sources near fitted spandex are a hazard, so use cloth that is rated for it or keep fuel cans on a tray.
- **No walkway test.** Mark the curve with tape on the floor at your walkthrough. Walk it with a plate in your hand.

## A simple planning checklist

1. Decide whether you need food service, display or seating.
2. Get exact dimensions of every curved piece you plan to rent.
3. Draw the curve on your floor plan at scale, with 4 to 5 feet in front for the line.
4. Choose fitted or skirted linens that suit curves.
5. Ask your venue whether outside rentals can be set up in the room before doors open.

Chicago venues often have set load-in windows, and a curved buffet takes longer to set than a straight one because the sections need to be lined up and levelled. Build in extra time on delivery day.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a serpentine table?

It is an S-shaped curved table section that joins with others to make wavy lines. It is mostly used for buffets, dessert displays and bars.

### What is a crescent table?

A crescent table is a half circle or curved segment, used for head tables, lounge groups or displays. Guests sit on the outside curve.

### Do serpentine tables seat guests?

Not well. Their shape is built for food and display. Use rounds or banquet tables for seated meals.

### How big is a serpentine table?

Sizes vary by manufacturer. Many are 5 to 6 feet along the curve, but ask for length and depth before you plan food service.

### What is the difference between a crescent and a half-round table?

The terms are often used interchangeably. A half-round is a precise semicircle of a round table, and a crescent can be a narrower curved piece. Ask the rental company.

### Can I use serpentine tables outside?

Yes, on level ground. Wind affects linens, so use fitted covers. See [rental for outdoor events](/blog/table-rental-for-outdoor-events-level-ground).
