**Quick answer**

Most DJs need one or two 6-foot banquet tables (72 x 30 inches) and about 10 x 10 feet of floor space with power close by. A band needs a stage or a floor area of roughly 10 x 12 feet for 4 players, plus a table for the sound mixer. Ask your DJ or band for a tech rider with exact sizes, and place the setup near the dance floor but away from the buffet and the quietest guest tables.

## What a DJ usually needs

<div class="table-wrap">

| Item | Typical need | Ask your DJ |
|---|---|---|
| Table | One 6 ft banquet table, 30 in tall | Do you bring your own table? |
| Cloth | Floor-length black or white | Do you need a skirt to hide cables? |
| Floor space | About 10 x 10 ft | Do you need more for lights? |
| Power | 1 to 2 outlets within 25 ft | Dedicated circuit? |
| Setup time | 1 to 2 hours | When do you need access? |
| Chairs | 1 to 2 | Do you need a stool? |

</div>

Check that the table height works with the DJ's gear and whether they bring their own table. A few use a booth with a front panel, which saves you a rental.

## What a band needs

A band adds floor space and gear. A 4-piece band needs around 10 x 12 feet. A larger band or a horn section needs more, often a stage. Ask for a stage plot and a tech rider, which show where each instrument goes and how much power it takes. Then reserve:

- A 6-foot table at the side for the sound mixer or merchandise.
- A table or two for the band's drinks and a meal.
- Clear access from the load-in door to the stage.

## Where to place the setup

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Pick the end of the dance floor</strong> with a wall behind the setup.</li>
<li><strong>Point speakers at the dance floor</strong> not at the head table.</li>
<li><strong>Keep it 20 to 30 ft from the nearest guest table</strong> Louder at the dance floor, calmer at dinner tables.</li>
<li><strong>Check the power</strong> Use a circuit that is not shared with the kitchen.</li>
<li><strong>Check the load-in route</strong> DJ gear travels through doors and halls.</li>
</ol>

The goal is a sound that fills the dance floor without blasting the grandparents. Ask your venue whether there is a sound limit. Many Chicago venues, especially hotels and event spaces near residential areas, have noise rules or curfews, and your DJ should know them.

## Dress the table

A DJ table with a floor-length black cloth hides cables and boxes. You can add a branded front panel or a floral garland at the front. Do not drape fabric over vents, speakers or lights. Fans and heat matter, so leave the cloth short at the back.

## Safety and cables

Tape cables across walkways with gaffer tape. Keep drinks off the table. Weigh the table with equipment: a 6-foot folding table can hold 200 to 300 pounds on a rating, but ask the rental company about the specific table, since weight limits differ.

## Chairs for the band

Bands often bring their own seats. If they ask, a simple [bentwood chair](/chairs/bentwood) is sturdy and quiet. Count them in your [chair total](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Common problems and quick fixes

- **The DJ table is too short.** A single 6-foot table fits a DJ with a laptop and controller, but lights, a mixer and a case can overflow. Rent a second table at the end so they can spread out.
- **No power near the stage.** Extension cords are fine if rated for the load, but tape them down. Ask the venue where the nearest outlet is during your walkthrough.
- **The table blocks the dance floor.** Place the DJ against a wall so the whole floor is open.
- **Speakers aimed at the head table.** Ask your DJ to point speakers toward the dance floor and test the volume from the head table.
- **The band has no table for drinks.** A small 30-inch round at the side keeps glasses off the mixer.

## Timeline for the setup

Gear usually arrives one to two hours before guests, and a band needs more time for a sound check. Tell your venue what time the band or DJ needs access, and make sure the tables and linens are in place before they arrive. A DJ who arrives to find no table loses setup time.

## A note on lighting

Uplights and dance lights add heat and cords. They need power too, so ask your DJ how many outlets their lighting uses. Lighting and power rentals are part of the whole event plan; our guide to lighting and power covers the details.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size table does a DJ need?

A 6-foot banquet table (72 x 30 inches) is standard. Some need two. Ask your DJ.

### How much space does a DJ need?

About 10 x 10 feet including the table, speakers and a place to stand.

### Where should the DJ set up at a wedding?

At one end of the dance floor, with a wall behind and speakers facing the floor.

### How much space does a band need?

A 4-piece band needs about 10 x 12 feet. Larger bands need a stage.

### Do I need to feed the DJ or band?

Many contracts require a meal. Check your contract and tell your caterer.

### Does my venue have noise rules?

Possibly. Ask your venue about sound limits and end times. Check with the village, park district or building.
