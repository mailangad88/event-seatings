**Quick answer**

Make a list of what you need, ask for quotes from at least three vendors in each category, and compare them line by line. Ask about availability, what is included, insurance, backup plans and cancellation terms. Check reviews and ask for references. Get everything in a written contract that lists the date, time, price, deposit and what happens if something changes, before you pay a deposit.

Hiring vendors is where events succeed or stumble. A great caterer or DJ makes the day, while a late or unclear one adds stress for weeks. Most trouble comes from vague agreements, not bad people. This guide shows how to find vendors, how to compare quotes honestly and what to put in writing, so you are protected and so are they.

## Start with what you need, not who you know

Write down the services the event requires: venue, food, drinks, rentals, music, photos, decor, staff and permits. Add the date, hours and headcount to each. A clear brief gets better quotes and prevents miscommunication.

## Where to find vendors

Ask friends and family who have hosted recently, ask your venue for its preferred or approved vendor list, and search local directories and social media. A venue's list may be required, so ask early. A single rental company can often cover chairs, tables, linens, tents and dishes, which cuts down the number of contracts. Our guide on [one-call planning versus many vendors](/blog/one-call-planning-vs-many-vendors) compares the approaches.

## Ask the right questions

Whatever the category, ask:

1. Are you available on my date and for my hours?
2. What exactly is included in the price?
3. What is not included, and what do extras cost?
4. How many events do you handle on the same day?
5. Do you carry liability insurance, and can you give the venue a certificate? See [certificate of insurance for event rentals](/blog/certificate-of-insurance-event-rentals).
6. What is your deposit, payment schedule and cancellation policy?
7. What is your backup plan if staff or equipment fails?
8. Who will be the day-of contact?

For rentals, use [questions to ask an event rental company](/blog/questions-to-ask-an-event-rental-company).

## Compare quotes fairly

Put every quote into the same columns: price, inclusions, fees, hours, setup and teardown, overtime and deposit. [How to compare event rental quotes](/blog/how-to-compare-event-rental-quotes) walks through that for rentals, and the method works for other vendors too. The cheapest quote is often missing something, and the most expensive may include extras you don't need.

## Check reviews and ask for references

Read recent reviews on several sites, look at full galleries instead of highlights, and ask for the contact of a recent client. Be careful about claims you cannot verify. Do not pay for a vendor's name alone.

## Read the contract

Look for the date, time, address, services, total, payment schedule, cancellation terms, force majeure language, overtime rates and substitution rules. See [how to read an event rental contract](/blog/how-to-read-an-event-rental-contract) and [event rental red flags](/blog/event-rental-red-flags).

## Confirm and share

A week before, confirm the arrival time, access, contact, parking and final count with each vendor. Share one master timeline so vendors know how their work connects. A shared contact sheet lets them reach each other.

## Chicago-area notes

In the Chicago area, summer, fall and December weekends book early, so ask about your date first. Some Chicago venues require vendors to carry insurance and provide a certificate, and may restrict loading times or freight elevator use. Delivery to a high-rise or a historic block takes planning. Suburban village halls and park districts may have approved lists or rules about alcohol, tents or amplified sound, so check with your village or park district before you hire. Always compare travel or delivery fees, since distance within the metro area changes cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find good event vendors?

Ask recent hosts, check your venue's preferred list and read recent reviews. Request a quote from at least three vendors and compare them line by line. Ask for references.

### How many quotes should I get?

Three per major category is a common standard. It gives you a sense of range without overwhelming you. For smaller services, two may be enough.

### What should be in a vendor contract?

The date, time, location, services, price, deposit and payment schedule, overtime rates, cancellation terms and substitution rules. It should also name a day-of contact. Ask for changes in writing.

### How much deposit do vendors usually require?

It varies. Many vendors request a deposit to hold the date, often a quarter to half of the total, with the balance closer to the event. Ask for their schedule and refund policy.

### What are red flags when hiring a vendor?

No written contract, pressure to pay in cash only, unclear pricing, no insurance and poor communication are common warnings. If something feels vague, ask for it in writing.

### Should I tip event vendors?

Practices vary by service, and some contracts include a service charge. Ask whether gratuity is included, then decide based on service. Bring cash or have envelopes ready if you plan to tip.

### When should I book event vendors?

Book major vendors as soon as you know your date and headcount. Venues and caterers often go first. Smaller services can wait a bit, but popular dates fill quickly.
