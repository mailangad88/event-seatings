**Quick answer**

A Vietnamese wedding often includes a family ceremony with an ancestral altar, a tea ceremony and a procession in which the groom's family visits the bride's home. For the setup, plan an altar table with a cloth, chairs for the parents and elders facing the couple, a tea table, kneeling cushions if used and a clear floor of about 10 x 12 feet. The details vary by region and family, so ask both sets of parents what to include before you rent.

Vietnamese-American weddings in the Chicago area often blend traditions from Vietnam with American wedding customs. A common pattern is a morning home or hall ceremony with family, followed by a larger reception in the evening. The morning event can include an engagement ceremony with gifts, an offering at the family altar and a tea ceremony where the couple serves tea to their elders. This guide focuses on the setup and leaves the order of events to your families.

## What the room needs

- **An altar table.** A table covered with a cloth for incense, fruit, flowers and photos, if the family uses one. The altar is often against a wall with a few feet of clear floor in front.
- **A tea table and tray.** A small table to the side for the tea set.
- **Elder chairs.** Armchairs or sturdy chairs for the parents and honored elders, set in a row or an arc facing the couple.
- **Kneeling cushions or a clear floor.** Some couples kneel or bow in front of the altar.
- **Guest seating.** Chairs along the walls or behind the elders.
- **A photographer path.** Leave a clear lane on one side.

## Room size and flow

A living room of 12 x 16 feet fits an altar, six elder chairs and a clear floor for the couple. For a bigger family, use a banquet room or a hall. The processions and gift presentation need a clear path from the entrance to the altar, so remove coffee tables and rugs that trip people. If your parents' home is small, a second room can hold the gift trays and the family waiting to enter.

## The reception that follows

The evening reception is often a banquet with many courses, rounds of 10, a stage for the couple and a dance floor. Plan the same way as other large receptions: group family tables, size the dance floor and add a gift table. ## Chicago notes

- Many Chicago-area families hold the morning ceremony at a parent's home. Ask whether rentals can be delivered the evening before and picked up the next day.
- Check building rules for apartments, including elevators and delivery times.
- Incense, candles and open flame are part of some altar traditions. Check with your landlord or venue and follow fire safety rules.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Vietnamese tea ceremony?

It is part of the traditional wedding where the couple serves tea to their elders and honors their ancestors. Families vary in how they hold it. Ask both sets of parents.

### Do I need an altar?

Many families have one in the home already. If you hold the ceremony in a hall, you may need a table and decorations for it.

### How many chairs do I need?

Four to eight chairs for elders and 10 to 40 for guests. Count the people being honored.

### Where do I hold the ceremony?

Often at a parent's home, but a banquet room works too. Choose a space large enough for the family.

### Can I use incense indoors?

Ask the landlord, building or venue first. Fire alarms can trip, and some venues ban incense.

### Do I need to rent a tea set?

Most families use their own. Rental companies may offer tea service for the reception.
