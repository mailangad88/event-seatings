**Quick answer**

Provide one serving utensil per dish, matched to the food: tongs for salads, bread and grilled items, slotted spoons for vegetables, solid spoons for rice and potatoes, ladles for soup and sauce, and a spatula or lifter for lasagna. Add one spare utensil for every 4 hot dishes and one extra tongs per 50 guests. A 100-guest, 6-dish buffet needs about 8 to 10 utensils plus a few extras.

</div>

Utensils are the cheapest part of a catering list and the one that gets forgotten. Then someone pokes a roast with a soup ladle, or a guest uses a clean fork from their plate to spear a meatball. A good serving set is part of food safety, keeps the line moving and makes the table look finished. Here is the full checklist.

## How many utensils to rent

The number is nearly the number of dishes, but you need some extras for the real world.

- **One utensil per dish.** Never share a utensil between two foods, because of allergens and flavors.
- **One spare for every 4 hot dishes**, kept in the kitchen in a labeled bin.
- **One extra tongs per 50 guests** for the salad, bread and dessert, which see the most traffic.
- **Two ladles for soup or sauce stations**, because ladles drop into the pot.
- **A long-handled spoon** (12 to 14 inches) for deep chafers so no one burns a wrist.

So a 100-guest buffet with salad, two proteins, two sides, a vegetable and bread needs 7 utensils plus 2 to 3 spares, or about 10 total. If you run a double-sided line, double the working utensils, because both sides serve the same dishes. See [how many chafing dishes you need](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet) for the matching dish count.

## Food safety details

Utensils should rest on a small plate, in the dish with the handle out of the food, or on a clean tray, never directly on the tablecloth. Replace any dropped utensil immediately. If a utensil sits in a dish, the handle should not touch the food. Handles that sink into hot dishes are a classic cause of cross-contact and burns. For hot and cold holding temperatures, see [food safety at home parties](/blog/food-safety-at-home-parties).

Keep a labeled tub of used utensils in the back of house, where a staff member or helper can swap in clean ones every hour for long events.

## Materials and style

Stainless steel is the standard for catering and can go in a dishwasher. Silver-plated pieces look finer at weddings. Wood and bamboo suit casual picnics but can absorb flavors. Plastic is acceptable for kids' parties or for service where heat is low. Match the finish to your chair and flatware style: warm gold flatware goes well with [gold infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity), while matte stainless fits [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back).

## Chicago and suburb notes

Banquet halls and park district shelters often provide a basic kitchen but not serving ware, so confirm whether the venue includes utensils before you rent. Church halls may supply serving spoons and large pans but not tongs. For outdoor events, bring covered utensil holders to keep flies off in July and August.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many serving utensils do I need for a buffet?

One per dish, plus one spare for every 4 hot dishes and an extra pair of tongs for every 50 guests. A six-dish buffet usually needs 9 or 10 pieces.

### What is the best utensil for a pasta buffet?

Tongs or a pasta fork. Spoons let strands slip, and a slotted spoon leaves sauce behind. A ladle handles the sauce.

### Should each dish have its own utensil?

Yes. Sharing utensils spreads allergens and moves flavors between dishes. Label each one with the dish name if the buffet is crowded.

### Where should serving utensils sit?

On a small plate beside the dish, or in the dish with the handle out of the food. Never directly on the tablecloth.

### What serving utensils do I need for dessert?

A cake knife and server, tongs for cookies, and a small spatula for bars or brownies. Put them on a plate near the dessert plates.

### Do rental companies provide serving utensils?

Some do, and some caterers bring their own. Ask your caterer and venue first, then [request a quote](/quote) for whatever is left.
