In many South Asian traditions, relatives apply a paste of turmeric to the bride or groom before the wedding as a blessing, and the event often turns into laughter, photos and a lot of yellow. ## What a haldi is, and how it varies

Haldi is a pre-wedding ritual in many Hindu and some other South Asian communities. Turmeric paste, sometimes mixed with sandalwood, rosewater, milk or oil, is applied to the skin by family members, often in a set order beginning with elders or married women. The turmeric is believed to bless, purify or brighten, and the ritual is known by different names, such as pithi in Gujarati and Rajasthani families, or gaye holud in Bengali weddings. Some families hold separate ceremonies for each side, others hold one. Ask your elders what is done, who participates and whether any prayer or song accompanies it.

The setup below is meant to be flexible. Treat the specifics, including the number of people applying paste and the ritual items, as something your family will confirm.

## Supplies checklist

**For the ceremony.** A low stool, chauki or decorated seat, bowls of turmeric paste, fresh marigold and other flowers, mango leaves or other leaves if your tradition uses them, a bowl of water, and clean towels. Brass or steel bowls and trays are common; some families use small matching dishes.

**To protect the space.** Plastic sheeting or a washable runner under the seat, a tarp or flooring if you are on grass, and old sheets on low tables. Turmeric stains porous surfaces, fabrics and some stone, so ask the venue before you start.

**For guests.** Chairs for elders, shade or heat, water, snacks, and a spot for shoes. Provide hand towels and wipes, and a trash bin.

**For the person of honor.** A robe or old outfit to wear, a change of clothes, a place to shower or clean up, and a plan for the wedding makeup later.

**For photos.** A backdrop of flowers or fabric, a spot for the photographer, and something dry to hold on to between rounds.

## Running the ceremony

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Prepare the space.</strong> Lay the protective covering and set the seat, backdrop and flowers.</li>
<li><strong>Seat elders first.</strong> Give them chairs near the center, with a clear view.</li>
<li><strong>Begin with the family order.</strong> Follow the order your family uses, often elders and married relatives first.</li>
<li><strong>Let the fun start.</strong> Once the formal part is done, photos and playful applications often follow.</li>
<li><strong>Clean up the person of honor.</strong> Towels and a private place to rinse, with fresh clothes ready.</li>
<li><strong>Reset the space.</strong> Remove covers, bowls and flowers and wash any stained items quickly.</li>
</ol>

## Keeping the mess manageable

Use organic or cosmetic grade turmeric and test the paste on a small patch of skin first, since some people have sensitive skin or allergies. Keep paste off carpets and light stone. Dress in clothes you do not mind staining, often white or yellow. Place a few extra towels around, and put wipes near the exit. Plan a short rest after the ceremony before hair and makeup, because turmeric can leave a faint tint on skin for a day. Ask the photographer to arrive with a lens cloth. Talk to the venue before bringing a large amount of turmeric or flower petals to their space, since cleanup fees are common.

## Chicago-area notes

Haldi is often held in a backyard, a courtyard, a hotel patio or a banquet hall. In the Chicago area, weather can swing quickly: a hot afternoon one day and a chilly morning the next. Have a tent or indoor backup, especially in spring and fall, and consider heaters in cooler months. See [what size tent do I need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) for sizing. If you are in a rental home or a venue, ask what cleaning rules apply to turmeric. Check with your village or park district if you plan a larger tent or gathering, and plan parking for guests who will arrive in wedding clothes.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do you need for a haldi ceremony?

You need a low seat, turmeric paste, flowers or leaves, bowls, towels, water, and a protected surface. Add chairs for elders, shade, refreshments and a changing area. Ask your family which ritual items they use.

### How long does a haldi ceremony last?

Most haldi ceremonies run about one to two hours, including photos and the playful part. The formal ritual itself is often shorter. Plan extra time for cleanup and getting ready afterward.

### Who applies the haldi?

It depends on the family. Often elders and married women go first, followed by other relatives and friends. Ask your family for the traditional order.

### What should you wear to a haldi ceremony?

Many people wear yellow, white or other bright colors they do not mind staining. Wear comfortable, washable clothes and bring a change. Avoid delicate fabrics.

### Can a haldi be held indoors?

Yes, with protection for floors and furniture. Check with your venue first, because turmeric can stain. A tarp, plastic sheeting and a washable runner help.

### How many guests are at a haldi?

Many are smaller than the wedding, often 20 to 60 close family and friends, though some are much larger. Decide based on your space and family customs.

### How do you remove turmeric stains?

Rinse quickly with cold water, then wash with detergent and, if possible, dry in sunlight, which helps fade the color. Test any treatment on a hidden spot. Delicate or rented items should be returned as instructed.

### When is the haldi held?

Usually the morning of the wedding or a day or two before, depending on the community. Pick a time with good light and enough time to rest before the main events.
