**Quick answer**

Put the photo booth near the lounge or the cocktail area, 15 to 20 feet from the DJ speakers and off the main walkway. Give it a 10 x 10 foot footprint (more for a 360 platform), 10 feet of queue space in front, a nearby outlet and soft light. Never place it where its line blocks the bar, the buffet or an exit.

</div>

A photo booth is one of the most popular add-ons at weddings, graduations and corporate events, and one of the most common causes of a crowded room. It attracts a line, makes noise and needs power, so where you put it matters as much as which booth you pick. A good spot adds energy; a bad one blocks the bar.

## How much space

<div class="table-wrap">

| Type | Footprint | Queue space | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enclosed booth | About 4 x 6 ft, plus 3 ft clear | 6 to 8 ft | Fits tight corners |
| Open-air booth with backdrop | 8 x 8 ft | 8 to 10 ft | Most common, up to 6 to 8 people at once |
| Mirror booth | About 4 x 8 ft | 8 to 10 ft | Tall and heavy; needs a flat path |
| 360 platform | 10 x 10 ft or more | 10 to 15 ft | Needs ceiling or outdoor clearance |

</div>

Footprints vary by provider, so ask yours for dimensions and add 2 to 3 feet on each side. Our guides to [photo booth types](/blog/photo-booth-types-open-air-360-mirror) and [photo booth space and power needs](/blog/photo-booth-space-and-power-needs) cover each type.

## Where it goes and where it does not

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Near the lounge.</strong> Guests waiting for their turn can sit and talk.</li>
<li><strong>Away from the DJ.</strong> The booth host needs to hear guests, and the DJ's speakers do not fit with it.</li>
<li><strong>Off the main route.</strong> The queue must not cross the bar, buffet or doors.</li>
<li><strong>Near power.</strong> A wall outlet within 10 feet; keep cords out of the aisle.</li>
<li><strong>Soft, even light.</strong> Avoid bright windows or flashing dance lights behind it.</li>
</ol>

Also avoid dead ends. A booth in a hallway with no space behind it looks cramped, and a booth beside a dance floor can get bumped by dancers. See [lounge layout by guest count](/blog/lounge-layout-by-guest-count) and [selfie station ideas](/blog/selfie-station-ideas-for-events).

## The queue

A typical open-air booth session lasts 1 to 2 minutes per group. At a 100-guest party, a queue of 5 to 8 groups is normal in the first hour. Plan for 10 feet of queue in front of the booth. Use stanchions or a rug to define it. If you have a 360 platform, add a second queue lane, since sessions take longer.

## Timing

Put the booth in use during cocktail hour and again after dinner. Many events place it in a room away from the ballroom, which gives guests something to do while the room is flipped. Check the contract for hours and set-up time, and ask the provider when they arrive and leave. For events with a room flip, see [ceremony to reception room flip layout](/blog/ceremony-to-reception-room-flip-layout).

## Choosing between booth types for your room

The right booth depends on the room as much as the budget. An enclosed booth fits a corner and hides the line, which suits a small room. An open-air booth with a backdrop is the best all-rounder, because groups of six or more can fit, but it needs a flat 8 x 8 foot patch and a clear background. A mirror booth is a showpiece and draws a crowd, but it is tall and heavy, so check door widths and the route from the loading dock. A 360 platform is the most dramatic and needs the most space overhead and around the platform, so it often works best in a tent, a high-ceiling ballroom or on a patio.

Ask the provider three questions before you place it: what are the exact dimensions with props, how many outlets and what amperage does it need, and how long does setup and takedown take. A booth that needs 45 minutes to set up should arrive while the room is being flipped, not while guests are walking in. Add the provider to your delivery schedule and tell the venue so they can unlock the door and point out an outlet.

## Backdrop, props and chairs

A backdrop gives the booth a place; see [photo backdrop ideas for weddings and parties](/blog/photo-backdrop-ideas-for-weddings-and-parties) and [flower wall rental guide](/blog/flower-wall-rental-guide). Add a prop table, a bench or a couple of chairs for groups. A [rattan garden chair](/chairs/rattan-garden) or [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) adds a styled look that works well in photos. Also put a trash can nearby for prop packaging.

## Venue and Chicago notes

Ask your venue about outlet locations and floor loading limits, especially for a 360 platform or a mirror booth, which is heavy. Many Chicago venues require a certificate of insurance from vendors; ask who handles it. For outdoor events, protect the booth from rain and direct sun, and do not run cords across a lawn without covers; see [outdoor extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should a photo booth go at a wedding?

Near the lounge or cocktail area, 15 to 20 feet from the DJ speakers and off the main walkway, with queue space in front.

### How much space does a photo booth need?

An open-air booth needs about 8 x 8 feet plus 10 feet of queue space. A 360 platform needs 10 x 10 or more.

### Does a photo booth need power?

Yes, usually one or two outlets. Place it within 10 feet of a wall outlet or plan a generator.

### Can I put a photo booth next to the dance floor?

It is not ideal. Dancers can bump the booth and the lights compete. Keep it a few tables away.

### How long should a photo booth be open?

Many events run it for two to three hours, often from cocktail hour through the first part of dancing.

### Do I need a backdrop?

Not required, but it helps. Choose a solid or textured backdrop to keep the focus on guests.
