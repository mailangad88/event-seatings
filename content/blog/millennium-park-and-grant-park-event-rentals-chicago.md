Millennium Park and Grant Park anchor Chicago's downtown lakefront, and they host everything from corporate picnics to large fundraisers and public festivals. Both are run by the Chicago Park District, which means both come with permit rules, site restrictions and a process you need to follow before you can bring in rentals. Here's what to plan for.

## Two parks, one permit system

Millennium Park and Grant Park sit next to each other downtown, and both fall under the Chicago Park District's permit system for special events.

## Cloud Gate and the Great Lawn

Cloud Gate, often called "The Bean," is one of the most photographed landmarks in the city and sits in the middle of Millennium Park, which means heavy foot traffic nearby at almost any hour. Grant Park's Great Lawn is the open ground used for bigger festivals and large gatherings, and it's a common choice for corporate picnics and fundraisers that need open space. Both areas draw a lot of pedestrian and tourist traffic, so plan your seating layout and delivery route with that in mind.

## Wind and weather on open ground

Both parks sit right along the lakefront with very little shelter, so wind is a real planning factor for tents, table linens and any lightweight decor. Check our [lakefront wind guide](/blog/chicago-lakefront-event-wind-planning) before you finalize your layout, and have a plan for securing anything that can blow over. For cold-season events, our [winter event planning guide](/blog/chicago-winter-event-planning) covers additional considerations.

## Street closures and downtown loading

Larger events in Millennium Park or Grant Park sometimes require street closures or coordinated loading zones, since both parks border busy downtown streets. Your Park District permit process will usually flag this, but it helps to plan your delivery window early. ## Booking timing

Both parks book up fast for spring through fall, especially weekends and any date near a major festival already scheduled downtown. Apply for your permit well ahead of your date, and lock in your rental order once your layout is confirmed. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit to host an event in Millennium Park or Grant Park?

Yes. Both parks are managed by the Chicago Park District, and organized events need a Special Event Permit, with extra requirements for tents, alcohol or amplified sound.

### Can I set up near Cloud Gate for a private event?

Cloud Gate sits in a high-traffic public area, so private events are typically set up elsewhere in the park. Check with the Park District about approved event sites.

### Is Grant Park's Great Lawn good for a large corporate event?

Yes, it's commonly used for large gatherings and festivals because of its open space. You'll still need a permit and a plan for wind and loading access.

### How windy does it get in these parks?

Both sit along the open lakefront with little shelter, so wind can be significant, especially in spring and fall. Plan to secure tents and linens.

### Will I need to worry about street closures?

For larger events, possibly. Downtown streets border both parks, and big events sometimes require closures or coordinated loading zones.

### How far ahead should I book rentals for a park event?

As soon as your permit and site are confirmed. Popular weekend dates from spring through fall fill up months in advance.
