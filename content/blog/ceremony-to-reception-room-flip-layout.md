**Quick answer**

A room flip turns ceremony chair rows into reception tables while guests are at cocktail hour. Plan 60 to 90 minutes of crew time, a separate cocktail space, chairs that stack or move fast, and a marked floor plan for both layouts. Ask your venue how long its team needs and who does the work.

</div>

Many Chicago-area weddings use one room for both the ceremony and the reception, such as a ballroom, loft, barn or tent. It saves money and keeps the day compact, but it asks the room to do two jobs. The trick is the flip, the hour or so during cocktail hour when the room changes. A good flip is invisible; a bad one leaves guests waiting in a hallway.

## What moves, and where it goes

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Stage the reception.</strong> Tables, linens and place settings staged in a corner or side room before the ceremony.</li>
<li><strong>Seat the ceremony.</strong> Chairs set in rows, aisle runner down, arch in place.</li>
<li><strong>Clear the chairs.</strong> The crew stacks and carts the ceremony chairs, ideally 10 at a time.</li>
<li><strong>Set the tables.</strong> Rounds in position, then linens, chairs, place settings and centerpieces.</li>
<li><strong>Check and open.</strong> Lights, candles, music on; the planner or venue lead gives the go-ahead.</li>
</ol>

The ceremony chairs often become the reception chairs. If they match, the crew moves them straight to the tables. If they do not, you need two chair sets and extra storage. Our guide to [ceremony vs reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs) explains the choice, and [stackable vs non-stackable chairs](/blog/stackable-vs-non-stackable-chairs) shows how fast each moves.

## Time and crew

| Guests | Typical crew | Time to flip |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 75 | 4 to 6 | 45 to 60 min |
| 100 to 150 | 6 to 10 | 60 to 75 min |
| 200 to 250 | 10 to 14 | 75 to 90 min |
| 300 or more | 14 or more | 90 min or a second room |

These are rough planning numbers. Ask your venue or rental company for theirs, and ask who is responsible: some venues flip with their own staff, others expect your rental company, and some need both. For who does what, see [chair setup and breakdown](/blog/chair-setup-and-breakdown-who-does-what). Delivery windows and access in the city are covered in chicago venue rules for chair rentals.

## Cocktail hour space

Guests need somewhere to go for an hour. A foyer, a patio, a rooftop or a separate lounge works. If the weather is cold or wet, have a backup; see outdoor event chairs and Chicago weather. A cocktail layout is covered in [cocktail reception layout and standing ratios](/blog/cocktail-reception-layout-standing-ratio), and a seating section for older guests belongs there too.

## Making the flip faster

Most delays come from three things: not knowing where things go, carrying things the long way and waiting for someone with a key. Fix each one in advance. Give the crew a marked drawing with table numbers, so no one stops to ask. Stage tables, linens and chairs as close to the room as the venue allows, ideally in an adjoining space with a direct door. And tell the venue exactly when the crew can enter the room, so the doors are open the moment the last guest leaves.

Chair choice matters more than people expect. Chairs that stack in rows of 5 to 10 move on a cart, while chairs that must be carried one by one add 20 minutes to a 150-chair room. Linens should be pressed and folded by table so they can be placed in one pass. Centerpieces can sit on a cart, ready to go. If your ceremony includes an arch, aisle runner or signs, assign someone to remove them first, since they block the aisle that the carts need.

Finally, build a buffer. A ceremony that runs 15 minutes late eats the flip window, so ask the officiant and the photographer to keep the family photos out of the room if possible. Plan photographs in another space, and the room can flip while the couple is still taking portraits.

## Alternatives to a flip

- **Two rooms.** Hold the ceremony in one room, reception in another. No flip, but more rental chairs.
- **Ceremony in the round.** Use rounds as ceremony seating, then add the dining chairs. Works for small groups.
- **Seat guests at tables for the ceremony.** The couple stands at one end and guests turn their chairs. Fast, informal and used by small weddings.

## Chicago practicalities

Downtown ballrooms and loft buildings share freight elevators and narrow loading docks, so a flip means everything moves through a bottleneck. Ask your venue about elevator reservations, union rules and quiet hours. In the suburbs, a banquet hall may have a staff that flips daily, which makes it faster. For tents, store flipped chairs on the lawn and plan for weather.

## Chairs that flip well

Light, stackable chairs flip fastest. A [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) stacks and carts easily and works as a ceremony and reception chair. A [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) is a bit heavier but looks right in both settings. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to flip a room from ceremony to reception?

About 60 to 90 minutes for 100 to 200 guests, depending on crew size and layout. Ask your venue for its number.

### Who does the room flip?

Sometimes the venue, sometimes the rental company and sometimes a mix. Get it in writing.

### Can ceremony chairs be used at the reception?

Yes, if they match your table setup. The crew moves them to the tables during cocktail hour.

### Where do guests go during the flip?

To cocktail hour in another room, patio or foyer. Plan food, drinks and seating there.

### How many people does it take to flip a room?

About one crew member per 15 to 25 guests, plus a lead. Larger rooms need more.

### Is it better to have two separate rooms?

If the budget allows, two rooms avoid the flip. But a flip is cheaper and keeps the day compact.
