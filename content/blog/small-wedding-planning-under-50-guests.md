**Quick answer**

A small wedding of under 50 guests works best in a space that fits the group comfortably: a restaurant private room, a home, a garden or a small venue. Budget per guest rather than by category, so you can spend more on food and experience. Use five to six round tables, a small dance area and short timeline. Book three to six months ahead.

</div>

A small wedding is not a shrunken big one. With fewer people, you can spend more per guest, choose from venues that large weddings cannot use and shape the day around conversation rather than production. The flip side is that fixed costs like photography and music do not shrink, and the room must feel full without feeling crowded.

## Why small works

With fewer than 50 guests, you can host in places that are not an option for larger groups: a restaurant private room, a gallery, a garden, a family home or a small hall. These spaces often cost less than traditional venues and feel warmer. You can also invest in better food, a better photographer or a better band, because the guest-based costs are lower. See [micro wedding rentals](/blog/micro-wedding-rentals) for a rental perspective, and [how to cut a wedding guest list](/blog/how-to-cut-a-wedding-guest-list) if you are still deciding who is invited.

## Budgeting per guest

Start with the total you can spend and divide by your guest count to get a per-guest figure. This helps compare restaurants, caterers and venues. Fixed costs such as photography, music and officiant do not scale down, so a small wedding may have a higher per-guest cost than a large one. That is normal. Read [how to set a wedding budget](/blog/how-to-set-a-wedding-budget) and [how much a wedding costs in Chicago](/blog/how-much-does-a-wedding-cost-in-chicago) for ways to build the numbers.

## Layout and rentals

For 40 to 50 guests, five or six round tables of eight to ten work well. Add a sweetheart table or a head table, a small dance area, a bar and a cake table. Consider long farm tables for a family-style feel; see [long tables vs round tables](/blog/long-tables-vs-round-tables-wedding). Allow room for service paths, about three to four feet between chairs from adjacent tables. If you hold the ceremony in the same room, plan a flip; see [ceremony to reception flip](/blog/ceremony-to-reception-flip). Check [floor plan for 50 guests](/blog/floor-plan-for-50-guests) for more detail.

## Making a small wedding feel special

Invest in moments that people remember. Personalize the menu, seat guests with people they will enjoy, add a toast from each family and create a space for conversation. Skip filler traditions you do not care about. A small guest list lets you give each person a short note or a place card with a message. Because the group is small, everything is visible, so details like lighting, music volume and seating comfort matter.

## Menu, music and timeline for a small group

With a small group, plated or family-style dining can feel intimate and is often easier to serve than a large buffet. Ask your caterer or restaurant about set menus with a few choices. For music, a solo musician, a small trio or a well-planned playlist can fit the room better than a full band. Keep the volume low enough for conversation.

A simple timeline works well: ceremony of 20 to 30 minutes, a cocktail hour, a seated dinner with toasts, a short dance and cake. The whole event may last four to five hours. Because the group is small, you can speak with each guest, so build in time for that. Use the [wedding day timeline template](/blog/wedding-day-timeline-template) to adapt the schedule and share it with your vendors.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago has many small-wedding options: restaurant private rooms, lofts, small halls, church halls, park pavilions and backyards. Smaller venues book on weekends too, so reserve early. If you host in a backyard or park, check with your village or park district about permits, noise rules and tent requirements; see [Chicago tent permit](/blog/chicago-tent-permit). Winter small weddings can be cozy and affordable but need coat racks and a plan for snow. Parking at small venues is often limited, so tell guests about transit and rideshare options. If the group includes children or older relatives, add a few comfortable chairs with arms and a quiet corner away from the music. In a small room, every seat is close to the action, so think about sightlines for toasts and the first dance before you finalize the layout. Finally, remember that a small wedding can still include every tradition you value. You can keep the first dance, the cake cutting and the toasts, and simply shorten each one. Choosing which traditions to keep, rather than following a full script, is one of the quiet benefits of planning a smaller day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a small wedding cost?

It depends on venue, food and vendors. Costs per guest may be higher than at a large wedding because fixed costs do not shrink. Get quotes and compare totals.

### How many tables do I need for 40 guests?

Five round tables of eight is common. Add a sweetheart table or head table if you want one, plus tables for the bar and cake.

### How far in advance should I plan a small wedding?

Three to six months is common, and longer for popular dates. Small venues also book up.

### Where can I have a small wedding in Chicago?

Options include restaurant private rooms, small halls, lofts, church halls, park pavilions and backyards. Confirm capacity and rules.

### Do I need a planner for a small wedding?

Not always, but a day-of coordinator can help. See [wedding planner vs day-of coordinator](/blog/wedding-planner-vs-day-of-coordinator).

### What is the best timeline for a small wedding?

A short timeline with a ceremony, cocktail hour and dinner works well. Plan about four to five hours total, with time for toasts and a short dance.

### Can I have a small wedding with a big party later?

Yes. Some couples marry privately or with close family and host a larger casual party later.
