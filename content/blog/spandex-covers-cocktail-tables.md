**Quick answer**

Spandex covers are stretch fabric sleeves that fit a highboy cocktail table without ties or wrinkles. Most fit a 30-inch round highboy; check your table diameter and height before ordering. They suit receptions, lakefront terraces and windy tents, where a loose tablecloth would flap. A 120-inch round cloth tied at the waist is the alternative when you want a softer, draped look.

Cocktail tables are the small tables where guests stand for the first hour. They are also the hardest tables to dress: they are tall, narrow, and often outdoors. A loose cloth sails off in a Chicago wind or leaves the table legs showing. Spandex fixes both, and the right size is easy to confirm in advance.

## How to size a spandex cover

Spandex is cut to the table, so you need three numbers.

1. **Top diameter.** Common cocktail tops are 24, 30 and 36 inches.
2. **Height.** Highboys are typically 42 inches, though some are 36 to 48.
3. **Shape.** Round or square. Most are round.

Ask your rental provider for the table specs, then match the cover. A cover rated for 30 inches will strain on a 36-inch top. For general table sizes, see our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

## Where spandex works best

- **Outdoor receptions.** Wind off Lake Michigan or an open field makes loose cloth a chore.
- **Standing cocktail hours.** Guests lean on the table; a stretch cover stays put.
- **Modern venues.** Lofts and galleries suit the clean line.
- **Head count heavy events.** Covers go on quickly, which saves setup time.

## Where a cloth looks better

- **Vintage or romantic styling.** A draped cloth with a ribbon or sash has more softness.
- **Patterns.** Lace, floral or sequin overlays are cloth only.
- **Layers.** If you plan an overlay, a base cloth gives it something to sit on.

## Dress it up

A spandex cover is plain by design. Add interest with:

- A small floral arrangement or a candle in a hurricane glass
- A cloth napkin stack for the cocktail hour
- A table topper or runner in a second color
- A sash or ribbon around the waist for contrast

See [overlays and layered linens](/blog/overlays-and-layered-linens) for combinations.

## Plan the count

Cocktail tables are a ratio, not a rule. As a rule of thumb, plan one cocktail table for every 8 to 10 guests who will stand during cocktail hour, then adjust for room size. Confirm the number with your venue and caterer, since they may supply tables. The [linen ordering checklist](/blog/linen-ordering-checklist) shows how to add them to your order.

## Chicago planning notes

Lakefront and rooftop venues can be windy even in summer, so spandex wins there. Heated tents in fall and winter often mean tighter spaces, which also favors the compact look. Ask your venue whether it has house cocktail tables and covers; if not, they must come with your rental list.

## Common problems and how to avoid them

A few predictable issues come up with fitted covers, and almost all of them are fixed before the day.

- **Wrong top size.** The most common error. A cover for a 30-inch top will not close on a 36-inch top, and one that is too big bags at the edge. Get the diameter from your table supplier in writing.
- **Wrong height.** A cover made for a 36-inch table will look short on a 42-inch highboy. Ask for height as well as width.
- **Visible base.** Some highboys have a wide round base that sticks out. Check how the cover handles it, or add a table skirt.
- **Wrinkles from storage.** Covers shipped in a tight bag can arrive creased. Have someone smooth them on the table the morning of the event, and ask if they come pressed or steamed.
- **Color mismatch.** Stretch fabric often comes in a smaller color range than regular cloths. If you are matching a palette, ask for photos of the exact color.

## Spandex with other linens

Spandex can serve as a base layer, not just a finish. A solid ivory or white cover can sit under a runner, a small sequin topper or a square overlay set on a diagonal. The cover keeps the table clean and hides the base, while the top layer carries the color. This also lets you change the look between cocktail hour and dinner by swapping the topper. For layering rules, see our guide to overlays.

## Cocktail hour in Chicago weather

Cocktail hours often move between indoors and out, and guests stand for 45 to 60 minutes in whatever the weather gives. In spring and fall, a patio or tented cocktail area can be breezy, cool and damp. Fitted covers hold their shape, while a loose cloth can pick up damp from the ground and leave a wet hem. In summer heat, light colors are cooler to the touch. In winter, indoor cocktail spaces are tight, so slim covered tables take up less room than cloths with wide skirts. Whatever the season, ask your venue about the weather plan for the cocktail area and make sure the table count still works if it moves inside.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size spandex cover fits a cocktail table?

It must match the table top diameter and height. A common size fits a 30-inch round highboy about 42 inches tall. Confirm with your rental provider before ordering.

### Are spandex covers better than tablecloths for cocktail tables?

For wind and a sleek look, yes. For a soft, draped or patterned look, a tablecloth is better. Both can be styled with a topper.

### Can I put a tablecloth over spandex?

Yes. An overlay or runner on top of a spandex cover adds color or texture. Just make sure the cover is clean and snug first.

### Do spandex covers wrinkle?

They stretch smooth over the table and rarely wrinkle, which is one of their selling points. Stored folded, they may need a quick hand smooth.

### How many cocktail tables do I need?

A common rule of thumb is one for every 8 to 10 standing guests. Your room size and food service change the number, so check with your caterer.

### Can spandex covers be used outdoors?

Yes. They stay in place in wind, though they should not be left in heavy rain or direct dirt. Check your rental terms for outdoor use.
