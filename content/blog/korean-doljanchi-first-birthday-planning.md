In Korea, a baby's first birthday was historically a milestone because infant survival could not be taken for granted, and the celebration has remained a joyful family party. Today, many Korean and Korean American families hold a doljanchi with relatives, close friends and a photographer. The planning pain is a mix of tradition and toddler: a table that needs the right foods, a ceremony that needs the baby awake and calm, and a crowd that might include 40 adults and ten children. This guide shows how to handle both.

## What happens at a doljanchi

The baby wears a hanbok, a traditional Korean outfit, often with a hat and shoes. A decorated table called the doljasang is set with foods and symbolic objects. At the center of the ceremony is doljabi, in which the baby is placed in front of the table and picks one item. Each item stands for a future: thread for long life, a pencil or book for scholarship, money for wealth, a stethoscope or a microphone in modern versions, and so on. Families choose which objects to include and what they mean. Guests cheer, photograph and often bring gifts of gold rings (a custom in Korea) or money.

Traditional foods include baekseolgi (white steamed rice cake), songpyeon, sujebi (hand pulled dough soup) and colorful rice cakes, along with fruit, and a family meal that often includes miyeokguk (seaweed soup) and other Korean dishes. Details vary by region, family and religion. Some families hold a Christian prayer or skip certain foods. Ask your elders what is most important.

## Setting the doljasang

The doljasang is traditionally a low table, though many modern families use a regular table with a backdrop. A common layout includes a large rice cake arrangement in front, fruit stacks, a tiered display of colorful rice cakes, and the doljabi objects in a row or arc. Sets and arrangements can be bought, rented or made by a Korean bakery or party service, so ask your community for recommendations. For rental help, pick sturdy tables, floor-length linen in a plain color so the colorful foods stand out, and a backdrop stand. See [centerpiece risers and stands](/blog/centerpiece-risers-and-stands-guide) for height ideas.

Include a photo area. Many parents commission a custom photo backdrop with balloons, flowers and a banner with the baby's name and birth month. Include a small chair for the baby to sit in, a soft mat and a camera spot.

## The doljabi step by step

The baby is brought to the table, usually after the guests are seated and the baby has napped. The parents may place the baby in the center, step back and encourage the baby to reach for an item. It can take a few seconds or several minutes, and babies have their own ideas, so don't force it. Cheer when the baby chooses. Some families allow the baby to pick a second item. Then everyone takes photos. Keep it lighthearted, because the baby may cry, and that is also part of the story.

## Making it work for a one-year-old

Schedule around naps and feeding. Keep the formal portion short and bring snacks, a change of clothes and a quiet room for the baby. Have a plan for crowd noise. Seat grandparents with a good view, and ask someone to hold the baby between photos. Pick safe objects for doljabi (nothing sharp or small enough to swallow). For the outfit, check the fit and warmth beforehand. For gifts, keep a list and a plan for thank-you notes.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a doljanchi?

A doljanchi, or dol, is a Korean first birthday celebration. The baby wears a hanbok, family and friends gather, and the baby takes part in doljabi, picking an object from a table. The day marks the baby's first year.

### What is doljabi?

Doljabi is the ceremony in which the baby chooses one item from a table. Each item, such as thread, a pencil or money, symbolizes a possible future. Families choose their own items.

### What foods are on a doljasang?

Common foods include white steamed rice cake (baekseolgi), colorful rice cakes, songpyeon, fruit and sometimes sujebi. Families and regions differ, so ask elders.

### How many guests are at a doljanchi?

Anywhere from 20 close family members to 100 or more. Many Chicago-area families host 30 to 80 guests. Choose a number that fits the space and the budget.

### What should the baby wear for dol?

Many babies wear a hanbok with a hat and shoes, sometimes changing into a second outfit later. Choose a comfortable fit and check the weather.

### When should I send invitations for a doljanchi?

Three to four weeks ahead is typical, and earlier if relatives will travel. Include the time, location and any gift or dress notes.

### What gifts are given at a doljanchi?

Gold rings are traditional in Korea, and money in an envelope is common. Many guests also bring clothing or toys. Keep a list of gifts for thank-you notes.

### How long should a doljanchi last?

Two to three hours is usually enough, with the ceremony at the start and a meal afterward. Plan around naps and the baby's mood.
