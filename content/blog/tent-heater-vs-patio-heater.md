**Quick answer**

Use a tent heater (a forced-air or duct heater that sits outside and blows warm air in) to raise the air temperature inside a closed tent. Use patio heaters (radiant) in open spaces where wind blows warm air away. Propane patio heaters generally do not belong inside a tent unless the tent company and fire officials approve it. For any heater near fabric, get written approval from the tent company first.

</div>

Both types of heater make cold evenings bearable, and they work in very different ways. People often rent a patio heater for a tent, then discover the tent company will not allow it. This guide explains how each type works and which one fits your event.

## Two kinds of heat

**Forced-air heaters** warm the air. A fan pulls in air, heats it and sends it through a duct into the tent. The unit sits outside the tent, so combustion and exhaust stay outdoors. This approach works when the tent has sidewalls and the air can stay warm.

**Radiant heaters** warm people and objects directly, like sunshine. They work in open air because they do not need to heat the whole space, but they struggle in wind and need to be pointed at the guests.

## What the tent company will say

Most tent companies set their own heater rules, because tent fabric is flame retardant but not fireproof, and local fire officials may add requirements. A university guide to tent safety notes that heaters must keep combustible material at least 3 feet away, that propane heating equipment needs listing and proper clearances, and that tents with sides and propane heaters need detection. Rules like these vary by place, so ask your tent company and your fire marshal for your site's requirements.

Questions to ask:

1. Do you allow heaters inside the tent, outside only, or both?
2. Do you supply heating, or may I bring my own?
3. What clearances are required between the heater and the tent walls?
4. Who handles fuel and monitors the equipment?
5. Is a smoke or carbon monoxide detector required?
6. Do I need a permit or approval from the local fire department?

## Sizing a tent heater

Heater output is measured in BTUs per hour. The size you need depends on tent size, the outdoor temperature, wind, sidewalls and insulation. Suppliers use calculators that account for these, so give them the tent dimensions, the guest count, the coldest expected temperature, and whether the sides are solid or windowed. Do not guess. Underpowered heaters struggle and overpowered ones waste fuel.

Power for the fan and controls also matters: see [generator sizing](/blog/generator-size-for-events) and [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist).

## When patio heaters work best

If your event is on an open patio, a rooftop or a lawn without walls, patio heaters give the best return. Count them using [patio heaters per guest](/blog/patio-heaters-how-many-per-guests), place them between tables, and add windbreaks. Remember that radiant heaters are strongest right in front of the reflector.

## Tent lighting and heat

Lights and heaters share circuits, so plan them together. A string of LED bulbs is a tiny load, while one electric heater can be a big one. Our [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events) are light on power.

A Chicago spring or fall evening can drop 20 degrees from afternoon, and lakefront sites add wind. Plan on heat from September through early May for any evening event. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a patio heater inside a tent?

Usually not. Open-flame propane patio heaters inside tents are restricted or banned by many tent companies and fire officials. Ask for written approval before you try.

### What is the best way to heat a tent?

A forced-air heater placed outside with a duct into the tent is the usual approach, because the combustion and exhaust stay outdoors.

### How many BTUs do I need to heat a tent?

It depends on tent size, outside temperature, wind and insulation. Provide those details to your supplier, who will size it for you.

### Are electric heaters safer than propane?

They avoid open flame and exhaust, but they draw heavy power and still get hot. Keep them clear of fabric and use proper circuits.

### Do I need a carbon monoxide detector?

For fuel-burning equipment near an enclosed tent, many safety guides call for detection, and some fire officials require it. Ask your tent company.

### Who should handle propane tanks?

A trained vendor, away from guests and outside the tent. Do not swap tanks while guests are nearby.
