**Quick answer**

For a graduation party, rent seats for about half your guests, one food table per 25 to 30 guests, a drink station, and a tent if the party is outdoors and you can't count on dry weather. A 60-guest open house needs roughly 30 chairs, 4 to 5 tables for sitting, 3 to 4 tables for food, display and gifts, linens for every table, and shade or rain cover. Order four to six weeks ahead because graduation weekends in May and June book up.

Graduation season packs the whole Chicago area into the same six weekends. Every family is hosting at once, so the things that go wrong are predictable: not enough seats once grandparents arrive, a buffet line that backs up into the driveway, and a forecast that turns a backyard party into a scramble. A checklist fixes most of it. Count what you need by zone, then order everything together.

## Start with the guest count and the party style

Graduation parties are usually open houses. Guests arrive over three or four hours, stay 45 to 90 minutes, and leave. That is good news for your rental count, because you never seat everyone at once. A rule of thumb for open houses is seating for 50 to 60 percent of the total invited, plus standing room.

### Seating
- Chairs for half your guests, plus a few spares. [Garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) are estimated at $5 to $7 each and work on lawns.
- Round tables of 8 for guests who want to eat sitting down.
- A few lower chairs or benches for kids if there are younger cousins.

### Food and drink
- 8-foot banquet tables for the buffet line, one per 25 to 30 guests, so lines stay short.
- Chafing dishes for hot food. See [how many chafing dishes for a buffet](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet).
- A drink table or tub station, and a beverage dispenser for lemonade or water.
- Plates, cups, flatware and napkins. Count at least two plates per guest at an open house because guests come back for dessert.

### Display
- A table for the cake or dessert spread.
- A table for photo boards, a yearbook and a memory guest book.
- A gift and card table with a card box, since cards and envelopes are easy to lose.

### Weather and comfort
- A tent or canopy over food and the main seating area. Size it with our [tent sizing guide](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).
- Fans for hot afternoons, and sidewalls or a plan B indoors for cool, wet ones.
- Lighting if the party runs past dusk. [Backyard lighting ideas](/blog/backyard-party-lighting-ideas) show what works without a lot of wiring.

These are planning starting points based on open house traffic, not a guarantee. If your guests tend to stay for hours, move to the higher number.

## Chicago-area notes

Late May and June in Chicago can bring hot afternoons and fast thunderstorms. Tents need to be anchored, and a tent that sits on a driveway needs weights instead of stakes. If you are using a tent over a certain size in the city, read our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and check with your own village or park district for suburb rules. Many park pavilions have their own table and chair setups, so ask what is included before you order duplicates.

Also think about parking. Cars from 60 guests arrive in waves, and a delivery truck needs a clear spot on the street. Tell neighbors early.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent for a graduation party?

At minimum, chairs for half your guests, tables for food and for sitting, linens, and serving ware. Add a tent if you are outdoors. Cake, photo and gift tables are the pieces people forget, so count them on your plan.

### How many chairs do I need for a graduation open house?

Plan seats for 50 to 60 percent of invited guests. For a 60-guest open house that means about 30 to 36 chairs. If it is a sit-down meal, plan a seat for every guest.

### How many tables do I need for a graduation party?

Use one round table of 8 for every 8 seated guests, plus one 8-foot table per 25 to 30 guests for the food line, plus 2 to 3 small tables for cake, photos and gifts. A 60-guest party usually needs 8 to 10 tables in total.

### Do I need a tent for a graduation party?

You need one if you cannot move the party indoors when it rains or if your yard has no shade at midday. A tent over the food and a core seating area is the most useful. A tent also keeps cake and desserts out of direct sun.

### How far in advance should I book graduation party rentals?

Four to six weeks ahead is a safe window for May and June weekends, and earlier for large tents. Deliveries get tight on the Saturdays and Sundays after commencement. Our [booking timeline for the Chicago area](/book) can help you find open dates.

### Should I rent plates or use disposable?

Either works. Rented plates feel nicer for a dinner and cut down on trash, but disposable is fine for a casual open house. Ask for a quote on both and compare the total with cleanup time included.

### What time should the party start for the best turnout?

Most families schedule three or four hours in the afternoon, often 1 to 5 p.m. or 2 to 6 p.m. Check the graduate's ceremony and friends' schedules first, since overlapping parties split attendance.
