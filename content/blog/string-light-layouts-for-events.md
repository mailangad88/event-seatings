**Quick answer**

String lights are the most popular lighting upgrade for Chicago backyard weddings, school fundraisers and patio parties, and the most commonly under-planned. People buy a few boxes, climb a ladder at 4 p.m. and discover they have nowhere to hang them. This guide covers five layouts that work, the math for feet of lights, and the support problem that decides everything else.

## Start with the supports, not the lights

A strand is only as good as the places you can anchor it. Before you choose a pattern, walk the space and mark every spot that can hold weight: building walls with hooks, tree trunks, fence posts, tent poles (with the tent company's approval), and freestanding poles. Rental poles with weighted bases are usually the answer on an open lawn or parking lot.

Three rules keep strings looking intentional:

- **Height:** 8 to 10 feet above the ground at the low point. Lower than 7 feet and tall guests bump them. Higher than 12 feet and the light stops feeling intimate.
- **Sag:** Let each span between supports droop about 1 foot per 10 feet. Taut strands look like clotheslines.
- **Span:** Keep spans under 20 to 25 feet unless you use a steel support cable. Light strands are not built to carry their own weight over long distances.

### 1. Parallel rows
Straight rows running across the dining area, 6 to 10 feet apart. This is the best choice for a long backyard or a patio, needs the fewest supports, and is easiest to rehearse. Run rows across the short dimension so spans stay short.

### 2. Crisscross grid
Rows in both directions create a glowing ceiling. It is the fullest look and uses roughly twice the lights, so reserve it for the dance floor or a single dining zone rather than a whole yard.

### 3. Perimeter ring
Lights follow the edge of the space: fence line, patio border or tent walls. It defines the room without crossing the guests' heads, which helps if the middle needs to stay open for a rented tent or a dance floor.

### 4. Center starburst
Strands run from a tall center pole to the perimeter, like a circus tent. It needs a stable pole, usually 12 feet or taller, and works well over a round dance floor.

### 5. Canopy drape
Loose swags between supports at varying heights. It is forgiving and hides uneven spacing, which makes it a good fit for trees and old fences.

## How many feet do you need?

Use the formula in the figure, then check the plug count: every strand has a maximum number of connected sets, printed on the plug. Do not exceed it.

| Space | Layout | Approx. lights |
|---|---|---|
| 20 x 20 ft patio | 2 rows of 20 ft | 50 ft |
| 30 x 40 ft yard dining area | 4 rows of 40 ft | 190 ft |
| 40 x 60 ft open tent footprint | Perimeter ring, 200 ft of edge | 240 ft |
| 20 ft round dance floor | Starburst, 6 spokes of 16 ft | 115 ft |

These are rules of thumb. A tree-heavy yard can double the numbers, and a professional lighting company can measure on site.

## Bulb choices

- **Warm white around 2700K** flatters skin and food. Our [lighting color temperature guide](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events) explains why 2200K to 3000K is the sweet spot.
- **LED** bulbs draw a fraction of the power of old incandescent globes, which matters when you are on a generator. See [generator sizing for events](/blog/generator-size-for-events).
- **Shatterproof** plastic bulbs are smart over a dance floor and around children.

## Power and safety

String lights need outdoor-rated, weatherproof strands and cords. Plug connections should be off the wet ground and covered. Read [extension cord safety outdoors](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events) before you run anything across a lawn.

## Chicago notes

Lake-effect wind is real, even in suburbs like Naperville or Oak Park. Use zip ties or clips rather than tape and plan for gusts. If you stake poles into a lawn, call 811 first so utility lines are marked. For public parks, ask your park district whether string lights and poles are allowed at your pavilion or lawn; rules vary.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far apart should string lights be hung?

Hang rows 6 to 10 feet apart so every guest has a strand overhead or nearby. Closer rows give a brighter, fuller ceiling, and wider rows look sparse over a table.

### How high should bistro lights be?

Hang them 8 to 10 feet above the ground at the lowest point of the sag. That clears tall guests and servers and still feels intimate.

### How many string lights do I need for a backyard?

Multiply the row length by the number of rows, then by 1.2 for sag. A 30 x 40-foot dining area with four rows needs roughly 190 feet. Add a spare strand for every five you hang.

### Can string lights stay up in the rain?

Only if the strands are rated for outdoor use and the plugs are protected from standing water. Raise connections off the ground and cover them with a weatherproof box.

### Do I need poles for string lights?

Usually yes in an open yard or parking lot. Rental poles with weighted bases hold strands in places without trees or walls.

### Can I run string lights from a tent?

Ask the tent company first. Some allow clips on the frame, while others do not want weight on the poles or fabric. Never attach anything to a tent without permission.

### How many strands can I plug together?

Check the plug label for the maximum connected sets. Exceeding it can overheat the first strand in the chain.
