**Quick answer**

Logan Square events fall into three groups: private homes and yards along the boulevards, venues such as restaurants or halls, and public parks. For homes, plan around alley access and narrow gates. For venues, ask who supplies furniture. For parks, the Chicago Park District requires a permit at 50 or more guests or with a tent, alcohol or amplified sound. Pick the group first, then build the rental list.

Logan Square is known for its wide boulevards, tree-lined residential streets and a mix of old brick buildings and newer construction. Many events here are neighborhood-scale: a family party in the yard, a shower in a restaurant back room, a fundraiser in a community hall. The logistics are not exotic, but they reward preparation.

## At home or in a yard

Most homes in the area sit on city lots with an alley behind them. A yard can often hold a small seated party and a larger standing one. Use our [backyard party guide](/blog/chicago-two-flat-greystone-backyard-party) for counts and layouts, and our [alley delivery guide](/blog/chicago-alley-deliveries-event-rentals) to plan the route. Measure gates before choosing table sizes.

## In a hall or restaurant

Venues set their own rules about furniture, outside vendors and alcohol. Ask who provides tables, chairs and linens, whether outside rentals are allowed, and when vendors can load in. If the room is a parish or community hall, see our [church hall reception guide](/blog/chicago-church-hall-reception-rentals), which applies to any hall with a basic table-and-chair setup.

## In a public park

For an event in a Chicago Park District park, a Special Event Permit is needed at 50 or more people, or with features such as alcohol, tents, canopies, sound, generators or ceremonies. Apply early, and match your rental list to the features on your permit. See the [Park District permit guide](/blog/chicago-park-district-event-permit), and for tents, the [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit). A block party on your own street is a separate process; see the [block party guide](/blog/chicago-block-party-permit).

Neighborhood events mix ages. Plan comfortable chairs for older guests, easy-clean surfaces for kids and a few lounge spots. ## Small touches that make a neighborhood party work

Logan Square events are often casual, and that is their charm. A few small decisions make them run better.

- **Name one host for the delivery.** Someone who knows the layout should meet the truck and point to each setup spot.
- **Put drinks in two places.** A single bar makes a line. Two stations spread the crowd, especially in a yard.
- **Light the path.** Alleys and gates get dark early outside summer. Our [backyard party lighting ideas](/blog/backyard-party-lighting-ideas) show simple ways to light a route.
- **Keep a rain plan.** A garage, porch or indoor room that fits the core group saves the day.
- **Plan trash and recycling.** Extra bins near the food and the bar keep the yard clean and neighbors happy.

If you are not sure how many people will actually come, set the layout for the confirmed number and keep a few extra chairs stacked nearby. It is easier to add seats than to remove a crowded room.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit for a party in Logan Square?

At home, usually not. In a Chicago Park District park, yes at 50 or more guests or with features like a tent, alcohol or amplified sound. A street closure needs a block party or special event permit.

### Can I hold a block party in Logan Square?

Block party permits are for residents of the street, applied for through the alderman's office. See our block party guide for the rules CDOT publishes.

### How do rentals reach a Logan Square backyard?

Often through the alley and back gate. Measure the gate and tell your provider about stairs and obstacles.

### What chairs work for a family party?

Sturdy, easy-clean chairs such as garden folding or cross-back styles. Add a few comfortable seats for older relatives.

### Can I rent a hall in the neighborhood?

Many community and parish halls are available, with their own rules. Ask each hall for capacity, furniture and alcohol policies in writing.

### How many tables do I need for 60 guests?

About 8 rounds of 60 inches if everyone sits, since each seats 8 comfortably. Use fewer if some stand. See our table count guide.
