**Quick answer**

Start planning a company holiday party in August or September, since December Fridays and Saturdays in Chicago book first. Set the budget and date by early September, sign the venue and caterer by mid October, send invitations in early November, and finalize headcount and rentals two weeks out. A smaller party at the office can start six to eight weeks ahead.

</div>

Everyone wants the same three weekends in December. That is the whole problem with holiday party planning: the event is easy to imagine and hard to book, because the venues, caterers, DJs and rental trucks are all asked for the same dates in the same month. A timeline that starts earlier than feels necessary is the single best thing you can do for the party.

These are planning habits, not rules. Smaller offices with a party in the break room can move much faster. The earlier you are, the more choice you have on date and venue, and the less you pay in stress.

## Pick the format before the date

Decide what kind of party you are throwing, because format drives the timeline. A seated dinner needs a venue, a full catering contract and a seating plan. A cocktail reception needs less furniture but more standing room and bars. An afternoon lunch at the office can be set up with a caterer and a few rented tables.

Ask your team too. A short survey about preferred day, time, family inclusion and food preferences takes five minutes and prevents complaints later. Many companies find that weekday evenings or lunches in early December get better attendance than a Saturday night when everyone has family plans. Whether to include partners and children is a decision that changes your headcount by a large amount, so settle it early.

## Money, approvals and the guest list

Get the budget approved before you contact vendors. A rough Chicago-area range for per-person costs varies hugely by venue and menu, so collect real quotes rather than guessing, and see our [corporate event budget per person](/blog/corporate-event-budget-per-person) guide for how to split it. Hold back a contingency of 10 to 15 percent for add-ons.

Build the guest list from HR records, then decide on plus-ones. Remote employees, part-timers and contractors are a common source of hurt feelings, so make a clear and consistent policy. An RSVP tool or the [Guest List and RSVP Tracker](/downloads/guest-list-rsvp-tracker) keeps counts accurate as replies come in.

## Rentals and layout

If you are hosting at the office or in a raw space, rentals are the backbone of the party. Count seats, then tables, linens, a bar, a buffet line, a dance floor and coat racks. For a seated party, one chair per guest. For a cocktail party, many planners provide seats for roughly a third to half of guests. Our [holiday office party rentals guide](/blog/holiday-office-party-rentals-chicago) lists the typical pieces, and the [December holiday party rentals article](/blog/december-holiday-party-rentals-chicago) covers the seasonal quirks like heaters and coat storage.

## Timeline by party size

Lead times shift with the size of the party. Use this as a rough guide.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Party size | Start planning | Sign venue and caterer | Order rentals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 30 (office lunch) | 6 to 8 weeks out | 4 to 6 weeks out | 3 to 4 weeks out |
| 30 to 75 | 10 to 12 weeks out | 8 to 10 weeks out | 4 to 6 weeks out |
| 75 to 150 | 12 to 14 weeks out | 10 to 12 weeks out | 6 weeks out |
| 150 to 300 | 14 to 16 weeks out | 12 to 14 weeks out | 6 to 8 weeks out |

</div>

Larger parties often need a stage or dance floor and more staffing, so they carry extra lead time. When in doubt, book the venue first.

## Your holiday party checklist

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Choose format and survey the team.</strong> Seated, cocktail or casual lunch, with family or without.</li>
<li><strong>Approve budget and date.</strong> Pick two backup dates in case the first is taken.</li>
<li><strong>Book venue and caterer.</strong> Ask about holiday minimums and what the venue includes.</li>
<li><strong>Hire entertainment.</strong> DJs and bands fill December quickly, so book early.</li>
<li><strong>Send invitations.</strong> Include date, time, dress code, plus-one rules and a dietary question.</li>
<li><strong>Order rentals.</strong> Lock chairs, tables, linens, bar and dance floor pieces.</li>
<li><strong>Finalize the run of show.</strong> Share it with vendors a week ahead and plan a short day-of walkthrough.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-area notes

December in Chicago means snow, early darkness and tight traffic. Plan for coat check or racks, a clear salted entrance, and a rideshare or transit note in the invitation. Downtown loading docks can be limited, so ask the venue about delivery windows. Many suburban offices share parking lots, so confirm where guests and the delivery truck can go. If you are using an outdoor patio with heaters, check with your village or park district and the venue about rules, and read our [heating a tent in fall](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall) piece for safe approaches.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I start planning a company holiday party?

Many planners start in August or September for a December party. Venues and caterers in Chicago tend to fill the best December Fridays and Saturdays by the end of October. A small office party can come together in six to eight weeks.

### How far in advance should I book a holiday party venue?

Ten to twelve weeks ahead is a common target, and earlier for large groups. If you are flexible on the date, weeknights and early December dates have more availability.

### What is a good budget for a company holiday party?

It varies widely by venue, menu and headcount. Collect at least three Chicago-area quotes and compare totals including service charges, taxes and rentals. Hold back 10 to 15 percent for add-ons.

### Should we invite spouses and partners to the company holiday party?

That is a company policy decision. Including partners makes it more of a social evening and raises costs, while an employee-only party is cheaper and simpler. Whatever you choose, announce it early and apply it consistently.

### What day of the week is best for a holiday party?

Weeknights and lunch parties often draw strong attendance because they avoid weekend family commitments, and they can be easier to book. A short survey of your team gives a better answer than a guess.

### How do I handle dietary restrictions at a holiday party?

Ask on the invitation or RSVP, share the list with your caterer and label dishes at the buffet. Our guide to [dietary restrictions planning for events](/blog/dietary-restrictions-planning-for-events) has a simple method.

### What rentals do I need for an office holiday party?

Chairs, tables, linens, a buffet line, a bar, coat racks and sometimes a dance floor. Count seats first and build from there.
