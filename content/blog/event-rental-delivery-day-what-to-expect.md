**Quick answer**

On delivery day the crew arrives in your window, unloads, counts items with you, sets them up to your layout and leaves a contact number. To make it smooth, have a person on site with authority to sign, clear access to the room, a floor plan, and a venue contact. Count the items before the crew leaves and note any issues on the paperwork.

</div>

Delivery day is where the planning either pays off or does not. For a first-time host it can feel mysterious: who shows up, what do they do, what should you check? Here is the morning in order, with what to prepare and what to watch for. We use Chicago-style logistics as the example, with docks, elevators and tight windows, but the steps are the same for a suburban backyard.

## Before the truck arrives

- **Confirm the window** the day before. Ask whether the crew will call on approach.
- **Unlock and clear the space.** Furniture, boxes and trash in the way slow the crew.
- **Know the access.** Dock, elevator, parking, and who opens the door.
- **Post a floor plan** or hand one to the crew lead. See [setup and breakdown](/blog/setup-and-breakdown-fees-event-rentals).
- **Have a decision-maker** on site. Someone with authority, a phone and the contract.

If the venue requires paperwork, like a COI, confirm it is accepted. [COI for event rentals](/blog/certificate-of-insurance-event-rentals) explains the process.

## When the crew arrives

The crew lead usually checks in, reviews the order and walks the route to the room. Be ready to show:

- The loading spot and the path.
- The room or area for setup.
- The point of contact at the venue.
- Any no-go areas, like finished floors or delicate décor.

If something changed since booking, such as a different room or a blocked door, tell them now.

## The count

This is the most important five minutes of the morning. Count chairs, tables and linens against the delivery sheet. Check:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Quantity | Matches the contract |
| Style and color | The right items, not substitutes |
| Condition | No cracks, stains or missing parts |
| Extras | Spares and accessories included |
| Packaging | Crates and racks accounted for |

</div>

Note any problem on the paperwork and take a photo. A discrepancy raised on delivery is much easier to fix than one raised after the event. Match it with the [return condition rules](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning) so you know what shape things should be in at pickup.

## Setup and walk-through

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Chairs and tables placed</strong> to your layout and spacing.</li>
<li><strong>Linens placed</strong> if ordered, creases smoothed.</li>
<li><strong>Walk the room</strong> with the lead and confirm every table.</li>
<li><strong>Check aisle width</strong> and exits. Spacing matters for guests and safety.</li>
<li><strong>Sign the delivery sheet</strong> once the count and room look right.</li>
</ol>

If you want a quick sense of spacing, see our floor-plan help in [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## What to do if something is wrong

- **Short on items.** Ask the lead what the plan is and when they can fix it.
- **Wrong style.** Ask for a replacement or a credit, and get the answer in writing.
- **Damaged items.** Photograph and record on the delivery sheet.
- **Venue not ready.** Tell the crew; ask how waiting is handled.

A problem raised immediately gives the company time to solve it. A problem raised the next day is harder.

## Staging for pickup

Before the crew leaves, ask: "What do you need from me at pickup?" The answer helps you keep the room tidy. Common requests include chairs stacked, linens bagged, dishes scraped and crates in a known location.

## What not to do

- Don't leave the crew without a point of contact.
- Don't sign blank or partly filled forms.
- Don't move chairs around without the company's guidance once set; keep to the plan.
- Don't forget the other vendors. Florists, caterers and lighting teams often arrive after the chairs.

## Chicago tip

Downtown buildings can limit delivery times, elevator use and truck parking. Ask your venue manager for a written load-in plan and share it with the rental company. If the plan changes, call both. See [delivery fees](/blog/event-rental-delivery-fees-explained) for what access can do to the cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### What time will my rental delivery arrive?

Within the window in your contract. Many companies call or text when they are close. Ask how they handle the window so you can plan your day.

### Do I need to be there for delivery?

Yes, or someone you trust. A person on site can direct the crew, count the items and sign off on the order.

### How do I check the rental count?

Count each item type against the delivery sheet before the crew leaves. Photograph any damage and note it on the paperwork.

### What if the venue isn't ready when the crew arrives?

Tell the crew and call your rental company. Waiting time may be billed under your contract, so confirm the access arrangement ahead of time.

### Do rental crews set up my floor plan?

If setup is included, yes, based on the layout you provide. Give a clear sketch and walk through the room with the lead.

### What should I do after the crew leaves?

Keep the signed sheet, store the company's contact number and prepare for pickup. Also pass your layout to the other vendors who need access.
