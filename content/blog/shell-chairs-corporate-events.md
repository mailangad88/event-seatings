Corporate events ask more of a chair than weddings do. The same chairs may be used at an all-day conference, a sit-down awards dinner and a standing reception, and the logistics team wants them to stack, clean up fast and not look like a banquet hall. The shell chair does all of that. This guide explains where it fits, how to pick a color and how to plan the seats for the typical Chicago-area corporate event.

## What a shell chair is

Our [mid-century shell chair](/chairs/shell) has a molded seat and back in a single curve, on wood or metal legs. It comes in white, terracotta, sage and black. It stacks, is outdoor-friendly in the catalog and has no cushion. The molded shape supports the back without upholstery, which is why it works for a long day.

Why planners choose it:

- **Stackable.** Fast to deploy and clear.
- **Neutral or bold.** White and black fit any event, and terracotta or sage add some warmth.
- **Easy to wipe down.** Good for a breakout lunch with coffee and food.

## Picking a color

- **White:** the safest. It works with every brand and looks clean in photos.
- **Black:** formal and strong; good with white linen or a bright brand color.
- **Sage:** calm and friendly; suits wellness, nonprofit and design clients.
- **Terracotta:** warm and distinctive; good for a creative or hospitality launch.

You can mix two colors by table, but keep it to two so the room does not look like a classroom. See [mixing chair styles at one reception](/blog/mixing-chair-styles-at-reception).

## Logistics in the Chicago area

Most corporate events in Chicago happen in downtown offices, hotels and event spaces with strict building rules. Before you order, find out:

1. Whether outside vendors need a certificate of insurance.
2. When the freight elevator and loading dock are available.
3. How early you can set up and how late you must clear.
4. Whether the venue requires a union or in-house labor for load-in.

These vary building by building, so ask for the rules in writing. Stackable chairs help a lot here, because they cut the number of trips. For a deeper look at stacking, see [stackable vs non-stackable chairs](/blog/stackable-vs-non-stackable-chairs).

## Branding and photos

Corporate events are photographed for the website, social media and the annual report, so the chair is part of the brand. A white or black shell chair reads as neutral and lets your signage, step-and-repeat and table décor carry the identity. A colored shell chair can echo a logo, but test it against the backdrop first: terracotta against a red banner looks muddy, while sage against navy looks calm. If the client has a strict brand guide, ask for the exact color and compare it to the chair finish rather than guessing from a name.

Keep chair rows tidy for the camera. Straight lines, equal gaps and no stray chairs at the edge of the frame matter more in a corporate photo than any amount of décor. Assign one person to walk the room 15 minutes before doors open and straighten rows.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are shell chairs good for corporate events?

Yes. They stack, come in neutral and brand-friendly colors and are comfortable enough for a day of sessions.

### How much does it cost to rent shell chairs in Chicago?

Our estimate is $9 to $12 per chair. Quantity, delivery and building access affect the final price.

### Can shell chairs be used outdoors?

Yes. They are outdoor-friendly in our catalog, which helps with a rooftop or a company picnic.

### Do shell chairs stack?

Yes, so they are quick to set and clear.

### Are shell chairs comfortable for a full day?

They are comfortable for a day of sessions or a long dinner. If you want extra comfort, choose a chair with an included cushion, such as the infinity.

### What color shell chair should I choose for a brand event?

White or black for any brand, and sage or terracotta if they match your palette. Ask your brand team for the exact color before you order.

### How many chairs should I order for a 200-person event?

Order 200 for a seated event, plus 5 to 10 percent for late guests and staff. Standing receptions can use far fewer, about one seat per three guests as a rule of thumb.
