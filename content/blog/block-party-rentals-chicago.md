**Quick answer**

In Chicago, residents apply for a block party permit through their aldermanic (ward) office, and the request must reach CDOT at least three business days before the event, though some ward offices ask for more lead time. Once approved, rent long tables and folding chairs for about half your neighbors, a tent or two for shade, and barricade your own street. For 100 neighbors that is roughly 50 chairs, 6 to 8 long tables and a 20 x 40-foot tent.

</div>

A block party is the cheapest way to turn a street into a neighborhood. The city already has a process, and the rentals are straightforward once you know how much of the street you can use. The work is in the permit, the neighbors and the layout.

## The permit, step by step

The City of Chicago's Department of Transportation (CDOT) describes the process on its block party permit page. The main points as of the page we reviewed:

- Residents apply through their local aldermanic office, which reviews the request and enters it into the online permitting system.
- Requests must reach CDOT at least three business days before the event. Permits are first come, first served.
- You must show that a majority of residents on the affected block approve.
- Permits are for the street where you live, and for a single block, intersection to intersection.
- Permits are not issued on arterial streets or bus routes, for more than one day in a row, or for commercial purposes. Access to metered or pay-box parking must stay open.
- Businesses and outside organizations apply for a festival permit instead.

Individual ward offices add their own steps. One ward's page we read asks for requests at least two weeks ahead, closes streets from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., and says the office does not supply barricades. Another ward may differ. Call your own ward office early, and read the CDOT page for the current rules. For more city guides, see our [Chicago planning guides](/blog/topic/chicago-planning).

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Talk to neighbors</strong> and collect signatures or written support from the block.</li>
<li><strong>Contact your ward office</strong> for its form and deadline.</li>

Rules change, and suburbs run their own permit programs through the village hall or police department. If you are outside the city, ask your village.

Residential streets are often about 25 to 30 feet wide curb to curb, but yours will vary, so measure. A common practice is to keep a clear lane of at least 12 feet down the middle in case an emergency vehicle needs to pass. Ask your ward office or fire department if they have a specific requirement.

## Power, shade and cleanup

Neighbors can run an extension cord from a porch outlet, but cords across a street are a trip hazard and a liability, so cover them with a cord ramp and route them along the curb. Read [outdoor extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events) before you run any. Ask neighbors to set out their black and blue bins, as one ward suggests, and bring extra trash cans. Fireworks are illegal at one ward's block parties, and bounce houses go through a separate city program, so check before you book either.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit for a block party in Chicago?

Yes, to close the street. Apply through your aldermanic office, which enters the request into CDOT's system. CDOT's page says requests must arrive at least three business days before the event.

### How many chairs do I need for a block party?

Plan seats for about half of expected neighbors. For 100 neighbors that is about 50 chairs, and people will bring their own too.

### Can I close more than one block?

CDOT describes permits for a single block, intersection to intersection. Its page also mentions an outer limit, so ask your ward office about your case.

### Can I serve alcohol at a block party?

The CDOT page does not address it. Contact your ward office and check local and state rules before you serve.

### Who provides barricades?

At least one ward says it does not. Plan to arrange them yourself, and ask your ward office what is required.

### What about suburbs?

Each village has its own process, usually through the village hall or police. Ask early, as lead times differ.
