A garden party has a particular feel: long afternoon, lots of conversation, maybe a lawn game in the corner. The chairs should look like they belong to a garden and not like they were trucked in. Rattan does that. The challenge in the Chicago area is weather, because a garden party in May or September can swing from 80 degrees and humid to cool and damp in a week. This guide explains how to make rattan work, and when to pick something else.

## What makes rattan different

Rattan is a natural vine, woven over a frame. The weave gives you texture, which photographs well against linen, greenery and glass. Our [rattan garden chair](/chairs/rattan-garden) comes in honey and white wash, has a sturdy frame and can take an optional seat cushion.

Three things to know before you order:

- **It does not stack.** Each chair takes its own floor space in the truck and in storage, which changes delivery planning.
- **It is lighter than wood but not delicate.** Guests can move it easily, which is good for a mingling party and bad if you want a fixed layout.
- **Weave catches things.** Heels, hems and necklaces can snag, so avoid placing chairs at the edge of a dance area.

For the dinner zone, plan about 24 inches of table edge per guest and 36 inches of clear walkway around the table. That clearance matters more with non-stacking chairs because they are bulkier to push back.

## Weather and ground

Rattan is a natural material. Rain will not ruin a rental chair in a short shower, but it should not sit wet for hours or stand in a puddle. Humidity makes the weave swell a little and is normal. Direct, strong sun can fade the honey tone over many seasons, but it is not a one-event concern.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Is the ground firm and dry?</strong> If yes, place chairs on it. If no, use flooring or a hard patio.</li>
<li><strong>Is there rain in the forecast?</strong> A tent or covered porch protects the weave.</li>
<li><strong>Is it hot and sunny?</strong> Add shade and set chairs late so the cushions stay cool.</li>
<li><strong>Are guests in heels?</strong> Choose the tent or patio, not the lawn.</li>
<li><strong>Is the event over two hours?</strong> Add cushions.</li>
</ol>

## What to pair with rattan

The chair brings warmth and texture, so keep the rest simple:

- **Tables:** long wood farm tables, round tables with natural linen, or low coffee tables for the lounge corner.
- **Colors:** terracotta, sage, ivory and warm yellow. Avoid cool-toned neon.
- **Details:** pampas grass, dried florals, mismatched glassware and cloth napkins.

## Rattan versus cane-back

People often confuse rattan and cane. Rattan is the woven frame or seat. Cane is a flat, open weave panel, as on our [cane-back chair](/chairs/cane-back). The cane-back reads as more formal and vintage, while rattan feels more relaxed. If you cannot decide, read [cane-back chairs for weddings](/blog/cane-back-chairs-wedding).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can rattan chairs go outside?

Yes. Ours is outdoor-friendly, but it should sit on a firm, dry surface and be protected from long rain. A tent or covered patio is ideal.

### How much do rattan chairs cost to rent in Chicago?

Our estimate is $12 to $16 per chair. Final pricing depends on quantity, delivery address and setup needs.

### Are rattan chairs comfortable?

They are comfortable for casual events, and a cushion helps for anything over an hour. The woven back has a little give, which many guests prefer to a hard wood back.

### Do rattan chairs stack?

No. Plan extra floor space for delivery and storage, and ask your venue where chairs can wait before setup.

### What colors go with rattan chairs?

Terracotta, sage, cream, rust, blush and natural greens all work. They look strongest against linen and wood rather than glossy or neon surfaces.

### Will rattan chairs snag a dress?

Occasionally, especially a delicate fabric or beading. For a formal event, choose a smooth chair such as the velvet or Louis, or keep rattan for the lounge and cocktail areas.

### How many rattan chairs should I order for a garden party?

Order one seat per guest at a seated meal, or about 30 to 40 percent of your guest count if the party is cocktail style. That ratio is a rule of thumb, so adjust for older guests.
