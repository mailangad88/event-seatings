**Quick answer**

A church festival or banquet usually needs more tables and chairs than the parish hall holds, plus a tent for food and weather. For a 200-person dinner, plan 20 rounds or 25 long tables, 200 chairs, 2 serving tables per food line and a tent if more than 120 guests will be outside. Start with your hall's capacity and fire marshal limit.

</div>

Churches, parishes, mosques, synagogues and temples around Chicago host more events than most venues: annual festivals, fish fries, anniversary dinners, bazaars, weddings, funerals, confirmations and community fundraisers. Most congregations own some tables and chairs, but not enough for the biggest day of the year. Rentals fill the gap, and the best planning starts with an honest inventory.

## Tents and seating

A food and dining tent is the backbone of a festival. Plan about 10 to 12 square feet per seated guest at rounds and about 8 to 10 at long tables (rule of thumb). A 40 x 60 foot tent (2,400 sq ft) seats about 150 to 200 depending on layout. Add a side tent for food prep and storage. Tents on asphalt need ballast, since you cannot stake them. Tent permits and fire marshal rules vary by village, so confirm with your village or fire department; see [tent permits in Chicago and the suburbs](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).

## Banquets and receptions

After funerals, confirmations or weddings, many congregations host a reception in the hall. Rounds of 8 or 10 with linen tablecloths work for formal meals. Long tables work for family-style or potluck. [Cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) or [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) are practical options for large counts, and linens make even simple folding tables look dressed. ## Food lines and serving

Food booths need a table at counter height, cloths to the floor, signage and a path for volunteers behind. Provide a hand-washing station and a covered trash can. For potlucks, set a long buffet with two lines and a separate dessert table. See [food safety at home parties](/blog/food-safety-at-home-parties) and [how many chafing dishes for a buffet](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet) for service equipment.

## Respect the space

Rentals in a sacred space require sensitivity. Ask the clergy or building committee about where chairs may be placed, whether tables can enter the sanctuary, and how items should be handled during services. Cultural and religious events have their own customs; for example, some communities need separate seating, covered food areas or prayer space. Ask rather than assume. Our [cultural celebration guides](/blog/topic/cultural-celebrations) offer more.

## Accessibility and comfort

Congregations include older members, families with strollers and people who use wheelchairs. Keep a 36-inch path to every table, put a few chairs with arms near the entrance and the food, and place accessible tables on firm ground, not lawn. Chairs with a back and a firm seat are better for long services and meals than backless benches. Provide a shaded rest area with water for older guests during outdoor festivals, and make sure the restrooms are signed. If the festival includes alcohol, check your diocese or community rules and the village licensing office.

## Weather and the parking lot

Many parish festivals happen on blacktop that gets hot in July. Tents shade seating, but the pavement still radiates heat, so plan fans or misters if you can power them, plus water coolers. Never block fire lanes or hydrants with a tent or booth. If rain arrives, move chairs to the hall before the tent floods, and keep the tent sidewalls on hand.

## Volunteer logistics

- Assign one lead to meet the delivery driver
- Mark table spots with tape before setup
- Create a teardown crew list
- Keep the hall's key and alarm code with a named person
- Return rentals promptly to avoid late fees

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs and tables for a 200-person church dinner?

About 200 chairs and 20 round tables of 10 or 25 rectangular tables of 8. Add tables for food, drinks and a welcome desk.

### Do I need a tent for a church festival?

Usually yes for food and dining outdoors. It protects from sun and rain and gives you a clear boundary.

### Do I need a permit for a tent at a church?

Possibly. Tent permits and fire inspections vary by village, so check with your local fire department or building office.

### Can rentals be placed inside the sanctuary?

Only with the congregation's approval. Ask clergy or the building committee first.

### How do I handle insurance?

Many rental companies and sites ask for a certificate of insurance. Ask your church's insurance carrier to issue it.

### What if the festival is on asphalt?

Tents require ballast instead of stakes. Tell your tent provider the surface so they can plan.
