**Quick answer**

An event rental quote is a list of items multiplied by quantity, plus service charges. The usual layers are the item rentals, delivery and pickup, setup and breakdown, optional damage waiver, and tax. The same chairs can cost very different totals depending on which services are included, so always compare the full total, not the per-chair rate.

</div>

You asked for 120 chairs and got back a page of lines, codes and fees. That is normal. A good quote in Chicago or the suburbs is built in layers, and each layer answers one question: what are you renting, how does it get there, who sets it up, and what happens if something breaks. Once you know the layers, the page stops being a puzzle.

**1. Items x quantity.** Each product has a per-piece rate for the event. Chairs run from about $5 to $7 for a garden folding chair up to $20 to $30 for a Louis chair, and our [chair rental cost guide](/blog/wedding-chair-rental-cost) shows the full range. Linens, plates and glasses are priced per piece too, which is why quantities matter so much.

**2. Delivery and pickup.** Usually priced by distance, floor level and access. A Chicago high-rise with a freight elevator is a different job than a driveway in St. Charles.

**3. Setup and breakdown.** Labor to place the chairs and tables, then collect them afterward. Some quotes bundle this, some list it separately, and some leave it out completely, which makes the quote look cheaper.

**4. Damage waiver.** An optional fee that covers normal wear. We explain it in [damage waivers explained](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained).

**5. Tax.** Rental tax rules can differ by location, so ask whether the quote shows tax or leaves it for the invoice.

## What a real line item looks like

<div class="table-wrap">

| Line | What it means | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Qty 120, cross-back chair | 120 chairs at the unit rate | Does the count include spares? |
| Delivery, Zone B | Truck trip to your address | Is pickup included in the same fee? |
| Setup, 12 tables | Labor to place the chairs | Is it to your layout or a pile at the door? |
| Breakdown | Labor to collect and load | Is there a time cutoff? |
| Damage waiver | Percentage on the subtotal | What does it exclude? |
| Tax | Applied to taxable lines | Which lines are taxable? |

</div>

Anything labeled "service," "handling," "fuel" or "admin" should be defined in writing. If a line has no explanation, ask. It is a fair question and an honest company will answer it quickly.

## Why quotes change after the first version

Your first quote is an estimate built on your guess. It moves for three reasons:

- **Headcount shifts.** Chairs and place settings scale with guests. See [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) before you ask for a number.
- **Venue details appear.** Stairs, a long carry, a loading dock window or a no-vehicle plaza can change delivery and labor.
- **Items get added.** Most people forget the cake, gift, DJ and bar tables. [Tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) covers the extras.

Treat version one as a range. Lock the real numbers when your venue and headcount are firm.

## What a good quote should include

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Event date and times</strong> for delivery, event and pickup.</li>
<li><strong>Full address</strong> with floor, dock or load-in notes.</li>
<li><strong>Every item with a quantity</strong> and its unit rate.</li>
<li><strong>Each service fee listed separately</strong> or clearly marked as included.</li>
<li><strong>Payment schedule</strong> and the deadline to confirm the final count.</li>
<li><strong>Policies</strong> for cancellation, changes, damage and weather.</li>
</ol>

If a quote leaves out any of these, the number at the bottom is a guess, not a price.

## Common mistakes when reading a quote

- **Comparing rates, not totals.** A $7 chair with delivery, setup and breakdown included can beat a $6 chair with everything extra.
- **Assuming setup is included.** A pile of chairs at the curb is not a set room.
- **Ignoring time windows.** A delivery that can happen any time between 8 a.m. and 4 p.m. may not fit a Chicago venue with a tight loading slot.
- **Skipping the fine print.** Waivers, minimums and change deadlines live there.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is an event rental quote calculated?

It is the item rate times the quantity, plus delivery, setup, breakdown, any waiver and tax. Each company chooses which of those to bundle, so the lines vary but the logic is the same.

### Is a rental quote the final price?

No. A quote is an estimate until you confirm the headcount, address and item list. Changes to any of those can move the total, and many companies set a deadline after which the numbers are locked.

### Why do two rental quotes for the same chairs look so different?

The difference is almost always in services. One company may include delivery, setup and pickup in the chair rate while another lists them separately. Compare totals with the same services included.

### Does the quote include tax?

Sometimes, sometimes not. Ask whether tax is already in the total and which lines are taxable, because the invoice can show a bigger number than the quote you remember.

### How long is a rental quote good for?

Quotes are typically held for a limited time because inventory is shared across customers. Ask for the expiration date in writing and whether a deposit reserves your items.

### Can I get a quote before I choose my venue?

Yes, as a rough range. Delivery and labor will not be firm until you know the address, floor and load-in details, so treat the early number as a planning figure.
