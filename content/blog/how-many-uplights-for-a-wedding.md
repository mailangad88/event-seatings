**Quick answer**

Space uplights about 6 to 10 feet apart along the walls you want to color. A room with 120 feet of usable wall needs roughly 12 to 20 fixtures, and most weddings use 12 to 24. Pick fewer fixtures for a soft glow and more for an even wash. Each LED fixture draws only a small amount of power, but check the venue circuit and the cable run before you order.

</div>

Uplights are the quickest way to change a beige banquet hall into a room that feels designed. Small LED fixtures sit on the floor and throw color up the wall. The question is always the same: how many? Too few and you get scallops of light with dark gaps. Too many and you pay for fixtures nobody notices. This guide gives you a formula, a spacing chart and the Chicago-specific checks.

## The wall-length formula

**Fixtures = usable wall length in feet ÷ spacing in feet**

Spacing depends on wall height and the look you want.

Do not count doorways, bars with their own lighting, or walls covered by drapes you will not light. Count the walls guests will see.

## What changes the number

- **Ceiling height.** Higher ceilings need the beam to travel farther, so fixtures work better a little closer together and further from the wall (about 1 to 2 feet).
- **Wall color.** Light or white walls reflect color and need fewer fixtures. Dark or wood walls drink light.
- **Texture.** Brick and stone catch light and look dramatic; use closer spacing to show texture.
- **Fixture power.** Battery LED fixtures are less bright than wired ones. See [battery-powered event lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting).
- **Color choice.** Warm amber and blush read subtly. Saturated blues and magentas look strong with fewer fixtures but photograph unpredictably. Our [color temperature guide](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events) helps you match the glow to the rest of the room.

## Where to put them

1. **Walls behind the head table or sweetheart table.** The photographs will focus here.
2. **The dance floor perimeter.** Pair with [LED dance floor lighting](/blog/led-dance-floor-lighting).
3. **Pillars and columns.** One fixture per column makes the room feel taller.
4. **Tent poles.** Ask your tent company whether fixtures may sit against the sidewalls.
5. **Trees.** Uplighting a tree needs a stronger fixture and rain protection.

## A worked example

Picture a suburban banquet hall that is 60 feet long and 40 feet wide. The two long walls total 120 feet, but one has a 20-foot bar and a 12-foot service door, so 88 feet is usable. The end wall behind the head table adds 30 feet. That is 118 usable feet. At 8-foot spacing you need 15 fixtures. Add two spares and you order 17. If the budget is tight, light the head table wall and the dance floor side at 8 feet (about 8 fixtures) and leave the rest for room lights.

The mistake we see most often is lighting every wall evenly, which spreads a small budget thin. Concentrate fixtures where the camera and the eye land. A bright, well-lit focal wall with a quieter room beats a uniformly dim one.

## Power and cables

Most wired LED uplights draw very little power, but they still need outlets. Typically fixtures are daisy-chained, and a chain has a maximum, printed on the unit. Hide cables along baseboards and tape them down with gaffer tape that will not damage floors. If the venue has limited outlets, battery fixtures with wireless control solve the problem. Check [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) before load-in.

## Chicago venue notes

Many Chicago hotels, loft venues and suburban banquet halls have rules about who may install lighting, and some require a certificate of insurance from outside vendors. Ask your venue coordinator: Is outside lighting allowed? Which outlets can we use? What are the load-in times? Do not guess; policies vary by venue.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many uplights do I need for 100 guests?

Guest count matters less than wall length. A room for 100 guests often has 100 to 140 feet of usable wall, which means 12 to 17 fixtures at 8-foot spacing.

### How far apart should uplights be?

Place them 6 to 10 feet apart. Six feet blends into a wash, 8 feet is standard and 10 feet looks like accents.

### How far from the wall should an uplight sit?

About 1 to 2 feet. Closer shows texture and creates a tall scallop, while farther spreads the beam wider and softer.

### Do uplights need outlets?

Wired fixtures do, though daisy chains reduce the number of outlets. Battery fixtures need none but must be charged and are usually dimmer.

### What color uplights are best for a wedding?

Warm amber, soft pink and white are the safest. Saturated colors work but can look different in photos, so test one wall before the event.

### Can I use uplights in a tent?

Often yes, on the floor facing the sidewalls or poles. Ask the tent company first and keep cables off walkways.

### Should I rent or buy uplights?

For a one-time event, renting is simpler. It includes delivery, testing and spares.
