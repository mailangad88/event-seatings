**Quick answer**

Plan one 3-gallon dispenser for every 25 to 30 guests per drink flavor, and 2 to 3 flavors for most parties. A 3-gallon dispenser holds about 48 8-ounce servings. For 100 guests, two flavors and a water station take 4 dispensers of drink plus 1 or 2 for water, and a cooler of ice behind the table.

</div>

A drink table looks simple until the lemonade runs out at 7:30. Dispensers are the least exciting rental on the list and one of the easiest to under-order. Here is the math, a chart and the details that keep a Chicago July party from turning into a puddle of melted ice.

The math is simple: one gallon is 128 ounces, which is 16 servings at 8 ounces. In practice, some of each dispenser stays in the bottom, so plan on 90 percent of that.

The usual rule of thumb is about 1 to 1.5 drinks per guest per hour for an afternoon party, a little more in July heat. A 3-hour party with 100 guests therefore consumes 300 to 450 servings across all beverages, including the bar and coffee. Add more if the event is outdoors in the sun.

## Choose flavors that cover everyone

- **One sweet:** lemonade, punch or iced tea.
- **One light:** infused water with citrus, cucumber or berries.
- **One caffeine-free or kids' choice:** fruit punch or lemonade.
- **Plain water:** always, and in a separate dispenser.

Hot cider and hot chocolate also work in a dispenser with a heating element or an insulated urn, and Chicago fall and winter events often serve both. Read [how many coffee urns you need](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party) for hot drinks.

## Setting up the drink station

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Table:</strong> an 8-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth.</li>
<li><strong>Drip tray:</strong> a plastic or silicone mat under the spigot.</li>
<li><strong>Cups:</strong> stacks of 3 per guest for the whole event, plus a tub for used ones.</li>
<li><strong>Ice:</strong> in a bucket or cooler with a scoop, not poured into the dispenser.</li>
<li><strong>Signs:</strong> name each drink and list allergens.</li>
</ol>

Keep drink tables away from the buffet, because a shared line doubles the wait. See [buffet line flow](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed). Put the table in shade for summer parties and out of the doorway in winter.

## Ice and keeping drinks cold

A frozen ice core (a plastic insert) keeps drinks cold without watering them down, and many dispensers include one. Fill the core a day ahead. Use a separate cooler for cubes, and plan about 1 pound of ice per guest as a common rule of thumb for beverages only. See [keeping food cold at outdoor events](/blog/keeping-food-cold-at-outdoor-events) for coolers and ice.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Too few flavors per guest:** a 100-guest party with one dispenser makes a queue.
- **No drip tray:** sticky spills ruin a rented linen and attract bees outdoors.
- **Sugary drinks only:** at least one low-sugar option and plain water.
- **Putting ice in the dispenser:** it dilutes, and chips jam spigots.
- **Glass in pools or on grass:** use shatter-resistant cups at poolside and in parks. For the right cups, see [glassware and bar](/blog/topic/glassware-and-bar).

## Chicago weather notes

In July and August a drink table can empty twice as fast, so plan the high end of the table above. Cold-weather Chicago events can serve warm drinks: spiced cider, hot chocolate or coffee. Check your venue or park district about glass and alcohol rules before bringing dispensers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people does a 3-gallon beverage dispenser serve?

About 25 to 30 guests for a 3-hour event with one drink per hour, or 48 eight-ounce cups. Add more if guests drink two or more cups.

### How many gallons of lemonade for 100 guests?

About 6 to 8 gallons if lemonade is one of three drinks. If it is the only drink, plan 15 to 20 gallons across a 3-hour event.

### Should I put ice in the beverage dispenser?

Use a frozen core insert rather than loose ice, which dilutes the drink and can clog the spigot. Serve ice from a separate cooler into cups.

### How many drink flavors should I offer?

Two or three, plus water. More flavors split guests across more dispensers and leave leftover gallons.
