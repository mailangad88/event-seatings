**Quick answer**

Space lanterns or luminaries 4 to 6 feet apart along a path or aisle for a glow that reads as a line, and 2 to 3 feet apart where steps or turns need extra light. Use stable bases, flameless LED candles when open flame is restricted or the wind is up, and count 1 lantern per 5 feet of path plus extras at steps. Check venue and fire rules before using real flame.

</div>

A path of lanterns is one of the oldest and simplest ways to light an event, and it still works better than most gadgets. It marks the route, creates a mood and costs little. But it fails when the lanterns are too far apart to read as a line, too dim to see steps, or too light to survive wind. This guide helps you choose size, spacing and type.

## Lanterns versus luminaries

- **Lanterns** are frames with glass or metal sides that hold a candle or LED. They come in sizes from small tabletop ones to tall floor lanterns 2 to 4 feet high.
- **Luminaries** are paper or plastic bags or sleeves that hold a light in sand or a base. They glow softly and sit low.
- **Hurricanes** are glass cylinders with open tops, typically for pillar candles.

Each has a role. Tall lanterns line aisles and entrances. Low luminaries edge paths. Small lanterns sit on tables.

## How many do you need?

A quick count: path length in feet ÷ spacing, times 2 if you light both sides.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Scenario | Spacing | Count for a 40 ft path |
|---|---|---|
| Both sides, straight path | 5 ft | 18 |
| One side, straight path | 5 ft | 9 |
| Ceremony aisle, both sides | 4 ft | 22 |
| Steps or ramp | 2 to 3 ft | Add 4 to 6 per set |
| Table groups | 3 per table | Match table count |

</div>

These are planning rules of thumb. Brighter lights can be spaced farther, and curves and steps need more.

## Sizes that work

- **Small (8 to 12 inches):** tables, shelves and stair edges. Group them in threes.
- **Medium (12 to 24 inches):** aisle ends, entrances, ceremony arch bases.
- **Large floor lanterns (2 to 4 feet):** aisle markers, doorways and bar fronts. They add height, so use them in groups.

A tall lantern with a candle needs a stable base. A short, wide lantern is safer in wind.

## Real flame or flameless?

Real candles give the most natural flicker, but they bring risks: wind, spills, fire codes and tent rules. Many venues limit open flame; see [candles at venues](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules) for what to ask. Flameless LED candles are a good alternative:

- Choose warm bulbs, around 2200K to 2700K.
- Look for flicker modes for a natural effect.
- Use remote controls to switch on all lanterns at once.
- Buy rechargeable or battery-powered types; see [battery lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting).
- Test them in the dark for brightness.

If you do use real flame, use glass-enclosed lanterns, keep them clear of fabric and foliage, and assign someone to monitor.

## Wind and weather

Chicago wind comes off the lake and across open land. A tall lantern with a thin base will tip. Practical tips:

- Choose lanterns with a wide base, or weigh them with sand or stones.
- Use enclosed glass sides for open flame.
- Skip paper luminaries in rain; they collapse.
- Plan for snow and ice in winter, and salt the path.
- Check the forecast and have a backup plan.

## Placement and safety

- Do not place lanterns in the middle of the path. They are trip hazards.
- Keep them off the edge of steps, where they may be kicked.
- Make sure there is at least 36 inches of clear walking width for accessibility, and more if possible. For public rules, ask the venue.
- Keep lanterns away from guests' skirts and long dresses if they have real flame.
- Light the exit route as well as the entrance.

## Chicago notes

Public gardens, forest preserves and park pavilions often prohibit open flame and may limit decor on paths. Ask the site before bringing real candles. For city sidewalks, any decor placed on public property may require a permit, so check with the city or your village.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far apart should lanterns be?

Place them 4 to 6 feet apart along a path, and 2 to 3 feet apart at steps and turns.

### Can I use real candles in lanterns?

Often yes if enclosed in glass, but many venues restrict open flame. Ask first, and have LED backups.

### What is the difference between a lantern and a luminary?

A lantern is a frame with glass or metal sides. A luminary is a sleeve or bag that diffuses light from a candle or LED in a base.

### Do lanterns work in wind?

Wide, weighted bases and enclosed sides work. Tall, light lanterns tip over.

### How many lanterns do I need for an aisle?

For a 40-foot aisle lit on both sides at 4-foot spacing, you need about 22. Add extras at steps.

### Are LED lanterns bright enough?

They are enough for atmosphere, but dim for steps. Add brighter lights at stairs.
