**Quick answer**

Using one rental company for chairs, tables, linens and tableware means one quote, one delivery, one contract and one person to call, which usually reduces coordination errors and delivery fees. Using several vendors can win on a specialty item or a lower unit rate but multiplies trips, deadlines and risk. For most events, bundle the core list and add a specialist only for what your main company does not carry.

</div>

A typical wedding or party needs chairs, tables, linens, plates, flatware, glassware, maybe a tent and lighting. You can buy each from a different place, or send the whole list to one. Neither is always right, but the trade-offs are predictable, and the difference is mostly about coordination rather than price.

## What one company simplifies

- **One quote.** Everything is priced together, and you see the whole list. [How quotes work](/blog/how-event-rental-quotes-work) explains the layers.
- **One delivery and one setup crew.** Fewer trucks, fewer dock windows, less chaos.
- **Matching.** Linens, chairs and tableware are planned together, so colors and sizes line up.
- **One contract and deadline set.** Headcount, payment and cancellation terms all align.
- **One person to call** on event day.

## What many vendors can win

- **A specialty item** your main company does not carry, like a unique piece of furniture.
- **A lower rate** on a single large-volume item, such as many chairs.
- **A local pickup** where you can collect and return something yourself.
- **Redundancy.** If one vendor falls through, the others are not affected.

## The hidden costs of many vendors

Savings on individual lines can disappear in coordination:

- **Several delivery fees**, and several setup and pickup fees. See [delivery fees](/blog/event-rental-delivery-fees-explained).
- **Multiple dock windows** at the same Chicago venue, which may not fit.
- **Several COIs** if your venue requires them. Read [COI for rentals](/blog/certificate-of-insurance-event-rentals).
- **Mismatch.** A linen that does not fit the table or a chair that clashes with the color story.
- **Return confusion.** Which vendor owns which stray chair?

## A practical split

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Bundle the core:</strong> chairs, tables, linens and tableware with one company.</li>
<li><strong>Add specialists</strong> only for items your main company lacks.</li>
<li><strong>Share one floor plan</strong> and timeline with everyone.</li>
<li><strong>Appoint one lead</strong> for event day, such as a planner or family member.</li>
<li><strong>Stagger deliveries</strong> so vendors do not collide at the dock.</li>
</ol>

## Working with your caterer

Caterers often bring or arrange some of the equipment, like chafing dishes and serving ware. Ask what they provide so you do not rent duplicates. If your caterer also coordinates rentals, compare their fees to a direct order. Clarify who handles dishes at the end, which affects [return expectations](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning). ## When many vendors is the right call

If your event has unique needs, an unusual piece, a very large quantity of one item, or a vendor with a special relationship with your venue, mixing is fine. Just build the schedule and the budget as if each added vendor costs a delivery.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it better to rent everything from one company?

For most events, yes, because it simplifies delivery, setup, matching and contracts. Add a specialist for items your main company does not carry.

### Is it cheaper to use multiple rental vendors?

Sometimes on individual lines, but extra delivery, labor and coordination costs can cancel the savings. Compare totals.

### How do I coordinate multiple rental deliveries?

Make one timeline with each vendor's window, share it with the venue and give each vendor the same contact. Stagger arrivals to avoid dock conflicts.

### Should my caterer supply rentals?

It depends on what they offer and what they charge. Ask for the list and pricing, and avoid renting duplicates.

### What if one vendor cancels?

Your contract should explain what happens. With a bundled order the risk is concentrated; with many vendors you may recover faster, but you manage more moving parts.

### Who should be my point of contact on event day?

One person with the full timeline, such as a planner, venue coordinator or trusted friend, so vendors know who to call.
