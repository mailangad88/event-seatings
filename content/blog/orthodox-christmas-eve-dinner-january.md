For many Orthodox Christians, Christmas follows the Julian calendar, so it falls on Jan 7. The evening before, Jan 6, is the main family meal. In Chicago, the Ukrainian Village and nearby neighborhoods, with their churches and bakeries, have long been home to families who keep these traditions. This guide covers the table and the serving plan. It does not try to cover every custom, because families do it differently.

## What the evening looks like

The details vary by family, parish and country. These are common threads for Ukrainian families:

- **The first star.** The meal begins when the first star appears in the sky. In Chicago in January, the sun sets around 4:30 pm, so the star comes early.
- **Meatless dishes.** Sviata Vecheria is a fast meal with no meat or dairy in many homes. Twelve dishes are traditional, often counted for the twelve apostles.
- **Kutia.** A sweet dish of wheat, honey and poppy seeds, often served first.
- **Other dishes.** Borscht with mushroom dumplings, varenyky, pickled herring, fish, beans, cabbage rolls without meat, and compote of dried fruit.
- **Hay under the cloth.** Some families place a little hay under the tablecloth, and a sheaf called a didukh near the table.
- **An empty seat.** Some families set one extra place for a loved one who is absent or has died.

Serbian families keep **Badnje Vece** on the same date. Customs include the badnjak, a branch or log, and straw on the floor in some homes, and a fast meal. Other Orthodox families have their own versions. Ask the older relatives, because they will know which customs your family keeps.

## Seating a big family

A large family supper means grandparents, cousins and kids at the same table, because the meal is shared. Aim to seat everyone together, so the table feels whole.

Rules of thumb: two 8-foot tables end to end seat about 16 to 18. Three tables in a U seat about 24 to 26. Round tables of 10 work if your hall has the space. Compare options in [banquet table sizes](/blog/banquet-table-sizes-6-ft-vs-8-ft).

A white or cream floor-length cloth fits the tradition. A second smaller table for kids can sit near the main table, so they see the candles. See [kids tables and chairs](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events).

## Serving 12 small dishes

Twelve dishes is many bowls. A table that size does not fit them all at once.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Set the table first</strong> with the kutia, bread and candles, and keep the rest on a side table.</li>
<li><strong>Serve in courses.</strong> Soup first, then varenyky and fish, then sweets and compote.</li>
<li><strong>Use small serving bowls</strong> and platters you can pass, or have the youngest carry dishes.</li>
<li><strong>Keep hot dishes warm</strong> in chafing dishes or a low oven.</li>
<li><strong>Clear as you go</strong> to leave room, with a bus tub near the kitchen.</li>
</ol>

For a count of plates per guest, see [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest). Because the meal has many courses, count at least 3 plates and 2 forks per person, plus soup bowls. Ukrainian celebrations also have a long tradition at weddings, see [Ukrainian wedding reception in Chicago](/blog/ukrainian-wedding-reception-chicago).

## Candles, bread and the table setting

Keep the table simple. A white cloth, a few candles in low holders, a round loaf of bread such as a kolach or paska in some homes, and a small bowl of honey are common. Candles near hay or greenery need care, so keep flames away from the cloth edge and from the didukh. Check your building's candle rules if you host in a condo or hall. See [candles at venues and fire code rules](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules).

Stemware is usually small: a water glass and a small glass for compote or a toast. Plan a spoon for kutia and a bowl for soup at each place. Many families add a bread plate for each guest, and the [bread and butter plate guide](/blog/bread-and-butter-plates-do-you-need) can help you decide.

## Where to host

Most families host at home, and some use a parish hall after the service. In a Chicago two-flat or bungalow, 16 people is a stretch, so open the living room or use a hall. If your parish hall is free, ask the office about tables and chairs on hand before you rent extra. See [church hall and community center event planning](/blog/church-hall-and-community-center-event-planning).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Jan 6 is a weekday in 2027.** Many guests come after work or church. Plan a start around sunset and keep the evening unhurried.
- **Rental calendars are quieter after the holidays,** but some companies keep reduced hours. Ask for delivery on Jan 4 or 5, and pickup on Jan 8 or later.
- **Mind the cold.** Guests arrive in winter coats, so plan a coat rack near the door. A snowy walk means mats at the entrance.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is Orthodox Christmas Eve?

Jan 6, with Christmas on Jan 7. Some Orthodox churches follow a different calendar, so check with your parish.

### What is Sviata Vecheria?

It is the Ukrainian Christmas Eve supper, a meatless meal with 12 dishes. Families begin after the first star appears. Menus and customs vary by region and family.

### What is Badnje Vece?

It is the Serbian Christmas Eve, when families keep a fast meal and honor traditions such as the badnjak. Customs vary by family and region.

### Why is there an empty place at the table?

Many families set one place for loved ones who are absent or who have died. It is a custom of remembering, and some families skip it.

### How many tables do I need for a big family?

Two 8-foot tables end to end seat about 16 to 18, and a third table makes a U for about 24. Add a kids table if you have many children.

### Do I need to rent plates for 12 dishes?

Likely yes, because the meal has many courses. Count 3 plates and 2 forks per person, plus soup bowls. See our guide to counts per guest.
