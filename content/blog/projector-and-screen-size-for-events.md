**Quick answer**

Size the screen so the farthest viewer sits no more than 6 to 8 times the screen height away. A 7-foot-tall screen suits a room about 42 to 56 feet deep, and a 5-foot-tall screen suits one about 30 to 40 feet deep. The closest row should be at least 1.5 to 2 times the screen height away. Brightness matters as much as size: dim the room or choose a brighter projector.

</div>

A slide show at a wedding or fundraiser looks simple until the back tables squint at a screen the size of a bed sheet. Screen size, seat distance and brightness work together. This guide uses a published rule of thumb and a quick sizing chart for banquet rooms and tents in Chicago.

## The viewing distance rule

AV industry guidance commonly uses a family of rules based on screen height (H):

- **Closest viewer:** at least about 1.5 to 2 times H from the screen.
- **Detail viewing (spreadsheets, small text):** the farthest viewer within about 4 times H.
- **General presentations and slides:** the farthest viewer within about 6 times H.
- **Video and photos only:** the farthest viewer within about 8 times H.

These are rules of thumb. Use the 6-times rule if your content includes any text.

## Quick size chart

<div class="table-wrap">

| Room depth to last row | Suggested screen height | Approx. 16:9 width |
|---|---|---|
| 20 ft | 3.5 to 4 ft | 6 to 7 ft |
| 30 ft | 5 ft | 9 ft |
| 40 ft | 6 to 7 ft | 11 to 12 ft |
| 50 ft | 8 ft | 14 ft |
| 60 ft or more | Two screens or an LED wall | Ask a pro |

</div>

Screen width for a 16:9 picture is about 1.78 times the height. For a 4:3 picture, the width is about 1.33 times. Most modern content is 16:9.

## Brightness and room light

A projector's brightness is rated in lumens. The brighter the room, the more lumens you need. Banquet rooms with candlelit tables need less than a tent with sunlight leaking through. Rather than trusting a number, ask the AV vendor to match the projector to your room and tell them the lighting level. Typical ways to help:

- **Dim the lights near the screen.** Lights directly above the screen wash out the image.
- **Use a front or rear projection setup.** Rear projection hides the projector behind the screen and keeps people from walking through the beam, but needs depth behind the screen.
- **Choose a high-contrast screen** for bright rooms.
- **Keep the screen out of direct sun.** In a tent, face the screen away from open sidewalls.

For the lighting side of a presentation, see [corporate stage lighting basics](/blog/corporate-stage-lighting-basics).

## Where the projector goes

Projectors have a throw ratio, which tells you how far back they must sit to fill a given screen width. Ask the vendor for the throw distance for your screen size. Then plan where the projector sits: on a table at the back, on a truss, or behind the screen for rear projection. Keep cables short and covered, and watch out for guests tripping.

## Screens for tents and outdoors

Tents and backyards bring wind and daylight. Use a screen with a frame, not a free-hanging sheet. Add weights, and start after dark. The [winter lighting](/blog/winter-event-lighting-chicago-sunset) article lists sunset milestones in Chicago; in summer, sunset comes after 8 p.m. into early August, so evening video needs patience.

## Content tips

- Use big type: at least 28 points on slides.
- Use high contrast: light text on a dark background works well in dim rooms.
- Keep wedding slideshows short.
- Run audio through the room's speakers; see [sound for speeches](/blog/sound-system-for-speeches).

## Power

Projectors draw a few hundred watts or more, so put them on a dedicated outlet. See [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist).

## Chicago notes

Many hotel ballrooms and conference centers in Chicago have installed screens and projectors, sometimes with a fee for use or a required in-house operator. Ask what is included.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size screen do I need for 100 guests?

It depends on room depth, not guest count. A room 40 feet deep suits a 6 to 7-foot-tall screen.

### How far can the last row be from a screen?

Up to about 6 times the screen height for text and 8 times for video only.

### How bright should a projector be?

It depends on the room light. Dim rooms need fewer lumens. Ask your AV vendor to match it.

### Can I use a bedsheet as a screen?

For a casual backyard movie, maybe. For slides or text, use a proper screen.

### Should I use two screens?

For rooms over about 50 feet deep or wide, yes, so each side has a clear view.

### Do I need a projector or a TV?

Rented TVs and LED walls are brighter in lit rooms. Projectors give a bigger image for the money in dim rooms.
