**Quick answer**

A good Derby party needs four zones: a viewing area with a big screen and a seat for about 2 out of 3 guests, a drink bar, a hat and photo spot, and a table for the betting game. The Kentucky Derby is on the first Saturday in May, and early May in Chicago can be 50s and breezy, so plan for shade, wind and a way to warm up. Book chairs, tables and a tent about 6 to 8 weeks ahead.

The Kentucky Derby is a short race. The race itself lasts about two minutes. The party around it is what people come for: the hats, the drinks, the picking of horses and the shouting at a TV. In Chicago, the first Saturday in May is the first weekend when a backyard or patio party feels possible. It is also a weekend when a sunny 70 degrees can turn into a 48-degree lake breeze in an hour.

This guide covers how to lay out the party, what to rent and what to do about the weather.

## Plan the party around four zones

Most Derby parties run 3 to 4 hours, starting well before the race so people can arrive, eat and make their picks. Think of the space in four zones.

**Viewing area.** Seat about two thirds of your guests. People stand to cheer, but they sit for the 30 minutes before the race. For 30 guests, that is about 20 chairs. Put the screen at one end, angled away from the sun, since glare washes out a TV by late afternoon. If you need to rent a screen or speaker, look at [AV and sound rentals](/rentals/av-sound).

**Eating tables.** Derby food is easy to eat with one hand, such as pimento cheese, ham biscuits, deviled eggs and fruit. A few 30-inch cocktail tables and a couple of 6-foot tables beat a seated dinner. Our guide to [how much food per person](/blog/how-much-food-per-person-for-a-party) helps you size the spread.

## The hat and photo spot

Hats are half the fun. Set up a small area near the entrance so guests walk right into it.

- A table with a mirror, a few extra hats, pins and ribbon for people who forgot theirs
- A backdrop or a wall of greenery with a roses theme (roses are the traditional Derby flower)
- A basket of paper fans and a prize for best hat, judged by a guest who did not enter

Hats and wind are a bad mix. On a breezy Chicago day, have hat pins or a few clips handy. If you want a backdrop that holds up outside, see our [photo backdrop ideas](/blog/photo-backdrop-ideas-for-weddings-and-parties).

## The mint julep bar

A mint julep is bourbon, sugar, fresh mint and crushed ice, usually in a metal cup that frosts on the outside. It is easy to batch and slow to make one at a time, so set up a station.

1. Mix the mint and sugar syrup the day before.
2. Keep bourbon, syrup, mint and a big tub of crushed ice at the bar.
3. Offer a non-alcoholic version with lemonade and mint. Some guests will want it, and kids and drivers need something good to hold.

You will want glassware or cups for every drink. Plan on guests using 2 to 3 cups each over a long afternoon, and rent more than you think. A [glassware rental](/rentals/glassware) order covers highball glasses and rocks glasses if you do not have julep cups.

## A betting game that stays a game

Picking a horse is part of the day, and you can do it without real money.

- Write the horses and their numbers on a poster board or a dry-erase easel
- Give each guest the same amount of play money, or play for bragging rights
- Have each guest draw a horse from a hat, or pick one and write their name next to it
- Give a small prize to the winner, such as a bottle or a basket of snacks

Keep it for fun. Rules about real-money wagering vary, so do not run a pool for cash unless you have checked. Our [games and kids rentals](/rentals/games-kids) page has tabletop games that fit the mood.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **The race is early evening in Kentucky.** The Derby's post time is in the evening Eastern time, which is late afternoon in Chicago. Check the schedule for the day and start the food before the undercard races, not at post time.
- **Rental companies get busy in early May.** Graduation parties, Cinco de Mayo, first communions and Mother's Day all land in the same few weeks. Tables and chairs for a Saturday go first. Check availability early.
- **Chairs sink in soft spring grass.** After April rain, lawns are wet. If your viewing area is on grass, ask about flooring or put the chairs on a patio or a deck. See [chairs on grass](/blog/chairs-on-grass-sand-gravel).
- **A TV outside needs power.** Plan an outdoor-rated extension cord run, and keep it off the path. See [outdoor extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events).

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the Kentucky Derby?

The Kentucky Derby is on the first Saturday in May. The race is in the evening Eastern time, which is late afternoon in Chicago. Check the official schedule for the exact post time before you set your start time.

### How many chairs do I need for a Derby party?

Plan a seat for about two thirds of your guests. For 30 guests, that is about 20 chairs, plus a few extra at the eating tables. People stand and move around, so you do not need a seat for everyone at once.

### What do you serve at a Kentucky Derby party?

Serve finger foods that are easy to eat standing up: pimento cheese, ham biscuits, deviled eggs, fruit and small desserts. The classic drink is the mint julep. Offer a non-alcoholic mint lemonade as well.

### How do I set up a mint julep bar?

Use one table with bourbon, mint syrup made the day before, fresh mint and a tub of crushed ice. Have a person or a self-serve card show how to make the drink. Plan 2 to 3 cups per guest and rent extra glassware so you are not washing during the party.

### Can I have a Derby party in Chicago in early May?

Yes, but plan for weather. Early May can be warm or in the 40s with a lake breeze. A tent, a heater and a backup indoor space let the party go ahead either way.

### What is the best way to run a betting game at a party?

Use play money or bragging rights. Write the horses on a board, let guests pick or draw one, and give a small prize to the winner. Rules on real-money wagering vary, so keep it a game unless you have checked.
