**Quick answer**

To order linens correctly, list every table on your floor plan (guest, head, cake, gifts, guest book, DJ, bar, buffet, cocktail), pick a cloth size for each table type, add one napkin per seat plus a spare of about 10 percent, and add one or two spare cloths. Then confirm color, fabric, delivery and pickup times, and the damage policy in writing. Most linen mistakes come from forgotten tables, not wrong guest counts.

</div>

Linens are the part of an event order that is easiest to underorder. The guest tables get counted; the gift table, the bar front and the late-night snack table do not. A good checklist turns your floor plan into a clean order and gives your supplier exactly what they need. Use the list below for a wedding, gala or party in Chicago or the suburbs.

## Step 2: choose the size for each

Use the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for rounds and banquets, and the [round vs rectangular guide](/blog/round-vs-rectangular-table-linens) if you are mixing shapes. Decide floor length or lap length for each type.

## Step 4: confirm the details

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Color and fabric:</strong> get the exact names and photos in writing.</li>
<li><strong>Quantities by size:</strong> a line per cloth size, not a total.</li>
<li><strong>Delivery window:</strong> date, time and who receives it at the venue.</li>
<li><strong>Setup:</strong> who puts the linens on the tables.</li>
<li><strong>Pickup:</strong> date, time and how to bag them.</li>
</ol>

## Step 5: read the policies

Ask about damage, late returns and cancellations before you pay. See [linen stain and damage policies](/blog/linen-stain-and-damage-policies) and [do you wash rental linens](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-linens). Ask how far ahead you can change your count, because guest counts shift in the weeks before an event; for timing, see [how far ahead to order linens](/blog/how-far-ahead-to-order-linens).

## Venue and Chicago notes

Ask your venue whether they supply linens, and if they do, whether you can supply your own. Many Chicago-area banquet halls have house linens in limited colors, so bringing your own can mean extra setup and cleanup. Ask for delivery access: freight elevators, loading docks and parking rules in the city can limit when a delivery can arrive. See [Chicago planning guides](/blog/topic/chicago-planning) for the questions to bring.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many tablecloths do I need for a wedding?

One for every table on your floor plan, including guest, head, cake, gift, guest book, bar, DJ and cocktail tables, plus one or two spares. Count tables, not guests.

### How many napkins do I order?

One per seat plus about 10 percent as a spare, and paper cocktail napkins separately. See our napkin counting guide for a worked example.

### Should I order extra tablecloths?

Yes. A couple of spares cover spills, last-minute tables and any size surprise. Ask your supplier whether spares are charged only if used.

### Who sets the linens on the tables?

It depends on the vendor. Rental companies, the venue or your caterer may do it, sometimes for a fee. Confirm who is responsible and when they will arrive.

### Can I change my linen count after ordering?

Usually until a cutoff set by the supplier. Ask for the date, and plan to confirm your final guest count before then.

### Do I need to order linens for the ceremony?

Only if you have tables, such as a signing table or unity candle table. A small cloth on each is enough.
