A holiday craft fair in a Chicago school gym or church hall has a short window and a lot of people in winter coats. Vendors arrive with boxes, shoppers arrive with strollers, and everyone wants a good spot by the door. A clear layout fixes most of the trouble before it starts.

## Pick the vendor table size

Most vendors are happy with one 6-foot table. Jewelry, candles and cards fit easily. Bakers, woodworkers and anyone with large displays often ask for an 8-foot table or two.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Table | Size | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 6-ft rectangle | 72 by 30 in | Most vendors, jewelry, cards, candles |
| 8-ft rectangle | 96 by 30 in | Baked goods, art prints, larger displays |
| 30-inch cocktail round | 30 in across | Checkout or a cash box at the end of a booth |

</div>

Order a few more tables than you have vendors. Late sign-ups, a check-in table, a lost-and-found table and a donation table all appear on the day. See [banquet table sizes](/blog/banquet-table-sizes-6-ft-vs-8-ft) for how they compare.

## Linens that make it look like a fair

Plain tables look cold. A floor-length cloth on the front, with the sides draped, hides boxes stored under the table and gives vendors a clean base to show their things. A 90 by 132 inch cloth covers a 6-foot table to the floor on most setups. The [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) has the exact sizes.

Pick one or two colors for the whole room. Red, green, white or plain black all look good on a holiday fair floor. Tell vendors whether they can put their own cloth over yours, because some do and some do not.

## Booth spacing and aisle width

A rule of thumb is a 10 by 10 foot space per vendor, which is about one 6-foot table with room behind it. Shoppers need space to stop at a table without blocking others.

- **Main aisles:** 6 feet or wider, so two strollers can pass.
- **Side aisles:** at least 4 feet.
- **Exits:** leave the paths to exits and doors clear. Check with your school, church or park district on the capacity and fire rules for the room.

Do not place tables directly in front of an exit door, a fire extinguisher or a stage stair.

## Power and lighting

Not everyone needs power, but a few always do: a card reader that must stay plugged in, a hot glue gun, a cord-light display, a coffee urn. Ask on the vendor form who needs power and how much. Then place those vendors near walls with outlets.

Use heavy-duty extension cords rated for the load, and tape them down flat across any aisle. Do not daisy-chain power strips. The [event power needs checklist](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) lists what to ask.

## Check-in and load-in

Vendors arrive earlier than shoppers, and they carry things. Plan the order.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Send a map</strong> with each vendor's table number and door for unloading.</li>
<li><strong>Set a load-in window</strong> of about 60 to 90 minutes before doors open.</li>
<li><strong>Check in at the door</strong> with a printed list and a table number card.</li>
<li><strong>Point vendors to their spot</strong> and have a volunteer with a cart for heavy loads.</li>
<li><strong>Keep a spare table</strong> for late additions and mix-ups.</li>
</ol>

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Winter coats need a place.** Shoppers in December arrive in puffy coats and carry bags, and the room feels smaller. A coat rack near the door keeps aisles open. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).
- **Load-in is where the snow comes in.** A slushy entrance gets mats and a wet floor quickly. Put mats at the vendor door and a volunteer with a mop.
- **Tables arrive on a schedule.** Ask when your delivery will arrive and whether the room is open that early. Holiday weeks fill rental calendars, so book early.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big should a vendor table be at a craft fair?

A 6-foot table is the standard, and an 8-foot table works for larger displays. Each vendor usually gets a space of about 10 by 10 feet. Ask vendors what they plan to sell when they sign up.

### How wide should aisles be at a craft fair?

Main aisles should be at least 6 feet wide, and side aisles at least 4 feet. That leaves room for strollers, wheelchairs and people stopping at tables. Check room capacity rules with your school, church or park district.

### Do craft fair vendors need tablecloths?

A floor-length cloth is a good idea because it hides boxes under the table and looks finished. Tell vendors whether you provide cloths or they bring their own. Rental linens are usually priced per cloth.

### Do vendors need electricity?

Some do, for card readers, lights or small appliances. Ask on your vendor form, then group those vendors near outlets and use heavy-duty cords taped down flat.

### How many tables do I need for 30 vendors?

Order 30 tables for vendors and add 3 to 5 for check-in, a donation table and spares. If some want 8-foot tables, count those separately. A table count by size is easy to put on your quote.

### How do I handle check-in?

Set a table by the door with a printed vendor list and a map. Give each vendor a table number card and a load-in time, and keep a volunteer near the door with a cart.
