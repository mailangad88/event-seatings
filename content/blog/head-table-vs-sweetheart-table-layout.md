**Quick answer**

A sweetheart table seats two and needs about 5 x 5 feet of floor. A head table seats 8 to 16 and needs a 12 to 20 foot wall with 5 feet of space in front. Choose a sweetheart table when floor space is tight or family seating is complicated, and a head table when you want the wedding party beside you.

</div>

The head table is the old standard: the couple and the wedding party in one row facing the room. The sweetheart table is the modern alternative: just the two of you, usually in a prime spot. Neither is better. The right choice depends on room size, guest count, family dynamics and how you want the evening to feel. Here is a clear way to compare them for a Chicago-area ballroom, banquet hall or tent.

## When a head table makes sense

Head tables work in large rooms with a wall or stage to anchor them, a big wedding party and a formal feel. They keep the party together and make toasts easier because everyone sits facing the guests. They are less ideal when guests must turn around to see the couple. In a 150 or 200-guest ballroom, one 24-foot head table along a wall may actually give a better view than a sweetheart table in the middle. See [floor plan for 150 guests](/blog/floor-plan-for-150-guests).

## When a sweetheart table makes sense

A sweetheart table wins in smaller rooms, in tents where floor space is tight, and when family seating is complicated. It also gives the couple a quiet meal. See [seating chart for divorced parents and blended families](/blog/seating-chart-divorced-parents-blended-families) for why it avoids the awkward "who sits at the head table" conversations. Where the wedding party sits becomes a separate decision: a table next to yours, or at their own table.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Count your wedding party.</strong> More than 8 people points to a head table or a reserved round.</li>
<li><strong>Measure your wall.</strong> You need 2 feet of table per person plus 5 feet in front.</li>
<li><strong>Check the sightlines.</strong> Stand where the farthest guest sits.</li>
<li><strong>Check family needs.</strong> Divorced parents, grandparents, kids.</li>
<li><strong>Decide on the chair.</strong> A distinctive chair makes either one stand out.</li>
</ol>

## Hybrid options

Many couples choose a middle path: a sweetheart table with the wedding party at a reserved round next to it, or a head table for six with a mixed seating. Another option is a "king's table" or long farm table down the center. For that, see [long table layouts in a narrow room](/blog/long-table-layouts-in-a-narrow-room).

## Sightlines and sound

Head tables look good in the first photo but can be awkward for conversation. People at one end of a 20-foot table cannot easily talk to the other end, and the couple sits among their friends rather than across from them. A sweetheart table, in contrast, offers the couple a few moments to themselves in a busy room, which many couples value more than they expected.

For toasts, think about where the microphone will be and whether guests can see the speaker. If the head table is on a riser, guests in the back can see the couple and the speakers. If it is a sweetheart table on the floor, put the microphone near the dance floor and turn the chairs so the room faces the speaker. The DJ position matters too; see [DJ and band placement](/blog/dj-and-band-placement-floor-plan).

Finally, decide where the wedding party eats. A reserved round table next to the couple, set with the same chairs and linens as the other tables, gives them a clear home base without the wall space of a head table.

## Setting the table

A head table is usually dressed on the guest-facing side only, with a floor-length skirt or cloth. Elevate it on a riser if the room is deep. A sweetheart table is dressed on all four sides and can have a distinctive chair, like a [louis medallion](/chairs/louis-medallion) or [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining), or a [royal throne](/chairs/royal-throne) pair. Tablecloth sizes for banquets and rounds are in our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

## Chicago venue notes

Some Chicago hotel ballrooms and loft venues have a built-in stage or an alcove ideal for a head table. Others have columns or windows that make a wall table awkward. Check the room before you decide. For lakefront and tent receptions, wind and sun can change which side of the room is comfortable, as covered in outdoor event chairs and Chicago weather.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a head table and a sweetheart table?

A head table seats the couple and the wedding party in a row. A sweetheart table seats only the couple.

### How much space does a sweetheart table need?

About 5 x 5 feet, including the chairs. A 36 to 48-inch round or a 6-foot banquet works.

### How long should a head table be?

About 2 feet per person. A table for 10 is about 18 to 20 feet long.

### Where should the sweetheart table go?

Facing the room, near the dance floor and away from the kitchen and bar lines.

### Should the wedding party sit at the head table?

Only if they fit. Otherwise, give them a reserved round close to the couple.

### Which is cheaper, a head table or a sweetheart table?

A sweetheart table needs fewer chairs and linens. But a head table can replace guest tables. Compare your whole rental list.
