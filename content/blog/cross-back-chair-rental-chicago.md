## What a cross-back chair actually is

A cross-back chair has two slats that meet in an X across the back. The frame is solid wood, and the seat is either woven or a flat plank. The style comes from the French farmhouse and bistro tradition, which is why it reads as casual and European at once. You will see it at barn weddings across the Fox Valley, vineyard dinners, restaurant patios and loft receptions.

Our cross-back chair comes in three finishes:

- **Walnut:** a deep brown that grounds light linens and looks rich in candlelight.
- **Natural oak:** a honey tone that brightens a room and pairs with greenery.
- **Matte black:** the modern version, good with white linens and for industrial spaces.

## How many cross-back chairs fit at your tables

Plan roughly 24 inches of table edge per guest. That is the number most caterers use for a relaxed plated dinner, and it keeps elbows out of each other's way.

Wooden cross-backs have a bit more visual weight than a slim chair, so avoid squeezing an extra guest in. Rows that look balanced from across the room make better photos and get served faster.

## Indoors and outdoors

Our cross-back is rated outdoor-friendly in the catalog, which means it works on a patio, a tented lawn or a barn floor. It is still wood, so a few rules apply for Chicago weather:

- Do not leave them in standing rain overnight. Set up as close to the event as your schedule allows.
- On soft grass, wooden legs sink. Lay a few sections of ground protection or choose a firmer spot. Our guide to [chairs on grass, sand and gravel](/blog/chairs-on-grass-sand-gravel) covers the options.
- For a cold-weather event under a tent, wood chairs feel warmer to touch than metal or acrylic.

## Cushions: when to add them

The seat is firm. For a 45-minute dinner and a dance floor, that is fine. For a three-hour plated dinner with toasts, add a cushion. Cushions are optional on this chair, and they also soften the look, so natural oak with an ivory pad is a very popular pairing. See [do you need chair cushions](/blog/do-you-need-chair-cushions) for a simple way to decide.

## Styling a cross-back chair

A cross-back does not need much. The wood is the decoration. If you want more:

1. **Greenery on the back:** a small eucalyptus or olive sprig tied to the center of the X.
2. **Linen napkin tied to the slats:** it reads as casual and costs nothing extra.
3. **Floral swag on the aisle chairs only:** use it for the ceremony and leave the reception chairs clean.

Skip heavy sashes. They hide the X, which is the point of the chair. For more ideas see [chair back decor](/blog/chair-back-decor-florals-and-signs).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to rent cross-back chairs in Chicago?

Our estimate is $8 to $11 per chair, depending on finish, quantity and delivery. Final pricing comes with your quote, because delivery location and setup needs vary by venue.

### Are cross-back chairs comfortable?

They are comfortable for a normal dinner. The wood seat is firm, so add a cushion if guests will sit more than two hours or if you have many older guests.

### Can cross-back chairs be used outdoors?

Yes, on patios, firm lawns and under tents. Keep them out of standing rain and avoid soft, wet grass where the legs sink.

### Which finish should I choose, walnut, oak or black?

Choose walnut for a rich, traditional look, natural oak for a lighter, greener one and matte black for modern or industrial rooms. If your linens are white, all three work; with dark linens, oak shows up best.

### How many cross-back chairs fit at an 8-foot table?

Ten is the comfortable number: four on each long side and one at each end. You can squeeze twelve, but elbows will collide.

### Do cross-back chairs stack?

Yes. The cross-back is stackable, which helps with ceremony flips and with venues that have limited storage during the event.

### Can I mix cross-back chairs with other styles?

Yes. They pair well with bentwood or cane-back chairs for a collected look, as long as the wood tones are related. See [mixing chair styles at one reception](/blog/mixing-chair-styles-at-reception).
