**Quick answer**

Tipping a rental delivery and setup crew is optional, not required, and etiquette varies. First check your contract: some companies include a service charge or ask you not to tip. If a tip is welcome, give it directly to the crew lead at the end of the job, in cash or as the company allows, and consider more for hard conditions like stairs, rain or a tight timeline.

</div>

Nobody puts "crew tip" on a wedding spreadsheet, and then a team of four carries 150 chairs up a freight elevator and you wonder what to do. This is a common question, and there is no single rule that fits every company. Here is how to think about it, without inventing numbers that could embarrass either side.

Three possibilities:

- **A service or gratuity charge is on the invoice.** The crew may already be compensated through it. A further tip is a personal choice.
- **The company says tips are welcome.** Ask how to give them, directly to the crew lead or through the office.
- **The company discourages tipping.** Respect it. A kind note or a public review means a lot.

If you are not sure, ask: "Is tipping customary for your crew, and do you have a preference on how it's given?" That is a normal question. Add it to your [questions for a rental company](/blog/questions-to-ask-an-event-rental-company).

## No set number

You will find numbers online for per-person tips, but we cannot verify a standard, and it varies by company, region and job. Instead of a rule, think in terms of what the crew faced.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Situation | Reason to tip more generously |
|---|---|
| Stairs or a long carry | More physical work |
| Rain, heat, snow or cold | Hard conditions |
| Tight timeline | Pressure and speed |
| Last-minute changes | Extra effort |
| A big layout | Larger job |
| A smooth, easy job | A standard thank-you is fine |

</div>

If you do tip, a pooled amount for the whole crew handed to the lead, with a quick note about who it's for, is simple. A tip paid in cash is usually easiest, but follow the company's guidance.

## When and how to give it

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Check the contract</strong> for gratuity or no-tip language.</li>
<li><strong>Ask the company</strong> about their preference ahead of time.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare it in advance</strong> in an envelope, so you are not hunting for cash on event day.</li>
<li><strong>Give it to the crew lead</strong> once setup is done and you have walked the room.</li>
<li><strong>Thank them by name</strong> and tell the office afterward.</li>
</ol>

For setup tips, give them after the walk-through, not before. For pickup, if you will not be present, ask the company whether you can leave a tip with the office or whether a tip is appropriate at all. See [what happens on delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect).

## Two crews, two moments

Delivery and pickup may be handled by different people. If you want to thank both, plan for both moments. A late-night pickup crew often gets overlooked. It pairs with [late-night and early pickup fees](/blog/late-night-and-early-pickup-fees-event-rentals), where the labor is already after hours.

## Other ways to thank a crew

- **Water and snacks.** On hot days, cold water is appreciated; in winter, hot coffee.
- **A clear plan.** A floor plan, an open door and a point of contact make the job easier.
- **Access.** Clear the room and a path before they arrive.
- **A review or a note.** Positive feedback to the company can help crew members.
- **Parking and dock arrangements.** Confirm them in advance. This is also a Chicago courtesy, because a blocked dock is a stressful start.

## Do not tip as a way to fix a problem

If a crew is late, careless or rude, a tip will not repair it and is not required. Raise the issue with the company. Our guide to [red flags](/blog/event-rental-red-flags) can help you think about when something is genuinely wrong.

## Budget it

If you like to tip, put a small line for it in your budget so it does not feel like a surprise. It belongs with other day-of items, as outlined in the [rental budget guide](/blog/event-rental-budget-by-guest-count).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you tip event rental delivery people?

It is optional. First check whether a service charge is included or the company has a tipping policy. If tips are welcome, give them to the crew lead.

### How much should I tip a rental crew?

There is no verified standard, so we do not give a number. Think about the difficulty of the job and the conditions, and ask the company for guidance.

### Should I tip for delivery, setup or both?

If you choose to tip, do it at the point the work is complete and you have seen the result, usually after setup. For a separate pickup crew, you can thank them too if it fits the company's policy.

### Is it rude not to tip?

No. Tipping is a way to say thanks, not an obligation. A courteous, well-prepared client is appreciated either way.

### Can I add a tip to the invoice?

Some companies let you, others do not. Ask when you book.

### What if the company says no tipping?

Respect the policy. Thank the crew, and leave a positive review or note for the company.
