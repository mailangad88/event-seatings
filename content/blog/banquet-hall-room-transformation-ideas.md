**Quick answer**

Transform a banquet hall in four layers: dim and color the lighting, cover the walls with drape, upgrade the tables with linens, chairs and centerpieces, and cover the floor with a dance floor or rugs. Get the venue's rules and access time first, because setup time is the real limit.

</div>

A banquet hall is a blank box with carpet, ceiling tiles and a stack of chairs in the corner. The same room can host a wedding on Saturday and a corporate dinner on Sunday. Room transformation means changing the look of that box with rentals and decor, and it works best when you plan it in layers instead of buying a pile of pretty things. This is how planners approach a Chicago-area banquet hall.

## Start with what you cannot change

Ask the venue for a list: ceiling height, carpet color and pattern, wall color, built-in lighting, columns, windows, a permanent dance floor, bar, stage and any rules on tape, nails, candles and ladders. Photograph the room empty at the time of day of your event. Many halls have strong overhead lights or a patterned carpet that becomes the first thing guests see.

## The four layers

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Ceiling and lighting.</strong> Dim the overheads, add uplights and, if allowed, drape or string lights. See [ceiling draping](/blog/ceiling-draping-for-weddings-and-banquet-halls).</li>
<li><strong>Walls.</strong> Cover bare walls with pipe and drape, a backdrop or a feature wall. See [pipe and drape](/blog/pipe-and-drape-uses-and-sizes).</li>
<li><strong>Tables.</strong> Linens, centerpieces and chairs do most of the work for the least effort.</li>
<li><strong>Floor.</strong> A dance floor, a rug under lounge pieces or an aisle runner covers the carpet.</li>
</ol>

## Time is the hidden cost

Transformations need load-in time. Many Chicago venues give you only a few hours before guests arrive, and some have another event the night before. Ask for your access window in writing, and sequence the work: ceiling and lighting first, then walls, then floor, then tables, and finally centerpieces. Ask each vendor how long they need and whether they can work at the same time without blocking each other.

## Chairs make or break the room

Rows of plain banquet chairs shrink the transformation. Upgrading the chair is one of the simplest ways to change a hall: the [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) for warmth, the [infinity](/chairs/infinity) for glam, the [ghost](/chairs/ghost) for a clean modern look.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying decor before you know the room's rules.
- Ignoring the carpet.
- Skipping the lighting.
- Not leaving time for setup.


## A simple walkthrough before you order

Visit the hall with a tape measure, your phone and a list. Photograph each wall, the ceiling, the carpet and every outlet. Measure the doorways and the elevator, since long drape poles, floor panels and sofas have to fit through them. Note where the sun comes in and when. Then send the photos and measurements to your rental planner and ask for a scaled plan that shows the tables, floor, bar, backdrops and aisles. A scaled plan finds problems, like a backdrop that blocks an exit, while they are still free to fix.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I make a banquet hall look better?

Dim the overhead lights and add uplights, cover bare walls with drape, upgrade linens and chairs, and add a dance floor or rugs. Lighting makes the biggest change.

### What decor hides a drop ceiling?

Ceiling draping or a dark ceiling with uplighting. Ask the venue where you may attach, and check fire rules.

### Can I decorate a banquet hall myself?

Yes within the venue's rules. Ask about tape, nails, candles, balloons, ladders and access time.

### How long does a room transformation take?

Several hours, depending on the number of layers. Confirm your access window with the venue in writing.

### What is the cheapest way to change a banquet hall?

Lighting and linens give the most change for the effort. Chair upgrades come next.

### What should I ask the venue first?

Ceiling height, attachment rules, fire policy, access times, power, and whether the venue has built-in features you must use.
