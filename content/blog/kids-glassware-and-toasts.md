**Quick answer**

Serve children in unbreakable cups of 6 to 8 ounces, such as acrylic tumblers or lidded cups, and give them a sparkling juice or cider toast in a small flute or tumbler so they join in. Plan 2 to 3 cups per child, poured by a server, and keep them away from the alcohol bar. Check any age limits on your event with your venue.

Children and glass do not mix. A wedding with 15 kids and a full glassware set invites a broken stem and a sliced finger. Smart planners give kids their own cups and their own toast, so they feel included and adults can relax. Here is how to set it up.

## What to serve

- **Water,** always available and refilled by a server.
- **Sparkling juice or cider** for the toast, in a small cup.
- **Lemonade or fruit punch,** with limited sugar.
- **Milk** for dinner or dessert.
- **Shirley Temples or a kid-friendly mocktail.** See [mocktail bar ideas](/blog/mocktail-bar-ideas).

Always ask parents about allergies and diet. Check ingredients in punches and sparkling drinks, and label them on the table.

## Counts

Plan 2 to 3 cups per child at the table. For a toast, add 1 per child. Spares matter: kids drop cups. For 15 kids, that means 45 to 60 cups, including spares. Order the same cups for the kids' table and a small stack for the buffet. Use [how many glasses per guest](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest) to count the adult side separately.

## Include kids in the toast

A children's toast makes the family moment. Hand them a small cup of sparkling juice and tell them when to raise it. If the flutes are glass, give the kids acrylic versions instead of skipping them. Pour small amounts, about 2 to 3 ounces. For adults, see [champagne flutes vs coupes](/blog/champagne-flutes-vs-coupes).

## Placement and safety

Seat kids' tables away from the bar, near the dance floor edge or a parent's table, so adults can keep an eye on them. Remove glass from the table before children sit, and use lower chairs or booster seats where needed. Our [chair collection](/chairs) includes light options, and a [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) suits a kids' table.

For alcohol, remember that the legal drinking age in Illinois is 21. Plan for staff to watch the bar and to keep drinks away from children. See [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events).

## Kid-friendly dinnerware

Cups are only part of a kids' table. Many families ask for smaller plates, plastic or melamine, kid-size flatware and a rolled napkin. Check with your rental company about sizes, and see [how many forks, knives and spoons per guest](/blog/how-many-forks-knives-spoons-per-guest) for counts.

## Chicago-area planning notes

Family events in the Chicago area often mix age groups at one long table, such as a quinceañera, a bar mitzvah or a family reunion in a suburban banquet hall. In those cases, mark kids' cups with a name or a color so they do not wander into the adult bar. Ask the venue whether it has a policy for guests under 21 near the bar, since some require minors to stay in a certain area. If the event is outdoors in summer, bring extra water and a place for kids to rest in the shade, and put the kids' station near a parent's table so refills are easy.

For a long evening, think about timing. Kids tire and spill more late at night, so move any toast earlier and plan a lidded cup for the final hour.

## Frequently asked questions

### What cups should I use for kids at a wedding?

Unbreakable 6 to 8 ounce tumblers or lidded cups. Acrylic looks close to glass and survives drops.

### Can kids have a toast?

Yes. Give them sparkling juice or cider in a small cup, and let them raise it with the adults.

### How many cups do kids need?

2 to 3 per child for the evening, plus 1 for the toast and a few spares.

### Should kids sit near the bar?

No. Place them away from the bar and near their parents, with servers who pour only water and juice.

### Can I serve kids soda?

Yes, if parents agree. Many events offer lemonade, water and milk instead, and limit sugar.

### Do I need a separate kids' menu?

Often yes. Ask your caterer about kids' meals, and remember that drinks need the same planning as food.
