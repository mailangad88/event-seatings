**Quick answer**

Nonprofits save most on rentals by renting only what the venue lacks, choosing one upgraded item instead of many, booking early and asking for line-item quotes. Count guests precisely, borrow tables where allowed and pick a style that needs fewer extras. Every dollar spent on rentals is a dollar not raised, so aim to make guests feel good, not to impress them.

For a nonprofit, rental costs sit on the wrong side of the ledger. The gala's goal is to raise money, and the chairs, tables and linens are the overhead. The aim is not to skimp, since guests judge the event by how it feels, but to spend where they notice and save where they do not. We do not publish prices for tables, linens or dinnerware, and we are not promising discounts. This guide is about decisions that reduce the bill, whichever company you rent from.

## Ten ways to lower the bill

1. **Ask what the venue includes.** Many venues own tables, chairs or linens. Rent only the gap. See [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).
2. **Count precisely.** Order for the real attendance plus a small spare, not the invitation list. Track RSVPs and decide a cutoff.
3. **Upgrade one thing.** Choose standard chairs and spend on linens, or choose a premium chair and keep the cloths simple. Mixed investments rarely show.
4. **Upgrade only the visible tables.** Sponsor and VIP tables get the nicer chair.
5. **Skip what guests will not use.** Chargers, extra glassware and chair covers add lines to the quote.
6. **Use fewer styles.** Each new item type adds handling and setup time.
7. **Book early.** More inventory, more options and less pressure on the delivery calendar.
8. **Bundle the order.** One quote for chairs, tables and linens avoids duplicated delivery fees.
9. **Pick a flexible date.** Ask your rental company whether a weekday or off-peak weekend is easier.
10. **Ask for line items.** A quote with each category priced shows where to trim.

## Borrow and share, safely

Parishes, schools, park districts and other nonprofits sometimes lend tables and chairs. Ask what they allow, who transports it and who insures it. Check the condition before the day, and keep a list of what you borrowed. A volunteer team can set up borrowed items, but they cannot replace professional delivery for a large order if safety is an issue.

## Ask the right questions in a quote

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>What is included:</strong> delivery, setup, pickup and linens.</li>
<li><strong>What is extra:</strong> stairs, long carries and after-hours.</li>
<li><strong>What is the damage policy:</strong> breakage and loss.</li>
<li><strong>What is the deadline:</strong> for final counts and changes.</li>
<li><strong>Is there a minimum:</strong> order size or delivery fee.</li>
</ol>

Tell the rental company you are a nonprofit during your first conversation. We can't promise a discount, but a transparent quote is easier to adjust than a flat total. Ask whether there are alternative styles that keep the look at lower cost.

## Where an upgrade pays off

An upgraded chair can lift the mood of a gala and raise bids. [Ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) or [velvet chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) at sponsor tables are a visible upgrade without changing the whole room. A floor-length cloth on every table, including the registration and auction tables, makes the event feel organized. See the [charity gala rental checklist](/blog/charity-gala-rental-checklist) for what to count.

## Tax and donated services

Some rental companies donate or discount items for causes they support. If you ask for an in-kind donation, get a written acknowledgement and consult your accountant about the receipt. We do not give tax advice. Thank donors publicly at the event and in your follow-up.

## Chicago timing

Chicago's gala season runs through spring and fall, with weekends filling early. Booking a weeknight or a Sunday afternoon may cost less in venue fees and ease the rental delivery calendar. Ask venues about nonprofit rates, since some offer them.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can a nonprofit lower rental costs?

Rent only what the venue lacks, count guests precisely, upgrade one visible item, and ask for a line-item quote to see where to trim.

### Do rental companies give nonprofit discounts?

Some may. We can't promise one, so tell the company you are a nonprofit when you request a quote and ask what options exist.

### Should I rent chairs or use the venue's?

If the venue's chairs are acceptable, use them and rent upgrades only for sponsor tables.

### How much extra should I order?

About 5% spare chairs is a common planning number, more for open-door public events.

### Can volunteers set up rentals?

For small orders, yes. For large ones, delivery and setup by the rental team reduces risk and time.

### When should a nonprofit book rentals?

As soon as the date and venue are set. Early quotes leave time to adjust.
