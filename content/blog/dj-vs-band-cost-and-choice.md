## How the two compare in practice

A DJ brings recorded music, speakers, lighting and a microphone, and can handle announcements, speeches and quick genre changes. A band brings musicians, instruments and sound gear, and offers live energy, interaction and a showpiece moment. A band usually takes longer to set up and needs a sound check.

Some hosts split the difference: a band for dinner and a first set, then a DJ for late-night dancing, or a DJ who adds a live musician. For layout, see [DJ and band placement floor plan](/blog/dj-and-band-placement-floor-plan) and [DJ and band table setup](/blog/dj-and-band-table-setup).

## How to book entertainment

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Set your music budget.</strong> Decide what share of the total you will spend; many planners set aside a modest share.</li>
<li><strong>Check the venue.</strong> Ask about stage space, power, load-in, sound limits and end time.</li>
<li><strong>Shortlist three options.</strong> Watch recent videos or ask to see them perform.</li>
<li><strong>Ask the right questions.</strong> Hours, breaks, setup time, backups, equipment, insurance, travel fees and overtime.</li>
<li><strong>Share a must-play and do-not-play list.</strong> Include family requests and cultural music.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm in writing.</strong> Date, hours, price, deposit, cancellation and arrival time.</li>
</ol>

## Questions to ask every entertainer

Ask what happens if the performer is sick, whether they carry insurance, how loud they play, whether they also act as MC, and what they need from you: power, a table, a shaded area, a meal. Provide a run of show so they know when to start, pause and stop; our [run of show template](/downloads/run-of-show-template) helps. Also confirm sound coverage for speeches; see [sound system for speeches](/blog/sound-system-for-speeches) and [microphone types for events](/blog/microphone-types-for-events).

## Hybrid and budget options

If a full band is out of reach, consider a smaller combo, a DJ with a saxophone or violin, or a live set during cocktail hour followed by a DJ. Some hosts hire a trio for dinner and a DJ for dancing. Ask each performer for a la carte pricing so you can match spend to the moments that matter most. Compare these options against your venue's space and sound limits before you decide.

## Chicago-area notes

Many Chicago-area venues and neighborhoods have sound rules, and some suburbs have noise ordinances with set end times. Ask your venue and check with your village or park district. High-rise and loft buildings often have specific load-in rules and limits on amplified sound. For outdoor events, a tent needs safe cord routing and sometimes a generator; see [event power needs checklist](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist), [generator size for events](/blog/generator-size-for-events) and [outdoor extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events). In winter, ask performers about load-in in the cold and bring dry space for instruments.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a DJ cheaper than a band?

Usually, yes. DJs generally cost less because there are fewer people and less equipment involved. Prices vary by experience and date, so compare written quotes.

### How much does a DJ cost in Chicago?

As a rough estimate, many Chicago-area DJs range from several hundred dollars to a few thousand for a typical event, depending on hours, lighting and services. Always get quotes.

### How much does a live band cost?

Bands generally cost more because of the number of musicians. A rough Chicago-area range starts in the low thousands and rises with size and experience. Ask for itemized quotes.

### How much space does a band need?

A small band may need a stage or area roughly the size of a large rug to a full stage, depending on members and equipment. Ask each band for its stage plot. See [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events).

### Can I have both a DJ and a band?

Yes. Many hosts use a band early and a DJ later, or a DJ with a live musician. Plan the handoff carefully and share the schedule with both.

### How long should I book music for?

Many events book three to five hours of music, with dancing as the main block. Consider cocktail hour and dinner background music separately.

### How early should I book?

For peak dates, many people book six to twelve months ahead. For smaller events, a few months is common. Popular performers fill quickly in June, September and December.
