**Quick answer**

For a chess tournament, set pairing tables for two players each, with enough space on both sides for a tournament director to walk every board without bumping a chair. Plan two chairs per entered player, not per team, since every player sits at their own board each round, plus a few extra for late sign-ups and byes. A school gym or library meeting room rarely has enough matching chairs for a full field of entrants, so most tournament directors end up renting.

Scholastic and club chess tournaments in the Chicago area usually happen in a school gym, library meeting room or community center, set up with folding tables in long pairing rows. The chair count sounds simple until you realize every single entrant needs their own seat for every round, at a table that's shared but a chair that isn't, and that most venues don't keep anywhere near that many matching chairs on hand.

## How to lay out a tournament room

1. **Set pairing tables in rows, with two chairs at each table**, facing each other across the board.
2. **Space tables so a director can walk between every row without brushing a chair**, since directors check clocks and scoresheets constantly during a round.
3. **Leave a wider aisle near the pairing sheet or demonstration board**, where players check their next matchup between rounds.
4. **Put a quiet buffer zone, not more tables, near the entrance**, so late arrivals and parents don't disrupt boards already in play.

## How many chairs to rent

Count by entered players, not by school or club team size:

- **Plan two chairs for every pairing table**, and plan as many tables as half your entrant count, rounding up for an odd number and a bye.
- **Add a handful of extra chairs near the director's table and scorekeeping area**, plus a few spares for a chair that squeaks or wobbles mid-round.
- **Check the final entry count the morning of the tournament**, since late sign-ups and no-shows both happen and the pairing software needs the real number before round one.

## Chairs for a tournament

Chess tournaments run long, often five or six rounds across a full day, so a comfortable chair matters more here than at a short ceremony. Players who are uncomfortable move more, which distracts the board next to them.

## What the venue usually has, and what it doesn't

- **School gyms and cafeterias** often have enough folding tables but far fewer matching chairs than entrants, especially once the field grows past 40 or 50 players.
- **Library meeting rooms** are usually sized for a much smaller crowd than a tournament draws, since most library chess clubs meet weekly with 10 to 20 regulars.
- **Confirm the room's chair count with the venue in writing**, and compare it against your actual entry count once registration closes, not an early estimate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a chess tournament?
Plan two chairs per pairing table, with as many tables as half your entrant count. Add a few spares for the director's table and for a chair that needs swapping mid-round.

### How should a chess tournament room be set up?
Set pairing tables for two in rows, spaced with a clear walking aisle so the director can check every board during a round without bumping a chair.

### Does our school or library already have enough chairs?
Sometimes partway. Most venues have fewer matching chairs than a full tournament field needs, especially once entries grow past 40 or 50 players. Confirm the venue's chair count in writing before you order.

### What's a good chair for a long tournament day?
A padded chair without arms, like a mid-century shell chair, holds up better over five or six rounds than a hard plastic cafeteria chair. Avoid armchairs, which can bump an opponent's elbow at a crowded pairing table.
