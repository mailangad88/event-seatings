**Quick answer**

You need separate ceremony and reception chairs only when both spaces are set at the same time. If guests leave the ceremony space for cocktail hour, one set can be flipped to the reception for a labor fee, which usually costs less than renting a second set of chairs.

</div>


There's no rule saying your ceremony and reception chairs have to match. Here's how to think about it.

## When matching makes sense

- **Same space for both.** If you're flipping the room, one chair keeps things simple.
- **A tight budget.** One style means one rental line and fewer moving parts.
- **A minimalist look.** One clean chair everywhere reads as calm and considered.

## When mixing works better

- **Outdoor ceremony, indoor reception.** Use a weather-friendly folding or garden chair outside and a more refined chair inside.
- **You want a moment.** Changing chairs marks the shift from the formal ceremony to the party.
- **A statement head table.** Guests sit in cross-backs while the couple sits in velvet or a carved sweetheart chair.

## Pairings we love

- Teak garden folding (ceremony) → Cross-back (reception)
- Ghost (ceremony) → Velvet (reception)
- Windsor (ceremony) → Bentwood (reception)

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a chair flip?

Moving the ceremony chairs to the reception tables during cocktail hour. It takes a crew about 45 to 90 minutes depending on guest count and distance between spaces.

### Is a flip cheaper than a second set of chairs?

Usually, for 100 or more guests. The flip is labor only, while a second set doubles the rental cost. For small weddings the two can be close, so compare both in your quote.

### Can ceremony chairs be a different style?

Yes. Many couples use folding or garden chairs for an outdoor ceremony and nicer chairs for dinner, which also avoids a flip.
