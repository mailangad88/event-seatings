**Quick answer**

A dry-hire (or "blank space") venue rents you the room or grounds, and you bring the caterer, bar, tables, chairs, linens, place settings, lighting and staff. An all-inclusive venue bundles most of that into a per-person price. Dry hire gives more freedom and can cost less for small or simple events; all-inclusive is easier and often better value for large, traditional dinners. Compare the full cost of each, not the venue fee.

</div>

Every venue sits somewhere on a scale from "four walls" to "everything included". In Chicago, lofts, warehouses, galleries, barns, backyards and many park venues sit at the dry-hire end. Hotels, banquet halls and country clubs usually sit at the all-inclusive end. Knowing where a venue sits is the only way to compare quotes fairly.

## When dry hire wins

- **You want a specific look.** Dry hire lets you choose every chair, linen and light. A [loft or warehouse](/blog/warehouse-and-raw-space-event-venues) can become anything.
- **The event is small or simple.** A cocktail party or a family-style dinner for 60 may need far fewer rentals than a 200-guest seated dinner.
- **You have a caterer in mind.** Dry hire usually lets you bring any licensed caterer.
- **Culture and food matter.** Many families choose dry hire so they can bring a caterer who knows their cuisine.

## When all-inclusive wins

- **Large, traditional dinners.** For 150 or more seated guests, a venue that already owns tables, chairs, place settings and staff can be simpler and better value.
- **Short planning time.** One contract replaces a dozen.
- **Winter.** Built-in heat, restrooms and coat check matter from November to March.
- **Peak dates with a tight budget for time.** On a busy June or October Saturday, an all-inclusive venue removes the risk of chasing a dozen vendors for the same date. See the [Chicago event planning calendar](/blog/chicago-event-planning-calendar-month-by-month) for the busiest weekends.

## Questions to ask a dry-hire venue

1. What is in the space today: tables, chairs, restrooms, power, heat, air conditioning?
2. When can rentals arrive and when must they leave?
3. Is there a loading dock, elevator or stairs?
4. Are there required vendors, security or cleaning fees?
5. What insurance does the venue require from vendors? (We do not issue certificates of insurance ourselves yet, so tell us at quote time.)

## Frequently asked questions

### What does dry hire mean for a venue?

Dry hire means you rent the space only. You bring the caterer, bar, furniture, linens, place settings, lighting and staff yourself, usually from vendors you choose.

### Is a dry-hire venue cheaper?

Sometimes. The venue fee is lower, but you add rentals, catering, staff and possibly restrooms and power. Small or simple events often come out cheaper; large seated dinners often favor all-inclusive. Compare the full totals.

### What do I need to rent for a dry-hire venue?

Usually tables, chairs, linens, place settings, glassware, serving equipment and lighting, and sometimes restrooms, power, heat or a tent. The venue's inventory decides the list.

### Can I bring my own caterer to an all-inclusive venue?

Usually not. All-inclusive venues normally require their in-house catering, and some allow exceptions for specialty or cultural cuisine for a fee.

### Which Chicago venues are dry hire?

Many lofts, warehouses, galleries, barns, backyards and park sites. Ask each venue what is included, because some lofts and barns include furniture and some include nothing.
