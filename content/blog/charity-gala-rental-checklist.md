**Quick answer**

A charity gala needs seven rental groups: seating, tables, linens, dinnerware and glassware, bar and service gear, auction and registration items, and stage or AV support. Start with the guest count, add one chair per guest plus a small spare, then rent tables, linens and place settings to match. Ask your venue what it already includes before you order anything.

</div>

Committees for charity galas usually start with the mission, the speaker and the auction. The rentals come last, and that is when a volunteer discovers nobody counted the registration tables, the coat racks or the napkins. This checklist works from the guest count outward so nothing falls through. It fits a 100-person dinner in a church hall as well as a 400-person ballroom night in downtown Chicago.

## Seating: the biggest line

Count every guest ticket, then add the people the ticket count misses: speakers, honorees, sponsors' staff, volunteers who sit down for dinner and any photographer who needs a seat. Rounds of 10 are the most space-efficient, but a table of 8 gives sponsors more room and is easier to serve. See [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) for a count by zone, and [gala seating beyond the banquet chair](/blog/corporate-gala-seating-beyond-banquet-chair) for style options.

## Tables and linens

A 150-guest dinner with 15 rounds of 10 also needs registration, silent auction, a bar front or two, a dessert table, a gift or giveaway table and a DJ or band table. That is a lot of cloths. Read the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) before you order and remember that auction tables benefit from a black or neutral cloth so bid sheets and item cards stay readable.

## Dinnerware and glassware

Ask your caterer what they bring. Many caterers supply plates and flatware, and some do not supply glassware or linens. A plated dinner usually needs a dinner plate, salad or appetizer plate, bread plate, dessert plate, and a fork, knife and spoon per course. Count glasses by what will be poured: water, wine, champagne for the toast and coffee cups. Our [place setting diagram](/blog/place-setting-diagram-formal-dinner) shows the layout.

Silent auction tables work best along a wall, at counter height, with a 6-foot table for every 8 to 12 items. Keep walkways open so bidders can stand without blocking the bar. If the event raffles tickets, Illinois regulates raffles and many counties and municipalities require a local license; ask your county clerk or village before you advertise one.

## Chicago-area logistics for a fundraiser

- **Load-in windows.** Downtown buildings often assign dock times. Plan delivery before the venue's own setup crew needs the room.
- **Winter coats.** From November through March, a coat check is part of the rental list.
- **Parking and valet.** Suburban banquet halls rarely need rentals for parking, but a covered drop-off area with a few lounge pieces improves arrivals.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent for a charity gala?

At minimum, chairs, tables and linens for every guest, plus dinnerware, glassware and bar gear unless the venue or caterer provides them. Add registration, auction and stage items. A walk-through with the venue shows what is already included.

### How many tables does a 200-guest gala need?

About 20 rounds of 10 or 25 rounds of 8, plus 6 to 10 utility tables for registration, auction, bar and gifts. Confirm the room fits them with 5-foot aisles.

### Does a charity gala need linens for every table?

Yes, usually, including utility tables. Bare rental tables look unfinished in photos and make auction items harder to read.

### Do nonprofits get a rental discount?

We can't promise a discount. Tell us you are a nonprofit when you ask for a quote and we'll work out what is possible for your list. See our [nonprofit rental budget tips](/blog/nonprofit-rental-budget-tips).

### When should I book gala rentals?

As soon as the venue and date are set, and no later than six to eight weeks out in the busy fall season. Last-minute orders are possible but limit the styles available.

### Who sets up the rentals at the venue?

It depends on the order. Many rental orders include delivery to the room and some include setup. Ask for a written scope so volunteers aren't setting 150 chairs themselves at 4 pm.
