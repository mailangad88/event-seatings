**Quick answer**

A Polish wedding reception is usually a long, multi-course, family-centered party, so plan a comfortable chair for every guest (not a mix of standing and sitting), a dance floor sized for the whole guest list, and a layout that keeps the couple's parents and grandparents close to the head table. Budget for 8 to 10 guests per table, extra table space for shared platters and bottles, and a clear spot at the entrance for the traditional bread and salt welcome. Customs vary by family and region, so confirm each one with your parents first.

</div>

Chicago has long-established Polish communities on the Northwest Side and in the suburbs, and many Polish-American couples blend traditions from Poland with American reception habits. The result is a day that often runs from a church ceremony into a reception that lasts well past midnight. That changes what you need to rent: people sit longer, eat more courses and dance harder than at a typical 4-hour reception.

## What shapes the seating at a Polish reception

Families differ, but several customs commonly affect the room plan:

- **Welcome at the door.** Parents traditionally greet the couple with bread and salt, often with a drink. You need a small clear area at the entrance, not a crowded foyer.
- **Many courses.** Receptions often include several food courses and platters set on the table, so tables need space for shared dishes, not just place settings.
- **Toasts and "Sto lat."** Guests stand, sing and toast often. Chairs need to slide back easily, and aisles between tables should be wide.
- **Late-night traditions.** Many families include a midnight custom, such as the veiling or capping ritual known as oczepiny, plus games that pull guests onto the dance floor.
- **Multi-generation guest lists.** Grandparents, godparents, cousins and children all attend, so comfort matters.

Treat the list as a starting point. If a custom matters to you, ask a parent or the family member who knows it best how it is done in your family, then tell your venue and rental planner.

## Table and chair counts for 100, 150 and 200 guests

Rounds of 10 are common at long-form receptions because the extra space handles shared platters. The 72-inch round fits 10 comfortably; a 60-inch round fits 8.

## The bread and salt welcome

Many families greet the couple at the reception entrance with bread and salt, often with a drink. It takes a minute or two, but it needs a clear, well-lit spot and a small table with a cloth. Ask your venue where the entrance sits, whether a doorway can handle a short pause and whether the photographer has a clear angle.

## Linens, tableware and the table itself

Shared platters mean you want more table surface per guest than at plated-only events. Floor-length linens on 72-inch rounds need a 132-inch round cloth (see our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart)). Count extra dishes and serving pieces, and ask your caterer what they provide and what you must rent. ## Chicago planning notes

- Many Chicago-area banquet halls include tables and chairs, so ask what is included before you rent anything. Rentals fill gaps, such as upgraded chairs, a head table or lounge seating.
- Confirm venue end times and music rules. Late-night dancing is common, but venues set their own limits.
- Winter weddings need a coat check plan. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).
- If your reception runs from afternoon into night, check load-in and load-out hours with your venue.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many guests sit at each table at a Polish wedding?

Most receptions seat 8 to 10 guests per round table. Ten per table works well at a 72-inch round, especially when courses and shared platters crowd the table. Choose 8 per table if your family wants more elbow room.

### Do I need to rent a head table for a Polish reception?

Many couples seat the bride, groom and parents at a long head table, but it is a choice, not a rule. Some couples seat parents at a nearby family table. Decide based on your family's customs and how many people you want beside you.

### How big should the dance floor be?

A common rule of thumb is about 4 to 5 square feet per dancing guest, assuming roughly half to two thirds of guests dance at once. For 150 guests, that points to about 20 x 20 feet. Polish receptions often get the whole room dancing, so lean larger.

### What do I need for the bread and salt welcome?

You need a small table with a cloth, a clear spot near the entrance and enough room for the couple and parents to pause. Your family usually supplies the bread, salt and drink. Ask your venue where the best spot is.

### Can I rent chairs if my banquet hall already has some?

Yes. Many couples keep the hall's standard chairs for most guests and rent upgraded chairs for the head table, family tables or ceremony. Ask the hall which items are included and whether outside rentals are allowed.

### How early should I order rentals for a Chicago summer or fall wedding?

Book as early as you can once your date and venue are set. Peak months fill up first. Our [when to book event rentals](/blog/topic/budget-and-booking) guide explains the timing.
