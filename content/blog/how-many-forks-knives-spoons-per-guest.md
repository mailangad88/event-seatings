**Quick answer**

Rent one dinner fork and one dinner knife per guest for a buffet, add a salad fork and dessert fork for a plated meal, and add a teaspoon if you serve coffee. A good rule of thumb is 3 to 5 pieces per guest for a plated dinner and 2 to 3 for a buffet, plus 10 percent extra. For 100 guests at a plated wedding, that is about 400 to 500 pieces.

</div>

Flatware is cheap per piece and miserable to run short on. Nobody can eat salad with a dessert spoon, and a caterer who finds only 80 knives for 100 guests will start washing in the middle of service. Here is how to count it up.

## Count by menu, not by guest alone

The number of pieces depends on the number of courses and how guests get their food.

## Counts for common guest lists

For a plated three-course dinner at 4 to 5 pieces per guest, with 10 percent extra:

- **50 guests:** 200 to 250 pieces, plus 20 to 25 spares
- **100 guests:** 400 to 500 pieces, plus 40 to 50 spares
- **150 guests:** 600 to 750 pieces, plus 60 to 75 spares

For a buffet at 2 to 3 pieces per guest:

- **50 guests:** 100 to 150 pieces
- **100 guests:** 200 to 300 pieces
- **150 guests:** 300 to 450 pieces

## Don't forget these

- **Teaspoons.** One per guest if you serve coffee or tea, plus a few for serving sugar. See [coffee and tea service](/blog/coffee-and-tea-service-rental).
- **Steak knives.** If the entree is a steak, a regular dinner knife may not be enough. Ask your caterer.
- **Serving utensils.** Buffet lines need a spoon, tongs or fork for each dish. Plan 1 to 2 per dish.
- **Cake knife and server.** One set for the cake table.
- **Cocktail forks.** Smaller forks for hors d'oeuvres or dessert stations.
- **Children's flatware.** Smaller sizes are available at some rental companies. See [kids' tableware](/blog/kids-glassware-and-toasts).

## Choosing a style

Flatware comes in silver, gold, matte black and rose gold finishes. The gold finish has become a popular wedding choice; the [gold flatware trend](/blog/gold-flatware-wedding-trend) explains how to use it without clashing. Pattern matters less than weight: heavier flatware feels better in the hand and photographs well. Ask the rental company whether the style comes in every piece you need, as some finishes do not include a dessert fork.

## Who washes what?

Ask your caterer or banquet hall whether they have flatware in-house. Many do, and renting it would be a waste. If you do rent, find out if the pieces arrive pre-washed, whether they go back rinsed or unrinsed, and how missing pieces are charged. Flatware vanishes into trash bags more than any other rental, so tell your catering staff to check the bins before they leave. See [glass rack logistics](/blog/glass-rack-logistics-rental-returns) for return tips that apply to flatware too.

## A Chicago note on venues

Venues across Chicago vary widely: a hotel ballroom provides everything, a gallery or loft provides nothing, and a forest preserve shelter or park pavilion may not allow certain items or events at all, so check the facility rules. Your caterer should be the last stop on your count. Send them your rental list and ask what they will bring.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many forks do I need per guest?

One fork per guest for a buffet, and two for a plated dinner with a salad. Add a dessert fork if the dessert is not eaten with a spoon. Always add 10 percent extra.

### How many pieces of flatware per person for a wedding?

Plan 4 to 5 pieces per guest for a plated wedding dinner and 2 to 3 for a buffet. This covers a salad fork, dinner fork, knife and dessert utensil.

### Do I need a separate knife for the salad?

Not usually. A dinner knife handles both the salad and the entree. A butter knife is only needed if bread plates are used.

### Do I need teaspoons?

Yes, if you serve coffee, tea or cappuccino. Plan one teaspoon per guest and a few extras for sugar and cream.

### Do I rent flatware if the caterer provides plates?

Check first. Caterers who provide plates often provide flatware too. Ask them in writing what is included before you rent anything duplicate.

### How much extra flatware should I rent?

Rent 10 percent more than your count. Pieces get dropped, thrown out and mismatched, and a spare tray costs less than a last-minute run.
