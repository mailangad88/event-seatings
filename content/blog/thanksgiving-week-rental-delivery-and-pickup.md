Thanksgiving is the one dinner where the table is the whole event. If you are hosting 16 or 30 in a Chicago two-flat, bungalow or suburban split-level, you probably need extra tables, chairs and linens. The trouble is timing. Everyone wants the same Wednesday, and the day after the holiday is slow for everyone who handles a truck. This guide shows how to pick days that work, what to do with rentals before the dinner, and how to plan for the pickup that does not go as scheduled.

## The week at a glance

The days around Thanksgiving are not like a normal event week. Delivery crews are in short supply on Thursday, and many rental companies are closed or have limited hours. Rules vary by company, so confirm each date when you book.

## Why Tuesday or Wednesday for delivery

Delivery the Monday before is often too early. You would live with stacks of chairs in the dining room for three days, and linens are easier to keep clean when they arrive close to the dinner. Wednesday is the most popular day, so it fills first. Tuesday is the safer ask. A Tuesday delivery gives you time to count items, set the tables the night before and find out that a cloth is the wrong size while there is still time to fix it.

If you can only get Wednesday, ask for a delivery window and have a plan for the pot of cranberries on the stove, because a window can be 2 to 4 hours long.

## Friday or Monday for pickup

Most hosts want pickup the next morning. That is the day the companies are least able to do it. Friday is a real option at some companies and a closed day at others. Many will offer Monday instead, and some charge extra for a holiday or weekend pickup. Ask before you book, not after. See [late-night and early pickup fees](/blog/late-night-and-early-pickup-fees-event-rentals) for how those charges usually work.

## Keeping rentals safe before and after

A cold November can be hard on rented items you keep outside. Linens left in a car trunk or a damp garage can pick up smells and moisture. Chairs left in an unheated garage are fine, but wet or frozen floors can warp the cardboard cartons and the plastic crates that hold dinnerware.

- Keep linens and dinnerware indoors, off the floor and out of the way of the oven.
- Chairs and tables can wait in a cold garage if it is dry and the door is closed.
- Do not stack rental plates in the oven drawer or near a heat vent.
- Count each item at delivery and again before pickup.
- Rinse and scrape plates, and put dirty linens into a bag, not a pile. Follow your rental company's rules on pre-washing and stains. See [returning rentals](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning).

## Order early, then lock the numbers

Thanksgiving week is one of the busiest of the year for tables and chairs, because homes cannot seat 20 people on their own. Order as early as you can, ideally in the first week of November. The final headcount can wait, but the date and the main items should not. See [when to book chair rentals in Chicago](/blog/when-to-book-event-chair-rentals-chicago) and [final headcount deadlines](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals).

A rough, adjustable plan: seat adults at tables of 8 to 10 where you can, and use a separate kids table. For the table math, see [Thanksgiving dinner for a crowd](/blog/thanksgiving-dinner-for-a-crowd) and [Friendsgiving for 20](/blog/friendsgiving-party-for-20-rentals). Linens are your biggest decision. A 6-foot banquet table takes a 90 x 132-inch cloth to the floor. Check the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

## If the pickup gets pushed

Sometimes the crew cannot make it on Friday. You are not stuck, but you do need a plan.

1. Ask for the new pickup window in writing, and ask whether the extra day has a charge.
2. Move the tables and chairs into the corner of the room or the garage, so the house is usable.
3. Bag and label dirty linens and keep them out of the cold, not on a porch.
4. Tell everyone at home who the pickup contact is, so a driver is not turned away.
5. Keep the packing crates and boxes. The company will want the same ones back.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I have Thanksgiving rentals delivered?

Tuesday or Wednesday before Thanksgiving works best. Tuesday gives you time to count items and set the tables, and Wednesday fills first. Delivery on Thursday itself is usually closed or limited, so do not count on it.

### Can I get a Friday pickup after Thanksgiving?

Sometimes. Some companies run Friday pickups and others are closed or short on crews and offer Monday instead. Ask when you book, and plan for the pickup to land a day or two later than you hoped.

### How early should I order Thanksgiving tables and chairs?

As early as you can, ideally the first week of November or sooner. Thanksgiving week is a peak week, and items run out before the date does. You can adjust the final headcount later, but the date and main items should be set early.

### Do rental companies charge extra for holiday week?

Some do. Holiday, weekend or off-hours pickups can carry extra fees at some companies. Ask for every fee on your quote so you can compare it to other dates.

### Can I leave rented chairs in a cold garage?

Yes, if the garage is dry and the door is closed. Keep linens and dinnerware indoors, off the floor and away from damp. Count items before the truck leaves and again at pickup.

### How many tables and chairs do I need for 20 people?

A common rule of thumb is 2 to 3 tables of 8 to 10 seats, plus a few extra chairs. A 6-foot banquet table seats 6 to 8 and an 8-foot table seats 8 to 10. Leave room to walk behind the chairs.
