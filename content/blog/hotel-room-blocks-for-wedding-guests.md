**Quick answer**

A hotel room block is a set of rooms a hotel holds for your guests at a negotiated rate. Book one about nine to twelve months ahead, hold roughly 70 to 80 percent of the rooms you think guests will need, and read the contract for the cutoff date and attrition terms. Share the booking code on your wedding website and with the save the date.

</div>

Room blocks protect your guests from sold-out hotels and can make travel easier. They can also create risk for you if the contract requires you to pay for unused rooms. Understanding the terms before you sign is the key. With a little planning, a block can be a simple gift to your guests.

## How room blocks work

You contact one or more hotels, share your dates and expected room count and ask for a group rate. The hotel holds a set of rooms and gives you a booking code or link. Guests book directly with the hotel at the group rate, usually until a cutoff date. After the cutoff, unbooked rooms return to general sale and the rate may change.

There are two main types. A courtesy block holds rooms without a financial commitment, usually with a cutoff. A contracted block may include an attrition clause, which means you may owe money if guests book fewer rooms than a set percentage. Ask which kind the hotel offers, and ask to see the contract.

## How many rooms to hold

Start with your out-of-town guest list. Count households, not people, because couples and families usually share rooms. Then estimate how many will stay at the hotel instead of with family or in other lodging. Many couples hold around 70 to 80 percent of the rooms they expect guests to need, which keeps risk low and still gives guests options. If the wedding is in a popular weekend, hold more. See [how to cut a wedding guest list](/blog/how-to-cut-a-wedding-guest-list) if your count is still changing.

Consider holding blocks at two price levels or two hotels, so guests with different budgets have options.

## Questions to ask the hotel

Ask about the group rate and how it compares with the public rate. Ask about the cutoff date, cancellation rules for guests and for you, attrition, parking fees, breakfast, shuttles and whether you can add rooms later. Ask what happens if the hotel sells out before the cutoff. Ask if there is a complimentary room for the couple or a discount on a suite. Get everything in writing.

## Sharing the block with guests

Put the hotel name, rate, booking code, cutoff date and a link on your wedding website and on your save the date or invitation. Remind guests as the cutoff nears. Explain that booking early is wise, and that guests can still choose different lodging. Do not make guests feel obligated to stay in the block. See [wedding weekend itinerary for out-of-town guests](/blog/wedding-weekend-itinerary-out-of-town-guests) for how to present travel information.

## Courtesy block vs contracted block

Understanding the type of block is the first step.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Feature | Courtesy block | Contracted block |
|---|---|---|
| Financial risk | Usually none | May owe for unused rooms |
| Group rate | Often offered | Often offered |
| Attrition clause | Not usually | Common |
| Cutoff date | Yes | Yes |
| Best for | Smaller weddings | Larger guest counts |
| Key question | What is the cutoff? | What is the attrition percentage? |

</div>

Always confirm the details in writing, since terms vary by hotel.

## Set up a room block

</ol>

## Alternatives to a room block

A formal block is not always necessary. For a small wedding, you can simply share two or three hotel options at different prices, with a note about booking early. For a larger wedding, a block gives guests a rate and a simple way to find each other. Some couples use rental apartments or houses for family groups, which can be cheaper per person but need coordination.

If you skip the block, tell guests which hotels are near the venue, how long the drive or ride is and when to book. Peak weekends can sell out, so mention that early. A short list of options, with links and rough price levels, helps guests make decisions without obligating you to any contract.

## Chicago-area notes

In Chicago, hotels near downtown fill quickly on weekends with conventions, sports and festivals, so book blocks early. Downtown rates may include higher taxes and parking costs, while suburban hotels often have lower rates and free parking. If your venue is far from the city, ask hotels about shuttles. For winter weddings, consider adding a few extra rooms in case weather delays travel. If your guests are arriving at O'Hare or Midway, tell them about transit and rideshares. Ask hotels about late checkout on the day after the wedding for your guests. Keep a copy of the block contract and the hotel's contact person in your planning folder, and check the pickup report with the hotel a few weeks before the cutoff. If bookings are slow, a personal reminder to guests often moves the number more than another website update.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a hotel room block for a wedding?

It is a group of rooms a hotel holds for your guests at a negotiated rate. Guests book directly using a code or link, usually until a cutoff date.

### How many rooms should I block for a wedding?

Many couples hold around 70 to 80 percent of the rooms they estimate guests will need. Count households rather than individuals.

### When should I book a hotel room block?

Nine to twelve months before the wedding is typical, and earlier for peak dates or destination weddings.

### Do I have to pay for unused rooms?

It depends on the contract. Courtesy blocks usually carry no financial risk, while contracted blocks may include attrition. Ask and read the contract.

### What is attrition in a hotel block?

Attrition is the percentage of rooms you must fill. If guests book fewer, you may owe the difference. Look for low attrition or a courtesy block.

### When should guests book their rooms?

Before the cutoff date, usually about 30 days before the wedding. Encourage early booking and send reminders.

### Are group rates cheaper than public rates?

Often, but not always. Guests can compare. Do not require anyone to stay in the block.
