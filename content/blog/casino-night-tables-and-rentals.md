**Quick answer**

For a casino night, plan about one gaming table for every 20 to 25 guests, since only some guests play at once. Blackjack and poker tables seat 5 to 8, roulette seats 6 to 8 and a craps table needs the most room. Give each table 10 to 12 feet of space, add high stools and standing cocktail tables around the edge, and check Illinois and local rules on charitable games before you promise prizes.

Casino nights are a classic for fundraisers, company parties and milestone birthdays. The room fills with felt and chips, guests dress up and everyone gets a reason to mingle. They also have more moving parts than most rentals: tables, dealers, chips, lighting, seating and legal rules. This guide helps you size the room, pick games and avoid the mistakes that cause long lines at the blackjack table.

These are typical dimensions from vendors and gaming suppliers, and not strict rules. Ask your vendor for exact footprints.

## How many tables for your guest count?

A good rule of thumb is one table for every 20 to 25 guests, because many guests mingle, eat or watch. Mix games so lines stay short: blackjack and roulette draw the crowds, and poker draws a smaller, longer-staying group.

## Room layout

Casino rooms look best when they feel busy but not crowded. Follow these steps.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Mark exits, bar and food</strong> first.</li>
<li><strong>Place gaming tables</strong> with 10 to 12 feet each and 4-foot aisles.</li>
<li><strong>Add seating</strong> at the edges: stools, cocktail tables and a lounge.</li>
<li><strong>Set a cashier or chip table</strong> near the entrance.</li>
<li><strong>Light the tables</strong> and keep the rest of the room dim.</li>
</ol>

Keep the busiest tables, such as roulette and blackjack, away from doors and the bar line. Put poker at the quieter end, where players sit for a while. For seating, plan 25 to 35 percent of guests in a lounge or at high tables, as in our post on [lounge layout by guest count](/blog/lounge-layout-by-guest-count). Highboys and bar stools around the tables let guests watch, and [lounge furniture](/blog/lounge-furniture-for-cocktail-hour) offers a break from standing.

## Dealers, chips and prizes

- **Dealers.** Most casino rental companies provide trained dealers. Ask how many are included and for how long.
- **Chips.** Fun money is the norm. Guests get a starting stack, and can buy more for a donation at fundraisers.
- **Prizes.** Raffle tickets and prizes bought with chips are the common approach at charity events.
- **Cashier.** A table where guests exchange tickets or money for chips.
- **Rules cards** at each table, for first-timers.

## Legal checks for Illinois events

Casino-style games, especially at fundraisers where guests give money, are regulated. Illinois law covers charitable games and raffles, and many cities and villages add local rules. Do not assume a license is not needed because chips have no cash value. Ask your village clerk, the Illinois Department of Revenue and your vendor what is required for your event type and guest donations, before you promote it. A vendor that supplies equipment for entertainment-only "fun money" games may have different requirements from a charitable gaming event, so ask which one you are running.

## Seating and furniture

Dealers stand and players sit, so the tables need either high stools or standard chairs. Many rental tables are set for standing play with rails. Add a few seated options, such as chairs at poker, and a lounge for guests who do not play. Provide a table for food, a bar and a prize display. See [card box and gift table ideas](/blog/card-box-and-gift-table-ideas) if guests will be handing in donations or tickets. Add cocktail tables with floor-length linens; the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) lists sizes.

## Chicago notes

Many casino nights happen in banquet halls, hotel ballrooms and union halls, where floor loads, lighting and sprinklers are not an issue, but table and cord placement are. Ask the venue about tape on floors, power for table lights and load-in times. Winter events need coat check; see [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many casino tables do I need for 100 guests?

About 4 or 5, with a mix of blackjack, roulette and poker. Add a craps table if the crowd is large and you have room.

### How much space does a casino table need?

About 10 to 12 feet square for most tables, and more for craps. Leave 4-foot aisles between tables.

### Do casino rentals come with dealers?

Often, yes. Ask the vendor how many dealers are included and for how long.

### Can I give real prizes at a casino night?

It depends on how the event is run and what Illinois and local rules require. Ask your village and the Illinois Department of Revenue before you plan prizes or charge entry.

### How do chips work?

Guests start with fun money chips, and many hosts sell more for a donation. At the end, chips go toward raffle tickets or prizes.

### What furniture goes with casino tables?

High stools, cocktail tables with linens, a lounge and a cashier table. A bar and food station complete the room.
