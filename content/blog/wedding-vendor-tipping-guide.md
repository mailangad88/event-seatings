Tipping is the last line of the wedding budget and the first one forgotten. Nobody wants to guess on the day, and nobody wants to overspend. The best approach is to decide in advance: read the contracts, mark where gratuity is already included, set an amount for everyone else, and put the cash in envelopes before the wedding.

## Tipping rules of thumb

Customs vary by vendor, region and the quality of service. These are common practices, not rules.

## Start with the contract

Before anything else, read each vendor contract. Catering contracts often include a service charge that may or may not be given to staff as gratuity. Venues may add an administrative fee. Ask directly: is gratuity included, and does it go to the staff? If a service charge is already in the price, you may not need to tip, though many couples add extra for exceptional service. If gratuity is not included, a tip is customary.

Note that a service charge may be different from a tip: it can go to the company rather than the workers. Ask how it is distributed so you know whether additional tips are appropriate.

## Who couples commonly tip

Common recipients include catering staff and bartenders, hair and makeup artists, drivers and shuttle operators, delivery and setup crews, valet attendants and coat-check staff. Many couples tip these people because they are hourly workers who depend on tips. Business owners, such as a photographer who owns the company, often do not expect a tip, but a thank-you note, a review or a modest tip is appreciated. DJs, bands and officiants vary: some contracts mention gratuity, while others do not. Ask the vendor or your planner what is typical.

Rental delivery crews can be tipped at delivery or pickup, especially for heavy loads, long carries or bad weather. Many couples hand a modest cash tip to the crew lead, who shares it.

## Putting numbers in the budget

Because the tipping amount depends on your contracts, build a simple tip line in your budget early. See [how to set a wedding budget](/blog/how-to-set-a-wedding-budget) and [how much a wedding costs in Chicago](/blog/how-much-does-a-wedding-cost-in-chicago). A common method is to set aside a small share of your total budget for gratuities, then decide who gets what. Ask your planner or coordinator for their local norms. Where percentages are used, they are usually applied to the service portion of the bill, not the total with taxes, but check your contract.

## Handing out the envelopes

Prepare envelopes ahead of time, with the vendor name and amount on each. Give them to your planner, coordinator, best man or a trusted family member to hand out at the end of service, not during it. Include a small thank-you note if you like. Keep a list so nothing is missed, especially vendors who leave early, such as the florist or rental crew. For vendors who finish before the wedding, tip at the delivery.

This table summarizes common practices. Amounts depend on your contract and service.

## When tips go wrong, and how to avoid it

The most common tipping problems are practical. The cash is left at home, the envelopes are given to the wrong person, or a vendor leaves before anyone thanks them. Prevent all three with a short plan. Put the envelopes in a bag with a list, give the bag to your coordinator or a reliable friend and ask them to hand each envelope over at the end of that vendor's service. For vendors who leave early, give their envelope when they arrive.

If cash is not possible, ask the vendor whether they accept a check or a digital payment, and add the gratuity to the final invoice instead. For vendors you will not see on the day, such as a stationer, a written thank-you and a good review mean a lot. Keep a list of who received what, so you can reconcile with your budget afterward.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area customs follow the same general pattern, but many downtown venues and caterers include service charges. Check each contract and ask how it is distributed. Cash is common, but make sure you have small bills and a plan for carrying them safely on the day. If you have rentals delivered to a high-rise or a venue with a long carry, consider a tip for the crew. Winter or bad-weather deliveries are good moments to show appreciation. Your planner or venue coordinator can tell you what is typical locally.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who should you tip at a wedding?

Commonly, catering staff and bartenders (if gratuity is not included), hair and makeup artists, drivers and delivery crews. Others, such as photographers and DJs, are optional.

### How much should you tip wedding vendors?

It depends on the vendor, contract and service. Check what is already included, then ask your planner or the vendor what is typical in your area.

### Is a service charge the same as a tip?

Not always. A service charge may go to the company, not the workers. Ask how it is distributed before deciding on additional tips.

### Do you tip wedding photographers?

It is optional. Many photographers own their business and do not expect a tip, but some couples give a modest tip or a thank-you note and review.

### When do you give wedding tips?

Usually at the end of the vendor's service, or at delivery for items like rentals and flowers. Ask a trusted person to hand out the envelopes.

### Should I tip a wedding planner?

Optional. If your planner is the business owner, a thank-you note may be enough. For an employee who goes above and beyond, a tip is appreciated. See [wedding planner vs day-of coordinator](/blog/wedding-planner-vs-day-of-coordinator).

### Do I tip the officiant?

Customs vary by tradition. Some officiants accept a donation or honorarium, while others do not. Ask when you book.
