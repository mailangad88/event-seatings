**Quick answer**

Plan about 6 to 8 square feet per guest for a standing cocktail party, 8 to 10 for theater seating, 10 to 12 for a seated banquet with no dancing, 12 to 15 with a dance floor, and 15 to 20 with a buffet, a stage and a dance floor. These are rules of thumb, so confirm the final number with your venue, because fire codes set the legal limit.

</div>

If you are touring venues in Chicago or the suburbs, you will hear a lot of numbers: "capacity 200," "seats 150," "holds 300 standing." They are not the same. A room's posted capacity can be a legal maximum for standing, while a comfortable seated dinner with a dance floor holds half that. Square feet per guest is the number that lets you compare rooms fairly.

## How to calculate your own room

**Capacity = usable square feet / square feet per guest.** Usable means the floor you can actually fill with tables, not the total. Subtract the stage, permanent bars, columns, coat check and the area around doors.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Measure length times width.</strong> Use the room dimensions, not the listing's capacity.</li>
<li><strong>Subtract unusable area.</strong> Stage, built-in bar, columns, kitchen door swing, entry.</li>
<li><strong>Pick your style.</strong> Use the table above.</li>
<li><strong>Divide.</strong> Usable sq ft / sq ft per guest = guests.</li>
<li><strong>Check with the venue.</strong> Their legal limit overrides your math.</li>
</ol>

For example, a 50 x 40 foot room has 2,000 square feet. Subtract 150 for a bar and entry and 100 for a stage, and you have 1,750 usable. For a banquet with a dance floor at 14 square feet, that is 125 guests. For a standing cocktail at 7, it is 250.

## Why seated needs more than standing

Seated guests take up more room than standing guests. A chair alone is 16 to 18 inches wide and needs about 18 inches of clearance behind it for guests to push back. A 72-inch round with 10 chairs occupies about 12 x 12 feet, 144 square feet, or 14 square feet per guest before you add aisles. Our [round table size guide](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch) shows the footprints.

## What pushes the number up

Several things add square feet per guest:

- **A dance floor.** Add 2.5 to 3 square feet for every guest who dances at once.
- **A stage or band.** A five-piece band needs about 16 x 12 feet.
- **Buffets and stations.** Lines need 5 to 6 feet of clear floor in front.
- **Wheelchair or mobility needs.** Wider aisles, as covered in [ADA accessible seating](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways).
- **A bar and lounge.** Count 10 to 12 feet of bar and 6 feet of space in front.

## Chicago-specific cautions

Older Chicago buildings, such as converted warehouses, lofts and church halls, often have columns that remove usable floor. Suburban banquet halls often list capacity based on rounds of 10 and no dancing. In a tent, subtract a perimeter band for poles and sidewalls. For a rough tent size, see [what size tent you need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many square feet per person for a wedding reception?

Plan 12 to 15 square feet per guest for a seated dinner with a dance floor, and 15 to 20 if you add a buffet, a band or a lounge.

### How many square feet per person for a standing party?

About 6 to 8 square feet per guest. Below 6, guests cannot move easily or reach the bar.

### How many people fit in 1,000 square feet?

About 125 standing, 80 seated at rounds with a dance floor, or 100 to 125 in theater rows.

### Is a venue's posted capacity the same as a comfortable capacity?

No. Posted capacity is often a legal maximum for standing. Comfortable seated capacity is usually half that. Ask for the number with your layout.

### Do I count the dance floor in the room size?

Yes, count it as part of the usable floor. But remember a dance floor takes room only for dancing guests, so the rule of thumb adds a premium rather than a flat number.

### What square footage per guest do tents need?

Use the same ranges, then add a perimeter allowance for poles and sidewalls. A tent company will give a more exact figure for your frame.
