**Quick answer**

A good indoor backup plan names a backup space, a decision time, a layout that fits the same guests, and a message plan for guests and vendors. Choose the space before you book anything else, check its capacity, and set a clear call time the day before. A tent can also serve as a backup, but it protects only against rain, not cold, lightning or high wind.

</div>

Most outdoor event plans fail in the same way: nobody decided what happens when the forecast turns. By the morning of the event, guests are on the way, vendors are on site and someone is staring at a radar map. A backup plan with a decision time lets you make the call early, calmly and without chaos.

## Pick your backup first

Your backup can be an indoor room at the venue, a banquet hall, a garage or house, or a tent with sidewalls. Check that the backup holds all guests at the layout you plan: standing receptions use less space than dinners with a dance floor. Our [square feet per guest guide](/blog/square-feet-per-guest-by-event-style) and [floor plans](/blog/floor-plan-for-100-guests) help.

Tents help in rain but are not a full backup in storms. See [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent), [rain on event day in Chicago](/blog/rain-on-event-day-chicago) and [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety).

## Set thresholds before you need them

Agree on these in advance with your venue, host and vendors. The numbers below are examples to adapt, not standards.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Condition | Example trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Rain | Steady rain forecast during the event | Move indoors or use a tent with sidewalls |
| Thunderstorm or lightning | Any storm in the area | Move everyone indoors; a tent is not safe shelter |
| Wind | Gusts above your tent's rating | Move indoors; check with your tent provider |
| Heat | Very high heat index | Add shade, water, fans; consider moving indoors |
| Cold | Temperatures too low for guests | Add heaters or move indoors |

</div>

Ask your rental company and venue for their weather policies; see [rain clause in rental contracts](/blog/rain-clause-event-rental-contract).

## Make your backup plan in six steps

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Choose a backup space.</strong> Confirm it is yours for the event time and holds the guest count.</li>
<li><strong>Draw the backup layout.</strong> Sketch it before you need it, with tables, buffet and dance floor.</li>
<li><strong>Set thresholds and a call time.</strong> Pick who decides and when, such as noon the day before.</li>
<li><strong>Tell vendors.</strong> Share the plan with caterer, rental company, DJ and photographer.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare your guest message.</strong> Write the text now with a place to add the decision.</li>
<li><strong>Keep supplies handy.</strong> Umbrellas, towels, mats and signs; see [umbrella stands and rain plans](/blog/umbrella-stands-and-rain-plans).</li>
</ol>

## Money and contract notes

A backup plan can cost something: a second room, extra staff or a tent. Ask whether your rental company can switch to indoor delivery and whether there are fees. Read how weather and date changes are handled in your contract; see [event rental cancellation and date change policy](/blog/event-rental-cancellation-and-date-change-policy). Also ask if your venue has a rule for moving indoors, such as a deadline.

## A short script for the day-before call

On the day before, check two forecasts and compare them with your thresholds. If you decide to move, send a message that says where the event will be, what time it starts, where to park and what to wear. Keep it to a few lines. Then call or text your caterer, rental company, entertainment and photographer with the same information, and confirm what time they can set up. Writing the message ahead of time makes the decision much easier to carry out.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago weather can turn quickly, with strong winds off the lake, fast summer storms and cold snaps in spring and fall. Lakefront and open-field sites can be windier than wooded suburban yards. Park pavilions give some cover but may not be enclosed. Check with your park district or village about indoor options, and see [chicago lakefront event wind planning](/blog/chicago-lakefront-event-wind-planning). If you plan an outdoor ceremony, see [outdoor ceremony on grass](/blog/outdoor-ceremony-on-grass). Always tell your vendors the plan before the day.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good backup plan for an outdoor event?

A backup space that fits all guests, a layout for it, a decision time and a message plan. A tent can help for rain, but not for storms.

### When should I decide to move indoors?

Many planners decide the day before, with a check on the morning of. Agree on a time with your vendors.

### Is a tent enough of a backup?

It covers rain, but it does not protect from lightning or high wind. Plan an indoor option for storms.

### How do I tell guests about a change?

Send one clear message by text or email and post it on your invitation page. Include the new location and any parking changes.

### Who decides on the weather call?

Name one person, usually the host or planner, and share the contact with vendors.

### Does the rental company need to know?

Yes. Tell them early, since delivery location and setup can change.

### What if there is no indoor space?

Consider a sturdy tent with sidewalls, a smaller guest list or a rain date. Ask your venue about options.
