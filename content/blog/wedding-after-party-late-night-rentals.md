**Quick answer**

A wedding after-party usually needs lounge seating, a few highboy tables, a bar or drink station, a late-night snack table, a coffee station and softer lighting. Plan seats for about a third of the guests who stay, one highboy per 8 to 10 guests, and a snack and coffee table with floor-length linens. The reception ends at 11 and half the room is not ready to go home. Chicago's bars and hotel lounges are an easy option, but a private after-party at the venue, a tent or a house lets everyone stay in their outfits and finish the night. Rentals for it differ from the main event: it should be comfortable, flexible and quick to set up.

## What changes after dinner

By late night, guests have eaten, danced and mingled. The rentals shift from dining to lounging, snacking and drinking.

## The after-party rental list

**Lounge seating.** Sofas, loveseats and a few low tables, in groups of 4 to 8 seats. Aim for seats for about a third of the guests who stay. See [lounge furniture for cocktail hour](/blog/lounge-furniture-for-cocktail-hour) and [velvet sofas and settees](/blog/velvet-sofas-and-settees-for-events). A few extra chairs, such as [rattan](/chairs/rattan-garden) or [bentwood](/chairs/bentwood), give flexible seating.

**Highboys.** One per 8 to 10 guests, with sash-tied or spandex covers.

**Late-night snack table.** A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth, trays, napkins, small plates and trash bins. Pizza, sliders, tacos, donuts and fries are classic. Your caterer or a food truck usually supplies the food and equipment; ask what they bring. See [food station ideas](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment).

**Coffee and water.** A coffee urn, cups, cream and sugar, and a water dispenser. See [how many coffee urns](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party).

**Lighting.** Lower the main lights, add lanterns or candles in safe holders, and keep paths bright. Check your venue's fire and candle rules.

**Photo and fun.** A photo booth or a Polaroid table, lawn games for outdoor weddings.

## Venue rules to check

Many venues end alcohol service and music at a set time. Cook County and village noise ordinances and the venue contract may also affect hours. Ask your venue about its end time, whether rentals can stay up past midnight, and the pickup time. Ask your caterer about late service staffing. Avoid planning an after-party that depends on a rental truck arriving after midnight; instead schedule items for the next morning.

## Indoor vs outdoor

An outdoor after-party feels relaxed but needs warmth. In spring and fall Chicago nights can turn cold, so budget patio heaters or blankets (see [fans and heater guides](/blog/patio-heaters-how-many-per-guests)). Indoors, a separate lounge room or an unused corner of the ballroom does the job.

## Who stays, and how to plan for them

Most weddings lose a chunk of the guest list after dinner: older relatives, parents with young children and people who have a long drive home. Ask your wedding party for a rough headcount of who will stay, and size the after-party for that number, not the full guest list. A group of 40 people in a ballroom built for 150 feels thin, so use a smaller section of the room with lounge pieces pulled into a tight cluster.

Think about transportation too. Guests who plan to keep drinking need a ride home, so put a rideshare pickup sign or shuttle schedule at the exit, and tell your venue where cars can wait. In a Chicago neighborhood with limited curb space, a staff member at the door makes the departure smoother.

## Keep the cleanup simple

Late-night food creates trash and spills. Put a floor-length cloth on the snack table, place two bins at either end, and ask the crew to bag rental linens separately from trash. Plan for a returns window the next morning for dinnerware, glasses and linens so nothing sits overnight in a venue that will charge for storage.

## The 20-minute flip

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Before dancing ends,</strong> have the snack table hidden behind a cloth.</li>
<li><strong>Move dining chairs</strong> into small conversation circles.</li>
<li><strong>Bring in lounge pieces</strong> from a staging area.</li>
<li><strong>Turn on the lantern lighting</strong> and dim overheads.</li>
<li><strong>Open the snack table</strong> and coffee station.</li>
</ol>

Hire a crew or assign family helpers to do it so the DJ never stops the music.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need for a wedding after-party?

Lounge seating, highboy tables, a snack and coffee station, softer lighting and a bar or drink station. Everything should be easy to move.

### How many late-night snacks should I plan?

Plan one portion for about half to two thirds of the guests who remain, and ask your caterer for their rule. Fewer than expected is usually safer than too many.

### Can I keep rentals past midnight?

Often yes, if the venue and rental contract allow it. Ask the venue about end times and the rental company about pickup the next morning.

### Do I need a separate space for the after-party?

No. A corner of the ballroom or a tent section works. Having a different feel, with sofas and lower light, makes it feel like a new event.

### Who staffs a late-night snack?

Usually your caterer or a food truck. Ask what the staffing includes, and who handles trash.

### How do I keep older guests comfortable?

Offer comfortable seats, water and a quiet corner. Many older guests leave before the after-party, and that's fine.
