**Quick answer**

Color temperature is measured in Kelvin (K). Lower numbers look warm and orange, higher numbers look cool and blue. For dinners and weddings, 2200K to 3000K flatters skin and food. For galas and presentations, 3000K to 4000K looks cleaner. For work tasks and cameras, 4000K to 5600K is closer to daylight. Pick one range for each space and make sure bulbs match.

</div>

Everyone has seen a room where half the lights are creamy and half are bluish. It looks accidental because it is. Color temperature is one of the quickest ways to make an event feel planned, and it costs nothing to choose well. The problem is that bulbs are labeled in a number most people never learned. This guide decodes it.

## The Kelvin scale

Kelvin describes the color of white light. A candle flame sits near the bottom, and midday sunlight sits near the top.

The numbers do not mean heat. A "warm" bulb has a low Kelvin number and gives off orange-yellow light. A "cool" bulb has a high number and looks blue-white.

## Which range for which event

<div class="table-wrap">

| Setting | Suggested range | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wedding dinner, candlelit tables | 2200K to 2700K | Matches candle and flatters skin |
| String lights, lanterns, patios | 2200K to 2700K | Cozy, intimate |
| Gala or formal dinner | 2700K to 3000K | Warm, but a little crisper |
| Dance floor, wash lights | Any, plus steady white | Color is the point |
| Corporate presentation | 3000K to 4000K | Clean, readable |
| Stage faces on camera | 3200K to 5600K, matched | Cameras need consistency |
| Registration, work areas | 4000K | Practical |

</div>

These are common choices, not rules. Test in the room.

## Why mixing hurts

Human eyes adjust, but cameras and phones do not do it well in a mixed room. Photos show orange faces next to blue ones, and white dresses take on a tint. See [night event photo lighting](/blog/lighting-for-night-event-photos).

Common mixing mistakes:

- Warm string lights beside cool-white venue downlights.
- LED uplights in a different white from the table lamps.
- Daylight from open tent sides at dusk mixed with warm interior lights.
- Different brands of bulbs in the same strand run.

The fix: choose a target, ask each vendor for bulbs in it, and switch off or cover any venue lights that do not match. Ask the venue whether their lights can be dimmed or turned off.

## Dimmers

LED bulbs can shift color when dimmed. Incandescent bulbs get warmer as they dim, which people find flattering. Some LEDs are "dim to warm" and mimic that effect. If you plan to dim string lights or table lamps, ask whether the bulbs and the dimmer are compatible, and test them. Some LED bulbs flicker or buzz on the wrong dimmer.

## CRI: a second number to know

Color rendering index (CRI) says how accurately a light shows colors on a scale up to 100. A higher CRI makes food, flowers and skin look more natural. For event lighting, ask for high CRI fixtures where color matters, such as bars, food stations and portrait areas. It is a quality measure, not a color.

## A walk-through test

Before you finalize anything, do a ten-minute test at the venue or at home. Turn on one bulb of each type you plan to use and hold a white napkin, a sample of your linen and a flower under each. Photograph them with your phone. You will see right away which bulbs look yellow-green, which look gray and which match your palette. Do this at night with the daylight gone, because windows change everything. If you cannot visit the venue at night, ask the coordinator to turn on the house lights and the dimmers on a quiet evening, and bring the samples with you. Take notes on which house lights can be turned off, because those are the ones most likely to clash with what you bring.

## Colored lighting

Uplights and wash lights add color on top of white. Keep the white consistent and use color on walls and dance floors. See [uplight counts](/blog/how-many-uplights-for-a-wedding) and [LED dance floor lighting](/blog/led-dance-floor-lighting). Saturated colors on faces look unflattering, so aim color away from the portrait and dining areas.

## Chicago notes

Many Chicago venues have mixed lighting from fixtures installed over decades. Do a walk-through at the time of day your event will happen, and look at the lights on and off. In winter, early sunsets mean you rely on electric light early; see [winter event lighting](/blog/winter-event-lighting-chicago-sunset).

## Frequently asked questions

### What color temperature is best for a wedding?

2200K to 2700K for dinner and string lights. This warm range flatters skin and matches candles.

### What does 2700K mean?

It is the Kelvin rating for a classic warm white light bulb, similar to an incandescent bulb.

### Is 3000K warm or cool?

It is soft white. It is warm, but slightly crisper than 2700K.

### Can I mix 2700K and 4000K bulbs?

You can, but it looks mismatched in photos. Keep each space within one range.

### What is the best color temperature for photos?

Consistent warm white around 2700K to 3000K, or daylight matched on stage. Consistency matters more than a number.

### Do LED bulbs change color when dimmed?

Some do. Ask for dim-to-warm LEDs if you want them to warm as they dim.
