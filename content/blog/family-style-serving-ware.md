**Quick answer**

For a family-style dinner, each table needs one platter or bowl for every dish, a serving utensil for each, and room to set them down. A table of 8 with 4 dishes needs about 4 serving pieces, 4 utensils and trivets for hot items. Use a 72-inch round for 8 to 10 guests, or a long table with width of 36 inches or more. Skip chargers and bread plates to save space.

</div>

Family-style service feels generous: platters pass hand to hand, guests take what they like and the table turns into a shared meal. It also needs more of nearly everything except plates. Where a plated dinner needs 3 plates a guest, a family-style dinner needs about 1 to 2 plates but a full set of serving ware on every table. This guide lists the pieces and the space they need, for a wedding hall in the suburbs or a long table at a loft in Chicago.

## Table size matters

A 60-inch round is too small for family-style. A 72-inch round gives about 18 inches in the center for dishes after the place settings. A long banquet table works well because dishes can line up the middle; see our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for 8-foot cloths. A farm table is the classic look, and works with a runner and no cloth.

## Dishes that work

- **Roasted meats** sliced on a platter.
- **Pasta and grains** in wide bowls.
- **Salads** in large bowls with servers.
- **Roasted vegetables** on long platters.
- **Sauces** in small bowls with spoons.

Foods that must stay hot work less well, because a table of 8 takes 15 to 20 minutes to finish a dish. Ask the caterer to refill platters, or serve smaller dishes in waves.

## Plates and flatware

Guests use one dinner plate for the whole meal, so a salad course is served on the same plate or in a bowl. That cuts down the plate count. Reduce flatware too: one fork, one knife, one spoon is enough. See [casual place setting diagram](/blog/casual-place-setting-diagram). Chargers get in the way, as covered in [are charger plates worth it](/blog/are-charger-plates-worth-it).

## Washing and pickup

Platters are large, and they come back greasy. Scrape and stack them in the crates with a divider. Many companies ask you to remove any foil, parchment or wax. Read the rules in [do you wash rental dishes](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-dishes).

## Staff and flow

Family-style needs servers to bring the dishes out at once and refill. A plated dinner can run with fewer staff but needs more plates. Ask your caterer for staff counts and plan a pass-through space. In a Chicago venue with narrow aisles, a 72-inch round plus servers needs more floor space than a 60-inch; see [place setting spacing](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements) for spacing basics.

## Common mistakes

The usual errors are too-small tables, too few utensils and cold food. Count utensils separately from platters, since one spoon on a table of 8 means a long wait. Keep trivets handy so hot dishes do not mark the linens. Tell your caterer the table size so they can plan dish quantities and bring the right platters. Plan where empty dishes go, because they pile up on the table fast. A side cart for used platters keeps the table open.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need for a family-style dinner?

You need plates, flatware and glasses for each guest, plus platters, bowls and serving utensils for each dish at each table. Add trivets for hot dishes and a bread basket.

### How many platters per table?

One for each dish, so usually 4 to 5 for a table of 8 to 10. Repeat the whole set at every table.

### What size table is best for family-style?

A 72-inch round or a long table at least 36 inches wide. A 60-inch round gets crowded.

### Is family-style cheaper than plated?

It can be, since it uses fewer plates and servers. But it needs more serving pieces and bigger tables, so compare a quote for both.

### How do I keep food warm family-style?

Serve in waves, use warm platters and keep refills coming from the kitchen. Trivets and covered dishes help.

### Do guests get chargers?

Usually not. Chargers take room that shared dishes need.
