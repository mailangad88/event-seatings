## Rooftop or backyard, know your space

Wrigleyville mixes rooftop decks, courtyard buildings and classic Chicago backyards, and each one has its own access and capacity limits.

| Backyard or two-flat yard | Yard size, alley access | Gate width, fence lines |

## Street closures and parking on game days

The city regularly closes streets around Wrigley Field on game days, and residential parking restrictions tighten up in the surrounding blocks. If you're hosting near the ballpark, check the closure schedule for your date and plan your own delivery time, and your guests' arrival, around it. See our [parking and loading zone guide](/blog/chicago-event-delivery-parking-loading-zones) for how commercial deliveries work around closures like this.

## Noise and neighbors

Wrigleyville is a dense residential neighborhood, and a rooftop or backyard party carries sound further than you'd expect. Let your neighbors know ahead of time, especially for a day game or a long event, and keep music and noise contained to your own space. A little notice goes a long way in a neighborhood this close together.

## Timing deliveries around game start

Wrigleyville traffic gets heavy well before first pitch and stays heavy for a while after the game ends. Morning delivery, ahead of any pre-game buildup, is usually the smoothest option. Pickup is easiest once the post-game crowd has cleared the area, which can take a while depending on attendance and whether traffic.
