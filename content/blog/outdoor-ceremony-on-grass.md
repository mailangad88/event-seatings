Chairs on grass sink and tip unless you pick the right chair and prep the ground. Choose chairs with wide feet, mow short the day before, avoid soft or sloped spots, and lay boards or an aisle mat where traffic is heaviest. Grass looks beautiful in photos and behaves badly under chairs. Wet lawn compresses, slopes tilt rows and heels punch holes. A little prep fixes most of it, and the right chair choice fixes the rest. Here is what to check on a Chicago-area lawn, from a suburban backyard to a forest preserve clearing.

## Why chairs sink

A person weighs around 150 to 200 pounds, and a chair concentrates that weight on four small feet. On dry, firm turf that is fine. On soft or wet turf the feet press in, one corner dips and the chair leans. The fix is more surface area under the feet or a harder surface under the chair.

## Pick the right chair

- **Garden folding chairs** have flat feet and are easy to nudge back into place. They are the practical choice for big lawns, and [the teak garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is the budget option at an estimated $5 to $7 each.
- **Cross-back chairs** look better but have narrow feet. Fine on firm turf, risky on soft turf. See [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back).
- **Ghost chairs** work on firm lawns but can scuff and sink at the back legs. Check with the rental company about outdoor use on grass.
- **Heavy upholstered or throne chairs** should sit on a board or platform if used outdoors.

Ask your rental company what they do about chair feet. Some provide small pads. Do not stack boards under rows unless you tell them first, because crews need to know their footing.

## Prep the ground

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Walk the lawn after rain</strong> and note puddles and soft ground.</li>
<li><strong>Mow short</strong> the day before; skip the morning of so clippings do not stick to shoes.</li>
<li><strong>Turn sprinklers off</strong> and cap heads that stick up.</li>
<li><strong>Check the slope</strong> and keep rows across the slope, not down it.</li>
<li><strong>Mark the aisle</strong> and the footprint with stakes or paint before the crew arrives.</li>
</ol>

Call JULIE (811) before any stake goes in the ground. Utilities can run shallow, and the free locate request takes a couple of working days.

## The aisle

The aisle is where everyone walks, including heels. Options:

- **Fabric runner:** pretty, but it can bunch. Pin it down with stakes.
- **Plywood or boards under the runner:** invisible and firm; ask your rental or tent company.
- **Mowed path:** a stripe of mown lawn within longer meadow grass reads as a path.
- **Mats:** rubber or coir mats at the entrance and exit.

## Grass damage and the cleanup

A day of foot traffic and chair legs leaves marks. Tell the property owner or park office what to expect, and ask whether the lawn needs to be reseeded. Public sites may require you to restore turf or pay fees. Keep chair stacks on a tarp so they do not stain the lawn, and don't drag them.

## Heat, sun and shade on an open lawn

A lawn has no ceiling. In July and August, guests in the afternoon sun get uncomfortable fast, and chairs with dark finishes absorb heat. Face the rows so guests are not looking into the sun, add shade where you can with a tent or large umbrellas, and keep a cold water station near the first and last rows. In spring and fall, ground chill is the issue, so offer blankets and plan a warm place to wait. Walk the site at the planned ceremony time on a sunny day before you commit to a layout.

## Who sets up and who takes down

Ask your rental company whether the crew places each chair or only drops stacks. For a lawn ceremony, you want the crew to set rows to your marked layout, check them for level, and return for pickup after the party. If the chairs need to move to the reception after the vows, assign two or three helpers and give them a diagram so the flip takes minutes, not half an hour.

## Timing

Set up the same morning if you can, after the sun has dried the dew. If a thunderstorm threatens, hold the chairs on the truck until the last practical moment. For a ceremony that ends with a flip to a tent, see [ceremony vs reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you put wedding chairs on grass?

Yes, on firm, level lawn. Soft or sloped ground needs boards or flooring. Pick chairs with a wide foot, like garden folding chairs, if you expect damp turf.

### How do I stop chairs sinking in grass?

Mow short, avoid wet spots, use chairs with wide feet or add pads and boards under the rows. Setting up after the lawn dries helps too.

### What aisle works on grass?

A fabric runner pinned down over boards is the safest. A mowed path or stepping-stone path also works. Avoid loose runners in high-traffic spots.

### Is it okay to set up chairs the day before?

You can, but dew, sprinklers and wind can move them. Morning-of is better unless the venue only allows prior-day access. Ask your rental company about the crew's timing.

### Will chairs damage the lawn?

Chairs leave dents and some marks, especially if the ground is wet. Ask the property owner about restoration, and check public site rules before you book.

### How much space does an outdoor ceremony need?

About 8 square feet per guest including the aisle, as a rule of thumb. A 100-guest ceremony needs roughly 800 square feet.
