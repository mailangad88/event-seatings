**Quick answer**

Event insurance is a policy a host buys to cover the event, typically liability for injuries or property damage and sometimes cancellation. It is different from a vendor's certificate of insurance and from a rental damage waiver. Ask your venue whether it requires coverage, read the policy exclusions, and talk to a licensed insurance professional before you buy.

</div>

Three different things get called "insurance" in event planning, and mixing them up leaves gaps. The rental company has its own coverage and may offer a waiver. The venue may ask for vendor certificates. And you, the host, may be asked for a policy of your own. We are a rental business, not an insurance agent, so this guide explains the concepts and sends you to the right people for specifics.

## What host event insurance may include

Policies vary by provider, so read yours. The kinds of coverage you will see offered include:

- **General liability.** Helps with claims if a guest is hurt or property is damaged at your event.
- **Liquor liability.** Relevant if alcohol is served; ask about requirements for your event.
- **Cancellation or postponement.** May cover certain covered reasons for cancelling. The covered reasons are narrow, so read them.
- **Property or vendor-related coverage.** Sometimes available as an add-on.

What it does not do: it will not cover everything that could go wrong, and it usually excludes things you could have prevented or known about. That is why reading exclusions matters.

## When to consider it

<div class="table-wrap">

| Situation | Why consider coverage |
|---|---|
| Venue requires it | Required to host |
| Large guest list | More exposure to claims |
| Alcohol is served | Liability considerations |
| Big non-refundable spend | Cancellation protection may matter |
| Outdoor or tented event | Weather and safety factors |
| Event at your home | Check your homeowner's policy first |

</div>

Your homeowner's or renter's policy may already cover some events at home, but may exclude larger or commercial-style gatherings. Call your agent and ask. For corporate or nonprofit events, your organization may already hold liability coverage, so ask your administrator.

## Questions to ask your venue

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Do you require host insurance?</strong> Ask for it in writing.</li>
<li><strong>What limits and types?</strong> Liability, liquor liability, others.</li>
<li><strong>Must the venue be named as additional insured?</strong></li>
<li><strong>When is proof due?</strong> Usually before the event.</li>
<li><strong>Do vendors need their own COIs?</strong> Then forward the rules to your rental company.</li>
</ol>

Some park districts, villages and private venues in the Chicago area have their own insurance rules. We cannot list them, and they change, so ask the organization that controls your site. See our [Chicago tent permit](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) guide for a related check.

## Questions to ask an insurance professional

- What does this policy cover and not cover?
- Does it cover my event type, location and guest count?
- Does it cover alcohol service?
- What are the cancellation conditions, and what proof is needed?
- Do rented items have coverage under it, or only through the rental company?
- What do I do if something happens?

A licensed agent or broker can answer these for your situation. We are not insurance advisors, and nothing here is legal or insurance advice.

## How it fits your budget

Insurance is a line item, usually modest compared with rental and venue costs, but it varies. We do not quote it. Add it to your [rental budget](/blog/event-rental-budget-by-guest-count) after you have a real quote. If the amount seems high, ask what drives it, such as guest count or alcohol.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming the waiver is insurance.** It is not.
- **Buying cancellation coverage and not reading what counts.**
- **Waiting too late.** Some coverage must be bought well before the event.
- **Not telling the venue** what you have.
- **Ignoring weather** if you are outdoors. See [rain clauses](/blog/rain-clause-event-rental-contract).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is event insurance?

It is a policy a host buys to cover an event, commonly liability for injuries or property damage and sometimes cancellation. The specifics depend on the provider and policy.

### Is a damage waiver the same as event insurance?

No. A damage waiver is a contract term with a rental company about rented items. Event insurance is a policy from an insurer that covers the event itself.

### Does my venue require event insurance?

Some do. Ask for the requirements in writing, including limits, additional insured wording and the deadline.

### Does homeowner's insurance cover a party at my house?

It may cover some liability, but coverage and exclusions vary, especially for large or catered events. Ask your agent before the event.

### Does event insurance cover cancellation?

Some policies offer it for specific covered reasons. Read what is included and excluded, because many common reasons for canceling are not covered.

### Who should I talk to about coverage?

A licensed insurance agent or broker, plus your venue's event manager. We can help with the rental side, but we are not insurance advisors.
