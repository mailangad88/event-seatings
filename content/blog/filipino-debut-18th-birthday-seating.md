**Quick answer**

A Filipino debut celebrates a young person's 18th birthday and often includes the 18 roses and 18 candles, speeches and a choreographed dance. Plan a stage or throne area for the celebrant, a clear dance floor of about 20 x 20 feet for 150 guests, family tables close to the front and seating for the court of honor. Rounds of 10 are common at banquet-style debuts. Traditions differ by family, so confirm the program with the celebrant and parents before you size anything.

</div>

A debut is a coming-of-age celebration, usually for a girl turning 18, though some families hold one for boys too. In Chicago and the suburbs, many Filipino-American families host debuts in banquet halls with a program that feels like a small wedding: entrance, dances, tributes, dinner and a party. The program drives the layout, so build your seating plan from the order of events.

## What a typical program asks of the room

Programs differ, but many debuts include:

- **Grand entrance** of the celebrant and her court, with a clear aisle.
- **18 roses**, where 18 guests (often male relatives and friends) present a rose and dance briefly with the celebrant.
- **18 candles**, where 18 guests (often female relatives and friends) light a candle and share a message.
- **Cotillion or choreographed dance** with the court, which needs open floor space and good sightlines.
- **Speeches and tributes** from parents, godparents and friends, often with a slideshow.
- **Dinner, cake and open dancing.**

Each element has a physical need: an open floor, a stage or focal seat, enough chairs near the front for those taking part and a small table or stand for the cake and candles.

## Count your seats by role

Start with the people who have a part in the program, then fill in guests.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Group | Typical count | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|
| Celebrant | 1 | Throne or sweetheart seat on stage |
| Parents and godparents | 4 to 10 | Front tables beside the dance floor |
| Court (18 roses and 18 candles) | up to 36 | Reserved tables near the front |
| Grandparents and elders | varies | Comfortable seats near the front, away from speakers |
| Other guests | remaining | Rounds of 10 toward the back |

</div>

The court can be large. If 18 roses and 18 candles are both on your program, reserve four or five tables near the front and decide whether the court will sit together or with their families.

## Plan the sightlines

Seat people so they can watch the program without turning their chairs around. Put the stage on a short wall and the dance floor in front of it. Place speakers away from grandparents' tables if your family prefers quieter seating. If your hall has pillars, ask the venue for a floor plan and check each table's view. See [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events) if you want to add a platform.

## Chicago notes

- Ask your venue about decoration rules, especially candles. Many venues restrict open flame, which matters for the 18 candles ceremony. Read our guide to [candle rules at venues](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules) and consider flameless candles.
- Check the end time and music rules before you finalize the program length.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many guests attend a Filipino debut?

Guest counts vary widely, from 50 to 250 or more, depending on the family. Many debuts have large guest lists because extended family and friends are invited. Plan table and chair counts from your confirmed list, not a guess.

### How do I seat the 18 roses and 18 candles?

Reserve tables near the dance floor for those taking part, so they can step forward easily. Some families seat the court together, others seat them with their families. Decide before you finalize the table count.

### Do I need a stage for a debut?

Not always, but many families use a low stage or platform so everyone can see the celebrant and the program. A stage is helpful for speeches, the candle ceremony and the cake. Ask your venue what they have.

### What chair should the celebrant sit in?

Many celebrants use a throne-style chair or a decorated armchair for the program and photos. Pick one that matches the party colors and is comfortable to sit in for a long evening.

### Can I use real candles for the 18 candles?

Check with the venue first. Many venues ban open flame or require a permit, so flameless candles may be the safer choice. Your venue manager can tell you what the fire code allows.

### How early should I book rentals for a debut?

Book once your venue and date are confirmed. Spring and fall weekends fill quickly. Our [booking timeline guide](/blog/topic/budget-and-booking) explains when to order.
