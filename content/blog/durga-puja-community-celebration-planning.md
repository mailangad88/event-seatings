**Quick answer**

A hall-based Durga Puja needs three zones: a pandal and stage for the idol and the puja, rows of chairs for the evening program, and long tables with a serving line for the bhog meal. For about 200 guests, plan 200 chairs in rows, 20 to 25 eight-foot tables for meals and a stage of about 12 by 16 feet. Dates follow the lunar calendar and fall in September or October, so check this year's date and book the hall and rentals 8 to 12 weeks ahead.

Durga Puja is the biggest festival in the Bengali calendar. Over about five days in the fall, communities honor the goddess Durga with a decorated idol, daily prayers, flower offerings, shared meals and evenings of music, dance and drama. Dates follow the lunar calendar, so check this year's date.

In and around Chicago, many Bengali associations rent a hall, school gym, community center or banquet space for the days of the puja. A hall is a blank room. This guide helps committees turn it into a pandal, a dining hall and a theater, and plan what to rent.

Practices differ among communities, families and priests, so use this as a base and adapt it to your committee's tradition.

### The pandal and stage

The pandal is the decorated space where the idol sits. In a hall, it is often built as a stage with a backdrop, fabric, lights and flowers. A priest sits near the idol, and the main puja happens here.

- **Stage size.** A 12 x 16 foot platform is a common size for a pandal plus a small area for the priest. A bigger stage helps if the cultural program uses dance or drama. See [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events) and [staging rentals](/rentals/dance-floors-staging).
- **Backdrop.** Draping and pipe and drape can frame the idol. See [pipe and drape uses and sizes](/blog/pipe-and-drape-uses-and-sizes).
- **Lighting.** Soft, warm light on the idol and bright light for the stage. See [lighting rentals](/rentals/lighting) and [corporate stage lighting basics](/blog/corporate-stage-lighting-basics).
- **Fire and smoke.** Incense, lamps (diyas) and dhunuchi (a clay incense burner used in the evening aarti) make smoke. Check with the hall about candles, open flame and smoke detectors. See [candles at venues and fire code rules](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules).

### Rows of chairs

For the evening program, set chairs facing the stage with a center aisle. Rule of thumb: 8 to 10 square feet per seat in theater style, which includes the aisles. See [how many chairs fit theater seating](/blog/how-many-chairs-fit-theater-seating) and [classroom vs theater seating](/blog/classroom-vs-theater-seating-layout).

- Seat older guests and families with young kids near the aisle.
- Keep the first row a few feet from the stage.
- Reserve a few seats at the front for the priest, committee and the elders.
- Leave floor space at the side for guests who prefer to sit on the floor during the prayers, as many do.

### The bhog meal

Bhog is the offering to the goddess, which becomes the lunch shared by everyone. A typical bhog meal has khichuri, mixed vegetable, labra, chutney, fried vegetables and payesh or another sweet. It is vegetarian. Menus vary by community.

Serving hundreds of guests is the real task.

- **Serve in shifts.** For 200 guests, two seatings of 100 each is easier than one big rush. Many committees serve on the floor in rows (pangat style), on long tables or buffet.
- **Long tables.** An 8-foot banquet table seats 8. For 100 per shift, plan 12 to 14 tables. Add 2 to 3 more for serving.
- **Volunteers.** Plan 1 volunteer for every 15 to 20 guests to serve, refill and clear. See [how to delegate event tasks to volunteers](/blog/how-to-delegate-event-tasks-to-family-and-volunteers).
- **Equipment.** Large pots, serving spoons, warming pans, hand-washing stations and trash bins. See [catering equipment rentals](/rentals/catering-equipment), [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) and [how many chafing dishes for a buffet](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet).

## The cultural program

Evenings usually include songs, dance, drama and sometimes a children's program. Plan the stage, sound and seating around that.

- **Sound.** Microphones for the emcee, a monitor for performers and speakers pointed toward the audience. See [AV and sound rentals](/rentals/av-sound) and [microphone types for events](/blog/microphone-types-for-events).
- **Backstage.** A curtained area or a side room for costume changes. Pipe and drape does this well.
- **Kids.** Give young performers a place to wait with a parent.
- **Dance floor.** If you plan dhunuchi dancing or a social evening, keep an open floor near the dining area.

## Hall tips for a Chicago committee

Most community groups rent a hall for the whole festival, or one hall for the main days. The right place depends on your guest count, parking and kitchen. See [church hall and community center event planning](/blog/church-hall-and-community-center-event-planning) and [community center event rentals in the Chicago suburbs](/blog/community-center-event-rentals-chicago-suburbs).

Questions to ask the hall:

1. What is the fire-code capacity, and does it include chairs and tables?
2. Can we use incense, lamps and a dhunuchi? Where is the smoke detector?
3. Is there a kitchen, and can we bring in our own caterer or home cooks?
4. When can we load in, and what is the latest load-out?
5. Can we leave the pandal up for several days?
6. Where do guests park, and is there step-free access?

Also see [navratri garba night planning](/blog/navratri-garba-night-planning-chicago) for another fall festival hosted in halls, and [religious events](/events/religious-events) for the types of gatherings we help with.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Halls book far ahead for fall weekends.** Weddings take many halls from September to October. Book the hall first, then the rentals. Our team can check the date with the rental companies once you have the hall.
- **Multi-day rentals need a plan.** A five-day festival means the items stay put. Ask the rental company for the cost and the pickup day.
- **Count at delivery.** Count chairs, tables and serving pieces when they arrive, and photograph the room before setup.
- **Check doors and ramps.** Chicago halls with stairs or a narrow door may need carts. Measure first.
- **Shoes.** Many guests remove shoes before entering the pandal. Plan a shoe area with racks near the door.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a Durga Puja program?

Plan one chair per guest, in rows with a center aisle, which is about 8 to 10 square feet per seat. For 200 guests, that is 200 chairs and a hall of about 1,600 to 2,000 square feet for the seating alone. Add space for the stage and the dining area.

### How do you serve bhog to hundreds of people?

Serve in shifts. For 200 guests, two seatings of 100 each is easier than one big rush. Use long tables, a serving line and volunteers who refill and clear. Plan about 1 volunteer for every 15 to 20 guests.

### How big should the pandal stage be?

A 12 x 16 foot platform is a common size for the idol, the priest and the puja. If the stage also hosts dance and drama in the evening, a bigger or second stage helps. Ask your hall about ceiling height and load-in.

### Can we use incense and lamps in a rented hall?

Ask the hall first. Smoke, open flame and dhunuchi can set off smoke detectors, and some halls limit them. Get the rule in writing and plan the location, ventilation and a fire extinguisher.

### When is Durga Puja?

It is in the fall, usually in September or October, and dates follow the lunar calendar. Check this year's date with your committee or a Bengali calendar before you book.
