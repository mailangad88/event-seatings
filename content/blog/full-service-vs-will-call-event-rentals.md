**Quick answer**

Full service means the rental company delivers, sets up, breaks down and picks up. Will-call means you pick up the items and return them yourself. Will-call can lower the rental bill but costs you a vehicle, helpers and time, and it moves breakage and cleaning risk to you. Choose full service for weddings and large events, and will-call only for small, simple orders.

</div>

The question usually starts with a quote. Delivery, setup and pickup add up, and someone says "I have a van, I could just pick it up." Sometimes that is smart. Often it is not. Here is how to decide, with Chicago-area details in mind.

## When will-call makes sense

- A small order that fits in a car or van.
- A casual event in a home or backyard with willing helpers.
- You are flexible on timing and the event is not time-critical.
- The pickup location is convenient and has easy loading.

A set of folding chairs and a few tables for a family party is a classic will-call job. A full ballroom is not.

## When full service is worth it

- **Large counts.** 100 or more chairs plus tables is a truck and crew job, not a van trip.
- **Formal events.** Weddings, galas and corporate events need the room ready on time and looking right.
- **Tough access.** Stairs, a long carry, or a Chicago venue with dock rules.
- **Tight timelines.** One delivery window and a crew that follows the layout saves hours.
- **Risk.** Professional handling reduces damage and counting errors.

Don't forget the soft costs: fuel, a van rental if you need one, helpers' time and the stress. The extra labor on a wedding morning is real. See [how rental quotes work](/blog/how-event-rental-quotes-work) for the line items you may be saving or skipping.

## Hybrid options

Some companies offer middle paths:

- **Delivery only.** Items drop at the door; you set up and prep for pickup.
- **Delivery and setup, you stage for pickup.** Common and fair when you have a team to stack.
- **Pickup only.** You collect, they deliver on return, or vice versa.

Ask what combinations are available, and how each affects the price. [Delivery fees](/blog/event-rental-delivery-fees-explained) and [setup and breakdown fees](/blog/setup-and-breakdown-fees-event-rentals) cover the pieces.

## How to decide

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Count your items.</strong> Will they fit in the vehicle you have?</li>
<li><strong>Check access.</strong> Can you load and unload at both ends?</li>
<li><strong>List helpers.</strong> Who will lift, stack and drive?</li>
<li><strong>Check timing.</strong> Can you return items on time?</li>
<li><strong>Compare totals.</strong> Include fuel, time and risk.</li>
</ol>

## Rules to check for will-call

- **Condition at return.** You are responsible for returning clean, counted items. See [returning rentals](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning).
- **Counting at pickup.** Count everything in front of staff and note issues.
- **Transport protection.** Chairs and glassware need padding and secure loads.
- **Hours.** Pickup and return times are fixed; late returns may be charged.
- **Insurance.** Ask whether your vehicle is allowed to carry the items and whether the waiver covers transport damage.

## Chicago note

City driving, parking and dock rules turn a simple pickup into a morning-long job. If your event is downtown, delivery is often easier than finding legal parking with a loaded van. In the suburbs, will-call is easier if the warehouse is close, which is a question for each company. Ask each company where its warehouse is and what its delivery zones look like.

## A sample weekend, both ways

Picture a 60-guest graduation party in a suburban backyard. With will-call, someone borrows a van Friday afternoon, loads 60 chairs and 8 tables, drives them home, sets them up Saturday morning, tears down Saturday night and returns everything Monday. That is easily a full day of combined effort across several people. With full service, the crew arrives in a window, sets the yard, and returns after the party. The host spends that day on food, guests and the details only they can handle. The right answer depends on whether your time or your budget is tighter.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does will-call mean for event rentals?

It means you collect the items from the rental company and return them yourself, instead of the company delivering and picking up.

### Is will-call cheaper than delivery?

The rental total is often lower, but you add vehicle, fuel, helpers and your own time. Compare the full picture before you decide.

### Can I do delivery without setup?

Many companies offer delivery to the door or room, and you handle setup and breakdown. Ask for pricing for each combination.

### Who is responsible for breakage with will-call?

Usually you, from the time you take the items until you return them. Ask whether a damage waiver covers transport and how condition is checked.

### What size vehicle do I need for 100 chairs?

It depends on the chair type and how they stack, so ask the rental company. Don't assume a car will do it.

### Is full service worth it for a wedding?

For most weddings, yes. The timeline, access and risk make a professional crew a good value, though a small ceremony at home may be an exception.
