Naperville is the biggest suburb in this guide, and one of the busiest event markets in the west suburbs. It straddles DuPage and Will counties, has a famous Riverwalk, and strict, well-documented rules. If your event is public, plan for weeks, not days.

## Naperville's permit process in plain terms

The City of Naperville has a Special Events Permit and Planning Procedures page, run through the Community Services Department. For events that need additional approval, City Council review can take up to 60 days. The City also opens applications for the following year in advance: its 2026 announcement covered 2027 events, so popular dates can be taken early. Tent permit questions go to the Building Permits page, and the tent permit page itself does not list size thresholds, so ask the City about yours.

The Naperville Park District has its own side: pavilion rentals, special event rentals and a facility rental permit. If you want a park pavilion, go to the district first.

## Where Naperville events happen

- **The Riverwalk.** The Naperville Riverwalk follows the DuPage River through downtown. It is a public space with its own rules, so ask the City what is allowed.
- **Park district pavilions and parks.** The district rents pavilions and runs a special events rental process. Centennial Beach is a well-known district facility.
- **Downtown Naperville.** Streets and sidewalks fall under City rules.
- **Banquet halls, hotels and clubs.** Naperville has plenty of indoor options. Ask each what furniture is included.
- **Private homes and backyards.** Lots vary a lot; many are narrow, so measure before you order.

## Tips for Naperville planners

Match your permit plan to your risk. A private backyard with a tent still needs a tent conversation. A public event on the Riverwalk needs the City. A park pavilion needs the district. Our guide to [tent permits in Chicago suburbs](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs) shows what to ask any village.

## Naperville setup example

For a 120-guest corporate reception in a pavilion, plan 15 rounds of 8 (120 seats) or 12 rounds of 10. A [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) (about $18-25, an estimate) works for a head table, and [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) fill the room.

## Building a Naperville rental list

Naperville events skew large, so the list is where you save the most time. Work in this order.

1. **Count seats.** Add up dinner guests, then add the head table, DJ and band.
2. **Pick the table plan.** A 60-inch round seats 8 comfortably and a 72-inch round seats 10. See [how many tables for 50-200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests).
3. **Match linens.** Order floor-length cloths for guest tables and add spares for the gift and cake tables. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).
4. **Add comfort.** Tents, heaters in cool months and fans in summer all change power needs.
5. **Plan the load-in.** Ask your venue where trucks can stop and how far the carry is.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a Naperville special event permit take?

For events that need additional approval, City Council review can take up to 60 days. Apply as early as the City allows. It also opens next-year applications in advance, so check the City page.

### Do I need a tent permit in Naperville?

Ask the City. Tent permits go through the Building Permits process, and the tent page does not list size thresholds, so confirm yours. Read our tent permit guide for the questions to ask.

### Who handles Naperville park pavilion rentals?

The Naperville Park District. It has pavilion rentals and a special event rental process.

### Can I hold an event on the Naperville Riverwalk?

Ask the City of Naperville. The Riverwalk is a public space with its own rules, and large events usually need the Special Events Permit.

### Do you deliver in Naperville?

Yes. First deliveries begin with the April 2027 Founding Season, and we avoid peak rush hours on I-88 and I-355.

### Is Naperville in DuPage or Will County?

Both. The city straddles the county line, so the same city rules apply across it, but county-level resources can differ.
