**Quick answer**

Puerto Rican celebrations in Chicago tend to be big, loud and food-centered, so plan long or round tables for family-style meals, a cooking area with its own power and safety space, a music and dance area, lounge seating for elders and shade or a tent for summer parties. As a rule of thumb, plan one chair per guest for a seated meal and a tent with about 12 to 15 square feet per seated guest. Rent early for summer weekends, which book first.

Chicago has a large Puerto Rican community, especially around Humboldt Park, with a festive calendar that includes weddings, baptisms, quinceañeras, birthdays, holiday parties and summer cookouts. Whether you are planning a backyard party, a parrandas-style holiday gathering or a banquet hall wedding, the pattern is the same: lots of family, lots of food, lots of music. The rentals follow the same logic. Plan for sitting, serving, cooking and dancing, and give each its own space.

## Plan the party in zones

A good Puerto Rican gathering has distinct zones that guests move between:

- **Cooking and serving.** A grill, roaster or caterer station with its own power, fire safety space and serving tables.
- **Eating.** Long tables or rounds with chairs for everyone at once. Meals are often family-style.
- **Music and dancing.** A DJ or live band area and an open floor.
- **Lounge.** Comfortable seating for grandparents and for anyone not dancing.
- **Kids.** A kids table and a play space visible from the adult tables.

## Cooking and safety

If you roast, grill or fry at the party, plan the cooking area before everything else. Keep it away from the tent, trees, fences and walkways, and keep a clear path for guests and servers. Check local rules, ask your landlord or HOA if applicable and keep fire extinguishers handy. ## Holiday parrandas and winter parties

Holiday gatherings in Chicago move indoors. Plan for coat storage, a clear entry and furniture that lets guests move between rooms. A parranda-style visit often arrives by surprise and wants a lively living room, so open floor space and flexible seating matter. Rent stackable chairs and small tables so you can rearrange quickly. ## Chicago notes

- Public parks and park district facilities have their own rules for tents, grills and amplified music. Check with the park district before you plan.
- Humboldt Park and neighborhood events draw big crowds in summer. Rent early for weekends.
- Alleys, backyards and small city lots need delivery access. Measure gates and walkways first.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs should I rent for a family party?

One for every guest at a seated meal, plus 5 to 10 extra for elders and drop-ins. For open-house parties, plan seating for 60 to 70 percent of guests.

### Do I need a tent for a summer party?

A tent gives shade and rain cover, which Chicago summers often demand. Check village rules and permits, and compare with market umbrellas for smaller events.

### How far should the grill be from the tent?

A common rule of thumb is at least 10 feet from tents and structures, but follow your tent vendor, manufacturer and local fire code. Never cook under or inside a tent.

### Can I use long tables for family-style meals?

Yes. Eight-foot banquet tables seat 8 to 10, and long tables encourage sharing. Make sure there is 36 inches of aisle space.

### What about music and dancing?

Plan an open area of 4 to 5 square feet per dancer. If you use live music or a DJ, confirm power and noise rules.

### Can I rent for a banquet hall party?

Yes. Banquet halls often have basic tables and chairs, so rent upgrades where you want them.
