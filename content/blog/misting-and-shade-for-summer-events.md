**Quick answer**

Start with shade, then add airflow, then misting. A tent or umbrellas cut the sun load first, fans move air and misting fans cool guests further when the air is not too humid. A July wedding in a Naperville garden or a corporate picnic in a Lake County park sounds perfect on paper. At 3 p.m. in direct sun, guests in suits and dresses suffer, older relatives wilt and kids melt down. You do not need much equipment to fix this, but you need the right order of steps and honest expectations about what misting can and cannot do. This guide covers both.

## Why Chicago summers need a plan

At O'Hare, the normal July high is about 84 degrees F and the August high is about 82, with normal lows near 64 and 63, according to the Illinois State Climatologist's data. Those are averages. Heat waves push well past them, and Lake Michigan humidity makes the air feel heavier. Cooling must work for both dry heat and sticky heat.

## The cooling ladder

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Shade:</strong> tents, umbrellas and tree cover cut direct heat.</li>
<li><strong>Airflow:</strong> pedestal or tower fans keep air moving.</li>
<li><strong>Misting:</strong> fine droplets cool by evaporation.</li>
<li><strong>Hydration:</strong> water, ice and iced drinks within reach.</li>
<li><strong>Retreat:</strong> an air-conditioned room for anyone who needs it.</li>
</ol>

Each layer adds comfort, and shade matters most. A shaded spot can feel noticeably cooler than a sunny one at the same air temperature, because the sun's heat on skin and clothing is removed. See [market umbrellas for shade](/blog/market-umbrellas-for-shade) and our [tents guides](/blog/topic/tents).

## How misting works and when it fails

Misting fans spray a fine mist that evaporates and cools the air around it. It works best in dry air and less well in humid air, because the water has nowhere to evaporate. On a humid Chicago day, a mist can feel clammy and leave surfaces wet.

**Good uses:** entrances, queue areas, lawn game zones and spots where guests stand.

**Poor uses:** over seated dinner tables and in areas where guests are in formal clothes, since droplets can wet hair, clothes and food.

**Requirements:** misting fans need a water source (a hose or a tank) and often power. Ask your vendor about water pressure, hoses, outlets, extension cords and runoff. Place units so water does not drain across walkways or into electrical gear.

## Schedule around the heat

If you can pick the time, do it. Early afternoon is the hottest period. A late afternoon ceremony in the shade of a building, followed by a tented dinner, is more comfortable than a 1 p.m. ceremony in open sun. Check the sunset time for your date, since a golden-hour ceremony is cooler and photographs better. Look up sunset for your date at timeanddate.com or the National Weather Service. Put lawn games in the shade or at the end of the day. Our post on [lawn games for weddings](/blog/lawn-games-for-weddings) covers planning a game zone.

## Guest care

- **Water everywhere.** More stations than you think, with cups or bottles.
- **Shaded seating.** Priority for older guests, pregnant guests and children.
- **Fans for the ceremony.** Programs as fans are a classic, cheap touch.
- **Retreat room.** A cool space near the restrooms. See [restroom trailer: when you need one](/blog/restroom-trailer-when-you-need-one) for trailers with A/C.
- **Watch for heat illness.** Staff should know the signs and where to get help.

## Questions for your venue

Ask the venue about water access, outlets, tent rules, fire code for fans and misters near tents, noise limits for generators and whether they allow rented cooling equipment. Rooftops and parks may restrict equipment. In public parks, check with the park district. Rules vary by site.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do misting fans work in Chicago humidity?

They help on dry or moderately humid days and do less on very muggy days. Use them for standing areas and pair them with shade and fans.

### What is the first thing I should do to keep guests cool?

Provide shade. A tent, umbrellas or tree cover reduce heat from the sun more than any fan or mister.

### Do misting fans need power and water?

Usually both: a water hose or tank, and an outlet. Ask the vendor about requirements and runoff.

### Can I use misters over dinner tables?

Better not. Mist can wet food, linens and clothing. Place them at entrances and standing areas instead.

### How many water stations do I need?

A common rule of thumb is one station for every 80 to 100 guests, more on hot days. Place them away from the bar to reduce lines.

### What if the weather is too hot?

Have an indoor backup or retreat, shift the schedule and tell guests to dress for the heat.
