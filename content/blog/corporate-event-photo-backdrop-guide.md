**Quick answer**

A step and repeat banner is usually 8 feet tall and 8 to 12 feet wide. Use 8 x 8 for small check-ins and 8 x 12 for galas, repeat logos in staggered rows, light the guests from the front, and leave 8 to 10 feet for the camera. Put it near check-in with room for a queue.

</div>

A corporate photo backdrop is not just a place for selfies. At a Chicago conference, a gala for a nonprofit or an office holiday party, it becomes the image your sponsors, speakers and guests post online. Done well, it puts your logo in every photo without looking like an ad. Here is how to plan one that works, from banner sizes and lighting to placement and power.

## Step and repeat, drape or something else?

<div class="table-wrap">

| Backdrop | Best for | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step and repeat banner | Sponsor logos, red carpet | Brand visible in every crop | Looks like an ad if overdone |
| Pipe and drape in brand color | Subtle brand | Clean, flexible | Logo needs a sign |
| Neon or light box with logo | Lobbies, launches | Eye-catching | Needs power |
| Flower or greenery wall | Hospitality, nonprofit galas | Warm, photo-friendly | Less brand visibility |
| LED screen | Tech, product launches | Changes with the event | Higher cost, needs operator |

</div>

Step and repeat banners are printed vinyl or fabric with a repeating logo grid, mounted on a frame or stand. Fabric is easier to ship, travels well and resists glare, while vinyl is cheaper and stiff. Pipe and drape in a brand color is a subtler option, and you can hang a printed sign or light box in the center.

## Sizing it

Guests stand in front in groups of 2 to 6. A banner 8 feet wide and 8 feet tall works for individual and small group photos. Go to 10 or 12 feet wide for galas where groups of 8 want a photo. Leave 8 to 10 feet in front for the photographer, and room to the side for a queue.

## Logo layout rules

- Keep logos about the same size so no sponsor looks smaller.
- Stagger rows so a crop shows a logo at any angle.
- Use a solid dark or light background for contrast, not a busy pattern.
- Keep logos between knee and head height, which is the main photo zone.
- Get sponsor approval for logo versions and colors before printing.

## Lighting and photos

A banner under a spotlight will glare. Light the guests, not the wall, with soft front light from the side, and keep the banner out of direct window light. A professional photographer will bring lights, but a casual booth needs a ring light or a softbox. Avoid glossy vinyl under overhead lights. Fabric reduces glare.

## Placement

Put the backdrop near the check-in or the reception area, not in a corridor. A good location has room for a queue, soft light and an uncluttered background behind the photographer. Avoid placing it near the bar line or the exit doors. For a gala, use it as an entrance feature, and add a stage or a podium to the side. Read [gala stage decor ideas](/blog/gala-stage-decor-ideas) and [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events) to coordinate the room.

## Chicago venue notes

Chicago hotels, convention centers and downtown venues often have rules about rigging, freight elevators and union labor for large installs, so ask your venue for its exhibitor or vendor manual before you order a large frame. For suburban office parks and banquet halls, simple stands usually work. Winter events need a plan for coats, since the check-in photo spot is also the coat zone.

## Seating and brand touches

Pair the backdrop with simple seating. A branded sign on an [easel](/blog/signage-and-easels-for-events) near the entrance helps guests find it. A [velvet chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) or [shell chair](/chairs/shell) near the backdrop makes an executive portrait seat.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Placing the logo too high for the camera.
- Ordering the print before the sponsor list is final.
- Using glossy vinyl under direct lights.
- Forgetting a queue space.


## Frequently asked questions

### What size should a step and repeat banner be?

8 x 8 feet for small groups, 8 x 10 for standard events and 8 x 12 for galas or larger groups.

### What is a step and repeat?

A backdrop printed with a repeating pattern of logos, so any photo shows the brand. It is common at red carpets, galas and conferences.

### Is fabric or vinyl better for a backdrop?

Fabric reduces glare and travels well, while vinyl is cheaper but stiffer and shinier. Choose by lighting and budget.

### Where should the backdrop go at a corporate event?

Near check-in or the reception area, away from the bar and exit doors, with room for a queue and soft light.

### How far should the camera be from the backdrop?

About 8 to 10 feet, with guests standing 3 to 4 feet in front of the wall to reduce shadows.

### Do I need power for a backdrop?

Only for lights, neon or LED screens. Ask the venue about outlets and use cord covers.
