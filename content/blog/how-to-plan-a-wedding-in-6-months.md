**Quick answer**

Yes, a wedding can be planned in six months. Book the venue and date in the first two weeks, then secure photographer, caterer and music by month two. Send invitations about eight weeks out, order rentals by the end of month four, and keep the guest list modest. Flexibility on date and day of the week is the biggest advantage of a shorter timeline.

</div>

Six months is enough time to plan a wedding, but it asks for faster decisions. The venues you love may be booked on your first-choice date, and popular vendors may be gone. The trick is to decide the few non-negotiables quickly and keep everything else flexible. Fewer decisions made sooner beats more decisions made slowly.

## Weeks 1 to 2: the three decisions

Decide your budget, your guest ceiling and your date flexibility. These three set everything. Then tour venues immediately and book one, and consider accepting a Friday, Sunday or off-peak date to widen your options. Read [how to choose a wedding venue in Chicago](/blog/how-to-choose-a-wedding-venue-chicago) for a tour checklist, and [how to set a wedding budget](/blog/how-to-set-a-wedding-budget) to make the numbers realistic.

If you have a shorter list of must-haves, say so to every vendor. Phrases like we are planning for April and are flexible on the day get you more useful responses.

## Months 1 to 2: the vendor sprint

Book the vendors that cannot be replaced easily: photographer, caterer if your venue does not provide one, band or DJ, and officiant. Ask directly about availability on your date before you spend time on a proposal. If you want a planner, hire one now; our guide on [wedding planner vs day-of coordinator](/blog/wedding-planner-vs-day-of-coordinator) can help you decide. Use [the wedding vendor booking order](/blog/wedding-vendor-booking-order) to avoid wasting time.

In a short timeline, the best vendors are not always available, so keep a second choice in each category. A vendor tracker helps you manage the replies.

## Months 2 to 4: invitations, attire and rentals

Order attire early, because alterations take time. Design and print invitations, or send digital ones if time is tight. Mail invitations around eight weeks before the wedding; see [when to send save the dates and invitations](/blog/when-to-send-save-the-dates-and-invitations). Send a quick save-the-date by email or text right after you set the date, so out-of-town guests can plan.

By month four, request rental quotes and place the order. Use the [complete wedding rental checklist](/blog/complete-wedding-rental-checklist) to avoid forgetting items. Tents, specialty chairs and large orders may have limited availability on peak dates, so do not wait until the last month.

## Months 5 to 6: confirm, seat and rehearse

RSVPs come due about four weeks before the wedding. Chase late replies, finalize the headcount and build your seating chart. Confirm all vendor arrival times in writing and send your day-of timeline. See [wedding RSVP deadlines and follow up](/blog/wedding-rsvp-deadline-and-follow-up) and the [wedding day timeline template](/blog/wedding-day-timeline-template). Plan a rehearsal and rehearsal dinner if you want one; [rehearsal dinner rentals](/blog/rehearsal-dinner-rentals) shows what you might need.

## What to compress and what to protect

Short timelines work when you know what can flex and what cannot.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Area | Protect | Flex |
|---|---|---|
| Venue and date | Book first, within two weeks | Day of week, season |
| Photographer | Book by month two | Package size |
| Food | Caterer booked early | Menu style, buffet vs plated |
| Music | Book DJ or band by month two | Live vs recorded |
| Invitations | Mail 8 weeks out | Digital if time is short |
| Decor | Rentals reserved by month four | Elaborate extras |
| Guest list | Set ceiling quickly | Number of guests |

</div>

The more flexible you are about date and style, the faster your planning goes.

## Where short timelines go wrong

The most common problem is waiting to decide. A week lost on the date can cost you the venue you wanted, and a week lost on the venue can cost you the photographer. Set a decision deadline for each step and stick to it. The second problem is trying to do everything alone. Hand off research and errands to family and friends, and use one shared checklist. The third is skipping the budget. Fast decisions can add up fast, so log every quote and deposit as you go.

## Chicago-area notes

In the Chicago area, the main challenge of a six-month plan is availability on summer and early fall Saturdays, which often book a year ahead. Consider a Friday, Sunday or weekday wedding, or a winter or early spring date, when venues and vendors are more available and sometimes cheaper. If you want a tent or outdoor space, check with your village or park district about permits early, as processes can take time. Rental companies also fill up for peak weekends, so request quotes as soon as you have a date, rather than waiting until the venue contract is complete.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 6 months enough time to plan a wedding?

Yes. Many couples plan in six months or less. You need to decide the budget, guest ceiling and date quickly, book the venue and key vendors first, and stay flexible on day and details.

### What should I book first when planning a wedding in 6 months?

Book the venue and date first, then the photographer, caterer, music and officiant. These are the vendors most likely to be booked on your date.

### How do I find a venue with short notice?

Be flexible on the day of the week and the month. Fridays, Sundays and off-peak months are easier to book. Ask venues about recent cancellations.

### When should I send invitations if I have six months?

Mail them about eight weeks before the wedding. Send a quick save the date by email or text as soon as the date is set.

### Can I still get a wedding dress in 6 months?

Many shops offer off-the-rack or sample dresses, and some made-to-order gowns take several months, so ask about timelines before you buy.

### When should I order rentals for a 6-month wedding?

Request quotes as soon as you have a venue and rough guest count, and place the order by the end of month four. Peak dates and tents can book up.

### How much does a short engagement change the budget?

It can add rush fees for invitations or attire, but flexible dates can also lower venue costs. Track rush fees in your budget.
