**Quick answer**

Plan 6 to 8 square feet per guest for a standing cocktail reception, one cocktail table per 6 to 8 guests, and seating for 30 to 40 percent of guests. A 100-guest cocktail party needs about 700 to 900 square feet, 12 to 16 highboy tables, two bars and two or three food stations.

</div>

A cocktail reception is the most space-efficient event you can run. Guests stand, mingle and move, so you need fewer chairs and less floor. But it is also the easiest to get wrong: bars in the wrong place cause lines, food stations clog walkways and there is nowhere to set a drink. Use these ratios to plan receptions at Chicago rooftops, galleries, lofts and tents.

## The standing ratio

Standing guests need less room than seated ones, but still need space to turn, pass and use a bar.

| Style | Square feet per guest | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tight, short mixer | 5 to 6 | Short duration, light food |
| Standard cocktail reception | 6 to 8 | Drinks and passed appetizers |
| Cocktail with stations | 8 to 10 | Food stations and some seating |
| Cocktail with seating for half | 10 to 12 | Longer events, older guests |

These ranges are common in the event industry, but vary by source; adjust for your crowd and venue. See [square feet per guest by event style](/blog/square-feet-per-guest-by-event-style) for the full chart.

## Cocktail tables: how many

Highboy cocktail tables are typically 30 inches across and 42 inches tall. A common rule of thumb is one table per 6 to 8 guests, so 100 guests need 12 to 16. If your guests will eat, add more. See [how many cocktail tables for a standing reception](/blog/how-many-cocktail-tables-for-standing-reception) and [cocktail highboy vs lowboy tables](/blog/cocktail-highboy-vs-lowboy-tables).

Stagger the tables in a loose grid, at least 5 feet apart, rather than in rows. Rows read like a waiting room. A staggered pattern leaves paths and gives people places to drop a drink.

## Bars and stations

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>One bar per 50 to 75 guests.</strong> Two bars for 100, at opposite ends.</li>
<li><strong>Bars off the main path.</strong> Put them against a wall with 6 feet of clear floor.</li>
<li><strong>Stations away from bars.</strong> So food lines do not cross drink lines.</li>
<li><strong>Passed food covers the middle.</strong> Servers circulate, so tables stay clear.</li>
<li><strong>Seating at the edges.</strong> A few chairs and a lounge area for guests who want to sit.</li>
</ol>

See [bar placement](/blog/bar-placement-floor-plan) and [food station ideas](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment) for stations and footprints.

## How many chairs?

Even at a standing event, plan seating for about a third of guests, more for older crowds or longer receptions. A mix of stools at highboys, a lounge and chairs along the wall works well. ## Common cocktail reception mistakes

**Putting every table in a row.** Rows turn the room into an aisle. Staggered tables break up sightlines and make the space feel smaller and more social.

**Forgetting trash and glass pickup.** Guests will set empty glasses anywhere. Place a few discreet bins or ask staff to circulate. Highboy tables near the bars fill first.

**Placing the food station beside the bar.** The two lines merge and block each other. Put food at least 15 feet from any bar.

**Too few chairs.** Even a short reception loses older guests and guests in heels if there is nowhere to sit. A lounge group near a wall gives them a place without taking over the room.

**No coat plan.** In a Chicago winter, 100 guests bring 100 coats. A coat rack or a coat check near the door keeps the highboys free; see [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## Cocktail hour before dinner

If cocktail hour is followed by a seated dinner in a different room, you can use a smaller space. Cocktail hour at a wedding usually runs 45 to 60 minutes. If it is in the same room as the ceremony or dinner, the crew resets the room during it. See [ceremony to reception room flip layout](/blog/ceremony-to-reception-room-flip-layout).

## Chicago practicalities

Rooftop and terrace cocktail receptions in Chicago depend on weather; have a plan for rain and wind, as in outdoor event chairs and Chicago weather. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much space per person for a cocktail party?

About 6 to 8 square feet per guest. Add 2 to 4 square feet for stations and seating.

### How many cocktail tables do I need for 100 guests?

12 to 16 highboy tables, or one per 6 to 8 guests.

### How many guests should have a seat at a cocktail party?

Plan seating for 30 to 40 percent. Increase it for older guests or an event longer than two hours.

### How many bars for a cocktail reception?

One bar per 50 to 75 guests. Two bars for 100 guests.

### How far apart should cocktail tables be?

About 5 feet apart, staggered, so guests can pass and servers can circulate.

### Can I host a cocktail reception in a tent?

Yes. Plan the same ratios and add space for sidewalls and a prep area. See our tent size guide.
