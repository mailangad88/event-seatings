**Quick answer**

Pay a deposit to hold your date, the balance by the due date in your contract, and use a traceable method such as a credit card or a business bank transfer so you have a record and, with cards, some dispute protection. Avoid paying everything far in advance, never send money to a personal account for a business order, and keep invoices and receipts together with your contract.

</div>

Most rental disputes are not about money itself but about what was paid, when and for what. A clean payment trail makes every problem easier to solve. Here is how to handle the money side of an event rental in Chicago or anywhere else, from the first deposit to the last receipt.

## Common payment methods

<div class="table-wrap">

| Method | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card | Record and dispute options | Possible processing fee |
| Debit card | Convenience | Fewer protections than credit in some cases |
| Bank transfer or ACH | Larger balances | Confirm the account is the business's |
| Check | Traditional | Slower to clear; hold time |
| Cash | Rarely needed | No record unless you get a receipt |
| Payment apps | Quick | Not ideal for business orders; check the terms |

</div>

Which methods a company accepts is its choice, and some add a fee for cards. Ask what is accepted and whether a fee applies. We are not going to quote card fee percentages, because they vary by company and card type. Check protections with your card issuer, because they differ.

## How much to pay and when

A typical pattern is a deposit at booking and the balance before the event. Look at your contract for exact amounts and dates, and read [deposits explained](/blog/rental-deposits-explained). Match the balance due date to the [final headcount lock](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals), so you pay for the right quantities.

Don't pay in full much earlier than the contract requires. A smaller upfront payment leaves you more leverage if something changes. Read the [cancellation terms](/blog/event-rental-cancellation-and-date-change-policy) before you pay anything.

## What to keep

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>The signed contract</strong> with all pages.</li>
<li><strong>The final quote</strong> that matches the contract.</li>
<li><strong>Every invoice</strong> and receipt for deposit and balance.</li>
<li><strong>Emails</strong> confirming changes, dates and counts.</li>
<li><strong>Delivery and pickup sheets</strong> with notes and photos.</li>
</ol>

Store them in one folder or email label. After the event, check for any final charges, such as damage or late fees, against the contract. See [returning rentals](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning).

## Safe payment habits

- **Pay a business, not a person.** Check the name on the invoice and the payment account.
- **Use the official payment link or portal** from the company's own email or site.
- **Be wary of urgent requests** to change bank details by email; call the company at a known number to confirm.
- **Never pay for an order without a written agreement.** See the [red flags](/blog/event-rental-red-flags) list.
- **Pay with the method that gives you a record.**

Payment scams exist in the event world, mostly through fake invoices and changed account details. When in doubt, call.

## Splitting payments among people

Many events have several payers, such as parents and a couple. Decide who pays what and who is the contact on the contract. A single payer for the rental company simplifies receipts and communication. If more than one person pays, ask whether the company can split invoices or accept separate payments, and keep each receipt.

## Taxes, fees and extras

Ask whether the quoted total includes tax and which fees are added after the event, such as damage charges or late returns. [How quotes work](/blog/how-event-rental-quotes-work) shows where tax appears. If you need an invoice for an organization, nonprofit or business, ask for the details you need on it, like a purchase order number or tax ID. For a corporate or nonprofit event, check your own accounting rules for approved payment methods.

## If you are billed something unexpected

1. **Ask for an itemized explanation** in writing.
2. **Compare it to the contract.** Is it listed?
3. **Check your photos and sheets.** Do they support or contradict the charge?
4. **Talk to the company** before disputing with your bank.
5. **Keep your tone factual** and your records handy.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you pay for event rentals?

Usually a deposit at booking and the balance before the event, by card, bank transfer or check, depending on what the company accepts. Check your contract for the exact schedule.

### Should I pay for rentals with a credit card?

A credit card gives you a record and, depending on your issuer, possible dispute options. Some companies add a processing fee, so ask first.

### When is the balance due for event rentals?

Usually before delivery, often tied to the final headcount deadline. The exact date is in your contract.

### Is it safe to pay a rental company by Venmo or cash?

It can be, but it leaves a thinner record, and some business orders should not go through personal accounts. Prefer a traceable method and get a receipt.

### What should I do if the rental company asks for payment to a different account?

Call the company at a number you already trust to confirm before sending anything. Changed payment details by email are a known scam pattern.

### Can I split the payment between two people?

Often yes, if the company agrees. Ask how to document it so both payments are tied to the same order.
