**Quick answer**

Most rental contracts treat rain as your risk, not theirs: the items are reserved and the crew is scheduled whether it rains or not. Look for what the contract says about weather cancellations, rescheduling, wet or muddy items and delivery in storms. The best protection is a backup plan, such as a tent or an indoor option, decided before the event.

Chicago weather does not need an introduction. A June wedding can be hot, windy and stormy in the same afternoon. The question for your rental contract is not whether it might rain, it is who pays when it does. The answer is rarely "nobody," and the clause that decides it is easy to miss.

## The four clauses that matter

**1. Weather cancellation.** Does the contract allow you to cancel for rain? Most do not, because the company has already reserved your items and crew. Some allow a reschedule within a window. Others have a limited weather policy for severe conditions, like a storm warning. Ask for the exact wording.

**2. Delivery in bad weather.** If it is storming, will the crew still deliver and set up? Safety is often a factor, and policies for lightning or high winds differ. Ask what happens if the crew cannot deliver or set up safely.

**3. Wet, muddy or damaged items.** Chairs on wet grass come back dirty. Linens come back soaked. Check whether cleaning or excess damage is charged. Read [returning rentals](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning) and [damage waivers explained](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained).

**4. Outdoor surface rules.** Heavy furniture on soft ground can sink. Some companies recommend flooring or ask you to confirm the ground is suitable.

## The backup plan is the real protection

<div class="table-wrap">

| Backup option | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Tent on site | Permit, sidewalls, flooring. See [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent) |
| Indoor alternative | Capacity, layout, deposit, and the cutoff to switch |
| Covered area or pavilion | Whether it can hold your full count |
| Date change | Rental company flexibility and your venue's availability |

</div>

Decide the cutoff for the backup call, for example "by 10 a.m. the day before," and tell the rental company, the venue and key vendors. A decision at the last minute is much harder to carry out. For tent rules, see our guide to [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and your own village or city office.

## Build the rain decision into your timeline

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>One week out:</strong> watch the forecast, confirm backup availability.</li>
<li><strong>Two to three days out:</strong> decide between Plan A and Plan B if the forecast is uncertain.</li>
<li><strong>Day before:</strong> confirm the final location with the rental company.</li>
<li><strong>Delivery day:</strong> walk the surface and confirm the layout.</li>
<li><strong>After the event:</strong> stage wet items for pickup.</li>
</ol>

## A short script for your rental company

You do not need legal language. Ask these four questions and write down the answers: "If it storms on the day, what happens to my delivery?" "If I move indoors, can the order move with me, and by when?" "Will you charge extra for wet or muddy items?" "Is there a weather-related change or cancellation window, and is it in the contract?" Send the answers to yourself in an email so the thread exists.

## Weather is not only rain

Outdoor events in the Chicago area face heat, wind and cold as well. Wind can move lightweight items and a tent can be unsafe in high gusts. We cover that in [outdoor event chairs and Chicago weather](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues). A clause that mentions only rain misses other conditions; ask about wind, lightning, extreme heat and cold too.

## What a fair weather clause says

- It states who decides when conditions are unsafe, and how.
- It offers a reschedule option if the company cannot deliver for safety reasons.
- It explains charges for wet or dirty items.
- It does not promise things it cannot control.

Be wary of a contract that says nothing about weather. Silence usually means the standard terms apply, and that normally leaves you holding the risk. This ties into the broader [red flags](/blog/event-rental-red-flags) list.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I cancel my rental if it rains?

Usually not for ordinary rain. Many contracts treat weather as your risk because items and crew are already reserved. Some allow a reschedule or have a severe weather policy, so read the clause.

### What happens if it storms on delivery day?

It depends on the company's policy. Crews may delay or reschedule for safety. Ask what the plan is and how a delay would affect your event.

### Will I be charged for muddy or wet rentals?

Possibly. Policies on cleaning and excess wear vary. Ask whether weather-related soiling is covered by a waiver or billed after the event.

### Do I need a tent for an outdoor event?

If a weather backup is not available, a tent is a common choice. Check permit and venue rules first. Our guide on [tent size](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) helps you plan.

### When should I decide on a rain plan?

As early as possible, and set a decision cutoff in advance, such as the day before. Tell all your vendors the plan and the cutoff.

### Does a rain date mean my deposit moves?

That depends on the contract. Ask whether the deposit and your reserved items transfer to a new date and whether there is a fee.
