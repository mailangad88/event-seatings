**Quick answer**

Choose flutes if you want steady bubbles, easy carrying and a classic look. Choose coupes for a vintage, Gatsby feel when guests will mostly stay seated, because the wide bowl spills more and loses fizz faster. ## Why flutes are the default

A flute's narrow opening keeps carbonation in the glass and the bubbles rise in a visible line, which looks great in a close photo. Flutes are easier to hold while clinking and standing, and they survive a crowded room better than a coupe. Most wedding toasts in a banquet hall or ballroom use flutes for that reason.

The downside is a narrow mouth, which hides the aroma and makes a flute hard to wash by hand. Rental flutes arrive washed and racked, so that does not affect you.

## Why couples pick coupes

A coupe has a wide, shallow bowl that is easy to sip from and looks like a classic cocktail glass. It suits a Great Gatsby, art deco or retro wedding. Coupes also work for a champagne tower, where the glasses stack in a pyramid. A tower needs sturdy, matching coupes and is best done by a professional. Check your venue's rules and ask the rental company about stackable glasses.

The downside is spillage. A full coupe tilts easily, so a standing toast in a crowded room or a breezy tent may end with wet sleeves. Keep coupe toasts seated or in a calm indoor setting.

## Counting your toast glasses

Rent one per adult guest. Add 5 to 10 percent for spares and for people who want a second pour. If your party includes guests who do not drink, offer sparkling juice or cider in the same glass so everyone raises the same shape. Our [glasses per guest guide](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest) shows where the toast flute fits in the whole count.

## Pouring and timing

- **Pre-pour or pass?** Servers can pre-pour flutes on a tray as guests sit down. This is fastest for a 100-guest room.
- **Cold glass matters.** A chilled glass keeps bubbles lively. Ask if the rental glass can be refrigerated for an hour.
- **Pour size.** Plan about 4 oz for a toast, which gives about 6 to 7 pours per 750 ml bottle. For 100 guests, that is about 16 bottles.
- **Collect afterward.** Glasses left at tables get mixed with water goblets. Have staff gather them after the toast.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are flutes or coupes better for a wedding toast?

Flutes are better for most weddings because they keep bubbles and spill less. Coupes are better for a vintage look when the toast is seated. Choose by theme and how crowded the room will be.

### Do champagne coupes lose their bubbles faster?

Yes. The wide surface area lets carbon dioxide escape more quickly, so a coupe goes flat sooner than a flute. Pour closer to the time of the toast to offset this.

### How many champagne glasses do I need for 100 guests?

Rent 100 for adults plus 5 to 10 spares. That covers the toast, with a few left for the head table or a second pour.

### How much champagne do I need for a toast?

A 750 ml bottle gives about 6 to 7 four-ounce toast pours. For 100 guests, plan 15 to 17 bottles for the toast only. Add more if guests will continue drinking sparkling wine after.

### Can I use wine glasses instead?

Yes. A white wine glass works as a toast glass if you want fewer glass types. The bubbles will fade faster than in a flute, and the look is less festive.

### Can I stack coupes into a champagne tower?

You can, but it takes a flat, stable table, matching coupes and a careful setup. Ask your rental company whether they supply tower-ready coupes and whether your venue permits it.
