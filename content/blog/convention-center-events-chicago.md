## Convention centers around Chicago

- **McCormick Place**, on the lakefront just south of downtown Chicago, is one of the largest convention centers in North America, with several buildings and many ballrooms and meeting rooms. Big shows like the Chicago Auto Show are held there.
- **Donald E. Stephens Convention Center**, in Rosemont near O'Hare, is close to many airport hotels.
- **Schaumburg Convention Center**, connected to the Renaissance Schaumburg hotel, serves the northwest suburbs. See our [Schaumburg area guide](/chair-rentals/schaumburg).
- **Tinley Park Convention Center** serves the south suburbs.

Hotels with large ballrooms and conference centers also host convention-style events across the area, especially in [Oak Brook](/chair-rentals/oak-brook), [Schaumburg](/chair-rentals/schaumburg) and downtown [Chicago](/chair-rentals/chicago).

**The venue** provides the space and usually has exclusive rights to food and beverage, and often to electrical, internet and rigging. You order those through the venue.

**The official service contractor** (sometimes called the general service contractor) is chosen by the show organizer or the venue. It typically handles booths, pipe and drape, carpet, signs and freight handling, often called drayage or material handling.

**Outside vendors**, like a rental company bringing chairs, lounge furniture or decor, may be allowed under rules: registration, insurance certificates, delivery through a specific dock at a specific time, and sometimes restrictions on who can carry items onto the floor. Some facilities have labor rules about which work must be done by specific trades. Ask the venue and your service contractor before you assume you can bring something in.

## What to ask before you book

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| Which rooms fit our format, and at what capacity? | Theater, classroom and banquet setups hold very different counts. |
| Is catering exclusive? | Most convention centers require their own caterer. |
| Which items must come from the venue or the contractor? | Power, internet, carpet and rigging are often exclusive. |
| Can outside rental companies deliver, and how? | Dock, time window, insurance and handling rules. |
| What are move-in and move-out times? | Late move-out can cost extra. |
| What insurance is required, from whom? | Venues often require certificates from every vendor and the organizer. |

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I bring my own rentals into McCormick Place?

It depends on your event and the venue's current rules. Large convention centers usually set rules on outside vendors, deliveries, insurance and who can handle items on the floor. Ask the venue and your official service contractor before you order anything.

### What is an official service contractor?

It is the company appointed to provide services like booths, pipe and drape, carpet, signs and freight handling for an event. Exhibitors usually order these services from the contractor using forms in the exhibitor manual.

### What is drayage?

Drayage, or material handling, is the charge for moving your freight from the dock to your booth and back. It is usually billed by weight and handled by the official contractor.

### Is catering exclusive at convention centers?

Usually, yes. Most convention centers have an exclusive food and beverage provider, so outside caterers are not allowed. Ask about exceptions for specialty or cultural food.

### What insurance do convention centers require?

Many require proof of insurance from the organizer and from each vendor working on site. We do not issue certificates of insurance ourselves, so tell us the requirements at quote time.

### Which convention centers are near Chicago?

Well-known options include McCormick Place in Chicago, the Donald E. Stephens Convention Center in Rosemont, the Schaumburg Convention Center and the Tinley Park Convention Center. Many large hotels also have convention-style space.
