**Quick answer**

A ceremony usually rents chairs, an arch or backdrop, an aisle runner, a signing table with two chairs, a welcome sign on an easel, sound, and shade or heat as needed. Plan one chair per guest plus a few spares, and confirm anchoring, rain plans and venue rules before delivery.

A ceremony needs far fewer items than a reception, but they are easy to forget because they live at the edges: a place for the officiant to stand, something to sign on, a way to keep programs dry. This checklist covers the rental items most Chicago-area ceremonies use, with the questions that matter for each.

## Count the chairs carefully

The rule of thumb is one chair per guest plus a few spares for the wedding party and the elderly. Many planners add 5 to 10 percent extras. Read [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [ceremony vs. reception chairs](/blog/ceremony-vs-reception-chairs) for the details. If the ceremony and reception are in the same room, ask about the flip: the crew has to move chairs, usually during cocktail hour.

## Outdoor ceremony extras

Outdoor ceremonies in the Chicago area need more than chairs. Check these items:

1. **Shade.** Umbrellas for the first rows in summer, and ask guests to bring hats.
2. **Wind.** Weights for arches and signs.
3. **Sound.** A speaker and a mic, because vows do not carry on a lawn.
4. **Restrooms.** Close and clearly signed.
5. **Rain plan.** A tent, a covered patio or an indoor option. Decide the call time ahead.

## Style choices that matter in photos

The chair you pick will be in every ceremony photo. A white [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is quiet and affordable, a [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back) suits barns and gardens, and a clear [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) works in a formal room. Pair with a [round, hexagon or square arch](/blog/wedding-ceremony-arch-round-hexagon-square) and an [aisle runner](/blog/aisle-runner-length-and-material-guide).

## What is not in this list

Florals, officiants, music, marriage license and the venue's own policies are not rentals, so ask your planner or venue. We do not stock non-chair items ourselves yet, but we help plan the full rental list so the pieces come from the right sources.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Forgetting the signing table until the morning of.
- Choosing an arch that blocks the sun on the couple's faces.
- No rain plan for an outdoor ceremony.
- Assuming the venue provides chairs for the ceremony.


## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent for a wedding ceremony?

Chairs, an arch or backdrop, an aisle runner, a signing table and two chairs, a welcome sign and easel, plus sound and shade if outdoors. Add heaters or umbrellas based on the season.

### How many chairs do I need for a ceremony?

One per guest plus a few spares, and extra for the wedding party if they will sit. Many planners add 5 to 10 percent.

### Do I need a signing table?

Only if you plan to sign the marriage license during the ceremony. A 4 to 5 foot table and two chairs is typical. Check your officiant for requirements.

### What should I rent for an outdoor ceremony?

Add shade, a sound system, weights for signs and arches, and a rain plan such as a tent or indoor flip. Check wind, temperature and the venue's rules.

### Can ceremony chairs be reused for the reception?

Often yes, if the ceremony and reception are in the same space. Ask about flip time and crew.

### When should I book ceremony rentals?

As soon as you have a date and venue. Popular dates fill, especially for ceremonies in May through October.
