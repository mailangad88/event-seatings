The Mid-Autumn Festival is a harvest and family festival celebrated by Chinese, Vietnamese and many other East and Southeast Asian families. It falls on the full moon of the eighth month of the lunar calendar, so the date moves each year, usually in September or early October. Check this year's date.

The heart of the evening is simple: family together, a full moon, food to share. In Chicago, many families celebrate at home, in a backyard or at a restaurant or hall, and Chinatown has its own events around the date. Check local listings for what is on. Traditions vary by family and by region, so adjust this guide to how you celebrate.

## What happens at the gathering

- **Moon viewing.** Families go outside after dinner to see the full moon. It rises in the east around sunset, so you do not need to wait until midnight.
- **Mooncakes.** Round pastries, often filled with lotus seed paste, red bean or a salted egg yolk. They are rich and are cut into small wedges and shared.
- **Tea.** Hot tea is the usual drink. Oolong, jasmine or pu-erh are common.
- **Fruit.** Pomelo, grapes, pears, persimmons and apples.
- **Lanterns.** Paper or battery lanterns for children. In some communities, kids carry them in a short walk.
- **Stories.** The legend of Chang'e, the moon goddess, is often told to children.

Vietnamese families call the festival Tet Trung Thu, and it focuses on children, with lanterns, lion dances and star-shaped lanterns. Chinese families often focus on reunion and the round shape that stands for family togetherness. The details change from home to home.

## Seating for the family

A reunion table is often round. A round table means everyone faces one another, and it fits the shape of the moon and the mooncake.

## Mooncakes and tea

Mooncakes are dense and sweet, so serve small pieces. A plate of cut wedges and a pot of tea beside it is the classic pairing.

| Item | How much, rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Mooncakes | 1 whole cake per 3 to 4 guests, cut in wedges |
| Tea | 2 cups per guest |
| Fruit | A few pieces per guest |
| Small plates and forks | 1 per guest, plus spares |

Serve tea in pots with small cups or a hot water urn. For 20 or more, an urn saves a lot of trips to the kitchen. See [how many coffee urns for a party](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party). ## Lanterns and a kids lantern walk

Lanterns are what kids remember. Paper lanterns with a battery light are safer than candles.

- **Battery tea lights** inside paper lanterns avoid fire risk. Open flames and kids do not mix, especially near paper. See [lanterns and luminaries for events](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events).
- **A short route.** Walk around the yard or down the block with the kids, 10 to 15 minutes. Have an adult at the front and the back.
- **Hang lanterns** from string over the table or from a shepherd's hook. See [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events).
- **Check the street.** If you walk on public streets, keep it short and stay on the sidewalk. Ask your village or city if you plan a bigger group.

## The evening in Chicago

September and early October evenings are often cool. The sun sets around 6 or 7 pm and it gets chilly fast once the moon is up.

- **Warmth.** Patio heaters or blankets. See [patio heaters: how many per guests](/blog/patio-heaters-how-many-per-guests).
- **Clear view.** Pick a spot with a clear view to the east, not under a big tree or behind a garage. Check where the moon rises in your yard.
- **Rain plan.** Fall rain is common. Have a table inside or a tent. See [outdoor events in Chicago in October](/blog/outdoor-events-chicago-in-october) and [outdoor events in Chicago in September](/blog/outdoor-events-chicago-in-september).
- **Light.** Soft light so the moon is still seen. Use warm string lights low and few. See [lighting color temperature](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events).

Many Chicago families also join community events. Chinatown on the South Side often has activities around the festival, and other communities host their own. Check local listings for the date and times.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Round tables are not always easy to get through gates.** A 72-inch round top may not fit through a narrow Chicago gangway. Ask about a folding top, or measure first.
- **Mooncakes are heavy and rich.** Guests often eat a small piece. Do not buy too many, and keep the leftovers cool.
- **Hot water needs power.** An urn needs an outlet, and the cord cannot cross a path. See [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist).
- **The date can move.** Because the festival follows the lunar calendar, a date on one calendar may differ from another. Confirm with your family before you book.
- **Count at delivery.** Count chairs and tables at drop-off.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the Mid-Autumn Festival?

It falls on the full moon of the eighth lunar month, usually in September or early October. The date moves each year, so check this year's date on a lunar calendar or with your family before you book.

### How many people fit at a round table?

A 72-inch round table seats about 10 and a 60-inch round seats about 8. For a family of 20, use two 72-inch rounds. Leave about 5 feet around each table for chairs and walking.

### What do you serve at a Mid-Autumn Festival gathering?

Mooncakes, tea, fruit such as pomelo, grapes and pears, and often a family dinner. Traditions vary by family and region. Cut mooncakes into small wedges to share.

### How many mooncakes do I need?

A rule of thumb is one whole mooncake for every 3 to 4 guests, since they are rich and cut into wedges. For 20 guests, 5 or 6 cakes is plenty. Add fruit so there is something lighter.

### Is it safe to use candles in lanterns?

Battery lights are safer, especially with kids and paper lanterns. Open flames near paper can catch fire fast. Battery tea lights give a similar glow.

### Can I rent a round table for a backyard gathering?

Yes. Most rental companies have 60-inch and 72-inch rounds, along with chairs and linens. Measure your gate and path first, and ask about delivery the day before.
