**Quick answer**

A Jewish wedding ceremony takes place under a chuppah, a canopy on four poles. Plan ceremony chairs in two sections with a center aisle, front-row seats for parents and grandparents near the chuppah, and a dance floor sized for the hora, which lifts the couple on chairs. Reception tables are typically rounds of 8 to 10. Customs vary between Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and secular families, so ask your rabbi, cantor or officiant what seating they require, including whether guests sit separately.

Jewish weddings in the Chicago area range from small backyard ceremonies to ballroom receptions with hundreds of guests, and traditions differ by movement and family. Some ceremonies seat men and women separately, some do not. Some include a ketubah signing before the ceremony, and nearly all feature the chuppah and a joyful dance. This guide focuses on what the room needs and leaves religious decisions to your officiant and family.

## Ceremony seating around the chuppah

The chuppah is the center of the ceremony. It is usually open on all sides and stands at the head of an aisle, with the couple, officiant and often both sets of parents beneath or beside it. For seating, plan:

- **A center aisle** wide enough for the processional, which includes parents and grandparents. Four to six feet works.
- **Two sections of chairs**, one on each side. Some families use mixed sections, others use separate ones.
- **Front-row seats** for grandparents and close family, with a few seats reserved for people who cannot stand for long.
- **A clear area around the chuppah** for the couple, officiant and photographers.
- **Chairs for the ketubah signing** or a small reception before the ceremony, if included.

For chuppah structures and decor, see our [chuppah and mandap decor guide](/blog/chuppah-and-mandap-structure-decor-guide).

## Reception layout and the hora

Receptions commonly feature lively circle dancing. In the hora, guests circle the couple, who are lifted on chairs. That means:

- **A dance floor sized for a full circle.** For 150 guests, about 20 x 20 feet is a minimum, and larger is better.
- **Two sturdy chairs** for lifting the couple. Many couples use their own wedding chairs. Ask the rental company for sturdy armchairs rated for the weight, and let the people lifting know the plan.
- **Clear space around the floor.** Keep tables and bar lines back so the circle can grow.

Round tables of 8 to 10 are standard. Reserve a head table or a sweetheart table, parents' tables and a separate table for the officiant and honored guests, if desired.

## Dietary needs and table planning

Many Jewish weddings are kosher or kosher-style. A kosher caterer may need specific equipment, and some venues require kosher caterers. If your event is kosher, check which dishes, pots and serving pieces the caterer provides. Do not assume rented dinnerware can be used, because it may not be kosher. Ask your caterer and rabbi before renting any dishes or serving pieces, and see our [working with your caterer](/blog/working-with-caterer-and-rental-company) guide.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where does everyone sit at a Jewish ceremony?

Guests usually sit in two sections facing the chuppah with a center aisle. Parents and grandparents sit in the front rows. Some families seat men and women separately. Ask your officiant.

### How wide should the aisle be?

Plan 4 to 6 feet, enough for the processional and for the couple to walk together. A wider aisle looks better in photos and is easier for grandparents.

### What chairs do I need for the hora?

Two sturdy armchairs that can bear the couple's weight when lifted. Choose chairs without decorative parts that could snap, and tell the people lifting which chairs to use.

### How big should the dance floor be?

About 4 to 5 square feet per dancing guest as a rule of thumb. Jewish receptions often get everyone on the floor, so plan toward the larger end.

### Do I need a chuppah rental?

You need a chuppah, but you can build your own, rent a structure or ask your decorator. The decor guide linked above covers options.

### Can I use rental dishes at a kosher wedding?

Only with your caterer's and rabbi's approval. Many caterers bring their own dishes. Always ask first.
