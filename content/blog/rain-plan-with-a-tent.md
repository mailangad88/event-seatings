**Quick answer**

A tent keeps rain off your tables, but guests still need dry paths, a floor that does not turn to mud and sidewalls to block blowing rain. Build a rain plan with five parts: a floor or mats, sidewalls on hand, drainage and path planning, a second spot for coat check and restrooms, and a clear weather decision date. Call your tent company 48 hours before the event to confirm.

"We have a tent" is not a rain plan. Chicago springs and early summers bring heavy storms, and a lawn that was fine on Wednesday can be a mess by Saturday. Here is what really goes wrong and how to head it off.

1. **Puddles at the low edge.** Water flows downhill and pools where the tent meets the lawn.
2. **Mud inside.** Foot traffic on wet turf quickly churns it up.
3. **Soaked paths.** The walk from the house, car or driveway.
4. **Drips and blowing rain.** Open sides let water in, and seams can leak.

## Five-part rain plan

### 1. Floor or mats

A subfloor keeps guests above the water and levels the ground. Grass mats or carpet help if a full floor is too much. See [tent flooring and subfloors](/blog/tent-flooring-and-subfloors).

### 2. Sidewalls on hand

Even a summer event should have sidewalls on the truck. Rain blows sideways, and walls make the difference between damp and dry. See [tent sidewalls](/blog/tent-sidewalls-clear-window-solid). If wind rises, walls come down; see [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety).

### 3. Drainage and paths

Walk the yard with your tent company the week before. Look at where water flows. Ask about gutters and downspouts that can be directed away from the tent, and consider a drainage trench, which your company may or may not do. Lay mats or pavers on key paths, and have umbrellas ready at the cars.

### 4. A second place for the extras

Where do guests hang coats? Where does the DJ plug in? Is the bathroom inside the house, with a wet-weather path? A covered walk from parking to the tent is worth the cost on a rainy day.

### 5. A decision date

Pick a time, such as 48 hours before, to check the forecast and decide on sidewalls, floors or an indoor fallback. Share it with your caterer, DJ and tent company.

## What changes in the layout

When it rains, guests crowd the tent and the entrance. Move the bar and food away from the door. Keep the walkways wide so wet guests do not get in each other's way. Place the DJ off the low side. Use our [tent layout guide](/blog/tent-layout-dance-floor-and-bar) as a starting point.

## Protect the lawn and the equipment

Wet turf is damaged by chairs, tables and foot traffic. Floors spread the weight. Cover linens and sound equipment, and keep electrical cords raised and covered. Outdoor power connections should be rated for wet conditions. See [tent lighting ideas](/blog/tent-lighting-ideas) and [tent and grass damage](/blog/tent-and-grass-damage).

## Tell your guests and vendors

A rain plan only works if everyone knows it. Send guests a short note two days ahead with parking, the dry path and a reminder to bring a jacket or shoes they can walk in on turf. Tell the caterer where the covered prep area is, tell the DJ and photographer which tent entrance is driest, and make sure the florist knows the arrangements may need to move away from open sides. A single page of contacts with your tent company, venue and each vendor on it saves a lot of phone calls when clouds roll in.

## Dealing with the forecast

Check the National Weather Service forecast for your ZIP code and a radar app. A forecast of scattered storms usually means an event can go ahead with a plan, while a severe thunderstorm warning means guests leave the tent. Lightning is not a reason to stay under a tent.

## Seating in the rain

Furniture on wet ground sinks. Chairs with wide feet stay steadier on mats or floors. [Garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) are practical, and moisture-tolerant [rattan garden chairs](/chairs/rattan-garden) fit a casual look. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a tent enough for a rainy event?

It keeps rain off the tables, but guests still face mud, puddles and wind-blown rain. A floor, sidewalls and dry paths make it work.

### Should I rent sidewalls for a summer tent?

Yes, or at least have them available. They are the difference between a dry party and a damp one when rain blows sideways.

### How do I keep the tent floor from flooding?

Choose a site that drains, use a raised floor, and ask the company about directing downspouts and runoff away from the tent.

### What if there is lightning?

Everyone leaves the tent for a sturdy building. A tent does not protect from lightning.

### Can I cancel a tent if it rains?

Check your contract, because many companies set up regardless of weather. Ask about cancellation and rescheduling terms before you sign.

### What should I tell my guests about rain?

Share the plan: parking, path, umbrellas, and where coats go. A short note a few days ahead helps.
