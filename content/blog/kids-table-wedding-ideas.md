**Quick answer**

Seat children ages 3 to 12 at a dedicated table of 6 to 8, using a 48-inch round or a 6-foot banquet table, with one adult helper for every 6 to 8 kids. Regular rental tables are 30 inches tall, so use booster seats or kid-sized chairs for the youngest. Place the table near parents but away from the bar, speakers and the dance floor's busiest edge.

</div>

A kids table can save a reception, or become the loudest corner of the room. The difference is planning: the right table, the right chairs, a menu kids will eat and an adult nearby. Chicago families often travel with children of all ages to weddings, bar mitzvahs and quinceañeras, so this is a layout question to settle early.

## Kids table sizes and seats

<div class="table-wrap">

| Table | Seats | Ages | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 36 in round | 4 | 3 to 6 | Very small group, close supervision |
| 48 in round | 6 | 4 to 12 | Best all-around kids table |
| 6 ft banquet | 6 to 8 | 6 to 12 | Easier for crafts and coloring |
| 72 in round | 8 to 10 | 8 to 14 | Teens only |

</div>

A 48-inch round is the sweet spot. It is small enough that an adult can reach every child, and children can see each other. A banquet table is better if you plan on coloring pages, because you can give each child a placemat.

## Chair heights and booster seats

A standard rental table is 30 inches tall. A toddler in a normal chair cannot reach the plate. Options:

- **Booster seats.** For ages 3 to 6. Ask the rental company if they supply them.
- **Standard chairs with a cushion.** Works for 6 to 10 year olds.
- **Regular chairs for 10 and up.** They sit like adults.
- **A lower table.** Some companies stock 24-inch kids tables and benches.

Check the chair too. A light [bentwood chair](/chairs/bentwood) is easier for small hands to move than a heavy throne, and a [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is simple for a casual party. Avoid chairs with a delicate finish at a kids table.

## How many adults per kids table

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Ages 3 to 5</strong> 1 adult per 3 to 4 children, seated with them or at the next table.</li>
<li><strong>Ages 6 to 9</strong> 1 adult per 6 to 8 children, nearby.</li>
<li><strong>Ages 10 to 14</strong> A parent or cousin within sight is enough.</li>
<li><strong>Plan a backup</strong> Name one teen or relative as the helper.</li>
</ol>

Parents should stay close. A kids table at the far end of the ballroom is a recipe for a spill and a lost sandal.

## What kids eat

Ask your caterer about a children's menu early. Chicken fingers, pasta, fruit and a small dessert are standard, and a plated adult meal is a waste for most kids. Cater to allergies and dietary needs by asking each family when you collect RSVPs. Serve kids first, so they are fed before the toasts.

## Activities that work

- Coloring pages and a small cup of crayons.
- A sticker sheet or small puzzle.
- A treat bag with bubbles or a small toy.
- A kid-friendly photo prop, such as a blank sign.

Skip anything with small pieces for children under 3, and anything with glitter or slime at a table with white linens. A paper tablecover that kids can draw on protects the cloth. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for a cover that fits your table.

## Where to put the table

Choose a spot that is visible but quiet. Keep it away from the bar, the DJ speaker, the dance floor entrance and exits. Parents should see it from their own table. If you have two or three tables of kids, keep them next to each other. For outdoor events, shade matters; see [outdoor table rentals](/blog/table-rental-for-outdoor-events-level-ground).

## Table setup and safety

- Use a floor-length cloth only if it does not trail on the floor. Kids trip on loose fabric. A lap-length cloth is safer.
- Skip tall centerpieces and candles. Use a low flower or a toy centerpiece instead.
- Use plastic or acrylic glassware where the venue allows. Ask about dinnerware options in your quote.
- Keep walkways clear, 4 to 5 feet around the table. See [table spacing and aisle clearance](/blog/table-spacing-and-aisle-clearance).

## A simple plan for a 100-guest wedding with 12 children

Say 12 children, ages 3 to 12, attend your wedding at a suburban banquet hall. Here is a plan that works.

1. Divide by age: 6 kids under 7 and 6 older.
2. Make two 48-inch round tables, one for each group, placed next to each other.
3. Assign one adult helper per table, such as an aunt or a teen cousin.
4. Plan kid meals to arrive right after salads, before the adults' entrees.
5. Prepare two activity bags, one per table, and a trash bag for crayons.
6. Seat the older group nearest the dance floor so they can drift over when music starts.

Tell parents ahead of time where the kids table is and who is helping. Many parents prefer their youngest children at their own table, and that is fine. A few spare chairs next to the kids tables let parents join for cake.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size table is best for a kids table?

A 48-inch round table seats 6 kids and is easy to supervise. Use a 6-foot banquet table if you want coloring space.

### How many kids fit at a table?

Six at a 48-inch round, 6 to 8 at a 6-foot banquet table and up to 8 at a 60-inch round.

### Do kids need special chairs?

Kids under 6 usually need boosters or a lower table because rental tables are 30 inches tall. Older kids can use standard chairs.

### Should the kids table be near the dance floor?

Near enough to join in, but not at the edge where dancers bump into them. Put it a table away.

### Do I need an adult at the kids table?

Yes for children under 8. One adult can handle 6 to 8 children. Name a teen or relative to help.

### Should kids sit with parents instead?

For ages 0 to 4, usually yes. For ages 5 and up, a kids table is more fun and lets parents relax.
