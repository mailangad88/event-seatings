That means a lot of its events are company parties, association dinners, fundraisers and big family celebrations, usually held in a ballroom that already has basic furniture. Rentals fill the gaps. This guide shows where upgraded rentals make the biggest difference and how to count them.

Ask your hotel or banquet hall for a written list of what is included. Typical items are standard banquet chairs, round tables and basic linens, but it varies. Then decide where an upgrade will be seen.

## Outdoor and park events

Schaumburg has a village events calendar, including its Septemberfest, and a park district that publishes a park rental application. If you plan an outdoor party on public land, such as a company picnic, check with the park district about reservations, and ask about tents, alcohol and amplified sound. Do not assume anything is allowed.

For a backyard in a nearby subdivision, ask your homeowners association whether tents are allowed. Our [tent size guide](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) lets you estimate the footprint, and the [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) covers permit questions you should ask any village.

## Plan around the season

Chicago's cold months last long, so winter events need coat space, and summer events need shade. A company holiday party in December means coat racks and a clear entry. ## Questions to ask the hotel or hall

1. How many chairs and tables are included, and how many are in good shape?
2. Can an outside rental company deliver, and when is load-in?
3. Is there a fee for outside vendors, and does the hall need insurance paperwork?
4. Who sets up and who takes down?
5. Where do rentals wait if the room is booked until 5 pm?

Ask for the answers in writing and send them with your [quote request](/quote), so we can match the delivery plan.

## Corporate details people forget

Company events have needs that weddings do not. Registration tables need a clean front, a floor-length cloth and a spot for name badges. A presentation or awards segment needs a head table or stage, a podium and a clear sight line from every seat, so avoid putting round tables directly beside the screen. Sponsor logos and signage need easels, so plan for a few. If speakers will use a microphone, put the sound system on the side of the room away from the kitchen doors.

Seating charts matter more than hosts expect. Assigning tables by department mixes teams less than assigning by number, so decide whether you want networking or familiarity. Print table numbers large enough to read from 20 feet. Hold two or three spare seats at the back for late additions, and keep the last row of chairs by the entrance flexible. If your event runs long, pace the evening in blocks: arrival and drinks, dinner, program, then dancing or mingling. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I bring my own chairs to a Schaumburg banquet hall?

Often yes, but ask first. Some halls charge a fee or require vendor insurance paperwork. Get their policy in writing before you order, and share it with your rental company.

### How many chairs do I need for a 150-person company party?

Order about 158 chairs, which is 150 guests plus a 5% spare buffer. Add extra chairs for any stage, registration or lounge areas that sit outside the dining count.

### Which chair works best for a corporate event?

Clean, modern shapes like the shell chair or the ghost chair are the most popular, because they suit logos and neutral rooms. A velvet or Louis chair adds a premium touch to head tables.

### What size round table seats 10?

A 72-inch round table seats 10 comfortably and 12 if you squeeze. A 60-inch round seats 8. See our [round table guide](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch) for spacing.

### Do I need a permit for a tent in Schaumburg?

Possibly. Rules depend on tent size and use, and the village sets them. Ask the Village of Schaumburg building or fire department before you reserve a tent.

### When should I book rentals for a December party?

As early as your date is firm, ideally by early fall. December weekends are the busiest period of the year for company events, and limited styles run out first.
