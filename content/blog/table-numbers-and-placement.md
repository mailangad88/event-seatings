**Quick answer**

Put table numbers at eye level, 12 to 18 inches above the tabletop, on a stand or holder at least 5 x 7 inches with numerals 3 inches or taller. Number tables in a clear path starting near the entrance or head table, and place the numbers at the center of each table. Add a seating chart near the door, and give the head table a name instead of a number.

</div>

Guests walk in holding a drink, reading an escort card and scanning a ballroom for a number. If they cannot find it in 10 seconds, they stop and ask. Good table numbers are a simple fix that keeps your reception moving.

## Size and placement

<div class="table-wrap">

| Item | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Table number | 5 x 7 in card, numbers 3+ in tall | Center of table, 12 to 18 in up |
| Seating chart | 24 x 36 in or larger | Near the door, at eye level |
| Escort card table | 6 ft banquet | Entrance |
| Head table sign | Name or word | Front of the table |
| Directional sign | 8 x 10 in | Corridors to the room |

</div>

Holders vary, from clear acrylic stands to wooden blocks to sticks in a vase. Pick one that is stable on a round tabletop. A tall number on a stem beats a flat card behind a centerpiece.

## Numbering order

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Start</strong> at the head table or the door.</li>
<li><strong>Go</strong> clockwise or in rows, never random.</li>
<li><strong>Keep</strong> related guests near each other, but numbers are a path.</li>
<li><strong>Skip</strong> unlucky numbers only if your guests care; it is a personal choice.</li>
<li><strong>Print</strong> a master list for the venue and caterer.</li>
</ol>

## Matching the numbers to the decor

- **Rustic:** wood slices or painted boards.
- **Modern:** acrylic with white numerals.
- **Classic:** gold or silver frames with printed numbers.
- **Vineyard:** metal stakes in a floral arrangement.

Pick a style that matches your table and chair choices. Our [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) fit wood numbers, while [gold infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity) suit gold frames.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Numbers that are lower than the centerpiece.
- Fonts that are too fancy to read.
- Odd numbering that makes guests walk in circles.
- Numbers on only one side of the table. Guests walking from different directions need to see one.
- Forgetting a spare number card or two.

## Seating chart signs

A large seating chart should be readable in 5 seconds. Alphabetize guests, use a clear font, and light the sign. Place it in an open area so a line can form. Two charts, one on each side, speed up a crowd of 150 or more.

## Creative alternatives to numbers

Numbers are clear, but plenty of Chicago couples choose another system. Whatever you pick, keep the same rules: easy to read, easy to see and easy to find on a map.

- **Names.** Use streets, neighborhoods or stations from the L, like Wicker Park or Hyde Park. Only use names that mean something to your guests.
- **Photos.** A photo of the couple at each age is charming but hard to read from far away. Pair it with a number.
- **Colors or flowers.** Needs a legend, so it is slower than a number.
- **Books, songs or movies.** Fun for themed events, but print the title big.

The more creative your names, the more important the seating chart. See [reception table names and seating maps](/blog/reception-table-names-and-seating-map).

## Day-of setup tips

Ask your venue or planner to place table numbers after the tablecloths and before the centerpieces so they sit at the same height on every table. Walk the room from the entrance and read each number from 20 feet. If a number is hidden by a centerpiece or a candle, raise it or move it. Keep a small kit with spare holders, tape and a marker for the morning of the event.

At a Chicago hotel ballroom, you may need to give your numbers to the banquet captain. Ask whether the venue sets them or you do.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big should table numbers be?

Use cards at least 5 x 7 inches with numerals 3 inches tall or more, so guests can read them from 10 feet away.

### Where do table numbers go?

On a stand in the center of the table, 12 to 18 inches above the tabletop, facing the entrance.

### Should I number the head table?

Most couples name it or label it Head Table. Number only the guest tables.

### What order should tables be numbered?

In a path, starting at the head table or the entrance. Avoid random numbers.

### Do I need a seating chart if I use escort cards?

Escort cards tell guests their table. A chart is a backup and helps guests find friends.

### How many table numbers do I need?

One per guest table. Order two extras.
