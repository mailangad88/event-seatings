**Quick answer**

Keep hot food at 135 degrees F or above and cold food at 41 degrees F or below. Don't leave perishable food out more than 2 hours, or 1 hour when the air is above 90 degrees F. Cook poultry to 165 degrees F and ground beef to 160 degrees F, use a probe thermometer, separate raw and cooked foods and give guests a place to wash hands.

</div>

Food safety sounds like a caterer's job, but when you host at home or in a backyard, you are the caterer. Most food-safety problems at parties come from time and temperature, not from fancy mistakes. Here are the numbers that matter and the gear that makes them easy, with tips for Chicago-area parties.

Bacteria grow fastest between 41 and 135 degrees F. The 2-hour and 1-hour limits are standard guidance from food-safety agencies. They add up: if a dish was out for 40 minutes at lunch and again for 90 minutes at dinner, it has had 2 hours 10 minutes.

## Key temperatures

<div class="table-wrap">

| Food | Safe internal temperature |
|---|---|
| Poultry, stuffing, casseroles | 165 F |
| Ground beef, pork, lamb | 160 F |
| Steaks, roasts, chops | 145 F with 3-minute rest |
| Fish | 145 F |
| Reheated leftovers | 165 F |
| Hot-holding | 135 F or above |
| Cold-holding | 41 F or below |

</div>

Temperatures follow USDA and FDA guidance, so check the latest from those agencies if you cook unusual items. An inexpensive probe thermometer is the single best safety tool. Take temperatures in the thickest part.

## The six-rule checklist

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Clean:</strong> wash hands and surfaces for 20 seconds before and during prep.</li>
<li><strong>Separate:</strong> raw meat, cooked food and produce need their own boards and tongs.</li>
<li><strong>Cook:</strong> use a thermometer, not color.</li>
<li><strong>Hold hot:</strong> chafers, slow cookers and warming oven at 135 F or above.</li>
<li><strong>Hold cold:</strong> coolers, ice and bowls over ice at 41 F or below.</li>
<li><strong>Clock:</strong> note when food went out and discard after 2 hours.</li>
</ol>

## Higher-risk foods

Cut melon, rice, cooked pasta, potato salad, deli salads, dairy desserts, eggs, seafood and anything with mayonnaise-based sauces need temperature care. If you can't keep them cold, choose a vinaigrette-based salad instead. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F once, then discard.

## Allergies

Food allergies matter. The major allergens in the US include milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy and sesame. Label dishes, use separate utensils and ask guests about allergies on the RSVP. Never guess about an ingredient.

## When to hire a pro

A home party for 100 guests with five hot dishes is close to a catering job. If you don't have the fridge space, ovens and time, hire a licensed caterer. In Illinois, food businesses are regulated by local health departments, and the rules for selling food differ from the rules for a private party. Check with your county or city health department. Cook County, for example, has its own department, and the City of Chicago has its own. See [working with a caterer and rental company](/blog/working-with-caterer-and-rental-company).

## Chicago notes

Summer heat above 90 degrees F is common in July and August. Move the clock to 1 hour on those days. In winter, a garage can serve as a walk-in cooler, but only if you check the temperature, since a sunny day can warm it. Don't leave food in a car trunk.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long can food sit out at a party?

Two hours at normal room temperature, or one hour if the air is above 90 degrees F. After that, refrigerate or discard it.

### What temperature should hot food be at a buffet?

135 degrees F or above. Use chafers, slow cookers or a warming oven, and check with a thermometer.

### What temperature should cold food be?

41 degrees F or below. Nest bowls in ice and refresh the ice often.

### Is it OK to leave food out for 4 hours?

No for most perishable foods. Food held hot or cold for long times under controlled conditions is a different case, but time out of temperature counts toward the 2-hour limit.

### Can I reheat leftovers?

Yes, once, to 165 degrees F. Do not reheat the same food repeatedly.

### Do I need a permit to cater my own party?

Not for a private party at your home, but if you sell food or serve it at a public event, check with your health department.

### Who is the final authority?

Your county or city health department and the USDA and FDA guidance. This article is a planning guide, not legal advice.
