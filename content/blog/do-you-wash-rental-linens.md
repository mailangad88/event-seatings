**Quick answer**

No. In most cases you should not wash rental linens before returning them. Rental companies launder every linen with industrial equipment and specific methods, and home washing can set stains or damage fabric. Shake out crumbs, separate wet and dry items, keep candle wax and food off, and bag linens as the supplier tells you. Always follow your own rental agreement, since policies differ.

</div>

After a Saturday night wedding, nobody wants to launder 20 tablecloths. The good news is you almost certainly do not have to. The bad news is that a few common habits, such as stuffing wet linens in a pile or using stain remover, can cost you. Here is how the process normally works and how to handle your return.

## What suppliers normally do

Rental linen is cleaned after each event by the supplier. That means:

- Linens are inspected and sorted by color and fabric
- Stains are pre-treated with their own products
- Washing uses commercial machines and controlled temperature
- Pressing and folding happen before the next booking

Home washing with household detergent and bleach can leave residue, change colors and shrink fabrics. Many agreements say outright: do not wash.

## What to do instead

### Shake out food and trash

Take linens off tables carefully. Shake out crumbs, napkins and confetti. Remove candle wax with a dull edge only if your supplier allows it; otherwise leave it and note it.

### Keep wet and dry separate

Damp linens sealed in bags can grow mildew within a day. If something is wet, spread it out to dry or tell the supplier so they can process it quickly. Never bag soaking linens with dry ones.

### Do not treat stains

Spot cleaners, bleach and hot water can set a stain permanently or change the color. Instead, note what the stain is (red wine, oil, wax, makeup) and where it is, and tell the supplier. Some will ask for the details because the treatment differs by stain.

### Count what you return

Count cloths and napkins by size and color, and compare with your order. Missing items are a common reason for added charges. Take photos of piles before sealing the bags.

### Follow the return method

Most suppliers either pick up after the event or ask for drop-off the next business day. Confirm the time, who is present, and what the venue expects.

## Who handles it on the night

Venue and caterer staff may take linens off tables, and not all are careful. Check who will:

- Strip the tables
- Separate food-soiled linens
- Hand them to the rental company

Add this to your event day timeline. Our [linen ordering checklist](/blog/linen-ordering-checklist) has a section for pickup.

## Chicago note: tight venues and late exits

City venues and hotels sometimes allow only a short window for load-out, and some suburban halls require vendors to remove items by morning. Plan who bags linens at the end of the night. If you are packing after midnight, a simple sorted pile is better than a rushed knot. For other venue rules see [Chicago venue rules for chair rentals](/blog/topic/chicago-planning).

## What a good return looks like

A good return is boring. The supplier arrives or you drop off at the agreed time, the count matches the order, and the linens are sorted into bags by type. There is a short note about any stains or damage, and a photo or two from the night. No one argues about what was delivered because both sides counted at the start.

If you have a large order, a packing list helps. Make a simple table before the event: item, size, color, quantity delivered. After the event, fill in the quantity returned and any notes. It takes five minutes and prevents most billing disputes. For events that end late, hand the list to the person staying last at the venue, usually the planner, the venue coordinator or the caterer's lead.

## If something goes wrong

Suppose a tablecloth gets a large red wine stain or a candle burns a hole in a runner. Do not try to fix it. Take a photo, note which table, and tell the supplier when you return the linens. Most companies would rather know early than discover it during inspection. If a vendor, such as a caterer or a bartender, caused the problem, mention it, since some vendors carry their own insurance. Staying calm and being clear usually gets better results than hiding the problem.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to wash rental tablecloths?

Not normally. Rental companies launder after every event with commercial equipment. Check your agreement, but most say not to wash, because home methods can damage or set stains.

### What if a tablecloth has red wine on it?

Leave it, note the stain and let the supplier treat it. Household stain removers or hot water can set it permanently. Mention the stain when you return the linens.

### Can I return linens wet?

Avoid it. Damp linens bagged together can mildew. Spread wet items out to dry or tell the supplier right away.

### Who is responsible for stains on rental linens?

It depends on your rental agreement. Many suppliers include normal wear and spills but charge for permanent damage like burns or heavy stains. Read the policy beforehand, and see our [guide to linen policies](/blog/linen-stain-and-damage-policies).

### Should I iron rental linens?

Only if you were told to. Suppliers typically deliver pressed or ready-to-use linens, and your venue or caterer may steam them at setup. Ask who handles wrinkles.

### What if I lose a napkin?

Report it. Missing items are usually charged at a replacement rate listed in your contract.
