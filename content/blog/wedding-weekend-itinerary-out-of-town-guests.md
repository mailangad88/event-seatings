Guests who travel are giving you time, money and vacation days. What they want in return is clarity: where to stay, how to get around, what to wear and what to do between events. A good itinerary answers those questions before they are asked and saves you dozens of texts.

## What a good itinerary includes

Start with the core: every event, with date, time, address and dress code. Add parking or transit notes, shuttle times if offered, and who to contact with questions. Mark each event as required or optional. For the wedding day, include the ceremony time, when to arrive and the cocktail hour start.

Add the little things that help travelers: weather expectations, whether the ceremony is outdoors, where to find a pharmacy or grocery store near the hotel, and a suggestion for casual dining on free evenings. Keep everything on the wedding website and also in a printed insert in welcome bags. See [event guest welcome bags and hotel logistics](/blog/event-guest-welcome-bags-and-hotel-logistics) for ideas.

## Structuring the weekend

A three-day structure is common. Friday: guests arrive, a casual welcome party or open house in the evening. Saturday: a relaxed morning with optional activities, the wedding in the late afternoon or evening. Sunday: a farewell brunch. If you have a rehearsal dinner, it usually happens Friday evening with a smaller group. See [wedding welcome party planning](/blog/wedding-welcome-party-planning) and [day after wedding brunch rentals](/blog/day-after-wedding-brunch-rentals).

Leave free time. Guests want to see the city, rest or visit family. Offer a few optional suggestions rather than scheduling everything. A short list of local ideas works well.

## Transportation and lodging

Tell guests how far the hotel is from the venue and how to get there. Offer a shuttle if the venue is far from lodging or if parking is limited. Many couples reserve a hotel room block; see [hotel room blocks for wedding guests](/blog/hotel-room-blocks-for-wedding-guests). Post the block deadline and booking code clearly. If guests are staying in rentals or with family, give them parking and rideshare tips.

For older guests or those with mobility needs, mention accessible entrances, drop-off points and any stairs. Coordinate with the venue about accessible routes.

## Make it easy to use

Put the itinerary on the wedding website, in the email you send after the RSVP and in the welcome bag. Use a clear format with a table of events and short notes. Include a rain plan for outdoor activities. Update it if plans change, and send a short message to everyone. One person should be the contact, ideally a friend or family member, not the couple.

## Helping guests with special needs

Some guests need extra planning, and a short note on the itinerary goes a long way. Mention accessible entrances and parking, seating options at long events, and quiet spaces for guests who need a break. If you invite children, say which events are family-friendly and which are adults only, and suggest local babysitting services if you know of any.

For guests with dietary needs, note that the caterer has their information from the RSVP. For guests who do not drive, list transit and rideshare options. A final paragraph with a phone number for questions helps older guests who may not check the website. These small details show care and reduce stress for everyone involved.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago guests may arrive via O'Hare or Midway, and transit connects both airports to the city. If your venue is in the suburbs, check how far it is from the airport and whether rideshares are easy. Weather can change travel plans in winter, so recommend guests arrive a day early if your wedding is between December and March. Summer weekends often have festivals and sports events that affect traffic and hotels, so give extra time. Note whether your venue is accessible by transit or requires a car, and where guests can park. If you can, ask a local friend or relative to be the point person for questions during the weekend, and put their phone number on the printed itinerary. Travelers often want small answers fast, like where to get coffee or whether the shuttle is running, and a friendly local contact keeps those questions away from the couple.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I include in a wedding weekend itinerary?

List every event with the date, time, address, dress code and whether it is optional. Add transportation, parking, lodging information and a contact for questions.

### How do I help out-of-town wedding guests?

Provide clear travel and lodging information, a room block, transportation options and a simple itinerary. A welcome bag with a schedule and local tips is a nice touch.

### Do I need to plan activities for out-of-town guests?

Not necessarily. Offer a few optional suggestions and leave free time. Guests often want to see the city or rest.

### When should I send the weekend schedule?

At least a month before the wedding, on your website and by email. Include it in welcome bags too.

### Should I organize transportation for guests?

If the venue is far from lodging, parking is limited or guests will be drinking, a shuttle is helpful. Otherwise provide rideshare and transit tips.

### What should be in a wedding welcome bag?

Common items are a schedule, a map, water and snacks, and a note. See our [welcome bags guide](/blog/event-guest-welcome-bags-and-hotel-logistics).

### How do I handle guests who arrive early or stay late?

Share your plans, but do not feel obligated to host extra events. Offer suggestions for local activities and a contact for questions.
