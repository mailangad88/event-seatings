**Quick answer**

Rental glassware usually arrives clean in plastic racks that hold 16 to 25 glasses each, and goes back in the same racks, often unwashed. Keep glasses in their racks, never stack racks more than the rental company allows, count the racks at delivery and return, and ask in writing whether you must rinse, whether broken glasses are charged and whether unused racks may go back unopened.

</div>

Glassware is the item most likely to generate a surprise charge on a rental bill. It breaks, it goes missing and it comes back sticky. A little logistics before the event avoids all three. These are common practices; your rental company's terms are what apply, so read them first.

## How the rack system works

Rental glassware ships in plastic racks with dividers. A rack is a lightweight crate, typically about 20 inches square, divided into compartments. It protects glasses in transit and goes straight into a commercial dishwasher at the rental company. Glasses are counted by the rack, so a 100-guest order might be 20 or more racks.

## At delivery

1. **Count racks, not just glasses.** Compare the delivery slip to the order and sign only after counting.
2. **Check for chips and cracks** in a few glasses per rack. Photograph any damage right away.
3. **Store racks flat and level,** in a dry, shaded area. Never leave them in a hot car or in direct sun.
4. **Do not stack beyond the rental company's limit.** Racks are designed to stack a set number high, so ask.
5. **Keep the paperwork** where your event manager can find it.

## During the event

- Use clean glasses only from racks marked "clean." Mixing clean and used is how a guest gets a sticky glass.
- Have a staffer collect used glasses into empty racks, not trash bins. This also stops glasses being thrown away.
- Place glass drop stations near the exit of the cocktail area and the dance floor.
- If glasses break, sweep and bag them separately from other trash. Don't put broken glass in a rack.

If you are working with a caterer, ask who is responsible for racking glasses at the end of the night and whether it is in the contract. See [dry hire vs full service](/blog/dry-hire-vs-full-service-bar) for how staffing changes who does this.

## Returns: rinse or not?

Most professional rental companies prefer glasses come back unwashed but scraped of garbage and straws, since they run them through industrial machines. Some charge a cleaning fee if there are cigarette butts, food, wax or sticky residue. Ask these questions:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Do glasses return rinsed or unrinsed? | Avoids cleaning fees |
| Are unopened racks returned without charge? | Saves money on over-ordering |
| What is the replacement fee per broken glass? | Sets the breakage budget |
| When is pickup, and what window? | Avoids storing racks overnight |
| How are missing glasses counted? | Avoids disputes |

</div>

## Breakage and loss

Glassware is a high-turnover item, so some breakage is normal. Rental companies typically price in a small loss or charge replacement fees; ask for the schedule. Reduce loss by:

- Ordering 10 percent extra. See [how many glasses per guest](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest).
- Choosing sturdier styles for crowds, such as stemless wine glasses. See [glassware types](/blog/wedding-glassware-types-explained).
- Using plastic or compostable cups near the pool, dance floor or at an outdoor event. See [plastic vs glass outdoors](/blog/plastic-vs-glass-outdoor-events-parks).
- Telling guests where to put used glasses with a sign.

## Chicago logistics

City venues often limit loading times and elevator use, and high-rises may require a certificate of insurance from vendors. Plan delivery and pickup windows around those rules, and ask how a rental company handles a walk-up delivery to a third-floor loft. In winter, bring racks indoors promptly rather than leaving them in a truck or garage. Suburban barns and tents usually have easier access but less storage; request a covered, level space for racks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to wash rental glasses?

Usually not. Most rental companies prefer unwashed glasses returned in their racks, but confirm, since some require a rinse of food or wax. Cleaning fees apply for residue.

### How many glasses are in a rack?

Racks commonly hold 16 to 25 glasses, depending on the glass size. Check your rental company's rack size and count.

### What happens if a glass breaks?

You are typically charged a replacement fee for each broken or missing glass. Ask for the fee schedule and plan a small buffer.

### Can I return unused glassware?

Often yes, if the racks are unopened and clean. Ask about the policy before you order extras.

### Where should I store racks before the event?

Indoors, level, dry and out of direct sun. Keep them off the ground in a cool area, and keep clean and used racks separate.

### Who collects glasses during the party?

A bus person, server or volunteer. Without one, glasses pile up on tables and get thrown out. Ask your caterer or bar company who will do it.
