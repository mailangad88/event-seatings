**Quick answer**

Return rentals in the condition your contract describes: chairs and tables stacked or staged and free of heavy dirt, linens bagged without being washed, and dishes scraped but usually not washed. Rules differ by company, so ask what you need to do before the event. Staging items neatly and counting them with the crew prevents most return disputes.

</div>

The party is over, the room is a mess, and the pickup crew arrives in the morning. This is where small surprises, like a missing chair, stained linen or sticky glasses, turn into line items. What the company expects at return is written into your contract, and it is usually simpler than people fear. The key is to know it before the event, not after.

## Chairs and tables

Stack or stage items where the crew can reach them. Wipe heavy spills if you can, but you generally do not need to scrub. Mud, paint, wax, glitter and adhesive are another matter; keep them away from furniture. If chairs were on grass or in rain, see [outdoor event chairs and weather](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues) for how to handle them.

Also remove anything attached: ribbons, signs, floral wire and tape. Residue on a chair finish can lead to a charge.

## Linens

Many companies ask you **not to wash** linens, because they have a specific process. Shake out food, bag dry and keep wet linens separate from dry ones. Do not use bleach. Point out any stain or burn to the crew. Candle wax and some dyes can be hard to remove, which is why companies ask for notice.

## Dishes, flatware and glassware

Companies differ. Many want food scraped off but do not want dishes washed. Some require flatware in a soak bin. Glassware is typically returned empty in its original rack. Ask for the instructions in advance. The caterer may handle this, so confirm who is responsible. [Working with a caterer](/blog/one-call-planning-vs-many-vendors) is a good place to coordinate it.

## The return count

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Stage everything</strong> in one area, sorted by type.</li>
<li><strong>Count it</strong> against your delivery sheet.</li>
<li><strong>Walk the venue</strong> for strays: chairs on a patio, linens in a restroom.</li>
<li><strong>Photograph</strong> any damage that happened or the staged items.</li>
<li><strong>Count with the crew</strong> at pickup and get their sign-off.</li>
</ol>

Strays account for many "missing" items. A chair moved to the cocktail area and forgotten, or tablecloths in a coat room, can disappear from your count. The count at pickup is where it counts.

## Common charges, and how to avoid them

<div class="table-wrap">

| Charge | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Missing item | Not found at pickup | Walk the venue; count at delivery and return |
| Stained linen | Wax, wine, dye | Warn the crew; use protection |
| Damaged chair | Impact or misuse | Brief staff; do not overload |
| Excess cleaning | Heavy dirt or sticky residue | Wipe, scrape, bag as instructed |
| Late return (will-call) | Missed window | Confirm hours |

## Who is responsible if the caterer, DJ or guests cause damage?

Typically you are, as the renter, unless the contract says otherwise. That is why briefing your vendors and staff matters. Tell your caterer and bartender how to handle rented items, and let the venue know where rented items are staged.

## Questions to ask before the event

- What condition do items need to be in when picked up?
- Do you want dishes scraped, rinsed or racked?
- Should linens be bagged dry?
- How does the count work at pickup? Can I be present?
- What are the replacement charges for lost or damaged items?

These pair well with the [questions to ask a rental company](/blog/questions-to-ask-an-event-rental-company).

## Chicago winter note

In cold months, slush and road salt come into venues on shoes and carts. Salt can mark floors and stain fabric, so lay mats at entrances and keep linens off wet areas. Items left in a cold truck or unheated space overnight may need extra drying time, so tell the crew if anything got wet.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to wash rental dishes?

Usually not. Most companies want food scraped off and dishes racked, but not washed. Ask your company for its instructions before the event.

### Should I wash rental linens?

Generally no. Shake out food, bag them dry and let the company clean them. Washing or bleaching can make stains harder to remove or damage the fabric.

### What happens if a rental item is missing?

You may be charged a replacement fee according to your contract. Check the venue for strays and count items at pickup to avoid this.

### Do I have to be there for pickup?

It is best to be, or to have someone present who can count the items and sign off. If not, ask how the company handles counts.

### Can I leave chairs where they are after the event?

Only if the company agrees. Most want chairs staged and accessible. Leaving them scattered can slow pickup and cost you.

### Will I be charged for normal wear?

Normal wear is generally expected, but terms differ. Read your contract and ask what counts as damage versus ordinary use.
