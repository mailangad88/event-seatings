**Quick answer**

A four hour party works well as: one hour before for setup, 30 minutes of arrivals and drinks, an hour of food, 30 minutes of activity or toasts, 20 minutes for cake, then open mingling until the end, with 30 to 60 minutes after for cleanup. Put the key moments on a one-page timeline, name an owner for each, and share it with helpers and vendors.

</div>

Parties drift when no one owns the clock. Food comes out late, the cake is cut when half the guests have left and the toast happens while people are in line for the bar. A simple timeline keeps the energy up without making the party feel scheduled. Here is a four hour template that works for birthdays, graduations, showers and family celebrations, with notes on how to adjust it.

## Before the party: the hour of setup

Start setup at least an hour before the first guest. Rentals should be delivered earlier, so you have time to adjust tables and chairs. Set out food, drinks, music and decor, and check the bathrooms and trash. Put your helper list and the timeline where they can see it. If you have a tent or outdoor space, check the weather and the setup. See [event rental delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect).

## 0:00 to 0:30: Arrivals and drinks

Open the doors with music, drinks and a snack. Guests arrive in a trickle, so give them something to do right away: a sign-in or guest book, a photo spot or a drink station. A host or greeter should stand near the door to say hello and point to coats, drinks and seating. See [card box and gift table ideas](/blog/card-box-and-gift-table-ideas).

## 0:30 to 1:30: Food

Open the food line when most guests have arrived. Call tables or groups by name if the crowd is large. A buffet for 50 guests takes about 30 to 45 minutes if you have two lines, so read [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed). Keep drinks flowing and refill ice. Do not schedule a toast during the line.

## 1:30 to 2:15: Activity, toast or program

This is the one structured moment. Choose a toast, a short slideshow, a game or a speech. Keep it to 15 to 20 minutes. Use a microphone if the crowd is over 30, and see [microphone types for events](/blog/microphone-types-for-events). Games for kids can run in a separate zone.

## 2:15 to 2:45: Cake and photos

Cut the cake while most guests are still there. Gather everyone for a group photo, using your [photography shot list](/blog/event-photography-shot-list). Serve dessert while people mingle.

## 2:45 to 4:00: Mingle and wind down

Keep music on, refresh drinks and offer coffee or water. As the end approaches, start sending leftovers home and thank guests at the door. Give an announcement 15 minutes before the end.

## After: cleanup

Allow 30 to 60 minutes for teardown. See the [event cleanup plan](/blog/event-cleanup-plan). Keep rental pickups in mind.

## Adjusting the plan

For a kids party, shorten the food and activity blocks and cut the party to two or three hours. For a dinner party, move food earlier and let the evening run longer. For an open house, skip the structured moments and plan a short toast or cake cut in the middle of the party.

Adjust every time to match your event. Put names beside every row, and make sure each person has a copy. For other event types, read [event planning timeline by event type](/blog/event-planning-timeline-by-event-type).

<li><strong>Assign names.</strong> One person per row.</li>

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area weather can change your timeline. In summer, afternoon storms often pass quickly, so keep a backup spot ready and shift food earlier if skies look unsettled. Evenings cool off near the lake, and winter daylight ends early, so plan the toast and photos before dark. If you rely on a park, check the permit hours with your park district, since your setup and cleanup may have to fit within them. Traffic can delay deliveries, so plan an extra buffer on busy roads.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a party last?

Three to four hours is common for adult parties and graduations, and two hours for young kids. A longer party needs more food and a clear end time. Shorter is often better.

### What is a good timeline for a party?

Plan an hour for setup, 30 minutes for arrivals, an hour for food, 20 minutes for a toast or activity, 20 minutes for cake, and the rest for mingling. Add cleanup time after.

### When should I serve food at a party?

Open food about 30 minutes after the start so most guests have arrived. Serve earlier for kids. Keep the line short with multiple tables.

### When should I cut the cake?

Cut it about two hours in, while most guests are still there. Gather everyone for a photo, then serve dessert as people mingle. Do not wait until the last 15 minutes.

### How do I keep a party from dragging?

Plan one structured moment, keep music and drinks fresh and announce the end time. Avoid long gaps between food and cake.

### How early should I start setting up?

At least an hour before guests arrive, and earlier if you have rentals or a tent. Have deliveries scheduled well before that. Leave time to fix problems.

### Should I share the timeline with guests?

Not the whole timeline, but share the start time, end time and any special moments, such as a toast, so guests plan to be there.
