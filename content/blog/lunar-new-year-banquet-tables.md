**Quick answer**

A Lunar New Year banquet works best on round tables of 10 with a turntable (lazy Susan) in the center, so shared dishes travel around the table. A 72-inch round seats 10 comfortably and a 60-inch round seats 8. Plan one table per 10 guests, a head or family table, a red envelope or greeting table and red, gold or neutral linens. Lunar New Year is celebrated by many communities in the Chicago area, including Chinese, Vietnamese, Korean and others, each with its own customs. Family reunion dinners, office parties and community banquets all share one need: round tables where everyone can reach shared dishes. This guide covers the layout, the counts and the small items that make the dinner work.

## Why round tables of 10

Shared dishes are the heart of the meal. A round table means everyone sits at the same distance from the center, and a turntable lets guests rotate dishes without passing. A 72-inch round seats 10 with room for plates, bowls, teacups and the center dishes. A 60-inch round seats 8 and feels more intimate. If the table is too small, the dishes crowd the edges and the turntable cannot spin.

## Color, linens and chairs

Red and gold are common for the holiday, but you do not have to use both. Choose the color scheme with your family. A 72-inch round with a floor-length cloth needs a 132-inch round cloth (see the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart)). Chairs should be comfortable for a long dinner. [Gold Infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity) (estimated $9 to $13) suit banquet halls and glow under warm lights, and [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) ($10 to $14) keep red tables the focus. Many halls provide basic chairs, so rent upgrades only where you want them.

## What to put on the table

Plan for more than plates:

- A **turntable** on every table of 8 or more, if the venue does not provide them.
- **Bowls, teacups and small plates** in addition to dinner plates. See our [china rental guide](/blog/china-rental-guide-what-pieces-exist).
- **Serving spoons and chopsticks** for shared dishes.
- **Napkins**, which are often used heavily. See [how many napkins per guest](/blog/how-many-napkins-per-guest).
- **Tea service** at the table or at a station.

## Red envelope and greeting tables

Many gatherings include a red envelope exchange or a greeting table at the entrance. A 6-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth, a basket and a sign is enough. Place it away from the main door so guests do not block the entrance. For sign ideas, see [signage and easels](/blog/signage-and-easels-for-events).

## Chicago notes

- Chinatown and other Chicago-area communities host public Lunar New Year events. If you plan a private banquet near a busy date, check parking and street closures well before the day.
- Winter weather affects coat storage. Plan a rack or coat check. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).
- Many restaurants and banquet halls book out for the holiday. Reserve the room first, then order rentals to fill the gap.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people sit at a round banquet table?

Ten people sit comfortably at a 72-inch round, and eight sit comfortably at a 60-inch round. The table also needs space for a turntable and shared dishes, so avoid squeezing more in.

### Do I need a lazy Susan?

For shared-dish dinners, yes. A 24-inch turntable lets everyone reach each dish without passing. Many Chinese banquet halls provide them, so ask before you rent.

### What size is a good turntable?

Most are 24 to 30 inches across. A 24-inch fits a 60-inch round, and a 30-inch fits a 72-inch round with room for place settings. Confirm with your venue.

### What colors should I choose?

Red and gold are popular, but it depends on your family. Neutral linens with red accents work well and let you reuse the setup for other occasions.

### How early should I book?

Book your venue first, then rentals. Dates move each year and the weekends around the holiday are in demand. Read our [booking timeline](/blog/topic/budget-and-booking).

Yes. Offices often rent round tables and chairs for an in-house banquet. Give us the headcount and space and we will help you plan.
