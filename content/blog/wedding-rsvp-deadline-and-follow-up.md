**Quick answer**

Set your RSVP deadline three to four weeks before the wedding, and ask for responses by mail, a website or email. Send a friendly reminder the day after the deadline, call or text anyone still silent within a week, and give your caterer a final count about two weeks before the date. Keep a single tracker so you always know who has answered.

</div>

The RSVP deadline is the date when your wedding becomes real. Until then, every number is a guess: how many plates, how many chairs, how many cocktail glasses. Late replies are common, and chasing them can feel awkward, but a simple system makes the process easy and polite. The goal is a final count you can trust.

## Choosing the deadline

Work backward from your caterer's deadline. If the caterer needs a final headcount two weeks before the wedding, set your RSVP deadline at least a week earlier, which gives you time to chase late replies. For a wedding with out-of-town guests, many couples choose four weeks. Put the deadline on the invitation, on the response card or form and on your wedding website, and say it plainly: please reply by October 1.

If you send digital invitations, an online RSVP form saves time because answers land in a list automatically. For paper cards, number the backs and keep the address list nearby, so you can identify cards with missing names. Our [guide to timing invitations](/blog/when-to-send-save-the-dates-and-invitations) shows how this fits into the mailing schedule.

## The follow-up sequence that works

Most late responders are not refusing. They forgot, are waiting on someone else or lost the invitation. So make follow-up warm and low-pressure. The day after the deadline, send a group email or text to everyone who has not replied: we are finalizing our count and would love to hear from you by Friday. A few days later, send a personal message to each remaining guest. Within a week, call close family and friends.

Keep a script handy: Hi Aunt Lou, we are finalizing the guest list and want to make sure we have a seat for you. Can you let us know if you can make it? Do not be afraid to ask directly about plus-ones and kids. Clear questions get clear answers.

## From responses to final count

Once the deadline passes, count yes responses, add vendors who eat, such as your photographer and band if the contract includes meals, and add a small buffer if your caterer allows it. Compare the number to your rental order. Chairs, tables, linens, plates and glasses all depend on the count. See [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [wedding rentals by guest count](/blog/wedding-reception-rentals-by-guest-count).

Only after the count is final should you build the seating chart. Our [wedding seating chart guide](/blog/wedding-seating-chart-how-to) explains how to group tables and avoid awkward pairings.

## Handling the awkward cases

Some guests will RSVP for people you did not invite, such as an extra date or children. Respond kindly and clearly, thanking them for their reply and explaining that the invitation was addressed to specific people. Others will say yes and then cancel, which is common and usually unavoidable. Keep a short waiting list so you can fill an empty seat if a spot opens, and let your caterer know about changes within the allowed window.

If someone never responds, decide whether to count them as a no. Many couples count non-responders as a maybe until the final call, then as a no after a last text. Do not leave seats unassigned for people who have not responded.

## A simple RSVP tracking sheet

Build a sheet with one row per invited household. Columns like these keep you organized.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Column | What to record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Invited names | Everyone on the invitation | Prevents surprise extra guests |
| Reply status | Yes, no or no reply | Drives your reminders |
| Guest count | Number attending | Final headcount |
| Meal choice | Entree and dietary needs | Caterer instructions |
| Date contacted | When you reminded them | Avoids double-nagging |
| Table number | After the final count | Seating chart |

</div>

Add a column for kids and a column for notes about mobility or seating needs, since those matter for chairs and layout.

## Your RSVP checklist

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Set the deadline.</strong> At least a week before the caterer's deadline.</li>
<li><strong>Make replying easy.</strong> Online form, reply card or both.</li>

<li><strong>Reach out personally.</strong> Text close friends, call close family.</li>
<li><strong>Lock the count.</strong> Share it with caterer, venue and rental company.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area weddings often include guests from several suburbs and other states, so RSVPs can arrive in waves. Plan for slower responses from older relatives who prefer paper and from groups that need to coordinate travel. If your wedding is in winter, ask guests to confirm early because weather and flights can change plans. Some Chicago venues and caterers set their own final-count dates and fees for changes, so confirm the deadline in your contract rather than assuming. Rental companies typically need final counts before delivery routes are set, so ask your provider when changes are still easy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good RSVP deadline for a wedding?

Three to four weeks before the wedding is common. That leaves time for reminders and still meets most caterers' final-count deadlines, which are often about two weeks out.

### What do I do when guests do not RSVP?

Send a friendly group reminder the day after the deadline, then message or call each person individually within a week. Most late responders simply forgot.

### Can I send a wedding RSVP reminder?

Yes, and most guests appreciate it. Keep the message short and warm, and mention the date you need the answer by.

### What if someone RSVPs after the deadline?

Accept them if you have room and your caterer allows changes. If you do not, thank them and explain that you had to finalize the count. A short waiting list helps you plan.

### Should I ask for meal choices with the RSVP?

If you serve a plated dinner, yes. Collect entree choices and dietary restrictions on the response card or form so your caterer has them from the start.

### How do I handle guests who bring uninvited plus-ones?

Reply kindly and clearly that the invitation was for the named guests only. Most people understand, especially if you have a consistent rule.

### What is the difference between a deadline and a final count?

The RSVP deadline is the guest's date to reply. The final count is the number you give your caterer and vendors after you follow up, add vendor meals and settle changes.
