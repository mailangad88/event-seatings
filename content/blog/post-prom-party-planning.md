**Quick answer**

Post-prom parties work best when run by a parent committee or school group. Start four to six months ahead, set a budget, secure a safe supervised venue such as the school or a park district facility, plan activities and food that run from about 11 p.m. to 4 a.m., recruit plenty of adult volunteers, and line up donated prizes to give students a reason to stay.

</div>

A post-prom party exists for one reason: to give students a safe, fun place to be after the dance. That is a big job. It needs a venue, adult supervision, food, activities, prizes and a way to keep students inside until the party ends. The planning team is usually a handful of parents with a few months and a modest budget, so organization matters.

## Start with a committee and a goal

Form a committee of six to ten parents and a school staff liaison. Set a clear goal: a safe, drug-free and alcohol-free celebration with activities students want. Divide roles: chair, treasurer, venue, food, activities, prizes and donations, volunteers, decor and marketing. Meet monthly, then weekly in the final month. Check with school administration about policies, insurance and approval.

## Choose a venue

A school gym, cafeteria or a park district recreation center is the most affordable option. Bowling alleys, trampoline parks, arcades and movie theaters are popular outside venues that students enjoy. Check whether the venue allows overnight stays or after-midnight events, and ask about group rates and insurance. Make sure the space has enough room for activities and food, and that restrooms are accessible.

## Set a budget

Typical costs include the venue, food, activities, prizes, decor, rentals, security or staffing, insurance and printing. Funding often comes from parent donations, local business sponsors, fundraisers and ticket sales. Local businesses often donate gift cards and prizes. Set a per-student goal and track it with a simple spreadsheet. Add a cushion for surprises.

## Plan activities

Students come tired after the dance, so offer a mix of active and low-key options.

**Active:** inflatables, sports, dance, laser tag, trampoline, bowling and mini golf.

**Low-key:** movies, video games, board games, card games, photo booth and crafts.

**Experiences:** hypnotist, magician, DJ, caricature artist, casino night with fake money.

**Food:** pizza at midnight, a snack bar at 2 a.m., breakfast at 3:30 a.m.

## Safety and supervision

Have a clear policy on entry, re-entry and exit. Many post-prom parties check students in at the door and do not allow anyone to leave without a parent. Adult volunteers should wear name tags and be assigned zones. Plan for first aid and have the contact numbers of parents. Share the plan with school staff and local police if needed. Ask the venue for its safety requirements. See [event insurance basics for private events](/blog/event-insurance-basics-for-private-events).

## Food and drink

Plan on high-energy foods and lots of water. Pizza, sandwiches, snacks, drinks and breakfast items work well. Consider allergies and dietary needs. See [how much food per person](/blog/how-much-food-per-person-for-a-party) and [late night coffee bar](/blog/late-night-coffee-bar). Keep food stations stocked, and plan for trash and recycling.

## Rentals and setup

Many committees rent tables, chairs, linens, tents or inflatables. Plan seating for about a third to half of students at once, since many will be moving. Rent tables for food, check-in, games and prizes. See [how many tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) and [bounce house rental safety](/blog/bounce-house-rental-safety-and-space).

## Volunteers

Recruit early and often. Use a sign-up sheet with shifts. Thank volunteers with food and a note. Provide a short orientation before the party.

## Prizes and donations

Reach out to local businesses early, and offer thanks on signs or social media. Prizes can include gift cards, small electronics, dorm items, and experiences. Keep a list of donors for thank-you notes.

## Communicate with students and parents

Students decide whether a post-prom party is worth attending, so promote it the way you would a dance. Share the schedule, the prize list and the rules in advance through the school newsletter, social media and a short flyer sent home with prom tickets. Be clear about the check-in time, the policy for leaving and what students should wear or bring. Parents appreciate a one-page summary that explains supervision, food and pickup. Offer an easy way to ask questions, such as a committee email address.

## After the party

Count the money, pay vendors and send thank-you notes to donors and volunteers within a couple of weeks. Write down what worked and what did not: which activities were popular, when the food ran out, how many volunteers were enough. Hand those notes to next year's committee, along with the vendor contacts and your budget spreadsheet. A good handoff turns a stressful first year into an easier second one.

## Chicago-area notes

In the Chicago area, post-prom parties are often held at school gyms, park district facilities, bowling alleys, trampoline parks and indoor sports centers. Prom dates cluster in April and May, so book vendors and venues early. Weather can be wet and cool, so plan for coats and an indoor option. Parking and traffic at drop-off can be heavy, so assign volunteers to direct cars. Check with your school and with your village or park district for rules about overnight events, insurance and facility use. If you want a tent or outdoor space, see [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a post-prom party?

It is a supervised, safe party for students after prom, usually from about 11 p.m. to 4 a.m., with food, games and prizes.

### How do you plan a post-prom party?

Form a committee, set a budget, book a venue, plan activities and food, recruit volunteers and gather donations. Start four to six months ahead.

### How much does a post-prom party cost?

It depends on the size, venue and activities. Many are funded by donations, fundraisers and ticket sales. Get quotes and set a per-student budget.

### What activities work for post-prom?

Games, inflatables, a DJ, casino night, photo booth, movies, sports and a midnight meal. Prizes keep students at the party.

### How many adult volunteers do I need?

A common rule is one adult for every eight to ten students, but follow your school's requirements.

### How do you keep students at post-prom?

Offer prizes that are given out late in the night, require students to stay to be eligible and keep the activities fun.

### Where can we hold a post-prom party?

A school gym, a park district facility, a bowling alley or an indoor sports center are common. Check capacity, hours and insurance.
