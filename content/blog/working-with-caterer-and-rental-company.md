**Quick answer**

Ask your caterer in writing what they provide (food, staff, cooking equipment, often serving pieces) and what they expect from you (tables, linens, chairs, glassware, tents, sometimes plates). Give the same list to your rental company, reconcile counts, and assign one person to receive both deliveries. Start 3 to 4 months before the event, and confirm final counts 2 weeks before.

</div>

The caterer and the rental company never meet until delivery day, and that is where events go wrong. The caterer assumes the rental company has chafers, while the rental company assumes the caterer brings its own. Each is right in its own world. The planner pays the price. This guide gives a simple system to avoid gaps and duplicates.

## Questions to ask your caterer

<div class="table-wrap">

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What do you bring: chafers, utensils, platters, plates? | Avoids double-renting |
| Do you need tables for buffet, prep and bar? | Sets the table count |
| Do you provide linens for service tables? | Affects the linen order |
| How many staff will you bring? | Drives table and space needs |
| What power and water do you need? | Plan generators and circuits |
| How much prep space do you need? | Decides tent size |
| What time can you access the venue? | Sets delivery window |
| What do you need from us at teardown? | Pickup schedule |

</div>

Also ask what they charge for rentals, because some caterers rent items and add a service fee. Compare that with a direct rental quote. We cannot say which is cheaper, because it depends on the caterer and the list.

## Questions to ask your rental company

- What are the delivery and pickup windows, and can they work around the caterer's schedule?
- What is the damage and loss policy for food and fuel stains?
- Can the rental company deliver to the venue's loading dock or only the curb?
- Do you provide chafers and serving ware, or only tables, linens and chairs? (We help plan the whole list; ask for a quote that confirms what is included.)
- What are the cleaning and return requirements?

## The one-page handoff

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Write one list</strong> with every item, count and who supplies it.</li>
<li><strong>Send it to both</strong> the caterer and rental company at the same time.</li>
<li><strong>Reconcile</strong> any item listed twice or by neither.</li>
<li><strong>Name a receiver</strong> who signs for both deliveries.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm final counts</strong> 14 days before the event.</li>
</ol>

Keep the list on a shared document so both can see edits. Include the venue contact, parking and loading instructions, and the setup timeline. If you have a coordinator, that person owns the sheet.

## Timeline

- **3 to 4 months out:** Book caterer and rentals. Peak seasons in Chicago, from May to October, fill fast. See [when to book event chair rentals](/blog/topic/budget-and-booking).
- **8 weeks out:** Finalize menu and style, and get equipment lists.
- **4 weeks out:** Reconcile list, add tables, tents and power.
- **2 weeks out:** Final counts, final payment, delivery windows.
- **Day of:** One receiver, one timeline, one phone number for each vendor.
- **After:** Make sure both vendors pick up and report damage promptly.

## Common conflicts and fixes

- **Both bring chafers.** Decide who is responsible and cancel one. 
- **Neither brings tables for prep.** Add them to the rental order; see [tables and linens for caterers](/blog/tables-and-linens-for-caterers).
- **Venue rules.** Some venues require exclusive caterers or restrict outside rentals. Ask the venue first, and read [Chicago venue rules for chair rentals](/blog/topic/chicago-planning).
- **Late deliveries.** The caterer arrives before tables do. Align the windows.
- **Linen stains.** Hot fuel and sauces; use heat pads and the right cloth color.

## Insurance and permits

Many venues ask for certificates of insurance from caterers and rental companies. Ask each vendor whether they provide one, and what the venue requires. Do not assume. We do not offer legal advice, so rely on the venue contract.

## Chicago note

Downtown and city venues often have strict loading dock windows, freight elevator rules and union labor requirements. Suburban banquet halls tend to be more flexible. Always ask the venue manager for the loading rules in writing, and share them with both vendors.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a caterer provide tables and chairs?

Sometimes, but many caterers bring food, staff and cooking equipment only. Ask for a written list of what is included, and rent the rest.

### Who provides chafing dishes, the caterer or rental company?

It varies. Many caterers bring their own, while drop-off caterers usually do not. Confirm before you order.

### Can I rent from a company other than my caterer?

Usually yes. Some caterers charge a fee or prefer their own vendors, so ask. Venues may also restrict outside vendors.

### How far ahead should I book?

3 to 4 months for the caterer and rentals, and earlier for peak Chicago dates in summer and fall.

### Who accepts the delivery?

Name one person with a phone and a signed copy of the list. A coordinator, the venue manager or a trusted friend all work.

### What if the caterer and rental company disagree?

Go back to the written list. Decide who provides each item and update both. Do not rely on verbal promises.
