**Quick answer**

For a pageant or concert, set chairs in theater rows with a center aisle of at least 4 feet and side aisles of about 3 feet. A rule of thumb is 8 to 10 square feet per seat, with rows spaced about 36 inches apart. Reserve the first two rows for performers' families, and keep a clear wall or corner for photos after the show.

</div>

A Christmas pageant or winter concert has two crowds: the kids who perform and the families who come to watch. The room fills fast, parents stand at the back, and every child needs to be seen. In a Chicago church hall or school gym in December, you also have wet coats and boots. Good seating solves a lot of this.

## How many chairs fit your room

Use a simple count. A rule of thumb for theater seating is about 8 to 10 square feet per seat, including aisles. A 40 by 60 foot room (2,400 square feet) seats about 240 to 300, before you add a stage and a sound table. The guide to [how many chairs fit theater seating](/blog/how-many-chairs-fit-theater-seating) has more numbers.

Do not fill every inch. Leave space for wheelchairs, strollers and a clear path to exits. Check seating capacity and fire rules with your school, church or park district before you set the room.

## Rows, spacing and aisles

- **Row spacing:** about 36 inches from the back of one chair to the back of the next, so people can pass without everyone standing.
- **Center aisle:** at least 4 feet wide. Many halls use 5 feet when kids walk in procession.
- **Side aisles:** about 3 feet along each wall.
- **Row length:** keep blocks to 10 to 14 chairs across with an aisle at one end, so no one has to climb over 14 coats.
- **Chair ganging:** link chairs in rows so they do not shift when kids bump them. Ask your rental company about row linkers.

Compare layouts in [classroom vs theater seating](/blog/classroom-vs-theater-seating-layout). Theater style gives the most seats for a pageant or concert.

## Reserved rows for families

Families of performers sit where they can see their child. Two or three reserved rows at the front, marked with a folded sign on each chair, keep the front from being taken by early arrivals.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Count performers</strong> and estimate 2 to 4 family seats per child.</li>
<li><strong>Mark rows</strong> with signs or ribbon on the aisle chairs.</li>
<li><strong>Tell families</strong> the arrival time for reserved seats, such as 15 minutes before curtain.</li>
<li><strong>Release unused seats</strong> at curtain time, so the room fills.</li>
<li><strong>Keep two rows for grandparents</strong> and anyone using a mobility device, near an aisle.</li>
</ol>

## Risers and stage

A pageant with 20 to 40 children needs more than a floor. Risers lift the back row so each child is seen. For stage and riser sizes, see [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events). A common stage is 8 by 12 feet or larger, built from 4 by 8 foot sections, but sizes vary by company, so ask for what is available.

Leave a gap between the front row and the stage edge for a 3-foot walkway, and make sure kids can reach the steps without crossing a power cord. Rental stages come with steps and rails if you ask.

## Photo spots for families

Parents will take pictures. Without a plan, they stand in the aisle. Pick a spot away from the exit doors, with a wall or a tree and a good light. Hang a simple backdrop and put a small bench or a few chairs in front. After the performance, send families there by section, so the line does not block the doors. For backdrop ideas, see [photo backdrop ideas](/blog/photo-backdrop-ideas-for-weddings-and-parties).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **A December evening starts dark.** The sun sets around 4:20 to 4:30 pm in Chicago in December, so a 6 pm show means kids and families arrive after dark. Light the walkway and the parking lot.
- **Coats take up space.** Count a coat for every guest. Rent a rack or two, or leave the last row of chairs for coats. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).
- **Sound carries differently in a full room.** An empty gym echoes, and a full one does not. Test the microphone with people in the room, not just before the doors open.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do I need per seat for a concert?

A rule of thumb is 8 to 10 square feet per seat in theater rows, including aisles. Add space for the stage, the sound table and a back standing area. Check your venue's capacity rules.

### How wide should the aisles be?

Use at least 4 feet in the center and about 3 feet on the sides. A wider center aisle works if children walk in procession. Keep aisles clear of cords and coats.

### How far apart should rows be?

About 36 inches from chair back to chair back works for most halls. Closer rows save space but make it hard to leave a row. Leave extra room for the front row near the stage.

### Should we reserve seats for families?

Yes, for a pageant. Reserve two or three front rows for performers' families and release unused seats at curtain time. A sign on each chair makes it clear.

### Do we need risers?

If you have more than about 15 children in a group, risers help the back row be seen. Ask for stage and riser sections by size. Include steps and rails.

### How many chairs should I order?

Count expected guests, then add 10 percent for late arrivals and extra family. Fold-away chairs are easy to set up in rows, and you can see options at [/rentals/chairs](/rentals/chairs).
