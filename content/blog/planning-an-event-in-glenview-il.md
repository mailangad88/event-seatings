## Historic and public sites

The Grove in Glenview is a Glenview Park District site, and other public spaces, clubs and halls host private events across the village. If you are considering a historic or nature-oriented site, the rules often protect the property. Ask about:

1. Whether outside tables and chairs are allowed, and whether the site has its own.
2. Whether tents, stakes or heavy equipment can touch the ground.
3. How early you can arrive for setup and how late you can stay.
4. Whether alcohol, candles, music and food service are permitted.
5. Rain plans, restroom access and accessibility.

Do not assume. Contact the Glenview Park District or the site owner and keep their answers in writing. We can then match the rental list to what the site allows.

## Lighting and evening

Garden parties that run into dusk need light. Strings of lights along a fence or over the table area create a warm glow. Use battery lanterns on paths for safety. Chicago-area summers stay light until roughly 8:30 pm, but spring and fall evenings turn dark by 6 or 7. Plan lighting for the earliest sunset on your date. See our [string light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events) for spacing.

## Seating charts and guest comfort

A seating chart does more for a party than any centerpiece. Seat people who know each other at the same table, and place a few outgoing guests at each table to keep conversation moving. Keep older guests away from speakers, and near restrooms. Seat families with young children close to an exit. For a small garden party, you can skip assigned seats and use table signs for family, friends and neighbors, which keeps things relaxed. For a Chicago-area wedding or milestone dinner, assigned seats help the caterer plan meals and save guests the awkward hunt for an empty chair.

Allow 36 inches behind each chair for walking, and more where servers pass. If your room is tight, shrink one table size rather than squeezing chairs, since a crowded table is hard to serve. Mark any dietary needs on place cards or with a simple color dot so servers can match meals quickly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size round table is best for a party?

A 60-inch round seats 8 and encourages conversation. A 72-inch round seats 10 and uses fewer tables. Pick by room size and guest count.

### Can I rent tables and chairs for a Glenview park or historic site?

Check the rules for the site first. Contact the Glenview Park District or the site owner about outside rentals, tents and decor.

### How many chairs for 100 guests?

About 105. If you have a ceremony, a lounge or a head table, add those separately.

### Do round tables fit a 20 x 30 foot room?

About 5 to 6 rounds of 8 fit with space to walk, depending on doors and furniture. Measure the room and ask us for a layout.

### What chair is best for a garden party?

Lightweight, textured chairs like bentwood and rattan, or garden folding chairs for large groups.

### Do I need to cover tables to the floor?

Not always. Floor length looks formal, while lap length suits casual parties. Our [size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) explains each.
