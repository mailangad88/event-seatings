**Quick answer**

A Diwali party works best with a mix of seating: a puja or prayer corner, comfortable chairs for elders, lounge groupings for mingling, a few cocktail tables for standing guests and a buffet set so lines never block the walkways. Plan one seat for about 60 to 70 percent of guests at a mixer and a seat for everyone at a sit-down dinner. Dates shift every year, so check the calendar, and remember that Chicago evenings in late autumn are cold, so plan for coats and indoor space.

Diwali, the festival of lights, falls in autumn and brings families and friends together for puja, sweets, music and dinner. Chicago-area hosts celebrate in homes, community halls, banquet rooms and offices. Because the guest list often spans three generations, the main design problem is seating that works for the grandmother who wants a chair near the puja and the teenagers who want to move around.

## Seat for the program, not just the headcount

A Diwali party usually has a few distinct moments, and each needs a spot:

- **Puja or prayer.** A small table with a cloth, enough room for a few people to stand or sit, and chairs nearby for elders. Ask your family what they want to include.
- **Mingling and sweets.** Lounge groupings and cocktail tables where guests circulate.
- **Dinner.** Tables for a buffet or a plated meal.
- **Games, music or dance.** Open floor and a speaker area.
- **Rangoli and diya displays.** Floor or table areas that guests should walk around, not over.

The mixer ratio is a rule of thumb: most people prefer to stand and chat for part of the night. For a sit-down dinner, give everyone a chair. ## Chairs that work for all ages

Mix comfort and style. [Velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) ($18 to $25 estimated) in jewel tones suit the festival colors. [Rattan garden chairs](/chairs/rattan-garden) ($12 to $16) add warmth in a living-room style setup. For a stackable option that sits many people, [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) ($5 to $7) are a budget-friendly choice for a community hall. Leave aisles of at least 36 inches between rows so elders can pass.

## Diyas, candles and fire safety

Diyas and candles are central to the holiday. In a home, treat them as you would any open flame: place them on stable surfaces away from fabric, curtains and walkways. In a hall or rental space, ask the venue first. Many venues limit or ban open flame, and flameless candles or LED diyas can give the same look. See our guide to [candles at venues](/blog/candles-at-venues-fire-code-rules) and [lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events).

## Buffet flow

A buffet line should not cross the main walkway. For 60 guests plan a table of at least 8 feet, with serving spoons for each dish and plates at the start. Add a second line or double-sided setup beyond 80 guests. For equipment and layout see our [buffet line flow guide](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

## Chicago notes

- Late autumn evenings are cool. If you plan an outdoor diya display, have a heated indoor fallback. See [patio heaters](/blog/patio-heaters-how-many-per-guests) and [tent heating](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall).
- Sunset comes early in late autumn, so outdoor lighting matters by late afternoon.
- If you live in a condo or apartment, ask your building about open flame, deliveries and elevator reservations.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs should I rent for a Diwali party?

For a mixer, seat about 60 to 70 percent of guests, and give elders priority. For a sit-down dinner, rent a chair for every guest. Add a few extra for kids and late arrivals.

### Where should the puja area go?

Choose a quiet corner away from the buffet and speakers, with enough room for a few people to gather. Add a small table with a cloth and chairs nearby for elders.

### Can I use real diyas indoors?

Many households do, but venues often restrict open flame. Ask your venue or building. LED diyas are a safe look-alike for a hall.

### How do I plan for kids?

Give them a low table or a corner with seating, away from candles and the buffet line. See our [kids tables and chairs](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events) guide.

### When is Diwali this year?

The date changes each year because it follows a lunar calendar. Check a current calendar and your family's tradition, then book rentals as soon as you know the date.

### Can I rent items for a small apartment party?

Yes. Small cocktail tables, stackable chairs and a lounge piece fit many apartments. Measure your space and elevator first.
