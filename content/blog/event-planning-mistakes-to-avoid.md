## 1. No clear budget

Without a ceiling, every choice feels reasonable and the total balloons. **Fix:** set a maximum and a split by category before you shop. See [how to make an event budget](/blog/how-to-make-an-event-budget).

## 2. A guest list that outgrows the room

The list swells with plus-ones and late additions. **Fix:** set a cap from your budget and space, and invite in tiers. See [how to make a guest list](/blog/how-to-make-a-guest-list-for-any-event).

## 3. Booking vendors in the wrong order

Choosing flowers before the venue, or the DJ before the date, leads to rebooking. **Fix:** book in order of scarcity: venue, caterer, then entertainment and rentals. See [how to hire event vendors](/blog/how-to-hire-event-vendors).

## 4. Ignoring the weather

Outdoor events with no plan B turn into scrambles. **Fix:** decide on a tent or an indoor fallback early. Read [rain plan with a tent](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent).

## 5. Vague contracts and verbal promises

A promise that was never written is hard to enforce. **Fix:** get price, hours, inclusions and cancellation terms in writing. See [how to read an event rental contract](/blog/how-to-read-an-event-rental-contract).

## 6. A late or shaky headcount

Food, chairs and plates depend on the count. **Fix:** set a reply deadline before your vendors' deadline and chase replies. See [how to track RSVPs](/blog/how-to-track-rsvps) and [the final headcount deadline](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals).

## 7. Not enough seating or table space

Hosts often count only dinner seats and forget cocktail, gift and cake tables. **Fix:** plan zones and count [chairs](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need). Use a [floor plan](/blog/floor-plan-for-100-guests).

## 8. No day-of timeline

Without a plan, tasks fall to whoever is nearest. **Fix:** write a one-page run of show with names. See [four-hour party timeline](/blog/four-hour-party-timeline).

## 9. Trying to do everything yourself

The host ends up too tired to enjoy the event. **Fix:** delegate tasks to named helpers. See [how to delegate event tasks](/blog/how-to-delegate-event-tasks-to-family-and-volunteers).

## 10. Forgetting access and comfort

Stairs, long walks, loud music, no shade or no restroom plan can sink a day. **Fix:** walk the guest path and use the [inclusive planning checklist](/blog/inclusive-event-planning-checklist).

## 11. Underestimating setup and delivery

Rentals arrive late or the truck cannot reach the door. **Fix:** confirm times and access with every vendor. See [event rental delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect).

## 12. Skipping cleanup

The party ends and no one knows who is stacking chairs. **Fix:** plan a cleanup crew. See [event cleanup plan](/blog/event-cleanup-plan).

## Chicago-area notes

In the Chicago area, weather, parking and permits are the mistakes that surprise people most. Spring and fall can swing fast, summer storms can be sudden and winter planning needs coats and clear paths. Street parking, alley deliveries and building rules slow rental drop-offs, so confirm access. Tents, park pavilions and street events can need permits, and rules differ between the city, forest preserves, park districts and individual villages, so check with your village or park district well ahead. ## Frequently asked questions

### What are the biggest event planning mistakes?

The big ones are no clear budget, a guest list that grows past the room, vague contracts, no weather plan, a late headcount and no day-of timeline. Each is easy to prevent with early decisions.

### What is the most common mistake in event planning?

Underestimating the work and starting late. A schedule and a clear budget solve much of it. Start with the date, headcount and budget.

### How do I avoid going over budget?

Set a ceiling, split it into categories, hold a cushion and track every cost. Get at least three quotes. Review the sheet weekly.

### How do I avoid running out of food?

Base the order on a firm headcount and consider guests' appetites and the length of the event. Ask your caterer for amounts. Keep a little extra.

### How do I prepare for bad weather?

Pick a tent, an indoor backup or a rain date early. Ask rental and venue partners what is possible. Decide the call time for switching plans.

### What should I confirm a week before an event?

Arrival times, access, contacts, final headcount, payments, permits and your weather plan. Print the timeline and share it with helpers.

### Should I hire an event planner to avoid mistakes?

A planner can help with large or complex events. For smaller ones, a good checklist, written contracts and helpers are usually enough.
