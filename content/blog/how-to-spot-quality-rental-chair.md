**Quick answer**

A quality rental chair does not wobble, creak or rock when someone sits, has no cracks or loose joints, has a clean, even finish and sits level on a flat floor. Check ten things: stability, joints, legs, finish, seat, back, cushions, cleanliness, stacking and consistency across the set. Do it before you book (ask for photos) and again on delivery, before the truck leaves.

</div>

Most rental chairs look fine in a photo. The difference shows up when a guest sits down: a rocking leg, a squeaky joint, a scratched seat. By then it is too late to fix. A quick inspection takes two minutes per style and prevents those moments. This guide gives a 10-point check for what to look at before you book and on the morning of your Chicago event, plus the words to use if something is wrong.

1. **Wobble.** Sit on the chair and shift side to side. It should not rock or creak. Put it on a flat floor and press each corner; rocking can mean uneven legs.
2. **Joints.** Look where the back meets the seat and where legs meet the frame. Gaps, loose screws or glue stains are warnings.
3. **Legs.** Straight, even and the same length. A shorter leg makes a chair tilt.
4. **Finish.** Even color, no large chips and no exposed raw wood or metal. Small scuffs are normal on rentals.
5. **Seat surface.** No splits in wood, tears in upholstery, stains or loose weave in cane.
6. **Back.** No cracks at stress points, such as where a slat meets a rail.
7. **Cushions or pads.** Clean, intact and not flattened.
8. **Cleanliness.** No sticky spots, odors or dust. Wipe a corner to check.
9. **Stacking.** Stackable chairs should stack cleanly without scraping. Stacks should be stable.
10. **Consistency.** Chairs in a set should match in finish, size and condition. A few off-color chairs stand out in a row.

## Before you book

Ask your rental company:

- Can I see photos of the actual chairs, not just stock images?
- How old are they, and how often are they replaced or refinished?
- What is the rated capacity of this model? See [chair weight capacity and seat sizes](/blog/chair-weight-capacity-and-seat-sizes).
- What is the replacement policy if a chair fails?
- Who is responsible for damage, and how is it assessed?

## On delivery day

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Count</strong> the chairs against your order, including spares.</li>
<li><strong>Sample</strong> at least one of every 10 chairs by sitting on it.</li>
<li><strong>Check</strong> cushions, pads and any accessories.</li>
<li><strong>Photograph</strong> any issue with the delivery time visible.</li>
<li><strong>Tell the driver</strong> before they leave, and get a swap.</li>
</ol>

Do this before the delivery crew leaves, not at the event start. A swapped chair takes minutes when the truck is still there and hours when it is not. Also photograph the chairs when they are picked up, so any dispute about damage has evidence on both sides.

## Red flags in a quote

- No mention of delivery, setup or pickup details.
- No damage policy or an unclear one.
- Stock photos only for a chair style, with no real photos.
- No rated capacity available for a chair.
- A price that is far below others in the market for the same style. Cheap is fine; mysterious is not. See [wedding chair rental cost](/blog/wedding-chair-rental-cost).

## What to do if a chair fails

Remove it from use, photograph it, tell the rental company by phone and in writing and ask for a replacement. Don't try to repair it. Keep a few spares in your order so a swap takes seconds; see [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I tell if a rental chair is good quality?

It does not wobble, has tight joints and legs of equal length, an even finish and a clean seat. Sit on it and shift your weight to check.

### Should I inspect chairs on delivery?

Yes, before the delivery crew leaves. Count, sample and photograph any issue so it can be swapped on the spot.

### What counts as damage on a rental chair?

Cracks, deep scratches, tears, stains that don't wipe off and missing parts. Light scuffs and dust are normal wear. Ask for the damage policy in writing.

### What should I ask a rental company about quality?

Ask for real photos, the age and condition of the chairs, rated capacity and the replacement policy.

### Do cheap rental chairs mean lower quality?

Not always, but ask why the price is low. Stackable chairs cost less because they save labor, not necessarily because they are poorly made.

### How many spare chairs should I get?

One or two per 50 chairs is a common rule of thumb. Order a spare of each style.

### Who is responsible if a chair breaks during the event?

It depends on the contract. Ask who is responsible for guest-caused damage and what the policy is.
