**Quick answer**

Divide your guest count by the seats per table and round up. With 60-inch rounds that seat 8, you need 7 tables for 50 guests, 13 for 100, 19 for 150 and 25 for 200. With 72-inch rounds that seat 10, you need 5, 10, 15 and 20. Then add 4 to 8 service tables for cake, gifts, guest book, DJ, buffet and bar.

</div>

Table counts look easy until the venue sends a floor plan with a number that does not match yours. The gap is almost always one of three things: the seats-per-table assumption, a sweetheart table that takes a couple out of the guest count, or a pile of small tables nobody listed. This guide gives you the math, a chart for the four most common guest counts in the Chicago area, and a checklist for the tables that are not on the guest list.

## The two-step formula

**Guest tables = guests ÷ seats per table, rounded up.**
**Total tables = guest tables + service tables + one spare.**

Seats per table is the number that moves everything. Rental companies publish a comfortable number and a tight number. Use the comfortable number for plated dinners, where every guest needs elbow room for 3 or 4 courses of dishes and glassware. Use the tight number only when the room forces it.

## Why 8 per table beats 10 for most Chicago weddings

A 72-inch round saves you tables, linens and floor space. A 60-inch round gives each guest about 23 inches of table edge, which is comfortable. A 72-inch round at 10 guests gives about 23 inches too, but the middle is a 5-foot gap that makes conversation across the table harder and pushes centerpieces to the edge of reach. If your ballroom is tight, 10s are a smart tool. If your room has space, 8s feel warmer. Our guide to [round table sizes](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch) covers the trade-off in detail.

## A worked example: 120 guests

A couple has 120 guests, a sweetheart table and a 6 piece band.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Subtract the couple:</strong> 120 guests at tables, plus 2 at the sweetheart table.</li>
<li><strong>Pick the table:</strong> 60 in rounds seating 8. 120 ÷ 8 = 15 tables exactly.</li>
<li><strong>Add a buffer:</strong> Add 1 table in case of late RSVPs. That is 16 rounds.</li>
<li><strong>Add service tables:</strong> Cake, gifts, guest book, escort cards, band table, dessert: 6 tables.</li>
<li><strong>Total:</strong> 16 guest rounds, 1 sweetheart table and 6 service tables.</li>
</ol>

## Service tables people forget

These do not appear on the guest list but each one needs a table, a cloth and floor space.

- Guest book or welcome table
- Escort card or seating chart table
- Gift and card table
- Cake table (see our [cake, gift and guest book checklist](/blog/cake-gift-guest-book-table-checklist))
- DJ or band table
- Buffet or food station tables
- Bar tables, if the venue does not provide a built-in bar
- Cocktail hour highboys, which are counted separately

Cocktail tables are a separate count. A standing reception works on a different ratio; see [how many cocktail tables for a standing reception](/blog/how-many-cocktail-tables-for-standing-reception).

## Check the room before you check the count

A table count only works if the tables fit. A 60-inch round with chairs pulled out needs a circle about 10 feet across, and you need room to walk between tables. For 150 guests at 19 rounds, plan on 12 to 14 square feet per guest once you add the dance floor, buffet and bar. Many Chicago banquet halls publish a maximum capacity with rounds of 10; if you use rounds of 8, the same room holds fewer guests. Ask the venue for the capacity at your table size, not just the headline number. See [table spacing and aisle clearance](/blog/table-spacing-and-aisle-clearance) for exact distances.

## Mistakes that change the number

- **Counting kids at adult tables.** Kids can share a 48-inch round, which uses less space. See [kids table ideas](/blog/kids-table-wedding-ideas).
- **Forgetting vendors.** Photographers, DJs and planners often get a meal. Ask your caterer whether to seat them.
- **Rounding down.** 99 guests at tables of 10 is 10 tables. Never plan 9.9.
- **Ignoring odd tables.** Seven guests at a table of 8 is fine. A table with 3 people looks empty in photos.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many tables do I need for 100 guests?

You need 13 tables of 8 or 10 tables of 10, plus service tables. Add 5 to 7 more tables for cake, gifts, guest book, DJ and food or bar stations. That makes a typical total of 18 to 20 tables for a 100-guest wedding.

### How many tables do I need for 150 guests?

Plan on 19 rounds of 8 or 15 rounds of 10. Add 6 to 8 service tables, which brings the total to roughly 21 to 27 tables. The exact number depends on your buffet, bar and band setup.

### How many tables do I need for 200 guests?

A 200-guest event takes 25 rounds of 8 or 20 rounds of 10. With service tables, expect 27 to 35 in total. At this size, check that the room fits tables, dance floor and aisles before you commit to 8s.

### How many tables do I need for 50 guests?

Seven rounds of 8 or 5 rounds of 10 seat 50 guests. Add 3 to 5 service tables. A 50-guest dinner also works well with 2 long banquet tables.

### Do I count the couple in my table total?

Count the couple as 2 guests if they sit at a guest table. If they have a sweetheart table, subtract 2 from the guest count and add one small table of 30 x 48 inches or similar to your order.

### Should I order extra tables?

Order one spare. Late RSVPs, a table you forgot and last-minute vendor meals are common. A spare is cheap insurance against rearranging a floor plan on the day.

### Does the table size change how many chairs I need?

No. Chairs equal guests plus a few spares regardless of the table. See [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) for the full count.
