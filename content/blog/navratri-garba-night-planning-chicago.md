## About Navratri and what the night looks like

Navratri is a Hindu festival lasting nine nights, honoring the goddess in her forms, and observed in the autumn. In many Gujarati communities, the nights include garba, circular dances around a lamp or a statue of the goddess, and dandiya raas, a dance with decorated sticks. In the US, many cultural organizations, temples and community groups hold garba on the weekends of Navratri, and sometimes a night for each of the nine. Some events are devotional and begin with aarti (a prayer with a lit lamp); others are large social events with live singers. Ask your community leaders about the customs they expect, including dress, music and food. Many observe fasts and may prefer food without grains, onions or garlic on certain days.

If you are planning for a temple, a school, a community group or a family association, the key choices are the same: a space big enough for the dance, safe floors, and a timeline that respects the prayer and the dancing.

## Sizing the space

Start with the number of dancers, not the number of guests. Not everyone will dance at the same time, but peak moments, such as the main garba after aarti, can bring most people to the floor. A common planning figure is 8 to 10 square feet per dancer. A hall that fits 600 people for dinner may fit far fewer for dancing. Measure the clear floor, subtracting stage, pillars and seating. Plan wide aisles and an obvious exit path.

**Floor.** Hard wood or smooth floors are best. Carpet can cause trips and sore feet, and gravel or rough concrete is risky. If the floor is slick, ask about covering. If you rent a dance floor, look at [dance floor types and sizes](/blog/dance-floor-types-parquet-white-black-led) and [how big a dance floor should be](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count).

**Sound.** The singer or DJ needs clear speaker coverage across the whole circle. Test the sound with the room full, because bodies absorb sound.

**Seating.** Chairs line the walls for elders and parents with children. Do not put tables in the middle.

**Food.** Garba often has a snack stand or a full meal. Set food away from the dance floor so lines do not cross dancers. See [buffet line flow](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

## Safety and respect

Check the venue's capacity, fire exits and insurance. Keep aisles clear. Make sure first aid is available and that volunteers know where it is. Provide water. For children, set a quiet area. For respect, ask your community what is appropriate for dress and music, and brief the DJ on any songs or sequences that should be avoided. If outside guests join, a short welcome in English explaining the dances is warmly received. Keep an eye on sticks: dandiya sticks can cause injuries in a crowded circle, so set rules about spacing and keep the circles orderly.

## Chicago-area notes

Navratri usually falls in September or October, when Chicago weather moves from mild to chilly. Many events are held in suburban banquet halls, school gyms, community centers or temple halls, with strong South Asian communities in the western and northwestern suburbs. Parking is often the limit, so arrange shuttles or off-site lots with the venue. If you use a tent, read the [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and check with your village or park district, and plan heating for cooler evenings. Noise rules vary by town, so ask about curfews and amplified sound. Reserve space early, since weekends for Navratri are in high demand.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do you need for a garba night?

Many organizers plan about 8 to 10 square feet per dancer, so 500 dancers need roughly 4,000 to 5,000 square feet of clear floor. Subtract the stage, columns and seating. Always follow the venue's legal capacity.

### What is the difference between garba and dandiya?

Garba is a circular dance, often around a lamp or image of the goddess, usually done with hands and feet. Dandiya raas uses decorated sticks. Many events include both.

### How long does a garba night last?

Many run three to five hours, often beginning with an aarti and ending around midnight or earlier depending on the venue and local rules. Check noise and curfew rules.

### Do I need a permit for a garba night?

It depends on the venue and your village. A hall usually handles its own permits, but tents, amplified sound outdoors or street use may need approval. Ask your local office.

### What food is served at garba?

Many events serve snacks or a vegetarian meal. During Navratri, some people fast or avoid grains, onions or garlic. Label dishes clearly and ask your community what is expected.

### Should I hire a live singer or a DJ?

Both work. A live singer adds energy, but costs more. Ask for references and test the sound. Whichever you choose, share the song order and timing.

### How many volunteers do I need?

A common rule is one volunteer per 10 to 15 guests across parking, greeting, shoes, food, safety and cleanup. Give each team a lead.

### What should guests wear?

Many wear traditional chaniya choli or kediyu, but dress varies. Comfortable shoes or socks and lightweight clothing help. Ask your community about expectations.
