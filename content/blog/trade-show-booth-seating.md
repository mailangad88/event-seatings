**Quick answer**

In a 10 x 10 foot booth, use two to four seats at most: a high-top table with two stools, or a small round table with three chairs. A 10 x 20 booth can fit a lounge corner or a meeting table for four. Keep the front 3 feet open so visitors can walk in, and check the show's rules on carpet, tables, power and what exhibitors may rent.

</div>

Booth seating is a trade-off. Chairs invite longer conversations and make staff look relaxed, but they also fill a small space and can make visitors feel committed to a pitch. Most booths that use seating well decide what the seat is for first: a demo, a sit-down meeting, a place to charge a phone, or a rest for staff. This guide helps you choose the right amount and style for common booth sizes at McCormick Place, Donald E. Stephens Convention Center or other trade-show venues in the Chicago area.

## What the seat is for

<div class="table-wrap">

| Purpose | Seat type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Quick chats | High-top with 2 stools | Keeps visitors at eye level and moving |
| Demos | Stool at a counter | Visitor sits beside the screen |
| Private meetings | Round table, 3 to 4 chairs | Longer talks, contracts, quotes |
| Visitor comfort | Lounge chair or small sofa | Draws people in, good for long sales cycles |
| Staff rest | Hidden folding stool | Staff stay fresh without sitting in view |

In a 10 x 10, stay at one small table and two stools. In a 10 x 20 you can add a second area, but the aisle edge should stay clear. In an island booth (20 x 20 and larger), a lounge pocket with four seats and a low table can anchor the booth. Always check the show's height limits for signage and furniture.

## Choosing the furniture

Pick furniture the exhibitor can carry or have delivered. Trade show contractors, such as official service providers, usually rent furniture at the show, and exhibitors often order through the exhibitor kit. Some shows allow outside rentals at the booth; others require you to use the official contractor. Read the exhibitor manual before ordering. Ask whether a vendor that delivers directly needs to be on a list or provide a certificate of insurance; see [corporate event COI and vendor requirements](/blog/corporate-event-coi-and-vendor-requirements).

## Style and brand

Match the seat to your brand. A tech brand might use white [shell-style chairs](/chairs/shell) or [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost). A craft or food brand might use [bentwood chairs](/chairs/bentwood) and a wood table. A luxury brand might use [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining). Keep colors in line with the booth graphics, and wipe-clean fabrics. Keep a small cloth or throw on the table that matches your palette.

## Comfort for staff

Staff stand for eight hours a day. Plan a rest corner that visitors can't see, with a stool, water and a place for bags. Rotate staff every two hours. Add anti-fatigue mats under the counter if the show's rules permit. A booth where staff look comfortable invites visitors.

## Seating for a hosted reception at the show

Some exhibitors host a cocktail hour in or near the booth. Check the show's rules on food and alcohol, which often require the venue's catering. If you plan a nearby offsite party, a lounge reception or a hotel suite is easier. See [networking reception layout](/blog/networking-reception-layout).

## Common booth seating mistakes

The most common mistake is putting a chair directly in the front of the booth, where it blocks the entrance and tells visitors "I'm busy". Another is using a low sofa that visitors struggle to leave once seated, which makes salespeople reluctant to invite them in. Tables that are too big eat the floor of a 10 x 10, and a chair with a visible brand tag from the rental company looks off. Keep the cords from a charging station taped down and the furniture clear of the aisle line, since many shows enforce it.

## Charging stations and power

Visitors love a place to charge a phone, and a charging table with a stool near the booth edge draws traffic. Power at a trade show is usually ordered through the show's electrical contractor, so request it early. Use a power strip with a cover and keep cords out of walkways. A small sign reading "Charge here, chat with us" turns a service into a conversation.

## Chicago notes

Chicago hosts large trade shows year-round, and exhibitor shipping and drayage rules are strict. Furniture delivered by a third party may need to arrive within a set window and be checked in at the dock. Ask the show's official contractor and the venue about labor rules before ordering rentals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs should a trade show booth have?

Two to four in a 10 x 10 booth, up to eight in a 10 x 20 with a meeting area. Too many seats make the booth feel crowded.

### Are stools or chairs better for a trade show booth?

Stools at a high-top keep conversations short and let visitors leave easily. Chairs at a low table suit longer meetings.

### Can I rent furniture from outside the show contractor?

Sometimes. Read your exhibitor manual; some shows require official contractors and others allow outside vendors with rules.

### How do I make a small booth feel open?

Use clear or open-frame furniture, keep the front free and avoid large tables. Ghost chairs and a small round table take less visual space.

### What flooring do I need under booth furniture?

Many shows require carpet or provide it. Ask the show manual for floor requirements.

### How should staff sit at a booth?

Staff should avoid sitting in view when visitors approach. Use a hidden rest area and rotate often.
