Skokie is a close-in northern suburb with a mix of banquet halls, congregations, cultural centers, park district facilities and family homes. Celebrations here are often large: bar and bat mitzvahs, weddings, quinceañeras, graduations and anniversary dinners can easily top 150 guests with several generations at one table. This guide shows how to plan seating for a big family event, and how to avoid the common crowding mistakes.

## Start with the room, then the guest list

A large party fills a room faster than hosts expect, because tables, a dance floor, a buffet and a DJ all need space. Ask your hall for its seated capacity with rounds and a dance floor, not just its maximum. Then use a quick check.

## Seating plans for multi-generation families

Large families bring needs a basic chart ignores.

- **Elders near the action, not in the speaker path.** Place older guests with a clear path to the restroom and an arm-friendly chair.
- **A kids' table near parents.** Children's tables are easy to forget, and kid-size tables or chairs may be needed.
- **Family-style meals.** Platters and bowls take table space, so choose 72-inch rounds for large groups.
- **Reserved head tables.** The honoree's family table often needs extra room for gifts, candles or a speech.

If your celebration has traditions that affect layout, such as a ceremony space, a processional or a specific table order, tell your planner and the venue early. Our [South Asian wedding seating guide](/blog/south-asian-wedding-seating) is one example of how traditions shape the plan.

## Local places to check first

Skokie has a village government, a park district and many private halls, congregations and cultural centers. If you want a park facility, contact the Skokie Park District for availability and rules. For a hall, ask for the floor plan, a list of included items and the policy on outside rentals. Some halls have exclusive vendors for tables and chairs, so ask before you shop. Do not assume a policy; get it in writing.

## Winter in Skokie

Many celebrations happen in cold months, when guests arrive in coats. Plan a coat check or rack, a vestibule for wet boots and extra time for arrivals. Our [winter seating guide](/blog/winter-wedding-seating) explains how to lay out a room for cold weather. In Chicago winters, snow can delay deliveries, so build a buffer into the schedule and confirm the delivery window the day before.

## Food, flow and the buffet line

In a large family event, the food line is where time is lost. A single buffet table serves about 50 to 75 guests before the line gets long, so for 150 guests plan two serving lines or call tables by number. Place the buffet at least 10 feet from the nearest chair so people can queue without bumping the seated guests. If you serve family style, give each 72-inch round enough room for platters, 2 to 3 serving dishes in the middle and a clear spot for bread. Ask your caterer what table size they prefer, since they know what their trays require.

Bars need room too. A 6-foot bar serves 50 to 75 guests; two bars halve the wait for 150. Put one near the entrance and one near the dance floor so guests naturally spread out.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people fit in a Skokie banquet hall?

It depends on the hall, so ask for the seated capacity with rounds and a dance floor. As a rule of thumb, allow 10 to 12 square feet per seated guest plus space for the dance floor.

### How many chairs do I need for 150 guests?

About 158 chairs, which is 150 guests plus 5% spares. Add chairs for a DJ, a head table and any ceremony space.

### What table size is best for large families?

The 72-inch round seats 10 and gives space for platters. A 60-inch round seats 8 and feels cozier. Mix sizes if you have families of different sizes.

### Can I rent chairs for a hall that has its own?

Often yes, but ask the hall about outside rentals and fees. Some halls require insurance paperwork.

### What is the best chair for a mitzvah or wedding?

The gold infinity and ghost chairs are common for banquets because they photograph well. The Windsor suits more traditional rooms.

### How do I plan for older guests?

Choose chairs with a supportive back, avoid low lounge seating at dinner and keep a clear path to restrooms. See our [cushion tips](/blog) for added comfort.
