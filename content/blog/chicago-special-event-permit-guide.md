**Quick answer**

A City of Chicago special event permit from the Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events (DCASE) is required if your outdoor event closes a city street, prepares food on a public street or open-to-the-public private property, serves alcohol, uses a tent larger than 20 x 20 feet (400 sq ft), or uses a stage taller than 24 inches. You can apply up to 180 days ahead, and the processing fee rises as the date gets closer.

</div>

Most Chicago parties never need a city special event permit. A backyard birthday, a living room shower and a banquet hall reception do not. The permit matters when your event spills into public space or gets big enough to need a large tent, a stage or a liquor license. This guide sorts out which side of the line you are on, and what to rent once you know.

## The five triggers

DCASE's special events FAQ says an application is needed if your event includes any of these:

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Closing city streets.</strong> Any street closure, even for a few hours.</li>
<li><strong>Preparing food</strong> on a public street or sidewalk, or on private property that is open to the public.</li>
<li><strong>Serving alcohol.</strong> A state liquor license applies, with fees listed below.</li>
<li><strong>A tent bigger than 20 x 20 feet.</strong> That is 400 square feet.</li>
<li><strong>A stage taller than 24 inches.</strong> Two feet or more off the ground.</li>
</ol>

Bar crawls are covered separately, and need a permit only when they have more than 500 expected participants, more than 5 stops and 3 or more stops on the same block.

## Fees by how early you apply

The DCASE processing fee climbs as your event date approaches. The City's FAQ lists these tiers:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Days before the event | DCASE processing fee |
|---|---|
| 60 or more | $100 |
| 59 to 45 | $200 |
| 44 to 30 | $500 |
| 29 to 15 | $1,000 |
| 21 to 14 | $2,000 |

</div>

The 29-to-15 and 21-to-14 ranges overlap in the City's published list, so confirm the exact tier with DCASE if you are applying inside a month. The takeaway is plain: applying 60 days out costs a fraction of applying two weeks out.

## Other approvals that travel with it

- **Chicago Park District.** If the event is on park property, including most green space and the lakefront trail, you need a separate Park District permit with its own fees. See our [Park District permit guide](/blog/chicago-park-district-event-permit).
- **Police review.** A Police Commander Review Letter is required before a permit issues. DCASE and the City handle that communication, so organizers do not contact the district themselves.
- **Alderman.** A No Objection Letter is no longer required. Send the alderman's office a short description of your plans, since aldermen can still object to DCASE in writing.
- **CDOT fees.** If streets will be closed or used, CDOT charges fees. Traffic is managed by Traffic Management Aides or police. Volunteers and private security cannot direct traffic.
- **Food and retail.** The City lists a Special Event Food License at $75 and Pop-Up Retail at $25 per vendor.

## Liquor license costs

For-profit events pay a state liquor license fee of $150 (one day) or $250 (two to 15 days). A $25 late fee applies if the application is not received 14 days before the event, and the City fee of $150 applies if it is received 20 days before. Not-for-profit events pay a $25 state fee with the same late fee and no City fee. Alcohol rules change, so verify with DCASE before you budget.

## What this means for your rental order

Rentals do not need a permit themselves, but they can create the trigger. A 20 x 30 tent is 600 square feet and crosses the line. A riser for a band might be 12 inches and stay under it, while a 30-inch stage will not. Decide your tent and stage first, then count tables and chairs.

If you can keep the event on private property under the thresholds, you may avoid this permit completely. Smaller frame tents, two or three 10 x 10 canopies placed apart, or a venue with a covered patio are all common ways to stay under. A private-property tent may still need a building or fire permit, so check our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) for the separate rules.

## A simple planning timeline

- **180 to 60 days out:** pick the site, choose the footprint, apply for the lowest fee tier.
- **60 to 30 days out:** confirm alderman contact, food and liquor licenses, and the rental list.
- **30 to 7 days out:** lock the delivery window, truck access and street details.
- **Event week:** watch the forecast, confirm the setup crew and tie-down plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a special event permit for a block party in Chicago?

Block parties follow a separate process through CDOT and your alderman's office, not the DCASE special event permit. See our [block party permit guide](/blog/chicago-block-party-permit) for details. If a block party gets large enough to add a stage or a big tent, ask your ward office which permit applies.

### How far ahead can I apply for a Chicago special event permit?

You can apply up to 180 days before the event. Applying 60 or more days ahead gets the lowest processing fee, listed by the City at $100.

### Do I need a permit for a tent at a Chicago event?

For a tent larger than 20 x 20 feet (400 square feet), yes. Smaller tents do not trigger the DCASE special event permit, but the Park District treats any tent or canopy in a park as a permit feature, and building or fire rules can apply on private property.

### Do I need a permit to serve alcohol at a private party in Chicago?

DCASE lists serving alcohol as a trigger for outdoor events it covers. A private party inside a home or a licensed venue follows different rules. If you are unsure, ask DCASE or your venue's manager before you buy anything.

### Who directs traffic at a permitted Chicago event?

The City does, using Traffic Management Aides or police. Organizers cannot manage traffic, and volunteers or private security have no jurisdiction. The cost depends on the number of aides and the hours.

### Is the special event permit the same as a Park District permit?

No. They are separate permits with separate fees. An event in a park generally needs the Park District permit, and it may need the DCASE one too if it hits a trigger like a street closure or a large tent.
