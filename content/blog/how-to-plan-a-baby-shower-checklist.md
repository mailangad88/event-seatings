A baby shower looks easy from the outside, but the host has a lot to juggle: a guest list that mixes family, friends and coworkers, a date that depends on a due date, a gift table, food that works for everyone and a flow that keeps the guest of honor from being on display for three hours. A clear checklist keeps everything in order.

## Settle the basics first

**Who is hosting.** Decide who is the host or co-hosts, and who pays. Etiquette traditions have loosened, so it can be a friend, family member or coworker group. Confirm with the parents-to-be so you do not step on any plans.

**The guest list.** Ask the parents-to-be for names, then confirm whether partners, kids and coworkers are invited. Is it a co-ed shower? Is it a surprise? Each answer changes the invitations and food.

**The date and time.** Many showers happen four to eight weeks before the due date. Pick a weekend afternoon and avoid conflicts with holidays. Allow flexibility in case the baby comes early.

**The budget.** Decide a total and divide it among food, venue or rentals, decor, favors and games. Use the [event budget spreadsheet](/downloads/event-budget-spreadsheet) to keep it organized.

**The style.** Brunch, tea, backyard party, dessert shower or a sip and see after the baby arrives. See [sip and see party planning](/blog/sip-and-see-party-planning).

## Timeline

**Six to eight weeks out:** confirm the host, date and venue. Start a guest list and a registry link.

**Four to five weeks out:** choose a theme, menu and games. Send invitations or an online invite.

**Three weeks out:** order the cake and decor. Request a quote for rentals.

**Two weeks out:** finalize the headcount, buy supplies and confirm helpers.

**One week out:** buy food, print games and gift tags, and confirm deliveries.

**The day before:** set up tables, charge a camera, and set out the gift table.

Showers are often seated, because guests eat, play games and watch gifts being opened. Plan a seat for every guest. See [baby shower rentals and seating](/blog/baby-shower-rentals-and-seating).

## Food and drinks

Brunch or lunch fits most showers. A buffet or stations keep the host free to mingle. For amounts, see [how much food per person](/blog/how-much-food-per-person-for-a-party). A good rule is to plan two plates per guest and extra dessert. Offer non-alcoholic options such as a mocktail bar or lemonade. Check with the parents-to-be about food they need to avoid. See [dessert table equipment checklist](/blog/dessert-table-equipment-checklist).

## Decor and the gift table

A few simple elements go a long way: a balloon garland, a welcome sign, a cake table and a photo spot. Keep the gift table close to the host with a card box and a notebook for tracking gifts. See [card box and gift table ideas](/blog/card-box-and-gift-table-ideas) and [balloon garland rental vs DIY](/blog/balloon-garland-rental-vs-diy).

## Games and flow

A typical flow is arrival and mingling for 30 minutes, food for 45, two games for 20 minutes, gifts for 30 to 40, cake for 10 and goodbyes. Keep games optional. See [baby shower games and timeline](/blog/baby-shower-games-and-timeline) for ideas.

## Seating for the guest of honor

Give the parent-to-be a comfortable chair with good back support, and place it where guests can see them during gifts. Provide a footstool if they want one. Keep walkways wide and keep restrooms easy to reach. See [high chairs and baby stations at events](/blog/high-chairs-and-baby-station-at-events) for family-friendly planning.

## What people forget

Thank-you card list, a trash and recycling plan, a lighter for candles, a plan for transporting gifts to the car, and a spot for strollers and car seats.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area showers are often held at a home, a banquet room, a restaurant private room or a park district space. Spring showers can mean surprise rain, so have a tent or an indoor plan. Winter showers need coat space and a safe entry. If you host in a condo or townhouse, check building rules on deliveries and guests. Many suburbs have community rooms with kitchens, and rules about decor, food and alcohol vary, so check with your village or park district before you book. Parking can be tight for a crowd, so share directions and ride-share info.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a baby shower step by step?

Confirm the host and date, set a budget, build the guest list, choose a venue, send invitations about four to five weeks ahead, order food and rentals, and plan a simple schedule. A checklist keeps the order straight.

### When should I have a baby shower?

Many showers happen four to eight weeks before the due date, which leaves time for the host to be comfortable and for a buffer if the baby comes early.

### How far in advance should I send baby shower invitations?

Four to five weeks ahead is common. Set an RSVP date about ten days before the shower so you can finalize food and seating.

### How much does a baby shower cost?

It varies widely with guest count, venue and food. A home shower costs less than a catered hall. Many hosts set a per-guest amount and get quotes for rentals and food, since Chicago-area prices differ.

### How long should a baby shower last?

Two to three hours is typical, with time for eating, a couple of games, gifts and cake.

### How many guests should I invite to a baby shower?

Ask the parents-to-be for their list. Many showers have 15 to 50 guests, and about 70 to 85 percent of invitees often attend, but family may be higher.

### What do I need to rent for a baby shower?

Many hosts rent tables, chairs, linens, a tent and serving ware. Plan a seat for every guest since showers are usually seated. See our [shower table setup guide](/blog/bridal-shower-table-setup).
