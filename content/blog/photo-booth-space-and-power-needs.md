**Quick answer**

Most photo booths need about 8 by 8 feet of floor, one standard 120-volt outlet within about 10 to 15 feet and an extra 4 to 6 feet in front for the line. A 360 booth wants a clear circle closer to 10 by 10 feet. Confirm the exact footprint and power draw with your vendor, then confirm outlet access, load-in route and ceiling height with your venue.

</div>

The booth itself is easy to book. The trouble starts on event day, when the vendor arrives and finds the only open corner is next to the kitchen door, 40 feet from an outlet, behind a flight of stairs. A five-minute checklist before you sign saves that moment. This guide covers what a booth needs, how to measure for it and what to ask.

All of these are common planning figures. Equipment varies, so treat your vendor's written specs as the final answer.

1. **Floor space.** The booth, backdrop, light stands and a props table.
2. **Power.** A regular outlet nearby, ideally on a different circuit from the DJ or kitchen.
3. **Line space.** Guests waiting need room that does not block a doorway or bar.
4. **Ceiling height.** Lights and backdrops stand up to 8 feet or more; 360 arms sweep above head height.
5. **Access.** A loading path that fits the equipment cases, with a door width and elevator size that works.

## Measuring the space

Walk the room and mark an area using painter's tape or your phone's measuring app. Add the line space in front. For an open-air booth, the full zone is closer to 8 by 12 feet. For a 360 booth, mark the full 10 by 10 square, then another few feet for the line.

## Power: the details that bite

Most photo booths run on a standard household outlet. The vendor will bring extension cords and surge protection, but there are still things to confirm.

- **Distance.** Ask how far the outlet may be. Long runs mean tape-down cord covers, so guests do not trip.
- **Circuit sharing.** DJs, band amps, caterers' warmers and photo booth lights should not all share a circuit. A tripped breaker takes everyone down. Read more in our cluster on [lighting and power](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power).
- **Generators and outdoor power.** For tents and parks, power may come from a generator. Ask who supplies it and whether the booth can share.
- **Cord safety.** Cords across walkways need covers. Venue staff often require it.

## Questions for the venue

1. Which wall outlets are available, and are any on a dedicated circuit?
2. Is there an approved vendor list or insurance requirement for outside vendors?
3. What is the ceiling height and are there any chandeliers or fixtures in the way?
4. What is the loading route, and is there a freight elevator with a time window?
5. Can items be taped or stuck to walls or floors?
6. When is vendor access allowed for setup?

Many Chicago venues, from downtown hotels to suburban banquet halls and park district facilities, require a certificate of insurance from outside vendors. Ask early, because vendors often need a few days to supply it.

## Questions for the booth vendor

1. What is the exact footprint, including the backdrop and props table?
2. How much power does it draw and how many outlets?
3. How long is setup and teardown?
4. Is an attendant included?
5. What happens if the space turns out smaller than expected?
6. What are the backup plans for equipment failure?

## Where to put the booth

The best spots are near the cocktail hour or lounge, away from loud speakers, bright windows and kitchen traffic. A corner works, as does a hallway alcove with room for a line. Avoid placing the booth where its line will block the restrooms or coat check. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do I need for a photo booth?

About 8 by 8 feet for an open-air booth and around 10 by 10 feet for a 360 booth, plus 4 to 6 feet for the line. Confirm exact numbers with your vendor, since models vary.

### Do photo booths need special electrical outlets?

Usually not. Most run on a standard 120-volt outlet. Ask the vendor for the draw and keep the booth off the same circuit as heavy equipment.

### Can a photo booth work in a small room?

Yes, with an open-air or enclosed booth. Choose a corner, keep the line short and consider a smaller backdrop. A 360 booth is harder to fit.

### Can a photo booth be set up outdoors?

Often, with shade, stable ground and power. Direct sun washes out screens and wind can knock over backdrops.

### How early does the vendor arrive?

Many arrive 60 to 90 minutes before start, but ask your vendor. Make sure the venue allows that access time.

### Do I need to tell the venue about the booth?

Yes. Many venues need to approve outside vendors and may require insurance paperwork.
