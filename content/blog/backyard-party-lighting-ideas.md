**Quick answer**

Light a backyard party in four zones: a safe path from the street, a warm dining area, a brighter bar or buffet, and a dim, colorful social space. Use 2200K to 2700K bulbs for the warm look, add one bright task light at the grill or buffet, and plan for darkness 45 to 60 minutes after sunset. Keep cords off walkways and plug everything into a GFCI outlet.

</div>

A Chicago-area backyard looks beautiful at 6 p.m. and awkward at 9 p.m., because the lights come on and guests discover the steps, the hose and the dark corner by the fence. The best backyard lighting is not about quantity. It is about putting the right light in the right place, so people can see their food, their feet and each other.

### Zone 1: The path

Guests arrive in the dark and leave in the dark. Mark steps, uneven pavers and gate latches with low path lights or lanterns every 6 to 8 feet. Solar stakes work for short evenings, but they are dim; battery or plug-in LED lights are more reliable. See [lanterns and luminaries](/blog/lanterns-and-luminaries-for-events).

### Zone 2: Dining

This is where string lights earn their place. Hang them 8 to 10 feet up, rows 6 to 10 feet apart. [String light layouts](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events) has the math. Add candles or battery votives on the tables for the warm face glow.

### Zone 3: Bar and buffet

Servers need to see ice, labels and change. Use a brighter task light, aimed down at the work surface and away from guests' eyes. A clamp light on a tent pole or a small LED panel on a stand works. Keep the bar light at least a little warmer than a hardware-store white bulb.

### Zone 4: Lounge

Dim, colored or flickering light invites people to linger. Use lanterns, small lamps, a fire pit where allowed, or battery uplights on a wall or fence.

## How much light is enough?

Aim for a layered glow, not a floodlight. A rough guide:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Area | Light level | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| Path and steps | Low but continuous | Lantern or LED stake every 6 to 8 ft |
| Dining | Medium, warm | String lights and table candles |
| Bar and buffet | Brightest | One task light per station |
| Lounge | Lowest | Lanterns, lamps, uplights |

## Common mistakes

- **One bright floodlight.** It flattens faces and drives guests away from the lit corner. Use several low sources instead.
- **Cool white bulbs.** Anything above 4000K makes food and skin look gray. Choose warm bulbs.
- **Lights only overhead.** Overhead strands leave faces shadowed. Add low light on tables, such as candles, lanterns or small lamps.
- **Forgetting the exit.** The way out matters as much as the way in, especially after guests have been drinking. Light the gate, the alley and the parking spot.
- **No plan for rain.** If the yard floods, cords on the ground become a hazard. Raise connections and have a plan B, such as a rented tent with its own lighting.
- **Ignoring the restroom route.** If guests use your house bathroom, light the back door and the hallway so the trip is easy.

A good test is to stand at each seat at dusk and ask three questions. Can I see my plate? Can I see the person across from me? Can I see where I step when I stand up? If the answer is yes to all three, the yard is ready.

## Timing the lights

Check sunset for your date rather than guessing. In Chicago, sunsets fall after 8 p.m. into early August, then come earlier each week until they reach about 4:20 p.m. in December. Lights should be on before the sky turns gray, not after. For short winter days see [winter event lighting in Chicago](/blog/winter-event-lighting-chicago-sunset).

## Power and safety

Many older Chicago bungalows and two-flats have only a few exterior outlets. Count the outlets you can use and the circuits they share. A kitchen circuit that runs the oven and the refrigerator should not also power every light. See [extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events) and [cords on grass](/blog/cords-on-grass-outdoor-lighting-safety). If outlets are scarce, [battery-powered lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting) helps.

## Neighbors and noise

Brightness and music affect neighbors. Aim lights down, avoid floodlights pointed at windows, and check your village's rules on amplified sound and event hours. Ordinances vary by suburb, so ask your village hall.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best lighting for a backyard party?

Layered, warm lighting works best: string lights over the dining area, path lights for safety, candles or lanterns on tables and a brighter task light at the bar.

### How many string lights do I need for a backyard?

Multiply the length of each row by the number of rows, then by 1.2 for sag. A 20 x 30-foot dining area with three rows needs roughly 110 feet.

### How do I light a backyard without outlets?

Use battery lanterns, rechargeable uplights and LED candles. A small inverter generator can also power string lights if placed away from guests.

### How long before sunset should lights be on?

Turn them on 30 to 45 minutes before sunset so the transition feels natural and guests do not stumble in the dim.

### Are solar lights good for parties?

They are fine for paths but too dim for dining. Charge needs full daytime sun, so overcast days reduce output.

### How do I keep bugs away from the lights?

Warm amber bulbs attract fewer insects than cool white. Place a fan near the dining area and keep food covered.
