**Quick answer**

For a winter kids party indoors, pick a room with enough floor and enough ceiling, then split it into zones: a place to put coats and boots, a play area, and a table for food and cake. A Chicago winter takes the yard off the table from about November to March. Snow, ice and early sunsets mean the party moves inside, and most homes were not built for 15 kids running around. The good news is that a basement, a garage, a church hall or a community room can all work. You just need to plan the floor, the ceiling and the pile of coats by the door.

## Start with the room, not the theme

Measure the floor and the ceiling before you pick anything else. A room that is 20 by 20 feet is 400 square feet. Divide by 15 to 20 and you get a rough count of kids who can play and move without bumping into each other. Remember that parents and tables take space too.

## Ceiling height decides what you can rent

Many Chicago basements, bungalows and two-flats have low ceilings, often around 7 to 8 feet. That is fine for a table, a play mat and a game, but it rules out some rentals.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Rental | What it needs | Fits a basement? |
|---|---|---|
| Soft play mats and foam blocks | Floor space only | Usually yes |
| Kids tables and chairs | Floor space | Yes |
| Tabletop games and a ring toss | Floor space | Yes |
| Indoor bounce house | Floor space plus ceiling clearance above the unit | Only if the ceiling is high enough |
| Large inflatable slide | Very high ceiling | Usually a gym or hall only |

</div>

Ask the rental company for the exact height of the bounce house, plus space above it for kids jumping. Then measure your ceiling at its lowest point, including beams, pipes and light fixtures. A bounce house also needs a blower, an outlet and room on every side, so see our guide to [bounce house safety and space](/blog/bounce-house-rental-safety-and-space) before you book one indoors. If your ceiling is low, soft play is the better pick.

**Basement.** Warm, private and easy to close off. Check for a low ceiling, exposed pipes, one narrow stair and a single exit. If the party is big, limit how many kids are downstairs at once so an adult can see everyone.

**Garage.** Garages in Chicago alleys can work well if they are attached or heated. Clear the cars, sweep the floor and cover bare concrete with mats. Do not run a fuel-burning heater in a closed garage. Use the heater according to its instructions, and ask the rental company what is safe indoors. Our [garage party layout ideas](/blog/garage-party-layout-ideas) cover the setup in more detail.

**Hall or community room.** Park district fieldhouses, church halls and community centers often have more floor and higher ceilings, plus tables and a kitchen. Ask what is included and what the room rules are before you plan around it. Check with your village, park district or building.

## Boots, coats and the door

The first 10 minutes of a winter party happen at the door. Ten kids arrive in snow gear, and everyone needs a place to put it.

- Set a mat or a boot tray inside the door, and a second one for the way out.
- Use a rolling coat rack or a few hooks. A [coat rack rental](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago) keeps coats off the floor and off the cake table.
- Tape a name or color tag to each pair of boots, or put each pair in a labeled bag. This is the biggest thing that saves the end of the party.
- Keep a few extra socks. Wet socks end parties early.

## Game rotation in a small space

Kids lose interest in 10 to 15 minutes of one activity. In a small room, rotate rather than run everything at once.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Arrival (15 minutes).</strong> A table with coloring or a simple craft while coats go on the rack.</li>
<li><strong>Active play (20 minutes).</strong> Soft play, a bounce house if you have the ceiling, or a game like freeze dance.</li>
<li><strong>Sit-down game (15 minutes).</strong> Bingo, a scavenger hunt in the house or a simple craft.</li>
<li><strong>Food and cake (25 minutes).</strong> Everyone sits at the same time.</li>
<li><strong>Free play and goodbyes (15 minutes).</strong> Open the door area and bring out coats.</li>
</ol>

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Rental companies close early or charge more on some winter days.** Weekend slots fill up near the holidays, and snow can move delivery times. Book early and ask about the delivery window.
- **Doors and stairs matter.** Measure the narrowest door, the stair turn and the elevator if you live in a building. A folded table or a soft play kit is easier to carry than you think, but a large crate may not fit.
- **Count items at delivery.** Check chairs, tables and mats against the quote before the crew leaves, so you can sort out any shortage while they are still there.
- **Snow slows everything.** Shovel the path and the steps before the delivery window. An icy walk is a risk for guests and for the crew.

## Food and drinks

Keep it simple. A cake, a hot chocolate station, a tray of fruit and something savory like pizza or sandwiches covers most parties. Use covered cups for hot drinks and put them out of reach of toddlers. A [hot cocoa bar](/blog/hot-cocoa-bar-winter-event) is a winter favorite and takes little space.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big a room do I need for a kids birthday party indoors?

Plan on about 15 to 20 square feet per child as a rule of thumb, and add space for tables and parents. A 20 by 20 foot room is about 400 square feet and fits roughly 15 to 20 kids with some tables. If you want a bounce house, you also need clear space around it and enough ceiling height.

### Can I rent a bounce house for a basement party?

Only if your ceiling is high enough. Ask the rental company for the unit's height and the clearance it needs above it, then measure your lowest ceiling point. If the room is tight, soft play mats are a better fit for a basement.

### What can I do instead of a bounce house in winter?

Soft play blocks, a ring toss, a bean bag toss, a scavenger hunt, freeze dance and a craft table all work in a small space. Rotate them every 10 to 15 minutes. Our guide to [kids party games](/blog/kids-birthday-party-timeline-and-games) lists options by age.

### How do I handle coats and boots at a winter birthday party?

Set a boot tray or mat inside the door and a coat rack nearby. Label boots with tape or bags so nothing gets mixed up. Keep the coat area away from the food table.

### Is a garage a good place for a winter kids party?

It can be, if it is attached, clean and heated. Clear the cars, cover the concrete with mats and bring in tables and chairs. Do not use a fuel-burning heater in a closed space unless the manufacturer says it is safe for that use.

### How far ahead should I book rentals for a winter party?

Book as soon as you pick the date, especially for weekends close to the holidays. We check the date with the rental companies, so one request covers tables, chairs, mats and games.
