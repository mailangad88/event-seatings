The most common party mistake is guessing food amounts. Too little leaves guests hungry and the host embarrassed. Too much wastes money and leaves a week of leftovers. The good news is that portion planning follows a few simple rules, and once you know them, you can scale any menu for 10 people or 100.

## Start with the type of party

The same guest count needs very different amounts depending on the format. A cocktail party with light bites is different from a full dinner or a daytime brunch.

**Full meal (dinner).** A main, two or three sides, bread and dessert. Plan 12 to 16 ounces of food in total per person.

**Appetizers only.** If the party runs through a meal time and there is no dinner, plan 10 to 12 pieces per person for the first two hours, and 2 to 3 pieces for each additional hour.

**Appetizers before dinner.** 4 to 6 pieces per person is enough.

**Brunch.** Plan 2 to 3 main items and a sweet option per person, plus fruit and drinks.

**Open house.** Guests come and go, so food can be lighter, but it needs to stay fresh and stocked.

**Dessert party.** Plan 2 to 3 small sweets per guest, or one slice of cake.

## Amounts by food type

**Protein.** Plan 4 to 6 ounces cooked per person if there are multiple dishes, and 6 to 8 ounces if it is the star. For bone-in meat, plan more. Raw weight is about a third higher than cooked weight for most meats.

**Starch.** Rice, pasta or potatoes: about 1/2 to 3/4 cup cooked per person.

**Vegetables and salads.** Plan 1/2 to 1 cup per person.

**Bread.** One to two rolls per person.

**Dips and spreads.** About 2 tablespoons per person for each dip, or 2 to 3 ounces of dip for a crowd.

**Cheese and charcuterie.** 2 to 3 ounces of cheese and 2 ounces of meat per person as an appetizer, or double for a meal.

**Cake.** One slice per guest. A 9-inch round serves about 12 to 16. A sheet cake serves 20 to 40 depending on size.

## Why guest mix matters

Plan more food for teens, for parties that run long and for events built around a game or a big activity. Plan less for a party with a lot of kids or one that follows a big meal. Include vegetarian, vegan and allergy-friendly dishes and plan separately for them. If you serve a lot of vegetarian guests, increase the sides.

Amounts are typical starting points, based on 4 to 6 ounces of protein and half a cup per side. Always check with your caterer, who will have their own portion standards.

## Drinks, ice and coffee

Plan about 1 to 2 drinks per guest per hour for the first couple of hours, then about one per hour. A 16-ounce cup and a 12-ounce glass differ, so count by servings. Ice runs about 1 to 1.5 pounds per person for drinks and more for coolers. See [how much ice for a party](/blog/how-much-ice-for-a-party), [how many glasses per guest](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest) and [how many coffee urns](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party). A 100-cup urn serves about 80 to 100 cups. See [water station and infused water dispensers](/blog/water-station-infused-water-dispenser).

## Serving equipment

Food amounts affect your equipment. A buffet line for 60 usually needs two serving lines or two stations to keep waits under 15 minutes. See [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) and [how many chafing dishes for a buffet](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet). Plan one chafing dish per hot item, and a serving utensil for each dish. See [serving utensils checklist](/blog/serving-utensils-checklist-for-catering).

## Plates and utensils

For a buffet dinner, plan two to three plates per guest: one for dinner, one for seconds or dessert. See [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest) and [how many napkins per guest](/blog/how-many-napkins-per-guest).

## Food safety

Keep hot food above 140 F and cold food below 40 F. Do not leave perishable food out for more than two hours, or one hour in hot weather. Use chafing dishes and ice trays. See [keeping food cold at outdoor events](/blog/keeping-food-cold-at-outdoor-events) and [food safety at home parties](/blog/food-safety-at-home-parties).

## Leftovers

Plan containers for guests to take food home. For parties with leftovers, set out boxes near the exit. Chill leftovers within two hours.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area events vary from hot and humid in summer to cold in winter, and each changes how much food people eat. Guests tend to eat more at cold-weather outdoor parties and drink more at hot summer ones. For outdoor events, plan shade and ice to keep food safe. If you use a caterer, ask what their portion standards are, as many provide a per-person estimate. Check with your village or park district for rules on grills, food trucks and outdoor cooking in parks. Delivery can be tricky in city alleys and high-rises, so schedule ahead. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much food do I need per person for a party?

A common rule is 12 to 16 ounces of food per person for a full meal. For appetizers as a meal, plan 10 to 12 pieces per person.

### How much meat per person for a party?

Plan 4 to 6 ounces of cooked protein per person when you have multiple dishes, and 6 to 8 ounces if meat is the main dish. Raw weight is about a third higher.

### How many appetizers per person?

Plan 4 to 6 pieces if dinner follows, and 10 to 12 if the appetizers are the meal.

### How much food for 50 people?

About 15 to 20 pounds of cooked protein, 25 cups of each side and 50 slices of cake for a meal. Check with your caterer for their standards.

### How much dessert do I need per person?

One slice of cake per guest, or 2 to 3 small sweets if you offer a dessert table.

### How much food should I add for seconds?

Many planners add about 10 percent to cover seconds and last-minute guests.

### How much food is left if I plan too much?

Leftovers depend on the menu. Provide containers so guests can take food home, and chill leftovers within two hours.
