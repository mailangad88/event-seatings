**Quick answer**

A golf outing has four rental moments: registration at arrival, hole sponsor tables on the course, a lunch or snack stop, and a dinner or awards reception afterward. For 100 golfers, plan 4 to 6 registration and sponsor tables, 10 to 12 round tables for dinner, 100 chairs and a tent only if the clubhouse can't hold the group. Confirm what the club provides first.

A golf outing is two events stitched together: a morning or afternoon of play and a dinner or reception after. Each has different rental needs and different bottlenecks. Registration jams at 8 am, sponsors want a visible table, and the dinner hall is often smaller than the group once guests who didn't golf show up. Courses and clubs around Chicago and the suburbs vary widely, so the first rule is to ask what the club includes.

## Registration

Registration at a golf outing is a bottleneck because every golfer arrives in the same 45 minutes. Use one table per 30 golfers, sorted alphabetically or by foursome. Cover the tables with a floor-length cloth so bags and boxes can be stored underneath. A pop-up canopy or tent keeps paperwork and volunteers dry and cool. A rack or table for goody bags prevents clutter, and a large sign with sponsors helps the line move.

## Hole sponsors

Sponsors want visibility: a table, two chairs, a banner and a place to hand out product. A 6-foot table with a cloth, one or two [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) and an umbrella makes a station. If the course permits, a small pop-up tent or beach umbrella gives shade. Check with the club about carts, stakes and where rentals can be placed, since many courses restrict vehicles on greens.

## The dinner layout

If the club has a banquet room, rent only what it lacks: nicer chairs, linens or a stage. If you need a tent, 40 x 60 feet fits about 100 to 120 at rounds, but sizing depends on the stage, bar and buffet. See [what size tent do I need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need).

Count golfers plus non-golfers: sponsors who only come for dinner, spouses and staff. Many outings see a 10 to 20% jump in dinner headcount over golfer count; confirm with your own registration data.

## Sponsors, prizes and the awards moment

Most outings raise money through sponsorships, so the setup should make sponsors feel seen. Give title sponsors a prominent table at registration and at dinner, put their banner behind the awards stage and list hole sponsors on a sign by the entrance. A prize table near the stage, covered in a floor-length cloth, keeps trophies, raffle items and gift cards organized. Assign a volunteer to guard it. For the awards, a small riser of 8 x 8 feet with a podium is enough for most outings; larger groups benefit from a microphone and a screen for the leaderboard.

## Golf cart, shuttle and parking notes

Golfers arrive with clubs and shoes and want to park near registration. Ask the club where deliveries and volunteers should park so rental trucks do not block the path. If the dinner is at a different building, plan a cart or shuttle for non-golfing guests. Placing a few chairs and a shaded table at the first tee gives spectators, sponsors and photographers a place to wait.

## Weather and course rules

Illinois weather in spring and fall can swing from mild to windy to wet in a day. A golf outing without a rain plan loses sponsor dollars. Ask the club about its weather policy, lightning delays and the cart paths. Pop-up canopies need weights or stakes where permitted; ask the course before you drive a stake into a lawn near irrigation lines. For more on tent anchoring, read [tent permits in Chicago](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and talk to your tent provider.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to rent for a golf outing?

Registration tables and a canopy, sponsor tables and chairs, cocktail tables for the reception, and rounds, chairs and linens for dinner. A stage helps with awards.

### How many golfers fit in a tent?

About 100 to 120 at rounds in a 40 x 60 foot tent, depending on the stage and buffet. Open seating or cocktail tables fit more.

### Do golf courses allow rental deliveries?

Many do, with restrictions. Ask the club about vehicle access, delivery times, and where equipment may be placed.

### How many registration tables do I need?

One table per 30 golfers, with a volunteer for each. Add a sponsor and goody bag table.

### Should sponsors get tables?

Yes. A table, two chairs and a banner is a standard hole sponsor setup.

### What if it rains?

Ask the club about its weather policy, plan for a tent at dinner and make sure canopies are weighted.
