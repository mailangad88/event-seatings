If you're choosing between a cross-back chair and a Chiavari chair, you're choosing between two different feelings. Here's how they compare.

## At a glance

| | Cross-back | Chiavari |
|---|---|---|
| Look | Warm, natural, relaxed | Formal, polished, classic |
| Best venues | Barns, vineyards, gardens, lofts | Ballrooms, hotels, banquet halls |
| Material | Solid wood (often walnut, oak or black) | Wood or resin, often gold/silver/white |
| Comfort | Firm seat. A cushion helps for long dinners | Usually comes with a seat pad |
| Back view | The X back is the detail people photograph | Simple vertical spindles |

## When cross-back chairs work best

Cross-backs suit weddings built around **natural texture**: wood farm tables, greenery runners, candlelight and linen. They look especially good at long-table receptions where you see rows of X backs down the length of the room.

They also work well in **industrial lofts**, where black cross-backs pair with exposed brick and Edison bulbs.

## When Chiavari still makes sense

A traditional hotel ballroom with round tables and floor-length linens was designed with a Chiavari-style chair in mind. If you want that classic, formal look, it's a fine choice. It shouldn't be the *only* choice, though.

## A middle ground

If you like Chiavari's polish but want something less expected, look at:

- **Infinity chairs.** A similar metal elegance with a looped back.
- **Ghost chairs.** Formal, but nearly invisible.
- **Louis medallion chairs** at the head table, so the couple's seats stand out from the guests'.

## Our take

Pick the chair that matches the *room*, then the *mood*. A barn wants wood. A ballroom can go glam or classic. A garden can go almost anywhere.
