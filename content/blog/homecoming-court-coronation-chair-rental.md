Most Chicago-area high schools run the homecoming court coronation the same night as the dance, in the same gym, right before the music starts. That back-to-back timing is the main thing that makes this seating different from a wedding or a banquet: whatever chairs go down for the ceremony have to come back up fast, because the dance floor needs that same square footage a few minutes later.

## How to lay out the coronation

1. **Set a short row of chairs on the riser or stage** for the court, facing the student body, with enough space between seats for sashes and crowns to be handled comfortably.
2. **Face the student body rows toward the riser**, with a center aisle wide enough for the court to walk through together.
3. **Leave the back rows closest to the gym doors loose or stackable first**, so a crew can start clearing while the announcement and photos wrap up at the front.
4. **Confirm with the school how much time is budgeted between the coronation and the first song**, since that number decides whether chairs get carried out or just stacked and rolled to the side.

## How many chairs to rent

Count by students attending the dance, not by the size of the court itself:

- **Plan one seat per student who will be seated for the ceremony**, which is usually close to full dance attendance if the whole school sits down for it.
- **Add a handful of extra chairs for the court itself**, plus any escorts or presenters standing at the riser.
- **Check with the school on how many students typically sit versus stand**, since some schools only seat an honor section and let the rest stand around the edges.

## Why the turnover speed matters more than the headcount

A wedding ceremony and reception usually happen in different rooms, or with enough built-in time between them that a crew can reset at a normal pace. A homecoming coronation doesn't get that gap:

- **The ceremony and the dance share the same floor**, so every chair on the floor during the coronation has to be gone, or stacked out of the way, before the lights and music change over.
- **Schools often budget 10 to 15 minutes for the turnover**, which is tight for folding and carrying hundreds of chairs by hand.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a homecoming coronation?
Plan one seat per student attending the dance if the whole school sits down for the ceremony, plus a short row for the court itself. Ask the school whether it seats everyone or only an honor section, since that changes the count significantly.

### How should chairs be set up for a homecoming court coronation?
Put a short row of chairs on the riser or stage for the court, facing the crowd, and set student-body rows facing the riser with a center aisle for the court to walk through.

### How fast do the chairs need to clear for the dance?
Most schools budget 10 to 15 minutes between the coronation and the first song. Stackable chairs on dollies clear a gym far faster than chairs that have to be folded and carried out by hand.

### Can the same chairs work for the ceremony and then just get stacked for the dance?
Yes, that's the usual plan. We recommend a stackable style specifically so the crew can move chairs to the side quickly rather than carrying them out of the building between the ceremony and the dance.
