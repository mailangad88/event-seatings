**Quick answer**

Light a dance floor in three layers: soft wash lights for color, moving or effect lights for energy, and steady white or warm light so photos and faces still look good. Mount lights high on stands or truss, point them across the floor rather than into guests' eyes, and keep effect lights off during slow songs and the last dance. Ask your DJ for a rider that lists gear, mounting and power.

</div>

The best dance floors make guests feel they are somewhere special without making them squint. The worst blind them with a strobe at eye level. Modern LED fixtures are small, cool-running and efficient, which means a lot more is possible than it was a decade ago. This guide covers the types, how many, and how to keep the effect tasteful.

## Types of dance floor lighting

<div class="table-wrap">

| Type | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| LED wash lights | Color the walls, ceiling or floor | Setting the mood |
| Moving heads | Lights that pan and tilt to sweep beams | High-energy dancing |
| Pin spots and mirror ball | Sparkles that scatter across the room | Classic romance |
| Laser and effect lights | Beams and patterns | Late-night energy (check the venue) |
| LED dance floor panels | The floor itself lights up | Big visual statement |
| Haze machine | Makes beams visible | Needs venue approval |
| Monogram gobo | Projects initials | First dance |

</div>

Each has trade-offs. Haze or fog can set off smoke detectors in some venues, and lasers are restricted in some. Ask the venue whether they allow each type before you book.

## Size the floor first

Lighting follows the floor. A common rule of thumb is about 3 to 4.5 square feet per dancing guest, and only a portion of guests dance at once. A 16 x 16-foot floor (256 square feet) is a common starting size for around 100 guests. Check the [dance floor guides](/blog/topic/dance-floors-and-decor) for more.

## Mounting and aim

- **Height.** Mount effect lights 8 to 12 feet or more above the floor so beams cross above heads. Lights at face height are hard on eyes.
- **Aim across, not at faces.** Aim moving heads at the floor, walls or the ceiling.
- **Use stands, truss or ceiling points.** Ask the venue about rigging. A freestanding truss "goalpost" works in tents.
- **Keep stands off walkways.** Weighted bases and cable covers matter.

## Color choices

Strong colors look fun live, but they look different in photos. A reliable approach:

1. Start with your wedding palette for walls and the dance floor wash.
2. Use steady white or warm white lights on faces.
3. Keep effects to two or three colors, not a rainbow.
4. Change the mood by song: warm and slow for the first dance, vivid for the party.

For color consistency, review [lighting color temperature](/blog/lighting-color-temperature-events) and [night photos](/blog/lighting-for-night-event-photos).

## Safety and comfort

- Warn the DJ and venue if guests have photosensitive conditions. Rapid flashing can trigger seizures in people who are sensitive. Avoid strobe effects unless you know your guests, and keep any strobes brief and optional.
- Keep cables taped or covered.
- Make sure stands are weighted.
- Do not point lasers at people.
- Ask about haze and smoke detectors.

## Power

LED fixtures draw far less power than older lights. A typical DJ setup with speakers and lights can fit on one or two circuits, but ask for the total wattage. For tents and outdoors, see [generator sizing](/blog/generator-size-for-events) and keep audio on its own outlet. The [power checklist](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) covers it.

## Working with your DJ

Ask the DJ or lighting vendor:

- What fixtures are included, and how are they mounted?
- How much power do you need and on which circuits?
- Do you bring truss, stands and cable covers?
- Are you comfortable with a monogram or custom colors?
- Do you carry backup fixtures and a spare controller?

## Chicago notes

Many Chicago hotels and event spaces need an approved vendor or proof of insurance for outside lighting, and some limit haze and lasers. Tents get hot, so allow ventilation for fixtures. Outdoor tents need weatherproof connections; see [extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events).

## Frequently asked questions

### What lighting do I need for a dance floor?

A set of colored wash lights, a few moving or effect lights, and steady warm or white light for photos. Many DJs offer packages.

### How high should dance floor lights be?

At least 8 to 12 feet above the floor, so beams pass over heads.

### Are LED dance floors worth it?

They make a big visual impact but cost more and need power and setup. Ask for a quote based on your floor size.

### Are strobe lights safe at a wedding?

Fast flashing can trigger seizures in sensitive guests. Skip strobes or keep them brief and optional.

### Can I use a fog machine?

Only with venue approval, since haze can trigger smoke detectors.

### How much power does dance floor lighting need?

LED lighting is efficient. Ask your vendor for the total wattage and the circuits required.
