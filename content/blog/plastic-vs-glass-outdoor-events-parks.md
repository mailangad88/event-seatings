**Quick answer**

Choose glass for a contained venue with hard floors and trained staff, and shatter-resistant acrylic or polycarbonate for lawns, pool areas, beaches, dance floors and any public park. Many parks and beaches restrict glass containers, and rules differ by park district, so ask your park district or venue for the written policy before you rent. Good acrylic looks close to glass in photos.

</div>

A wedding or reunion in a forest preserve shelter, a park pavilion or a backyard has a different glass problem than a ballroom: grass swallows shards, bare feet are common and animals and children wander through. Park rules can also settle the question for you.

## What to check with the park or venue

Public parks and forest preserves set their own rules on glass, alcohol and what vendors can bring, and the rules differ by district and site. Before you plan:

- **Ask for the glass and container policy** in writing. Many public beaches and parks ban glass bottles; your site may too.
- **Ask about alcohol.** Chicago Park District's published FAQ says alcohol service is allowed only at its Special Event Venues, through caterers on its list, with insurance. Other districts have different rules. See [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events).
- **Ask about permits and tents.** The same FAQ says tents over 240 square feet need a City of Chicago building permit and must go through the park district's preferred professionals. Check this with your district, and read our [Seasons and Venues](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues) guides for more on venue rules.
- **Ask about cleanup and trash** rules, since broken glass and plastic both create work.

Acrylic stemware is a rental category for many companies and looks like crystal at a distance. Wine and champagne in acrylic loses some aroma and feel, but guests rarely mind outdoors. If you want real glass for the toast only, keep it for a controlled moment, such as seated guests in a tent with a floor, then switch to acrylic for the party.

## Wind, grass and heat

A stemmed glass on grass tips easily and spikes into soft ground. Stemless acrylic is more stable. A glass left in the sun turns drinks warm quickly, while an insulated tumbler stays cold longer. Water and cocktails in clear acrylic can show condensation, but it will not shatter if dropped.

For tent events, add a floor if you want glass: check the forecast a week out and again the day before. If your site is a lawn, use level chair footing and consider acrylic for the whole bar.

## What guests notice

Guests care less about the material than about the experience. They notice a glass that tips on uneven ground, a cup that feels flimsy or a warm drink. They rarely notice that a stemless acrylic tumbler is not crystal. If you want a premium look, spend on the details guests see: a clean garnish, a cold drink, a printed menu and a tidy bar. In a Chicago summer, a cold drink in a sturdy cup beats a beautiful glass that has to be replaced twice.

Also think about the end of the night. Plastic cups blow across a park and become litter, and parks may hold you responsible for cleanup. Ask your rental company about racks for acrylic, since many companies wash and rack it the same way as glass. Compostable cups only help if the venue actually composts them, so ask before you pay extra.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is glass allowed in Chicago parks?

Rules vary by park and by permit, and many public spaces restrict glass containers. Ask the Chicago Park District or your suburban park district for the written policy for your site before you plan.

### Can I use real glass at an outdoor wedding?

Yes, in a controlled setting such as a tent with a floor and trained staff. Use acrylic on lawns, near pools and on dance floors.

### Does acrylic look cheap?

Good rental acrylic looks close to glass in photos, especially stemware. Cheap thin plastic looks cheap, so ask for samples.

### Are plastic cups better for kids?

Yes. Use small unbreakable cups at the kids' table. See our kids' glassware guide.

### Can I serve alcohol in plastic cups?

Yes in most settings, but the alcohol rules for the site still apply. Check them with your venue or park district.

### What about glass bottles of beer or soda?

Many parks restrict glass bottles. Cans or kegs poured into cups are the common workaround. Confirm the policy.
