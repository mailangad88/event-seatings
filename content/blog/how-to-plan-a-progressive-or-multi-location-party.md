**Quick answer**

A progressive party moves guests from stop to stop, such as appetizers at one home, dinner at another and dessert at a third. Plan three or four stops, keep travel short, give each stop a clear time window and host, share a route in advance and have a plan for drivers. Check the logistics first, since travel time and parking decide whether it works.

</div>

A multi-location party sounds fun and can be, but the danger is that it turns into a scattered, late, confusing evening. People get lost, food runs cold, hosts are not ready and the schedule slips. With a simple route and a tight timeline, the format gives guests variety and gives hosts a lighter job.

## When a progressive party works

This format works best when stops are close to each other, ideally walkable, and when guests are comfortable moving as a group. It suits neighborhood dinners, holiday walks, birthday crawls and block parties. It works less well in bad weather or when guests have mobility needs. If so, consider one location with stations; see [food station ideas](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment).

Each host takes one part of the meal, which reduces the burden on everyone. Share a short guide so the pace feels consistent; see [dinner party for 12 to 24 rentals](/blog/dinner-party-for-12-to-24-rentals) for ideas on seating and tableware, and [housewarming party seating](/blog/housewarming-party-seating) for smaller homes.

## Assign roles and needs at each stop

Use this table to assign who does what. Adjust to your group size.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Stop | Host job | What to prepare | Rental or supply ideas |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Welcome drinks | Greet and set the tone | Drinks, light bites, name tags | Cocktail tables, glassware |
| 2: Appetizers | Serve small plates | Finger food, napkins, trash | Platters, small plates |
| 3: Main course | Seat everyone | Dinner, seating, drinks | Tables, chairs, linens |
| 4: Dessert | Offer sweets and coffee | Desserts, coffee, decaf | Dessert plates, coffee urn |
| Drivers | Plan rides | Designated drivers, rideshare info | Cards with rideshare tips |

</div>

If one stop is too small for the whole group, split the group into waves so they arrive in smaller batches.

## Plan the evening in six steps

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Choose the route.</strong> Pick close stops, ideally within walking distance.</li>
<li><strong>Assign courses.</strong> Each host handles one part, and they share dietary notes.</li>
<li><strong>Set the timeline.</strong> Give each stop a start and end, plus travel time.</li>
<li><strong>Handle transportation.</strong> Plan walking, carpool, rideshare or a shuttle.</li>
<li><strong>Share the plan.</strong> Send a map, addresses, times and contact numbers.</li>
<li><strong>Plan for weather.</strong> Have a rain plan, such as umbrellas or a single indoor stop; see [indoor backup plan](/blog/indoor-backup-plan-for-outdoor-events).</li>
</ol>

## Food, alcohol and safety

Every stop should offer water and a non-alcoholic option; see [mocktail bar ideas](/blog/mocktail-bar-ideas). Review [food safety at home parties](/blog/food-safety-at-home-parties) so that food is not left out too long. If alcohol is served, plan safe rides home and encourage guests to pace themselves. Share allergen information at every stop and label dishes. If any stop is a business or public space, check its rules first.

## Keeping the group together

Name a lead for each leg of the route who watches the time and nudges guests toward the next stop. Share a group text or a printed card with addresses and phone numbers so late arrivals can catch up. Start the first stop with a clear welcome so everyone hears the plan. Hosts at later stops should get a text when the group leaves, so food is warm when guests arrive. Small signals like these keep the evening flowing.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago neighborhoods with dense housing, such as those with two-flats and greystones, can make walkable routes easy but small spaces limit group size; see [Chicago two-flat and greystone backyard party](/blog/chicago-two-flat-greystone-backyard-party). Street parking rules, winter snow and early darkness affect planning. In the suburbs, stops can be far apart, so carpooling or a shuttle may be needed. If the route uses public spaces, check with the city or park district about permits. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is a progressive party?

It is a party where guests move between locations, with each stop serving a part of the meal or a different activity, such as appetizers, dinner and dessert.

### How many stops should a progressive party have?

Three or four stops are a manageable maximum. More stops add travel time and tire guests.

### How long should each stop last?

About 60 to 90 minutes is a common rhythm, with ten minutes or so for travel.

### How do I handle dietary needs?

Ask guests ahead of time and share restrictions with each host. Label dishes and offer a simple option at every stop.

### What if a stop is too small?

Split the group into waves or choose a different space for that course. A rented tent or tables can help.

### How do guests get between stops?

Walking is simplest. Otherwise use carpools, rideshare or a shuttle, and plan drivers in advance.

### Can I do this in winter?

Yes, but keep stops close and plan coat space and warm drinks. Snow can slow travel.
