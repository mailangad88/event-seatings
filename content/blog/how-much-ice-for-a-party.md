**Quick answer**

Plan 1 to 2 pounds of ice per guest, the range that ice suppliers such as Reddy Ice publish. Use 1 pound if drinks come from a bar with cups of ice only, and 2 pounds or more if you also chill bottles, cans and wine in tubs or the event is outdoors in summer. Ice is the cheapest thing to forget and the most expensive to fix on the day. Rental coolers, bar tubs and wine buckets all need it, and a hot Chicago July afternoon can melt a tub of it in an hour. Here is a simple method.

## The three jobs of ice

Ice does three different jobs, and they use very different amounts:

1. **In the cups.** About 1 pound per guest for a normal bar.
2. **Chilling bottles, cans and wine.** Another 1 pound per guest, or more for beer-heavy crowds.
3. **Keeping food cold.** Seafood bars, salad bowls and dessert displays need their own supply.

## Ice by bag count

Common retail and wholesale bags weigh 7 or 10 pounds, and many suppliers also sell 20-pound bags and block ice. Plan by weight and divide.

## Factors that change the number

- **Weather.** Hot, humid or sunny days melt ice faster. Add 25 to 50 percent for a summer tent. The Reddy Ice guideline notes warm outdoor events need more.
- **Event length.** Ice for a 3-hour party is a different order from a 7-hour wedding. Plan a second delivery for long events.
- **Drink type.** Cocktails use more ice than beer and wine. Frozen drinks and blended drinks use even more.
- **Cooler quality.** A thick, lidded cooler holds ice for hours. A thin tub does not. Ask your rental company about insulated tubs.
- **Shade.** A cooler in shade lasts much longer than one in direct sun.

## Storage and logistics

Ice should arrive as close to the start as possible. For a Chicago city venue, ask about loading access and whether a freezer is available. For a barn or backyard, bring clean coolers or a rented freezer chest. Do not use the same cooler for food and for drink ice, which is a food safety risk. Keep a separate bin of ice for the bar and one for chilling.

## Where ice goes at the event

A bar needs two bins: one at the bartender's station and one at a back table for refills. A beer and soda tub should sit near the bar but outside the work area. A water station needs ice in the dispenser's core or a bin nearby; see [water station and infused water](/blog/water-station-infused-water-dispenser). For layouts, see [bar setup for 100 guests](/blog/bar-setup-for-100-guests).

## Dry ice and specialty ice

Dry ice for smoke effects is a safety issue, since it needs ventilation and must not touch drinks. Ask your caterer or venue before you plan it. Clear block or spherical ice looks beautiful in cocktails, but your bartender will usually source that. For regular cups, cubed or crushed ice is fine.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much ice per person for a party?

Plan 1 to 2 pounds of ice per person. Use 1 pound for drinks served over ice and 2 pounds if you also chill bottles and cans, or if the weather is hot.

### How many bags of ice for 100 people?

About 15 to 30 seven-pound bags, or 100 to 200 pounds. Use 29 bags for a hot summer party with chilled drinks.

### How much ice for a wedding of 150 guests?

150 to 300 pounds, depending on whether you chill drinks and how hot it will be. Split between the bar and a beverage tub.

### Does ice melt faster in a tent?

Yes, especially in sun and humidity. Put coolers in shade, keep lids closed and plan a refill about midway through the event.

### Can I make my own ice?

Not enough of it. A home freezer makes only a few pounds a day, so buy bagged or delivered ice for anything over 20 guests.

### How long does ice last in a cooler?

It varies by cooler quality, shade and how often it is opened. A good lidded cooler in shade can last a long time, but a tub in the sun may melt in about an hour.
