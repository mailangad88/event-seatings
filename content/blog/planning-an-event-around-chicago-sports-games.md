## How game days affect an event

Game days affect four things: traffic, parking, hotel availability and noise or crowds. Neighborhoods near stadiums can see closed streets and surge pricing in lots. Guests who rely on trains and buses may face crowds. Vendors may have trouble reaching your site on time, which affects deliveries, setup and takedown.

If your event is in or near a stadium neighborhood, build extra buffers into the schedule. Ask your venue about game-day parking and street closures, and tell your vendors the date and address so they can plan. For delivery logistics, see [event rental delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect) and [loading zones](/blog/chicago-event-delivery-parking-loading-zones).

## What to check by event type

Different events care about different parts of a game-day impact. Use this table to decide what to check first.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Event type | Main concern | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Wedding | Guest arrival, hotel rooms, photos | Check the schedule; book a hotel block early; add travel buffer |
| Corporate event | Attendance, parking, late-day traffic | Pick another day or provide transit guidance |
| Gala or fundraiser | Guests dressed up in traffic, valet | Arrange valet or shuttle; set a later start |
| Private party | Guest arrival, vendor access | Share directions and arrive early |
| Watch party | Game schedule is the point | Plan seating, screens and food around the game |

</div>

If the date is firm, communicate. A short message to guests about expected traffic can save a lot of frustration.

## Plan around the schedule in five steps

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Check the calendars.</strong> Look at the home schedules for Chicago teams and for nearby venues.</li>
<li><strong>Compare your date.</strong> If it overlaps with a game, consider shifting the date or time.</li>
<li><strong>Ask your venue.</strong> Ask about game-day parking, closures and staffing.</li>
<li><strong>Warn guests and vendors.</strong> Tell them the expected delay and the best route.</li>
<li><strong>Build in buffers.</strong> Add a start-time cushion and an earlier vendor arrival.</li>
</ol>

## Turning a game into a party

If your guests love sports, a watch party can work well. You need clear sight lines to screens, comfortable seating, and food that is easy to eat; see [super bowl party seating for a crowd](/blog/super-bowl-party-seating-for-a-crowd), [projector and screen size](/blog/projector-and-screen-size-for-events) and [food stations](/blog/food-station-ideas-and-equipment). Plan for noise, since cheering competes with conversation, and use a separate space for guests who do not follow the game.

## Talking to guests and vendors

Send vendors a short note that lists the date, the address, the nearest cross streets and the expected game-day traffic, and ask them to arrive earlier than usual. Tell guests in the invitation or a reminder how long to allow for travel and which parking or transit option you recommend. A clear message prevents most problems, and guests usually appreciate the heads-up. If a game ends during your event, build in a short buffer before dinner or speeches so late arrivals do not miss key moments.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago has several home venues around the city and suburbs, and parking and transit differ by neighborhood. Check the schedules for any team near your venue, including smaller teams and concerts at big venues. Large citywide events matter too; see [planning around Chicago marathon weekend](/blog/planning-around-chicago-marathon-weekend) and [planning around Lollapalooza weekend](/blog/planning-around-lollapalooza-weekend). For overall timing, see [best month to host an event in Chicago](/blog/best-month-to-host-an-event-in-chicago). When in doubt, ask your venue and your guests' transit options.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I avoid a date with a Chicago game?

If you can, yes, especially near a stadium. If the date is firm, plan extra travel time and tell guests and vendors in advance.

### How do I find out about game days?

Check team and league schedules online, and ask your venue. Playoff dates can change with little notice.

### Will hotels be more expensive on game days?

They can be, and rooms can sell out. If you need a hotel block, book early and ask about dates.

### Can I host a watch party?

Yes. Plan screens, seating, sound and food. Check venue rules on audio and projectors.

### What if my vendors get stuck?

Ask them to arrive early and share the best route. Build buffer time into setup.

### Should I change my start time?

A later start can help with traffic, but consider catering and venue end times. Talk to your vendors first.

### Does this matter for suburban events?

Less so, but some suburban stadiums and venues host big games and concerts. Check nearby schedules too.
