**Quick answer**

A dessert table for 100 guests needs about 3 to 4 desserts per guest, spread across a 6 to 8-foot table with 4 to 6 risers or stands at varying heights. Rent a floor-length linen, small plates (1.5 per guest), forks and napkins, tongs, labels and a backup table behind for refills. Aim for a tallest centerpiece of 24 to 30 inches and keep the table out of the sun.

</div>

A dessert table is one of the most photographed parts of a party, and it fails the same way every time: everything is the same height, the plates run out and the frosting melts. The cure is simple equipment and a little layout. Here is the checklist and a plan.

## Counting desserts

A common guideline for a dessert table is about 3 to 4 bite-size desserts per guest if there is also a wedding cake, or 2 to 3 if the table is the only sweet. Mix by shape: cupcakes, cookies, macarons, brownies and a fruit option. Four types of dessert at 100 guests is a good floor.

## Keep sweets from melting or drying

- **Shade:** Chocolate and buttercream soften in heat. Keep the table out of direct sun.
- **Cover:** Use domes or cloches for cut cakes and cheesecake.
- **Chill:** Keep refrigerated items in the cooler until 30 minutes before. See [keeping food cold](/blog/keeping-food-cold-at-outdoor-events).
- **Replace:** Refill every 30 minutes rather than putting everything out at once.
- **Avoid wind:** Light napkins and signs blow off an outdoor table.

For ice cream, see [ice cream sundae bar setup](/blog/ice-cream-sundae-bar-setup). For a crowd, a second table keeps guests from clustering. Put the dessert table away from the main buffet so the dessert line forms later; see [buffet line flow](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

## Linens and styling

Use a floor-length cloth so you can hide boxes and refills under the table. Our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) gives the cloth for a 6 or 8-foot table. Add a runner for contrast, keep florals low so they do not block the desserts and choose stands that match the room. Gold and white stands go with gold [infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity), while wood stands suit [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back).

## Dessert table mistakes to avoid

Most dessert tables go wrong in the same few ways, and each is cheap to fix before the day.

- **All desserts at one height.** A flat table looks sparse in photos even when it is full. Use at least three heights.
- **Too few plates.** Guests take two or three desserts, so 1.5 plates per guest is the minimum. Stack extra plates in the back-of-house stock.
- **No serving tools.** Fingers on cupcakes are fine, but cut cakes, bars and sticky sweets need tongs or a server.
- **Candles and open flame.** Check your venue's rules before using candles or sparklers near the display, and keep them away from the table cloth.
- **Placing the table in the traffic path.** Leave 36 inches of walkway on the guest side and put the table where a line can form without blocking the bar.
- **Forgetting the guest with an allergy.** A small card listing nuts, dairy, egg and gluten helps everyone and takes a minute to print.

## Who does the setup

Ask your baker or caterer whether they set up the display or only deliver boxes. Many bakeries drop desserts and leave, which means a friend or the venue coordinator has to arrange them. Give that person a simple photo or sketch of the layout, the stand heights and the order of desserts, and plan 30 to 45 minutes before guests arrive for setup. Delivery windows and loading rules vary by venue, especially in Chicago high-rises and hotels with freight elevators, so confirm both.

## Chicago planning notes

Many Chicago banquet halls and churches allow outside desserts, but some charge a cake-cutting fee or limit outside food, so ask. At outdoor summer weddings, set dessert out late, and keep it in a cool room until then. In winter tents, a warm table is no problem, but check heaters and wax-based candles near desserts.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need for a dessert table?

A table with a floor-length cloth, stands at three heights, platters, small plates, forks, napkins, tongs, labels and a backup table. Add a cake knife and a cooler for refrigerated desserts.

### How many desserts per person?

About 3 to 4 bite-size desserts if you also have a wedding cake, or 2 to 3 if the dessert table is the only sweet.

### How big should a dessert table be?

A 6-foot table works for up to 75 guests and an 8-foot table for 100 to 150. Use two tables for larger events.

### How do I keep desserts from melting outside?

Shade, domes and small batches. Keep refrigerated items in a cooler until 30 minutes before serving.

### How many plates for a dessert table?

About 1.5 per guest, since some guests go back for another bite.

### Should desserts have labels?

Yes. Label each type and list common allergens like nuts, dairy and gluten.
