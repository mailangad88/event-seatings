Bachelorette planning is mostly group logistics. Everyone has a different budget, a different calendar and a different idea of fun, and the planner often ends up fronting money and chasing payments. A clear brief, an honest price and a simple schedule turn that around. Chicago is a strong host city because there is plenty to do indoors and out, which helps when the weather changes.

## Start with the guest of honor

Before you search for anything, ask the guest of honor three questions: who should be there, what kind of weekend sounds fun, and how much should guests be asked to spend. Some want a low-key dinner and spa day. Others want a big night out. Put the answers in a short brief that you can share with the group. This keeps the plan about her, not about the planner.

Then build the guest list and send a quick poll for dates. Aim to give guests at least six to eight weeks of notice, and earlier if people must travel or take time off. Chicago has busy weekends, especially with summer festivals and sports, so lock lodging early.

## Lodging, dinners and activities

Lodging is the biggest cost and the hardest to change, so book it first. A rented apartment or a group of hotel rooms can both work. Compare the per-person nightly price, not the total, and check cancellation rules. Then plan three anchors: an arrival dinner or night out, a daytime activity such as a boat tour, spa, class or lakefront walk, and a relaxed brunch. Keep one block of free time so guests can rest.

Make dinner reservations early, as large groups need advance booking, and ask restaurants about group minimums and deposits. Check with each place about its policy rather than assuming. If you need a private space for a gathering, see [restaurant private room vs full venue](/blog/restaurant-private-room-vs-full-venue).

## Money: splitting costs fairly

Decide how costs are shared before anyone books. A common approach is that guests share the guest of honor's costs, split evenly. Share the estimated per-person total early, with a line for lodging, food, activities and extras. Use a shared spreadsheet or payment group, and collect deposits first. A group that pays by a set date prevents the planner from fronting money.

If the group has uneven budgets, build in choices: a required anchor event and optional extras. Say clearly what is included and what is not. See [how to make an event budget](/blog/how-to-make-an-event-budget) for a quick method, and [how to plan a party on a budget](/blog/how-to-plan-a-party-on-a-budget) for ways to save.

## If you host a gathering at home

Some bachelorette weekends include a party at a rented space or home, such as a cocktail night or brunch. Count seating for about half of the group, a drink station, a food table and a spot for games or decor. For small groups, a few tables and chairs are enough. For larger ones, a lounge arrangement works well. Our [bridal shower guide](/blog/bridal-shower-planning-who-hosts-and-when) has hosting ideas that carry over.

## Sample budget split per guest

Costs vary widely. Use quotes to fill in your own numbers.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Category | How to estimate | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Lodging | Total nightly rate divided by guests, times nights | Each guest shares |
| Group dinner | Menu price plus tax and tip, per person | Each guest shares the guest of honor |
| Activity | Per-person ticket or group fee | Optional or required |
| Transportation | Rideshares, shuttle or parking, divided | Each guest |
| Decor and extras | Sash, signs, favors, games | Planner or shared |
| Guest of honor's share | Add her costs to everyone else's price | Split evenly |

</div>

Always state the total per person, including tax and tip, before collecting money.

## Keeping the group happy

Group trips go best when expectations are set early. Tell guests the schedule, the total price and what is optional. Check for dietary needs and mobility limits before you book restaurants and activities, and avoid plans that exclude anyone. Make one person responsible for money and another for reservations if you can, so the maid of honor is not carrying every task.

Expect a few people to drop out or join late. Build a small buffer into reservations and confirm headcounts a week before. If someone cannot afford the full plan, offer a lighter option, such as joining for dinner only. A kind, flexible tone keeps the focus on the guest of honor and makes the weekend feel generous instead of pressured.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago weather changes the plan. Summer weekends are great for patios and lakefront activities, but festivals and sporting events can fill hotels and restaurants, so reserve early. Winter weekends call for indoor options and coats, and late fall and spring can bring rain and wind. Rideshares are common, but parking downtown can be costly, so check with your lodging. Check restaurant group policies and deposit rules directly with each place. If your party has rented tables or lounge seating, ask for delivery windows that match the building rules.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far in advance should I plan a bachelorette party?

Start eight to twelve weeks ahead. Book lodging first, and give guests at least six to eight weeks of notice, longer if people must travel.

### How much does a bachelorette weekend cost per person?

It depends on lodging, food and activities, and Chicago prices vary by season. Build an estimate from real quotes and share the total per person before collecting money.

### Who pays for the bachelorette party?

Traditionally guests share the cost, including the guest of honor's share. Some groups cover her costs between them. Decide before booking and say it clearly.

### What are good bachelorette party ideas in Chicago?

Think in categories: a nice dinner, a daytime activity such as a boat tour, spa or class, a night out and a relaxed brunch. Pick based on the guest of honor's taste.

### How many days should a bachelorette party be?

Two nights is common for travel groups, while local groups may do a single evening or day. Fit the plan to the group's budget and calendars.

### How do I collect money from a group?

Set a deadline, share a simple per-person total and use one payment method or shared sheet. Collect deposits first to avoid fronting the cost.

### Should I plan a bachelorette party if I'm the maid of honor?

Yes, you can lead it, but share tasks. Ask someone to track money and someone to book dinners. See [how to delegate event tasks](/blog/how-to-delegate-event-tasks-to-family-and-volunteers).
