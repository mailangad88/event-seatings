**Quick answer**

A standard 13 by 13 foot bounce house needs a flat area of about 20 by 20 feet to leave room for the blower, an entry zone and clearance on every side. It must be anchored with stakes or weights, kept away from trees, fences and power lines, and shut down in wind. The CPSC advises against use in windy conditions and says to stop if treetops are swaying. Always have an adult attendant, and ask the rental company about insurance and anchoring.

Bounce houses are the centerpiece of many kids' parties, family reunions and company picnics. They are also the rental with the most safety guidance, because wind, overcrowding and poor setup cause injuries. This guide helps you plan one properly in the Chicago area: how much room, what to ask the rental company and when to say no.

## What the safety agencies say

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) has issued guidance on inflatables. It reports that more than 18,000 bounce house injuries were treated in U.S. hospital emergency departments in 2018, and says maximum wind speed for use is in the range of 15 to 25 mph depending on the unit. CPSC's practical test is simple: if the tops of the trees are swaying, it may be too windy. It also advises to follow the manufacturer's setup instructions, stake or anchor the unit on flat ground, keep it away from tree branches, power lines and fences, let only children of similar size jump at once, never exceed maximum occupancy, and supervise children at all times.

## Space requirements

Bounce houses come in several sizes. These are typical, not rules; confirm the exact footprint.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Unit | Typical footprint | Plan for | Typical occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small bounce house | 10 x 10 ft | 16 x 16 ft | 3 to 4 small kids |
| Standard bounce house | 13 x 13 ft | 20 x 20 ft | 5 to 6 kids |
| Large combo with slide | 15 x 25 ft | 22 x 32 ft | 8 to 10 kids |
| Obstacle course | 12 x 40 ft or longer | 20 x 50 ft | Takes turns |
| Water slide | 12 x 25 ft | 20 x 35 ft + runoff area | Takes turns |

</div>

Your plan area must be flat, clear of rocks, sticks, sprinklers and sharp objects, and large enough for a staging area where kids take off shoes. Leave at least a few feet on every side, plus room for the blower.

## Power and blowers

Inflatables run on a continuous electric blower, often on a 120-volt outlet, with a generator for parks and fields. The rental company should tell you the draw and bring heavy-duty outdoor cords. Ask:

- How many amps does the blower use, and does it need its own circuit?
- Who supplies the generator and fuel, if one is needed?
- How are cords protected from trampling and water?
- Do the blower and generator sit behind a barrier so children cannot reach them?

## Anchoring and ground

Stakes are common on grass; sandbags or weights are used on pavement. The rental company should state the number and type of anchors and set them up. Do not set up on a slope, wet clay or a surface with buried lines. Call JULIE (Illinois's one-call utility locator) if stakes will be driven into private property where buried utilities may exist, and check with your venue and village.

## Supervision and rules

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Attendant on duty</strong> at the entrance at all times.</li>
<li><strong>Sort by size,</strong> with similar ages and weights together.</li>
<li><strong>Shoes, glasses and sharp items off.</strong></li>
<li><strong>No flips, wrestling or roughhousing.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Exit immediately</strong> if the unit sags or wind rises.</li>
</ol>

Post a rules sign near the entrance. Keep food and drinks away from the unit, and make sure parents know that adults are not supposed to bounce with kids unless the manufacturer allows it.

## Insurance, permits and venue rules

Ask the rental company for a certificate of insurance, and ask your venue whether inflatables are allowed. Many park districts, forest preserves and condo associations have rules, and some villages require permits for inflatables on public property. Check with your village or park district. Illinois regulates amusement rides and some inflatables, so confirm that your vendor is a legitimate, licensed provider; ask them to explain how they comply. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do I need for a bounce house?

About 20 by 20 feet for a standard 13 by 13 foot unit, with extra room for the blower and entrance. Check overhead for branches and wires.

### How windy is too windy for a bounce house?

CPSC advises against use in windy conditions and notes a range of 15 to 25 mph depending on the unit. If treetops are swaying, it may be too windy. Follow the rental company's limit and shut down early.

### Can I put a bounce house on a driveway or patio?

Yes, with weights rather than stakes, and extra padding if the surface is hard. Ask the vendor how they anchor on pavement.

### Do I need an attendant?

Yes. CPSC says children should be supervised at all times, by a parent, caregiver or rental staff.

### Can a bounce house be used indoors?

Sometimes, in a gym or hall with high ceilings and power. Check ceiling height, sprinklers and venue rules.

### What should I ask a bounce house company?

Ask about insurance, anchoring, wind limits, power, occupancy, attendants and a rain or wind cancellation policy.
