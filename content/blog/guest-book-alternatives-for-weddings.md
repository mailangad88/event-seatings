**Quick answer**

Good guest book alternatives include a signed frame or mat, a photo guest book, a canvas or map to sign, an audio guest book, a wishing jar, a fingerprint tree and a recipe card box. Most need a 4 to 6-foot table near the entrance, enough pens or markers for 10 to 15 guests at a time and a sign that explains what to do. Pick a format that guests can finish in under a minute.

Few couples reread a traditional guest book. Many want something that goes on a wall, fills a shelf or gets used. Alternatives also give guests something to do during cocktail hour. But each idea has its own needs: a table, supplies, instructions and someone to keep it tidy. This guide walks through twelve options, what each needs and how to place one at your event.

## Picking one that fits

Think about three things: your crowd, your space and your budget of effort.

**Your crowd.** Older guests like a simple pen-and-paper activity, while younger guests enjoy photos and digital options. A mix helps.

**Your space.** If the entrance is crowded, choose a fast option such as a signed mat, so a line does not form. If you have a lounge area, a photo or audio guest book works well there. See [lounge furniture for cocktail hour](/blog/lounge-furniture-for-cocktail-hour) and [selfie station ideas](/blog/selfie-station-ideas-for-events).

**Your effort.** Some alternatives need someone to keep supplies stocked and ink pads clean. Assign a friend or ask the venue to help.

## Setting up the table

1. **Table.** A 6-foot rectangular table seats 2 to 3 guests at once; a 4-foot table suits quick formats. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for linens.
2. **Sign.** A short sentence that tells guests what to do, such as "Sign the mat and leave a wish."
3. **Supplies.** Pens that actually work. Test them, bring spares and choose archival ink for items you will keep.
4. **Seating.** For longer formats, add one or two chairs. A [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) is easy to move.
5. **Lighting.** A small lamp or candle so guests can read the sign in the evening.
6. **Cleanup.** Wipes for ink or paint, and a trash bin.

## Where to place it

At the entrance for the arrival moment, or in the cocktail area where guests have time. Avoid placing it next to the bar or in the doorway. Pair it with the gift and card table so one person can watch both. See [card box and gift table ideas](/blog/card-box-and-gift-table-ideas) for security tips. For winter events, keep it away from the coat check line.

A Chicago touch is easy to add: a signed map of the city and suburbs where guests place a pin for where they live, or a framed print of the skyline or the neighborhood where you met, which guests sign around. Both read well in photos and double as keepsakes.

## Ideas for specific events

- **Weddings:** signed frame, audio book or a photo book near the lounge.
- **Baby showers:** advice cards and a wishing jar.
- **Milestone birthdays:** a signed canvas or memory cards.
- **Graduation parties:** advice cards and a signed frame or a photo board.
- **Memorials:** a memory book or a photo table, with tissues and seating nearby.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are good alternatives to a guest book?

A signed frame or mat, photo guest book, audio guest book, wishing jar, canvas, fingerprint tree or recipe cards. Choose one that guests can complete in about a minute.

### How many guests actually sign a guest book?

It varies. Many guests skip it if the table is hidden or the task is slow. A visible table and a short instruction raise participation.

### How big a table do I need?

A 4 to 6-foot table is enough for most formats. Use a 6-foot table for a photo guest book or any format that needs space for supplies.

### Can I use more than one alternative?

Yes. Offering two formats, such as a signed frame and an audio book, gives guests a choice, but each needs its own space.

### What supplies should I have?

Working pens or markers, spare supplies, wipes for ink, a sign, a bin and a lamp for evening events.

### Who takes care of it after the event?

Name a person, such as a parent or a friend, to collect it with the gifts and cards.
