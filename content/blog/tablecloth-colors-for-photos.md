**Quick answer**

Matte mid-to-light neutrals photograph most reliably: ivory, champagne, soft gray and sage. Pure white can blow out in bright light, and shiny fabrics like satin can throw glare under flash. Black and jewel tones look rich in low light but can swallow detail. Choose matte fabrics, avoid exact matches between linen and chairs, and show your photographer a swatch before you order.

A cloth color that looks perfect in a showroom can look different in a photograph. Cameras exaggerate whites, flatten similar tones and turn glossy fabrics into glare. Since linens cover the largest area in many reception shots, it pays to choose with the camera in mind. Here is how common colors behave, and how to plan for a Chicago venue's lighting.

### Ivory and champagne
The safest. They hold detail, flatter skin tones and stay warm under candlelight. See [ivory vs white linens](/blog/ivory-vs-white-linens).

### White
Crisp in the right light, but easy to overexpose. Tent daylight and flash can turn white cloths into bright blobs that lose texture. A matte white lamour works better than a glossy one.

### Soft gray and sage
Forgiving and modern. They add color without fighting skin tones or flowers.

### Black
Dramatic, and rich with candles. In low light it can swallow detail, so add texture or contrast with napkins and chargers.

### Bright colors
Red, hot pink and electric blue can cast color onto faces and white dresses. Use them in small amounts, such as napkins or runners.

## Shiny versus matte

Satin and sequin reflect light. With a flash, they can create hot spots; in candlelight they glow. Matte fabrics such as lamour, cotton-feel polyester and linen scatter light evenly. If your photographer shoots with flash during dinner, matte is safer. See [linen fabrics compared](/blog/linen-fabrics-compared).

## Match the room, not just the cloth

A photograph includes walls, floors, chairs, flowers and guests' clothes. A cloth that looks good alone can look wrong beside an orange wall or a green carpet. Check the venue's colors and consider the whole scene. Use your venue's photos as a reference, and if possible view the room at the time of day of your event.

## Chairs, flowers and the bride

- **Chairs.** Contrasting linens make chairs show in the frame. See [matching linens to chairs](/blog/matching-linens-to-chairs).
- **Flowers.** Pick a cloth that lets the flowers stand out, not the same color as them.
- **Dress and suits.** A bright white gown beside a bright white tablecloth can look flat. Ivory or a soft tone helps.
- **Guests.** Dark cloths make faces stand out, while very light cloths can reflect light upward and wash people out.

## Photographer checklist

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Ask your photographer</strong> which colors they prefer in your venue.</li>
<li><strong>Show a swatch</strong> of each fabric, not just a color name.</li>
<li><strong>Check lighting:</strong> bulb color, tent windows and uplighting.</li>
<li><strong>Plan the table set</strong> at least 30 minutes before photos of the room.</li>
<li><strong>Press or steam</strong> cloths so wrinkles do not catch the light.</li>
</ol>

Many photographers shoot the reception room before guests enter, so linens, place settings and centerpieces need to be done by then. Tell your rental company the time linens must be on the tables.

## Chicago light

Light changes with the season. In Chicago summer, long daylight comes through tent walls and windows, and white cloths can glare. In late fall and winter, rooms are lit by candles and warm bulbs by dinner, which flatters ivory, champagne and deeper colors. If your venue has large windows with city or lake views, the light can be strong and cool in the afternoon and golden near dusk, so the cloth that photographs well in one hour might look different in another.

## Frequently asked questions

### What color tablecloth looks best in wedding photos?

Ivory and champagne are the most reliable. They hold detail, flatter skin tones and look warm in candlelight. Soft sage and gray also photograph well.

### Does white tablecloth photograph well?

It can, with matte fabric and soft light. In strong sunlight or flash it can overexpose and lose texture. Ask your photographer how they handle white in your venue.

### Is satin bad for photos?

Not bad, but it needs care. Satin glows in candlelight but can create glare under flash, so use it in small amounts or press it well.

### Do black tablecloths work for photos?

Yes, in low light with candles and texture. They can hide detail, so pair them with lighter napkins and plates.

### Should my tablecloth match my flowers?

Not exactly. Let the cloth support the flowers by contrasting or toning with them, not by matching.

### Who should I ask about linen colors and photos?

Your photographer and your venue. They know the lighting and can tell you what has worked in the room.
