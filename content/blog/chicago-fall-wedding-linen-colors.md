**Quick answer**

For a fall wedding in Chicago, build on an ivory base cloth and add one or two accents: rust and sage, burgundy and gold, deep green and champagne, navy and copper, plum and blush, or mustard and charcoal. Use velvet, linen or crinkle for texture, and keep napkins one step deeper than the cloth. Fall light is soft and dusk comes early, so warm candles and mid-tone colors read better than pale pastels.

Fall is one of the most popular wedding seasons in the Chicago area, and linen choices set the mood faster than almost any other decor. The colors you see on every fall board, rust, burgundy and gold, are popular for a reason: they echo the leaves outside and glow in candlelight. But they can also feel heavy or dated if combined without a plan. Here are six palettes that work and how to build each.

### 1. Rust and sage

Warm terracotta napkins against sage runners or greenery. Earthy and relaxed. Works with natural wood chairs like the [cross-back](/chairs/cross-back).

### 2. Burgundy and gold

Deep wine napkins with gold accents on an ivory cloth. Formal and rich. Pairs with gold [infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity).

### 3. Forest green and champagne

Dark green napkins on champagne or ivory. Classic and calm. The green picks up fall foliage and holds up in dim light.

### 4. Navy and copper

A cooler fall option. Navy cloths or napkins with copper candle holders and accents. Good for city ballrooms and black-tie dinners.

### 5. Plum and blush

Deep purple against soft pink. Romantic and less expected. Velvet runners work well in plum.

### 6. Mustard and charcoal

Modern and bold. Mustard napkins or runners on charcoal or gray cloths. Works well in loft venues with brick and steel.

## Texture does the heavy lifting

Fall linens are about touch. Velvet runners, crinkle or linen napkins, and lace overlays make a plain palette feel layered. Satin and sequin can work for evening, but too much shine takes away from the warm, natural look. See [textured linens](/blog/textured-linens-for-events) and [linen fabrics compared](/blog/linen-fabrics-compared).

## Lighting in fall

Daylight is softer and ends earlier in autumn, so many fall receptions are in candlelight or artificial light by dinner. Warm light turns ivory to a golden tone and deepens burgundy and green. Pale, cool colors such as icy blue or lavender can look gray. Ask your venue about the lighting at your start time, and plan candles or warm string lights to flatter your palette. If you are using a tent, see our guide to [seasons and venues guides](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues) for the comfort side of fall evenings.

## Don't forget the extras

Fall palettes show best when every table matches. Remember the cake, gift, guest book and escort tables, and decide whether the head table gets a special runner. ## Pairing fall palettes with Chicago venues

Chicago-area venues each pull a palette in a different direction. A downtown hotel ballroom with cream walls and crystal accepts almost any deep accent, and burgundy, navy and gold look especially at home. A brick-and-steel loft already has warm tones, so cooler accents like navy or charcoal balance them. A barn or farm venue has wood and natural light, which suits rust, sage and mustard. A garden or conservatory-style room with a lot of glass suits forest green and plum, because the surrounding foliage supports them.

Ask your venue for photos of past tables in the room, without naming anyone else's event, and look at how the existing colors behave. If the venue has fixed carpets or drapes in a strong color, build your palette around them instead of fighting them.

## Fall details that go beyond linen

Linen is only one layer. Fall weddings also carry color in candles, glassware, flowers and place cards. Amber and smoke-colored glass pair well with rust and burgundy. Copper and brass candleholders echo warm accents. Dried grasses and foliage add texture at little cost. If you pick a bold napkin color, make the rest quiet, and if your linens are neutral, let the florals and candles bring the season. Matching one flower color to one napkin color is usually enough to make the table feel planned.

## Frequently asked questions

### What colors are best for a fall wedding?

Rust, burgundy, forest green, navy, plum and mustard on an ivory or champagne base. They echo fall leaves and look good by candlelight.

### Should fall wedding linens be dark or light?

A light base with dark accents is the safest. Ivory cloths with deep napkins and runners keep the room bright and add color where you want it.

### Do pastels work for a fall wedding?

Yes, with care. Dusty rose, sage and champagne soften a fall palette. Avoid icy pastels that look gray in dim, warm light.

### Is velvet too much for a fall wedding?

Not if you use it as an accent. A velvet runner or napkin adds richness; a velvet cloth on every table can feel heavy.

### What color napkins go with an ivory tablecloth in fall?

Burgundy, forest green, rust, navy or plum. Pick one that matches your florals so the table feels coordinated.

### How early should I order fall linens?

Earlier than you think. Fall is busy, and specialty colors have limited stock. Ask your rental company about availability for your date as soon as you pick a palette.
