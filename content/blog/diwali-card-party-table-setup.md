Diwali falls on Sunday, Nov 8, 2026, and card parties usually happen in the days around it, on weekends and late into the night. Families in Chicago, from Devon Avenue to the western suburbs, often host a card night in a basement, a living room or a banquet hall. Teen patti, rummy and flush are common. Some families play for fun and some for small stakes. That is up to you and your guests. Whatever the game, the setup question is the same: where do 20 or 30 people sit, see their cards and reach the chai?

For the wider festival, including puja, lighting and buffet flow, see [Diwali party seating and rentals](/blog/diwali-party-seating-and-rentals). This guide covers the card tables.

## How many tables and chairs

A card table is not a dining table. Players need elbow room, a place for chips or a drink, and enough space to pass cards. A rule of thumb is 24 to 30 inches of table edge per player. Teen patti works well with 4 to 6 players. Rummy fits 2 to 4.

## Table type and size

- **36-inch square tables** seat 4 and fit a game of teen patti or rummy well. Square tables make it easy to join two for a bigger game.
- **48-inch rounds** seat 5 or 6 and keep everyone the same distance from the middle.
- **6-foot banquet tables** can serve as a snack counter or a buffet. Not as good for cards, because players at the ends sit too far apart.
- **Tablecloths** keep cards from sticking and chips from sliding. A felt or dark cloth works well, and many people use a plain solid color. See the [linens guide](/rentals/linens).

Sizes vary by company, so confirm what is available when you ask for a quote.

## Light the table, not the room

Card games need light on the cards. Overhead ceiling lights in a basement can cast shadows. Candles and diyas look good but do not give enough light to read cards, and open flames near paper, tablecloths and sleeves need thought.

- Put a warm lamp or a string of lights over each table.
- Use battery-powered diyas and candles on the tables for the glow. See [battery-powered event lighting](/blog/battery-powered-event-lighting).
- If you have a real diya for the puja, keep it away from the card tables and off the tablecloth.
- Plan outlets. Several lamps, a kettle and a warming tray can trip a circuit. See [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) and [lighting rentals](/rentals/lighting).

## The chai and snack station

Put chai and snacks on their own counter, away from the card tables. People will get up, refill and chat, and you do not want drinks near the cards and chips. A rough plan is one 6-foot table for every 20 to 25 guests, with the chai at one end, snacks in the middle and plates and napkins at the start. A coffee urn or two works well for chai. See [how many coffee urns](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party).

Think about cups. Small cups hold less and need more refills. A mix of paper and ceramic cups keeps the line moving.

## Late nights and older relatives

A Diwali card night can run past midnight. By 10 pm, chairs matter. Folding chairs are fine for an hour and tiring after four. If your players will sit for hours, ask about padded chairs.

Older relatives tend to be the heart of these nights, and they have the most trouble with low sofas and long walks. Seat them where they can see everything, near the snacks and the restroom, in a chair with arms. See [comfortable chairs for older guests](/blog/comfortable-chairs-for-older-guests) and [planning an event for older guests](/blog/planning-an-event-for-older-guests). Chicago in November is cold, so leave a place for coats near the door. See [coat racks](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Diwali weekends book early.** Tables and chairs for the weekend nearest Nov 8 are in demand, so ask for your date as soon as you know it.
- **Basement ceilings and stairs.** Measure the stairs and the turn. A table that will not fit down the stairs does not help you. Ask for tables that fold.
- **Venue rules.** If you host at a hall or community center, check what the building allows for candles, open flames and card games.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many card tables do I need for a Diwali party?

Plan one table for every 4 to 6 players. For 20 guests with 16 playing, that is 4 square tables. Add extra chairs along the walls for people waiting to join.

### What size table is best for teen patti?

A 36-inch square seats 4, and a 48-inch round seats 5 to 6. Both give each player about 24 to 30 inches of edge. Larger tables spread players too far apart.

### How do I light a card table?

Put a warm lamp or a string of lights over each table. Candles and diyas add mood but not enough light to read cards. Battery-powered ones are safer near tablecloths and paper.

### Where should the chai and snacks go?

On a separate table or counter away from the card tables. That keeps spills off the cards and gives people a place to line up. One 6-foot table per 20 to 25 guests is a rough rule.

### Do I need to rent chairs for older guests?

Often yes, if your home chairs are low or the sofas are deep. Chairs with arms and a firm seat make getting up easier. Put them near the snacks and the restroom.

### How early should I book rentals for Diwali week?

As soon as you know the date. Diwali is on Sunday, Nov 8, 2026, and weekends near it are busy. The earlier you ask, the more likely your first choice of items is open.
