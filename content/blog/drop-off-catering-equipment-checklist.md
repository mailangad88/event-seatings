**Quick answer**

Drop-off catering usually arrives in disposable pans with no heat, tables, plates or tools. To serve it, rent a table per 25 to 30 guests, a chafing frame and fuel for every hot pan, serving utensils for every dish, plates, flatware and napkins, a beverage setup and trash bins. Pick it up or confirm delivery time so food is out within 2 hours of cooking.

</div>

Drop-off catering is a great deal for Chicago-area graduation parties, office lunches and church events. The caterer brings the food in pans, and you handle everything else. That "everything else" is where parties go wrong: cold lasagna, no spoons, a buffet stacked on a card table. This checklist covers what you need to serve drop-off food well.

## What drop-off usually includes

Most drop-off caterers deliver food in aluminum pans with lids, and some provide serving utensils or heat sources for an added fee. Ask exactly what is included and what is not. Do not assume. Ask:

- Are the pans hot, and for how long can they hold?
- Are chafing frames, fuel or serving spoons included?
- Do they provide plates, napkins and cutlery?
- When will it arrive, and will they set it up?

## The checklist

<div class="table-wrap">

| Category | What to rent or supply | Quantity rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| Tables | 6 or 8-foot banquet tables for food | 1 table per 25 to 30 guests |
| Linens | Floor-length cloths or runners | 1 per table, plus 1 spare |
| Heat | Chafing frames, fuel, water pans | 1 per hot pan |
| Cold | Coolers, ice, bowls over ice | 1 cooler per 25 guests |
| Utensils | Tongs, spoons, ladles | 1 per dish, plus spares |
| Plates | Dinner plates or sturdy paper | 1.5 to 2 per guest |
| Flatware | Forks, knives, spoons | 1 set per guest, plus 10% |
| Napkins | Cloth or paper | 2 per guest |
| Drinks | Dispensers, cups, ice | See beverage guide |
| Trash | Bins with liners, recycling | 1 per 50 guests |
| Seating | Chairs and tables | Seats for 70 to 100% of guests |

## Heat is the thing people skip

Hot pans lose heat quickly. A foil pan from the caterer is not a serving dish, and food held under 135 degrees F for long becomes unsafe. A chafing frame holds a full-size pan over a water bath and fuel can. Use the formula in [how many chafing dishes you need](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet), and ask your caterer for the pan size so the frames fit. If you do not have fuel or electrical hookups, an insulated food carrier can hold hot pans for a few hours before service.

## Set up the table before the food arrives

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Drape tables</strong> and place them away from the door.</li>
<li><strong>Set frames</strong> and water pans, light fuel 15 minutes before food arrives.</li>
<li><strong>Stage plates, napkins and flatware</strong> at the end of the line.</li>
<li><strong>Place utensils</strong> beside each dish with name cards.</li>
<li><strong>Put drinks and trash</strong> at separate stations.</li>
</ol>

## Tips for office and church events

Corporate lunches in Chicago high-rises often have loading dock and elevator rules, and delivery windows can be narrow. Ask building management where the caterer can park and how long they have. Churches and community halls may have a kitchen, but check the oven, outlets and refrigerator capacity before you rely on them.

For outdoor graduation parties in the suburbs, hold the food inside or under a tent until serving, and read [keeping food hot outdoors](/blog/keeping-food-hot-outdoors).

## Leftovers and cleanup

Put leftovers in the fridge within 2 hours of serving (1 hour if it is above 90 degrees F), the standard guideline. Use shallow containers so they cool quickly. Foil pans can often go to recycling after rinsing, but check your local rules. Have a recycling and trash plan, see [trash, recycling and composting stations](/blog/trash-recycling-composting-stations-for-events).

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I need to serve drop-off catering?

Tables, linens, heat for hot pans, serving utensils, plates, flatware, napkins, drinks and trash bins. Ask the caterer what is included before you rent.

### How do I keep drop-off food hot?

Use chafing frames with fuel and a water pan, an insulated carrier, or an oven set to a low heat until service. Hot food should stay at 135 degrees F or above.

### How many plates should I get for a buffet?

About 1.5 to 2 plates per guest, because guests often go back for seconds or a dessert plate. Order sturdy disposable plates if you choose paper.

### How long can food sit out?

Two hours at room temperature, or one hour if the air is above 90 degrees F. After that, refrigerate or discard it.

### Does drop-off catering include serving spoons?

Sometimes. Many caterers include basic spoons or tongs for an added fee, but ask before the day.

### How early should tables be set up?

At least 30 minutes before the food arrives, with fuel and water pans ready. It is easier to wait for food than to hold food waiting for a table.
