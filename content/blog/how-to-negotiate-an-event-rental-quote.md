**Quick answer**

You can't haggle down the price of each chair, but you can change the things that drive the total: a wider delivery window, a different pickup day, a weekday date, bundling items, doing your own setup, or swapping a pricier style. Ask what you can change in exchange for a lower total. Don't expect cuts on damage waivers, insurance paperwork or stock that is nearly gone.

</div>

Most event rentals are not priced like a car. A rental company has a set rate for each item, a crew, a truck and a limited number of weekends. The room to negotiate is small on price and larger on how and when the job is done. This guide shows what to ask for, and what not to ask for, so you get a better quote without losing the service.

It builds on [how to save money on event rentals](/blog/how-to-save-money-on-event-rentals) and [how to compare event rental quotes](/blog/how-to-compare-event-rental-quotes). Read those for the big picture. This one is about the conversation.

## The flexible items

**Delivery window.** A wide window, like 8 am to 2 pm, lets the company plan one efficient route. A narrow window, like 1 hour, needs a special trip. If you are home all day, say so. If you need a tight window, expect to pay for it.

**Pickup day.** Next-morning pickup after a Saturday party is often easier for the company than a late-night collection. A Monday pickup may cost less than a midnight pickup. See [late-night and early pickup fees](/blog/late-night-and-early-pickup-fees-event-rentals). Ask what the trade is, and make sure the items are safe overnight.

**Weekday dates.** A Friday or Sunday event can be easier to book than a Saturday, and some companies price differently. If your date has room, ask. [Chicago winter planning](/blog/chicago-winter-event-planning) also shows how off-peak months affect your options.

**Bundling.** Putting chairs, tables, linens and tableware on one order often saves on delivery stops and paperwork. It is also simpler for you. Our quotes keep everything together, and we check the date with the rental companies for the whole list.

**Self-setup.** If your venue lets you set up, ask what the price is without setup and breakdown. The crew's time is part of the cost. Read [setup and breakdown fees](/blog/setup-and-breakdown-fees-event-rentals) first, because tents, dance floors and heavy items almost always need the crew.

**Swapping styles.** A less common chair or a specialty linen costs more than the standard version. Ask for a quote on the standard item and compare. You might keep a special style for the head table and use a basic style elsewhere.

## What is not flexible

- **Unit rates on a peak date.** If a company is almost sold out, it rarely cuts the rate.
- **Safety items.** Tent anchors, weights, fencing and fuel rules are not negotiable.
- **Damage waiver terms and paperwork.** You can decline an optional waiver, but you can't change what it covers. See [what a damage waiver covers](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained).
- **Stock.** If there are 120 gold chairs, you can't get 160.
- **Venue requirements.** If the venue needs a certificate or a certain crew time, the rental company can't skip it.

## A simple negotiation script

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Share your constraints first.</strong> Date, guest count, access and budget range.</li>
<li><strong>Ask what drives the total.</strong> "What are the biggest lines on this quote?"</li>
<li><strong>Offer a flexible choice.</strong> "I can take a wider window or a Monday pickup. Does that change the price?"</li>
<li><strong>Ask for options, not a discount.</strong> "What would you swap to bring this down 10 percent?"</li>
<li><strong>Get it in writing</strong> with the new quote, and check the terms again.</li>
</ol>

Asking "can you do better?" gets a shrug. Asking "what can change?" gets a list. It also keeps the service level the same, which matters on delivery day.

## Compare before you ask

Put the quotes side by side before you push. A cheaper quote may leave out setup, the waiver or delivery. [Compare the services, not just the unit rates](/blog/how-to-compare-event-rental-quotes). If you ask one company to match another's number, make sure the two lists are the same.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Trades are often one-way.** Moving to a weekday date can work. Moving back later may not, because the date may be gone. Don't change plans until it is in writing.
- **A discount in one place can turn into a fee in another.** A lower price with "no changes after booking" may cost you if the guest count changes. Check [the cancellation and date-change rules](/blog/event-rental-cancellation-and-date-change-policy).
- **Hard-to-get items are the weakest bargaining chips.** Chairs in a set color or a heater in October come first. Spend your flexibility on tables or linens.

## Be a good customer

Pay the deposit on time, give the final count when asked, keep your window, and have someone at the door. Companies remember hosts who make the day easy. That does more for you than any discount, and it shows in the care you get on delivery.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you negotiate the price of event rentals?

Sometimes, but mostly on the terms around the items instead of the item price. A wider delivery window, a weekday date, bundling and doing your own setup can lower the total. Peak-date rates and low-stock items rarely move.

### What can I ask a rental company to change?

Ask about the delivery window, the pickup day, setup and breakdown, swapping to a standard style and bundling items on one order. Ask what each change does to the price, and get the new quote in writing.

### Is it rude to ask for a discount on rentals?

No, as long as you ask politely and offer something, such as a flexible time. It's fair to ask "what could change to bring this down?" It is less likely to work to ask for a lower rate on items that are in short supply.

### Will a weekday date cost less?

It can, because weekdays are less busy, but it depends on the company and the item. Ask for the price on both dates. Also check that your venue and guests can make a weekday work.

### Can I get a discount for ordering more?

Maybe not on the rate, but large orders can save on delivery and handling per item. Ask whether bundling everything with one company changes the delivery fee or the setup charge.

### Should I decline the damage waiver to save money?

Only if you have read the replacement costs and you are comfortable with the risk. For parties with kids, an open bar or outdoor ground, it may be worth it. See our [waiver guide](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained).
