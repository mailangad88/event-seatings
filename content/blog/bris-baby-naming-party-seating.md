**Quick answer**

A bris (brit milah) and a baby naming are short ceremonies followed by a meal, usually a morning brunch or lunch for 30 to 80 guests. Plan rows of chairs facing the ceremony spot (for 20 to 40 minutes), a chair of honor and a table set aside for the ceremony items your mohel or rabbi requests, then brunch tables with a buffet, a quiet nursing corner and extra chairs for older relatives. Ask the mohel, rabbi or officiant what space and seating they need before you rent anything.

</div>

A bris takes place on the eighth day after a baby boy's birth, in Jewish tradition, and a baby naming (simchat bat or a naming ceremony) welcomes a daughter or a child of any gender. Families often hold both at home, a synagogue hall or a banquet room. Because the date is set by the baby's birth, there is little time to plan, so rentals are often ordered within days. This guide focuses on seating and room flow. The ceremony itself is directed by your mohel, rabbi or officiant, so take your cues from them.

## Two spaces in one morning

The event has two phases with different needs:

1. **The ceremony.** Guests sit or stand in a semicircle or in rows around the spot where the ceremony takes place. The room needs a clear sightline and a small table for items the officiant requests. Chairs are usually for older relatives and parents, with others standing behind.
2. **The meal.** Guests move to tables for brunch or lunch, with a buffet or catered stations.

If you have one room, you may reset chairs from rows into groups during a short break. If you have two rooms, the ceremony room needs fewer chairs than the meal room.

## Counts for 30, 50 and 80 guests

<div class="table-wrap">

| Guests | Ceremony chairs | Brunch seats | Buffet length | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 12 to 15 | 30 | 6 ft | 1 chair of honor |
| 50 | 20 to 25 | 50 | 8 ft | high chairs, nursing corner |
| 80 | 30 to 40 | 80 | 8 to 12 ft | second buffet side |

</div>

Many guests stand for the ceremony, so you do not need a chair for everyone. For the meal, give everyone a seat. Tables of 8 to 10 or 6-foot banquet tables work. See [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## The chair of honor and sandek chair

Some bris ceremonies include a special chair for the person given the honor of holding the baby. Ask your mohel what is needed. Many families use a regular chair, covered or decorated, with a small stool or table nearby. A [Louis Medallion chair](/chairs/louis-medallion) (estimated $20 to $30) or a [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) ($18 to $25) makes a good chair of honor. Do not order anything until you ask your mohel or rabbi what the ceremony requires.

## Keeping it kind to a new family

The parents are a few days postpartum, and a new baby adds stress. A few touches help:

- **A comfortable chair with arm support** for the mother, near the front.
- **A private nursing or resting corner** with a chair and a small table, away from noise.
- **High chairs and a changing area** for older siblings and cousins. See [high chairs and baby station at events](/blog/high-chairs-and-baby-station-at-events).
- **Delivery and pickup that do not disturb the family.** Ask the rental company for a delivery window the day before, if possible.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a bris?

A dozen to 40 chairs for the ceremony, depending on how many guests prefer to sit, and a seat for each guest at brunch. Ask your mohel what space they need.

### Where does the ceremony take place?

Usually in a living room, synagogue hall or banquet room. The officiant chooses a spot with room for the key participants.

### How fast can I get rentals?

Companies vary. Contact them as soon as you know the date. Ask for delivery the day before if possible.

### Do I need a special chair?

Some families use a chair of honor for the person who holds the baby. Ask the mohel and rabbi what your ceremony requires.

### How do I plan for the new parents?

Give the mother a comfortable chair, a quiet corner and a buffet that does not require her to stand in line. Ask a friend to host.

### Can I host at a synagogue hall?

Yes. Ask the synagogue what furniture is included and what rules apply to catering and decor.
