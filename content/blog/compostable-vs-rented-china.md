**Quick answer**

Rent china for seated, formal or long events: it looks better, holds hot food and creates no trash. Choose compostable plates for large outdoor parties, park events with glass restrictions and late-night snacks where plates leave the room. Compostable only helps the environment if a composting service actually takes them, because many Chicago-area trash systems send them to landfill. Ask your village, venue or hauler before you assume.

</div>

Hosts planning a graduation party, a company picnic or a wedding reception often reach this fork: pay to rent real plates and wash nothing, or buy cases of compostable plates and haul out bags of trash. Neither is always better. The right answer depends on guest count, setting and where the waste goes.

## The compost question

A plate labeled compostable is not the same as a plate that gets composted. Commercial composting needs a service that accepts it, and many curbside programs do not. If the plates go into a regular dumpster, they end up in landfill like any other. Before buying, ask your venue, caterer or waste hauler whether they take compostable service ware and how to sort it. The City of Chicago, each suburb and each venue handle waste differently, so verify locally and do not rely on packaging claims alone.

## When china is the better choice

- **Seated dinners** where guests spend an hour with the plate.
- **Hot, saucy or heavy menus** such as roasts, pasta and braised dishes.
- **Formal tables** with linens, chargers and flatware; see [matching dinnerware to linens](/blog/matching-dinnerware-to-linens).
- **Indoor venues** with staff to clear plates.
- **Events with a waste limit**, where trash removal is restricted.

## When compostable is the better choice

- **Park and lakefront events** where glass or ceramic is limited. Check your park district permit rules, since each sets its own.
- **Large casual parties** with 150 or more guests and little help.
- **Late-night snacks** on a dance floor.
- **Kids' parties**; see [dinnerware for kids](/blog/dinnerware-for-kids-at-events).
- **Food trucks and drop-off catering** with grab-and-go service.

## The hybrid approach

Many hosts mix the two: china for dinner, compostable for cocktail hour or late-night food. This keeps the dinner table polished and reduces how many pieces guests can carry away. Cocktail hour plate guidance is in [appetizer plates for cocktail hour](/blog/appetizer-plates-cocktail-hour).

## Hidden costs on both sides

Rented china adds delivery, pickup and breakage terms; see [rental breakage and loss fees](/blog/rental-breakage-and-loss-fees-explained). Compostable plates add the cost of enough bins and bags, and the labor to haul them. For a 150-guest outdoor party that needs 300 plates, you also need to plan where 300 used plates go. We do not publish dinnerware prices, so ask for a quote with both options.

## Chicago notes

Lakefront parks, forest preserves and many suburban park districts set their own rules on glass, alcohol and amplified sound. Rules change by site, so check with the park district or forest preserve before choosing china for an outdoor event. Wind off the lake can lift light plates, so compostable plates need weights or covers.

## A quick decision rule

If guests will sit for more than 30 minutes with a hot meal, rent china. If they will stand, walk or eat outdoors with limited staff, choose a sturdy compostable plate and confirm where the waste goes. If you cannot decide, order a small sample set of each and test them with your actual menu. Whichever you pick, count plates generously, because guests at casual parties use more than one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are compostable plates really better for the environment?

Only if they reach a composting facility. In a regular dumpster, they go to landfill. Ask your venue or hauler how they handle compostable service ware.

### Is rented china cheaper than compostable plates?

It depends on count and service. China has delivery and per-piece fees; compostable plates have case costs and cleanup. Request a quote to compare for your guest count.

### Can I use china at a park event?

Sometimes. Parks and forest preserves set their own rules on glass and breakables. Check with the permitting office first.

### Do compostable plates hold up to hot food?

Many do, but thin ones can bend with heavy sauces. Pick sturdy plates and test one with your menu.

### What about plastic plates?

They work but are not compostable. They often look casual and can bend under heavy food. Compare against compostable for your menu.

### Can I mix china and compostable?

Yes. Use china for the dinner course and compostable for cocktail hour or late-night food.
