**Quick answer**

A welcome party is a casual gathering the night before the wedding, or the night before the rehearsal dinner, for out-of-town guests. Plan it two to three hours, keep food simple (stations, appetizers or a casual buffet), invite everyone who traveled, and budget for about half the guest list. Seat about half your guests and leave room to mingle.

</div>

When guests fly in, they arrive tired, hungry and unsure of who they will know. A welcome party solves that. It lets people meet before the wedding, lowers the pressure on the big day and gives you a chance to say hello without a schedule. It does not have to be large or fancy, but it does need a plan.

## Who to invite and when to host

Decide first how big the gathering is. Some couples invite only out-of-town guests and the wedding party. Others invite everyone coming to the wedding. A smaller party is cheaper and easier to host, while a larger one avoids hurt feelings. Whatever you pick, state it clearly on the website and invitation, so no one assumes. Our guide on [how to cut a wedding guest list](/blog/how-to-cut-a-wedding-guest-list) helps with these choices.

The timing depends on the weekend. If you have a rehearsal dinner the night before the wedding, hold the welcome party the night before that or after the rehearsal for the larger group. See [rehearsal dinner rentals](/blog/rehearsal-dinner-rentals) for that event. Keep welcome parties to two or three hours so guests can rest.

## Venue and food ideas

Choose a place that feels relaxed. Options include a restaurant private room, a brewery or taproom with a reserved area, a park pavilion, a backyard or a hotel lounge. Casual food works better than a formal dinner: taco bar, pizza, barbecue, appetizer stations or a dessert and coffee bar. Make sure to have vegetarian and allergy-friendly options. Ask each venue about group pricing and what is included; see [restaurant private room vs full venue](/blog/restaurant-private-room-vs-full-venue).

Make the space easy to mingle in. Use high-top tables, a few lounge chairs and benches for older guests, and keep the food and drink stations separate to avoid crowding.

## Rentals and setup

If your venue is a blank space, a small rental order can make it comfortable. Plan seats for about half the guests, a few cocktail tables, long tables for food, a bar table and linens. See [how many cocktail tables for a standing reception](/blog/how-many-cocktail-tables-for-standing-reception) and [lounge furniture for cocktail hour](/blog/lounge-furniture-for-cocktail-hour). Add heaters or fans depending on the season, and lighting if it will be dark.

Welcome bags, signs and a seating hint are small touches that help guests feel expected. Put a simple schedule for the weekend on the table.

## Keep it simple for the hosts

The couple should be able to enjoy the night. Assign a friend to greet guests, one to handle the bar and one to clean up. Plan a short toast and then step aside. Avoid long speeches, games or elaborate programs. A welcome party works when people are relaxed, fed and talking. Arrange rides or give transit tips for guests unfamiliar with the area.

## Ideas that fit different budgets

A welcome party can be as simple as pizza in a rented room or as polished as a catered cocktail evening. For a small budget, consider a backyard or park gathering with a taco or barbecue spread, rented tables and chairs, and a simple drink station. For a medium budget, a restaurant private room or taproom with appetizer stations works well. For a larger budget, hire a caterer and bartender and add lounge furniture and lighting.

Whatever you choose, the details that matter are warmth and ease: name tags if the groups are mixed, a place to sit, something for kids if they are invited and a clear start and end time. Guests remember feeling welcome far more than they remember the menu.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago welcome parties work well in neighborhoods where guests can walk to restaurants or lodging. If your guests are staying in the suburbs, choose a location near the main hotel. Weather can change, especially in spring and fall, so have an indoor plan if you choose a patio or park. For public parks, check with your village or park district about reservations and rules. If you host in a backyard, consider neighbors, noise and parking, and tell them in advance. Winter parties need coat racks; see [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a wedding welcome party?

It is a casual gathering for out-of-town guests, usually the night before the rehearsal dinner or the wedding. It lets people meet and relax before the main events.

### Who hosts the welcome party?

Anyone can host: the couple, parents or friends. The host is whoever pays. State the arrangement clearly to avoid confusion.

### Who should be invited to a welcome party?

Many couples invite all out-of-town guests and the wedding party. Others invite everyone attending the wedding. Be clear about the group.

### How much does a welcome party cost?

It depends on guest count, venue and food. Casual stations are usually cheaper than a plated dinner. Get quotes for food, drinks and rentals.

### What food should I serve at a welcome party?

Casual options work best, such as tacos, pizza, barbecue or appetizer stations. Include vegetarian and allergy-friendly choices. See [dietary restrictions planning](/blog/dietary-restrictions-planning-for-events).

### How long should a welcome party last?

Two to three hours is plenty. Guests are tired from travel and have a big day ahead.

### Do I need a welcome party and a rehearsal dinner?

Not always. If your guest list is small, a single combined dinner can work. Many couples keep the rehearsal dinner small and the welcome party larger.
