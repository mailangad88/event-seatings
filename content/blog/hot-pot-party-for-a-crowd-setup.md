Hot pot is the best kind of party for a Chicago winter. Everyone sits around a steaming pot, cooks their own food and stays for hours. It is a natural fit for Lunar New Year on Saturday, Feb 6, 2027, and for any cold night. It is also the party most likely to trip a breaker, steam up your windows or leave you short on bowls. A little planning fixes all three.

## How many pots and burners

The rule of thumb is one pot for every 4 to 6 people. Any more and guests reach across each other, and the broth cools while they wait. A round table that seats 8 to 10 usually gets two pots, one on each side, so no one is far from the food.

A split pot, with two broths in one pot, is popular. It lets you offer a mild broth and a spicy one without extra burners. For 20 guests, plan 4 pots on 2 round tables. For 30, plan 6 pots on 3 tables.

## Electrical limits are the real limit

This is the part that surprises hosts. A standard household circuit in the United States is 15 or 20 amps at 120 volts. That is about 1,800 watts on a 15 amp circuit and 2,400 watts on a 20 amp circuit, and a circuit should not run at its limit for hours. Many portable induction burners are rated somewhere around 1,200 to 1,800 watts. Look at the label on yours.

Do the simple math. One 1,800-watt burner nearly fills a 15 amp circuit by itself. Two on the same circuit will usually trip the breaker, and hot pot runs for hours.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Read the wattage</strong> on every burner's label.</li>
<li><strong>Find your circuits.</strong> Outlets in one room are often on the same breaker, and kitchens and dining rooms are sometimes on different ones.</li>
<li><strong>Run one, or at most two, induction burners per circuit,</strong> and nothing else big on it.</li>
<li><strong>Use heavy-duty cords</strong> rated for the load, plugged directly into the wall. Do not use light cords or power strips for burners.</li>
<li><strong>Keep other loads off the circuit,</strong> such as a microwave, a rice cooker or space heaters.</li>
</ol>

Gas burners that use butane canisters avoid the electrical problem. They need fresh air, and butane canisters have their own safety instructions. Follow the label, and check your building rules, because some buildings restrict them. Our [event power needs checklist](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) helps you map outlets. For a larger party, ask a licensed electrician about adding capacity, or move the party to a hall with more circuits.

## Plates, bowls, ladles and more

A hot pot meal has many small pieces. Count for each guest:

## Ventilation and steam

A hot pot party fills the air with steam, oil and smell. A window cracked in a nearby room, a kitchen vent fan and an open door to a hall keep the air moving. Smoke detectors may go off, so check where they are. With gas burners, fresh air matters even more, and you should never run them in a closed room.

In January, opening a window lets in Chicago cold. Put guests with coats on the far side, or open a window in the kitchen instead of the dining room. A tent with side walls is not a good place for gas burners.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Tables get heavy and hot.** A pot of broth is heavy, and it spills. Use a table with a sturdy top, and put a heat-safe mat under each burner. Check that folding table legs are locked.
- **Linens and steam do not mix.** A cloth soaks up broth. Use a simple cloth you can wash, or cover the table with disposable paper over a cloth.
- **Count chairs and cords.** A cord across the floor is a trip hazard. Place tables near outlets, and tape cords down.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many hot pots do I need for 20 people?

Plan 4 pots, one for every 5 guests. That gives 2 round tables with 2 pots each. Add a spare burner in case one fails.

### How many induction burners can I plug into one outlet?

One, at most two, per circuit, and only if the labels show it is safe. A 15 amp circuit supports about 1,800 watts, and one burner can use most of it. Check the wattage on your unit.

### Do I need a special table for hot pot?

No, but you need a sturdy, level table with room for the pot and food. A round table of 60 or 72 inches works well. Use heat-safe mats.

### How much food do I need per person?

A common rule of thumb is 1/2 pound of sliced meat plus a plate of vegetables, tofu and noodles per guest. Plan more if guests are big eaters.

### Is it safe to use a gas burner indoors?

Follow the instructions on the burner and canister, and keep the room ventilated. Check your building rules, because some buildings do not allow them. An induction burner is an option where gas is not.

### Can I serve hot pot buffet style?

Yes. Put raw ingredients on a side table or sideboard with tongs and let guests bring plates to their table. This keeps the table clear.
