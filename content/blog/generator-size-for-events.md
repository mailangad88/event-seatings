**Quick answer**

Add up the running watts of everything you will plug in, then add the extra starting surge of motors like refrigerators and fans, then add about 20 to 25 percent headroom. A backyard party with LED string lights, a DJ and a few fans often fits within 3,000 to 5,000 watts. Add caterers' warming equipment or air conditioning and you may need 7,500 watts or more. Ask your generator supplier to confirm the size.

</div>

When there is no outlet near the party, a generator is the answer, and sizing it is straightforward math that people skip. Buy too small and the breaker trips during dinner. Buy too large and you pay for fuel and noise that you do not use. This guide shows the formula, the numbers to gather and a worked example.

## The formula

**Required watts = (running watts + largest starting surge) × 1.25**

- **Running watts** is what each device draws while operating. It is printed on the label or in the manual.
- **Starting surge** is the extra draw for a few seconds when motors like refrigerators, fans and pumps start. It can be two to three times the running figure. You only add the largest single surge because motors do not all start at once.
- **Headroom** keeps the generator below its limit, which helps reliability and fuel use.

If a device lists amps instead of watts, use **watts = volts × amps**. A device that draws 5 amps on a 120-volt supply uses 600 watts.

## Gather the list

Ask each vendor what they will plug in and what they draw. Do this early, because the caterer's list is the one that surprises people.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Equipment | What to ask |
|---|---|
| DJ or band | Total watts for speakers, mixer and lights |
| Lighting | LED or incandescent, total watts |
| Caterer | Warmers, coffee urns, induction, refrigeration |
| Bar | Ice machine, blenders, refrigerators |
| Fans, heaters, coolers | Watts each and how many |
| Photo booth, projector | Watts per unit |
| Charging station | Number of devices |

</div>

Many caterers bring propane or butane for cooking and ask for power only for warming and refrigeration. Confirm instead of assuming. The [event power needs checklist](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) lists each category.

## Single phase, 120 and 240

Portable generators offer 120-volt outlets, and many also offer a 240-volt outlet. Most event gear needs 120 volts. Each outlet has its own amp limit, so spread the load across outlets and across both circuits if the unit has them. A 20-amp, 120-volt outlet delivers up to 2,400 watts, but keep continuous loads below about 80 percent of that. Never overload one outlet with a multi-plug strip.

## Fuel and run time

Fuel use depends on the load, the engine and the fuel type, so check the manufacturer's run-time chart. Half-load run times are the most useful figure. Ask the supplier how long a full tank lasts at your load, and plan a refuel break. Never refuel a hot or running generator. For a four-hour reception, many planners prefer a generator that can run the entire event on one fill.

## Dedicated circuits for critical gear

Put the DJ and the sound system on their own outlet or generator if you can. Lights and caterer equipment cause voltage dips that produce hum in audio. A separate small inverter generator for audio costs little compared with a ruined toast.

## Chicago notes

Public parks and lakefront venues may restrict generators, and some suburbs limit noise hours. Ask your park district or village hall before you order. For the Forest Preserve District of Cook County, DuPage County or any other forest preserve, check the permit conditions for your site.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size generator do I need for a backyard party?

Most backyard parties with LED lights, a DJ and a few fans need 3,000 to 5,000 watts. Add caterer equipment, and you may need more.

### How do I calculate generator watts?

Add running watts of all devices, add the largest starting surge, and multiply by 1.25. If you only know amps, multiply by the voltage.

### Can one generator run a DJ and lights?

Yes if the total fits. Ideally, put audio on a separate outlet or small inverter generator to avoid hum.

### What is the difference between running and starting watts?

Running watts is the steady draw. Starting watts is the short surge when a motor starts, often two to three times higher.

### Do I need a generator if the venue has power?

Not usually, but venues can limit outlets. Ask for the number of circuits and amps available.

### Should I rent or buy a generator?

Rent for one event. Suppliers handle sizing, fuel and delivery.

### How loud is a generator?

Depends on the model. Inverter types are quieter. Check the decibel rating and the distance it was measured from.
