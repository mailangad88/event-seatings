**Quick answer**

Decorate a tent in layers: ceiling draping or a liner to hide the frame, hanging greenery or florals as focal points, lighting to show off both, and tables and chairs to ground the room. Get approval before hanging anything, since tents have weight limits at each rigging point. Choose fire-retardant fabrics where required, and keep decor away from heaters and exits.

A white tent is a blank canvas, and a blank canvas can look cold. Decor turns it into a room. It also adds weight, wind load and fire risk, so the plan has to include your tent company. Here is how to decorate a tent without breaking anything.

## Start at the ceiling

The ceiling is the largest surface and the most visible. Options include:

- **Liners.** Fabric panels that cover the roof and hide the frame. Many tent companies offer them.
- **Swagged draping.** Sheer fabric in swoops from the center to the sides, often with lights behind.
- **Canopy or ceiling tent.** A smaller draped area over the dance floor or head table.
- **Greenery garlands.** Eucalyptus, ivy or ferns along the frame.
- **Hanging florals.** Floral clouds or installations at the center.

## Rigging and weight limits

Tent frames are strong in the right places. Hanging a heavy floral cloud from the middle of a roof span can overload it. Ask your tent company:

1. Where can we hang decor, and what is the weight limit per point?
2. Do you provide rigging hardware, or must we use yours?
3. Can installers attach to the frame during the install day?
4. How does decor affect the wind plan?

Items hung from the tent also add to the wind load, so a heavy installation may mean a lower wind trigger. See [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety).

## Fire safety

Chicago's permit process requires tent fabric to have a fire resistance certificate under NFPA 701. Decor fabric needs similar attention in many places. Ask your village what it requires for draping and flammable decor, and request a flame retardant certificate from the fabric rental company. Keep decor away from heaters, lights that get hot and exits. Winnetka's tent form, for instance, asks for flammable materials to stay out of the tent. See [heating a tent](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall) and [permits in the suburbs](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs).

## Walls and entrances

Sidewalls are decor too. Clear panels show the garden, and solid white makes a backdrop for uplighting. See [tent sidewalls](/blog/tent-sidewalls-clear-window-solid). Entrances deserve attention: an arch of greenery, signage and a defined welcome table make a good first impression, but keep door paths clear for exits.

## Flowers in a hot or windy tent

Heat wilts flowers. Ask your florist about hardy choices and delivery timing. Place arrangements away from fans and open sides. Taller arrangements can block sightlines, so use clear stands or low arrangements for conversation. Our [tent lighting ideas](/blog/tent-lighting-ideas) pair well with florals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you decorate the inside of a tent?

Start with the ceiling using a liner or draping, add hanging greenery or florals, light it, then style the tables. Get your tent company's approval for hanging points and weight.

### Can I hang flowers from a rented tent?

Usually, at approved points and within the weight limit. Ask your tent company before the florist plans a heavy installation.

### Is ceiling draping flame retardant?

It should be, where required. Ask the fabric supplier for a flame retardant certificate and your village what it requires.

### How do I make a white tent look less plain?

Use a ceiling liner or draping, add greenery and lighting, and choose warm linens and wood-tone chairs. Sidewall choices help too.

### Can decor affect tent safety in wind?

Yes. Heavy hung items add load, so tell the tent company what you plan to hang so they can include it in the wind plan.

### What decor works best in hot weather?

Hardy flowers, greenery and light draping. Avoid heavy fabrics that trap heat.
