**Quick answer**

Most events need four glass types: a water goblet (about 10 to 12 oz), a wine glass (about 10 to 12 oz), a champagne flute or coupe (about 5 to 7 oz) and a rocks or highball glass for the bar. A smaller list works for casual events: a stemless wine glass and a highball cover almost everything. Order by what you serve, not by what looks fancy in a catalog.

Open a rental catalog and you can find 30 glass shapes. Most Chicago-area weddings and parties use four or five. This guide explains what each one does, how much it holds, and how to choose without overbuying.

## What each glass is for

**Water goblet.** A short-stemmed glass that sits above the dinner knife. It holds 10 to 12 oz and has a wide mouth so servers can top it off quickly. A stemless tumbler is a casual swap.

**Red wine glass.** A large bowl, usually 11 to 14 oz, shaped to give reds room to breathe. You fill it only about a third full.

**White wine glass.** A slightly narrower bowl, 8 to 11 oz, which keeps whites cool and concentrates aroma. Many events use one all-purpose wine glass for both and skip this.

**Champagne flute.** Tall and narrow, 5 to 7 oz, which preserves bubbles. Flutes are the classic toast glass. For a comparison, see [champagne flutes vs coupes](/blog/champagne-flutes-vs-coupes).

**Coupe.** A wide, shallow bowl, about 4 to 6 oz. It is a vintage look and is also used for cocktails. It spills more easily, which matters if guests are standing.

**Rocks (old fashioned) glass.** A short tumbler of 6 to 10 oz for spirits over ice and for short cocktails.

**Highball glass.** A tall tumbler of 10 to 14 oz for mixed drinks, soda and spirits with a mixer. If you can only rent one bar glass, rent this.

**Beer glass.** A pint (16 oz) or pilsner. Many weddings skip it and pour beer in a plastic or paper cup at a casual outdoor event.

## Stem or stemless?

Stemmed glasses look more formal and keep body heat off the drink. They are also easier to knock over and break. Stemless glasses stack better, travel in smaller racks and survive a lawn or a dance floor. For outdoor and tent receptions, stemless saves breakage. For a black-tie ballroom dinner, stems read as formal in photos.

## Match glassware to the table

Glass looks different against different linens and chairs. Clear stemware on a white [tablecloth](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) is the standard. Tinted glass in smoke or amber works on a dark table. Gold-rimmed glasses echo [gold infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity). A clean-lined glass with [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) keeps the see-through look consistent.

## Chicago planner notes

Venues across Chicago and the suburbs set their own glassware rules. Hotels often include in-house glassware, while a loft or gallery usually has none. Always ask the venue what comes with the space before you rent. Then ask your bartender or bar company whether they bring their own glass or use yours.

## Frequently asked questions

### What glasses do I need for a wedding?

A water goblet, a wine glass and a champagne flute cover a seated dinner with a toast. Add a rocks glass and a highball if you serve spirits or cocktails.

### What is the difference between red and white wine glasses?

A red wine glass has a larger bowl that gives the wine room to open up. A white wine glass is narrower and keeps the wine cooler. Many events use one all-purpose glass for both.

### Are stemless wine glasses okay for a wedding?

Yes. Stemless glasses are sturdier, stack more compactly and suit outdoor and casual receptions. Use stemmed glasses if the dinner is formal.

### What size glass is a standard wine glass?

A standard wine glass holds about 10 to 12 oz, though a typical pour is only about 5 oz. The extra room lets the wine be swirled and prevents spills.

### What glass do I use for a signature cocktail?

Use whatever the recipe calls for. Short spirit-forward drinks go in a rocks glass and long mixed drinks go in a highball. A shallow coupe works for drinks served up.

### How many types of glass is too many?

More than five is too many for most guest lists. Each type adds racks, counts and a way to run short. Pick one glass per drink category and stop.
