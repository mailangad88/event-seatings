**Quick answer**

A private boat or yacht charter on Lake Michigan or the Chicago River is a memorable venue for rehearsal dinners, birthdays, corporate outings and small weddings. Capacity is set by the vessel's certification, so ask for the legal passenger count, not the comfortable one. Also ask about the season and weather policy, the boarding dock, catering and bar rules, what decor is allowed on deck, accessibility and what happens on busy nights like fireworks or the Air and Water Show.

</div>

Few things say Chicago like the skyline from the water. Charter boats range from small yachts for a dozen guests to large ships with dining rooms for hundreds. A boat is a venue with tighter rules than most, because safety, weight and space on board are fixed. Here is what to check before you book.

## Questions to ask a charter

<div class="table-wrap">

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| What is the certified passenger capacity? | It is a legal limit; the comfortable number for dinner is lower. |
| What is the weather policy? | Who decides to cancel or stay docked, and what you get back. |
| Where do guests board? | Dock location, parking and walking distance. |
| Is catering in-house or outside? | Many boats have exclusive catering and bar service. |

| What are the busy nights? | Fireworks and big lakefront events can change routes and prices. |

## Season and weather

Most private charters on Lake Michigan run from late spring to early fall. Evenings on the water feel cooler than on land, so plan wraps or an enclosed deck even in summer. Wind and storms decide whether the boat leaves the dock, and that call belongs to the captain. Read [planning an event in Chicago in June](/blog/planning-an-event-in-chicago-in-june) and [August](/blog/planning-an-event-in-chicago-in-august) for weather and calendar details, including the Air and Water Show, which affects the lakefront in mid-August.

## What you can and cannot bring

Boats have fixed furniture, limited storage and strict rules about anything loose on deck. Most charters provide the tables and seating on board, and many have exclusive catering and bar service. That means rentals for a boat event are usually small and portable: [linens](/rentals/linens) if the boat allows them, centerpieces, a welcome sign at the dock, and battery [lighting](/rentals/lighting) instead of candles. Ask before you plan balloons, confetti or anything that could blow overboard.

Where rentals help most is around the boat: a welcome area at the dock, an after-party on land, or a rehearsal dinner that starts on the water and ends at a nearby restaurant. ## Guests, parking and timing

Boats leave on time. Build a 30-minute arrival window, tell guests exactly where to board, and plan parking or transit to the dock. Downtown docks fill on summer weekends and during festivals; see [guest parking and transit](/blog/guest-parking-and-transit-plan-chicago). For guests with mobility needs, ask about gangway slope, stairs between decks and restrooms before you book.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people fit on a party boat in Chicago?

It depends on the vessel. Ask the charter for its certified passenger capacity and, more usefully, how many guests fit for your format. A seated dinner holds far fewer than a standing cocktail party.

### When is boat party season in Chicago?

Most private charters on Lake Michigan run from late spring to early fall, with summer weekends the busiest. Evenings on the water are cooler than on land, so plan layers.

### What happens if the weather is bad on my charter date?

The captain decides whether it is safe to leave the dock. Ask about the weather policy before you book: some charters reschedule, some refund, and some can host the event at the dock.

### Can I bring my own food and drinks on a Chicago charter boat?

Some smaller charters allow it, but many larger boats require their own catering and bar. Ask before you book a caterer.

### Can I have candles on a boat?

Usually not open flame. Plan battery candles and lighting instead, and ask about any decor rules for the deck.
