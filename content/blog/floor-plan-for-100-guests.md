**Quick answer**

A 100-guest seated reception with dancing needs about 1,800 to 2,200 square feet: ten 72-inch rounds of 10, a 14 x 16 foot dance floor, a bar of 10 to 12 feet, a DJ and a small welcome area. That is 18 to 22 square feet per guest. Without dancing you can compress to about 1,300 square feet.

</div>

One hundred guests is the number most Chicago-area banquet halls are built around. It is also where a plan starts to need real structure: service aisles for waitstaff, a bar that can handle a line, and a dance floor sized for the crowd, not for the photos. This plan uses ten rounds in two rows with the dance floor in the channel between them.

## Why two rows and a channel

With ten tables, a single big block of tables leaves interior guests trapped. Two rows along the long walls keep every table on an aisle, and the channel doubles as the main walkway to the bar and the restrooms. The dance floor fills the middle of the channel and the rest stays open for movement.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Zone | Size | Planning rule |
|---|---|---|
| Guest tables | 10 x 72 in rounds | About 11 linear feet per table with chairs |
| Dance floor | 14 x 16 ft | Around 2.5 to 3 sq ft per dancer, half the guests dancing |
| Bar | 10 to 12 ft | Two bartenders, 6 ft clear in front |
| DJ or band | 6 x 8 ft (DJ), 12 x 10 ft (band) | Needs power and no table within 8 ft |
| Cake, gifts, guest book | 3 small tables | Near the entry or the dance floor |

</div>

## Aisle widths that keep service moving

Plated service for 100 guests needs at least 4 feet between chair backs, so servers can pass behind seated guests with a tray. Five feet is better. Between two rounds, leave 4 to 5 feet of aisle between chair edges. The mistake we see most is planning the tables at full capacity and finding no walkway. Our [guide to how many tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) has the spacing math.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Measure the usable floor.</strong> Subtract columns, the stage, coat check and any permanent bars.</li>
<li><strong>Place the dance floor.</strong> Center of the room or against a wall, but never beside the kitchen door.</li>
<li><strong>Add tables.</strong> Ten rounds, at least 4 feet between chair backs.</li>
<li><strong>Place the bar and DJ.</strong> At opposite ends, so speakers do not blast the bar line.</li>
<li><strong>Walk the exits.</strong> Keep direct paths to every exit door clear; your venue will confirm their rules.</li>
</ol>

## If your room is smaller

At 1,500 square feet, cut the dance floor to 12 x 12 and use 66-inch or 72-inch rounds only. Move the bar to a corner and put the DJ on a banquet table against a wall. If the room is a long narrow rectangle, see [long table layouts in a narrow room](/blog/long-table-layouts-in-a-narrow-room).

## If you add a buffet

A buffet for 100 guests should have two serving lines or one double-sided line, about 20 feet total. Place it on the wall opposite the bar. A staggered release by table number keeps the wait under 15 minutes. See [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

## Chicago planning notes

Many Chicago banquet halls and suburban hotels list capacity for rounds of 10 without dancing. Ask for the capacity with a dance floor. For downtown venues, ask about load-in time and whether a freight elevator limits the size of the tables you can bring up. For suburb venues, confirm who sets up tables and chairs so you do not pay for the same job twice. The chair rental delivery guide for the Chicago suburbs covers delivery logistics.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big a room do I need for 100 guests?

About 1,800 to 2,200 square feet for dinner and dancing. For a dinner with no dancing, about 1,300 to 1,500 square feet works.

### How many tables for 100 guests?

Ten 72-inch rounds of 10, or 12 to 13 rounds of 8 at 60 inches. Add three small tables for cake, gifts and the guest book.

### How big should the dance floor be for 100 guests?

A 14 x 16 foot floor, or about 225 square feet, supports roughly 50 guests dancing at once.

### How many bartenders for 100 guests?

Two for a full bar, and a third if you add signature cocktails. One is enough for beer and wine only, though lines will be longer.

### Where should the DJ go in a 100-guest room?

At one end, next to the dance floor, with power access and at least 8 feet from the nearest table.

### Can I fit 100 guests in a 40 x 60 tent?

Yes, 2,400 square feet fits ten rounds and a 16 x 16 dance floor. You will also want a catering prep area outside the tent.

### Should I use 60-inch or 72-inch rounds for 100 guests?

72-inch rounds of 10 need fewer tables and less floor. 60-inch rounds of 8 are more intimate but need two more tables.
