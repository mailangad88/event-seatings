**Quick answer**

For a winter delivery in Chicago, shovel and salt the whole route from the truck to the door the day before and again that morning, ask for a wider delivery window, and lay mats where the crew walks in. Tell the company about ice, slope and the alley so they send the right crew. Decide in advance who calls it if a storm hits, and set a backup time.

Chicago winters are cold and unpredictable, and snow usually shows up between December and March. Early sunsets, plowed-in street spots and icy alleys all slow a delivery. The rental company is not trying to be difficult when it asks for a wider window. Trucks, crews and hand carts all work slower on snow. Our [Chicago winter event planning guide](/blog/chicago-winter-event-planning) covers the season as a whole. This post is about delivery day.

## The alley, the street and the plow

On a snowy week, alleys can stay rutted, icy or blocked long after the main streets are clear. Street spots get plowed in, and a shoveled-out space can disappear. Neighbors and parked cars add to the squeeze. Walk the route the day before, and send the company a photo. If the alley is not workable, ask about a front delivery. For rules on parking and loading zones, read [Chicago delivery parking and loading zones](/blog/chicago-event-delivery-parking-loading-zones) and the [alley delivery guide](/blog/chicago-alley-deliveries-event-rentals), and check with your building or the city for anything that applies where you live.

## Protect floors from slush and salt

Salt and slush ride in on crew boots and cart wheels. Over a hardwood or tile floor, that means water, scratches and a white film.

- Put absorbent mats at each entry, and a runner along the delivery path.
- Ask the crew to wipe wheels and boots, and have towels ready.
- Stack items on a tarp or runner just inside the door, not directly on the floor.
- Mop up wet spots as they happen, since standing water leaves marks.

[Protecting floors for a home party](/blog/protecting-floors-for-a-home-party) goes deeper on chairs and tables. In a venue, ask whether the building has its own mats or a floor protection rule.

## What most hosts find out too late

**Salt is not ice melt in every temperature.** Rock salt slows down in very cold weather, and some products work better than others. Ask the hardware store what is right for the cold forecast, and keep sand or grit on hand for traction. Check any building rules about what product you can use.

**A storm can move a delivery, and the earlier you talk the better.** Companies make calls on the day based on the crews and roads. Ask what the cutoff is, who calls whom, and what the backup time is. The [cancellation and date change policy guide](/blog/event-rental-cancellation-and-date-change-policy) shows what to read in the contract.

**Short days squeeze everything.** With sunset around 4:20 to 4:30 p.m. in December, a late delivery means setup in the dark. Ask for a morning window and plan lights for the path.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do rental companies deliver in the snow?

Often yes, with slower trucks and wider windows. If a storm makes roads unsafe, the company may move the delivery. Ask what its policy is and who makes the call.

### Do I need to shovel for an event rental delivery?

Yes. Clear the truck stop, the path and the steps, and salt them. A crew will not carry tables over ice, and an unshoveled path can mean a delay or an extra charge.

### Can the truck get down my Chicago alley in winter?

Sometimes. Plowing, parked cars and ice can make it hard. Check the alley the day before and have a front-door backup.

### How do I keep slush off my floors during delivery?

Use mats and runners at entries and along the path, and keep towels handy. Ask the crew to wipe wheels at the door.

### Should I get delivery the day before a winter event?

If the weather forecast is rough or your event starts early, yes. A day-before drop in a garage or hall removes the pressure. You are responsible for the items overnight, so see [keeping rentals safe overnight](/blog/keeping-rentals-safe-overnight-outdoors).

### What if a snowstorm hits on my event day?

Talk to the company early about delivery changes, and plan for guests who arrive late. Decide ahead of time whether to move, shorten or postpone.
