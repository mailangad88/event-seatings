**Quick answer**

Plan a catering prep tent of about 10 x 20 feet for up to 75 guests and 10 x 30 or 20 x 20 feet for 100 to 200 guests. Place it within 30 feet of the buffet, out of sight of guests, with a covered path, shade, power and a way for delivery vehicles to reach it. Inside, allow work tables, cold storage, a hot-hold area, hand washing and trash.

</div>

A great backyard or park event runs on a space guests never see. The caterer needs a place to plate, hold, wash and stage, and when the venue is a lawn that place is a tent. If you skip it, food gets prepped on the garage floor, cooks stand in the rain and everything runs late. This guide helps you size and place back of house.

## Sizing the tent

Tent size follows the caterer's crew and the menu. A tent for a drop-off lunch needs less space than a plated dinner for 150. The sizes below are common starting points, and caterers differ, so ask yours how many staff and how much equipment they bring. See [what size tent do I need](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) for event tent sizing, and match the prep tent to the menu.

- **Up to 50 guests, drop-off or light menu:** 10 x 10 feet may be enough.
- **75 to 100 guests, buffet:** 10 x 20 feet.
- **100 to 150 guests, plated or stations:** 10 x 30 or 20 x 20 feet.
- **200 or more guests:** 20 x 30 feet or a second tent.

## Where to place it

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Close to the buffet:</strong> within 30 feet, with a level, covered path.</li>
<li><strong>Away from guests:</strong> behind the event tent, out of photos and sight lines.</li>
<li><strong>Vehicle access:</strong> a way for the caterer to unload.</li>
<li><strong>Power and light:</strong> a dedicated circuit and work lights.</li>
<li><strong>Drainage and shade:</strong> slightly sloped away from the tent, not under a downspout.</li>
</ol>

Check the placement with your tent company, because tents need clearance from each other, from utility lines and from buildings. Illinois requires utility locating before staking, and tent companies normally handle the call; see our [tent guides](/blog/topic/tents).

## Floors, power and water

A prep tent on grass turns to mud in rain. Plywood, rubber mats or a tent floor keeps work areas safe. Caterers need power for warming cabinets, mixers and refrigeration; sum up the load with your caterer and ask your venue about circuits and generators. See [lighting and power](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power). Water is harder: hand washing needs a station with warm water, soap and towels. A caterer may bring a portable sink.

## Tables, linens and gear to rent

Prep tents use plain tables: 6 and 8-foot banquet tables, usually without linen, or with inexpensive covers to keep surfaces clean. Add stainless worktables if your caterer requests them. See [tables and linens for caterers](/blog/tables-and-linens-for-caterers). Provide lighting, a trash station ([trash and recycling stations](/blog/trash-recycling-composting-stations-for-events)), and a fire extinguisher when cooking.

## Rules and heat

If the caterer cooks in the tent (grilling, frying or boiling), clearances for heat and flame are strict. Many tent makers prohibit cooking inside the tent. Move cooking outside and keep a 10-foot gap, or hold in a separate heat zone. See [grill rental for events](/blog/grill-rental-for-events). Ask your village about food service rules for large private events, because some require a health department review for a caterer cooking on site.

## Chicago and suburb notes

A backyard in Oak Park or Naperville is often 40 to 60 feet wide, which leaves little room for two tents. A prep tent can sometimes sit on a driveway or beside a garage. If space is tight, ask whether the caterer can use the garage with plastic sheeting and a hand wash station. Wind off the lake and sudden rain in spring and fall mean you should plan a covered path between the two tents.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big should a catering tent be?

10 x 20 feet for up to 75 guests, 10 x 30 for 100 to 150, and 20 x 30 for larger events. Always confirm with your caterer.

### Where should the catering tent go?

Behind the event tent, within 30 feet of the buffet, with vehicle access and a covered path.

### What equipment goes in a prep tent?

Tables, cold storage, warming equipment, hand washing, trash and lighting. Ask your caterer what they bring.

### Can the caterer cook in the tent?

Usually not. Many tent companies and fire codes limit cooking. Cook outside and keep a safe gap.

### Do I need electricity in a catering tent?

Almost always. Warming cabinets, refrigeration and lights need power. Plan circuits or a generator.

### Can I use a garage instead?

Often yes, if it is clean, has power and fits the caterer's needs. Ask them to see it.
