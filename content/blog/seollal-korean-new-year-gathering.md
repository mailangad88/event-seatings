Seollal is the Korean New Year, and it falls on the same day as Lunar New Year and Tet, Feb 6, 2027. In many families it means three generations in one house, elders in hanbok, a bowl of rice cake soup, and kids waiting for New Year money. Chicago has a large Korean community, with shops and restaurants along Lawrence Avenue and in the suburbs. The holiday asks a house to do several things in a few hours, so layout matters.

Customs differ by family, region and religion. Some families hold charye, and others do not. Use what fits your home.

## Charye, the ancestral table

Charye is a memorial ritual held on the morning of Seollal, to honor ancestors. Foods are laid on a table in a set order, which differs by family and region. Common rules are that rice and soup go near the front, fruit and rice cakes follow in rows, and a photo or name tablet sits at the back. Many families follow guides handed down in the family.

Plan for:

- A table big enough for the dishes, about a 6-foot table for a typical spread
- A cloth, simple and clean
- A clear space for the family to bow in front
- Candles or incense if your family uses them. Check your building rules first.

If you are not sure what your family does, ask the oldest relative. A table plan from them saves time.

## Sebae, the New Year bow

Sebae is the deep bow younger family members give to elders on New Year morning. The person bows with a wish for health, and elders respond with words of blessing, and often with sebaetdon, New Year money. Children often wear hanbok. The custom needs floor space and a soft surface.

A 6 by 6 foot clear area lets two or three people bow side by side. Add a thick mat or a rug, because bowing on hard floor is hard on the knees, especially for older guests. Elders can sit on chairs or cushions. In a Chicago apartment, clear a living room by moving the sofa and coffee table to the wall for the morning.

## Tteokguk and the New Year meal

Tteokguk is a soup of sliced rice cakes in beef or anchovy broth, and eating a bowl is said to add a year to your age. Other dishes vary, and often include jeon, japchae, galbi, dumplings called mandu, and fruit. Many families serve tteokguk first, then the rest.

For a family of 12 to 20, you need bowls for the soup, side plates and spoons and chopsticks. Count 1 soup bowl, 2 small plates and a spoon and chopsticks per guest. Soup holds heat, so choose bowls with a wide base. See [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest) and [family style serving ware](/blog/family-style-serving-ware).

## Low tables or regular tables

Many Korean homes eat at low tables on the floor. Many families in Chicago have dining tables and chairs, and many have both. Here is how to choose:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Option | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Low floor table with cushions | Traditional feel, small rooms | Hard for older guests and bad knees |
| Regular 30-inch table with chairs | Mixed ages, long meals | Takes more floor space |
| Mix of both | Elders on chairs, kids on floor | Two setups to plan |

</div>

Many hosts use regular tables for the elders and a low table for the kids, which also frees the middle of the room for yut nori. A rule of thumb is 2 feet of table width per adult, so an 8-foot table seats 8 and a 6-foot table seats 6. Check [banquet table sizes](/blog/banquet-table-sizes-6-ft-vs-8-ft) for details.

## Yut nori and games

Yut nori is a traditional board game played with four wooden sticks, thrown to move pieces around a board. It is played on a mat or a table, with two teams and lots of cheering. A mat about 3 by 3 feet is enough, and a sturdy table works too. Some families also play jegichagi, a kick game, or hwatu card games.

Put games where they do not collide with the meal. A corner with a mat, a few cushions and a small table keeps kids happy while adults finish eating. For a family with many kids, see [kids tables and chairs for events](/blog/kids-tables-and-chairs-for-events).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Shoes and floors.** Many families go barefoot or in socks. Plan a shoe rack, a mat at the door and a floor you can wipe, because boots track in snow and salt in a Chicago winter.
- **Counts change on the day.** Relatives bring relatives. Order a few more chairs than your headcount, and keep a stack folded in a closet.
- **Rental dates around Feb 6 fill fast.** Lunar New Year, Tet and Seollal share the date, so ask early. We check the date with the rental companies for you.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is Seollal in 2027?

Saturday, Feb 6, 2027. It follows the lunar calendar and falls on the same day as Lunar New Year and Tet.

### What is charye?

Charye is a ritual meal for ancestors on the morning of Seollal. Food is arranged on a table in a set order. Families follow their own tradition, and not all families hold it.

### What is sebae?

Sebae is the deep bow given to elders on New Year morning with a wish for health. Elders often respond with a blessing and sebaetdon, New Year money.

### How much floor space do I need for sebae?

About a 6 by 6 foot clear area is a rule of thumb for two or three people to bow at once. Add a mat or rug for comfort.

### What is tteokguk?

A soup of sliced rice cakes in broth, eaten on Seollal. Tradition says eating it adds a year to your age.

### Should I use low tables or regular tables?

Use what suits your guests. Low tables are traditional, and regular tables are easier for older guests. Many families use both.
