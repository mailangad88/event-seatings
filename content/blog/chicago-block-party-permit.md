**Quick answer**

A Chicago block party permit is for residents of the street being closed. You apply through your local aldermanic office to CDOT, and you must show that a majority of residents on the affected block approve. CDOT's page says requests must reach it within three business days before the event, but ward offices often ask for far more notice, so apply as early as your ward allows.

Block parties are some of the best events in Chicago. Neighbors drag out grills, kids take over the street and nobody needs a valet. They also run on one rule: the street belongs to the city until CDOT says otherwise. This guide covers how the permit works, what a block party actually needs from a rental order, and how to keep tables and chairs from becoming a safety problem.

## Who can apply

According to CDOT, block party permits are for Chicago residents, and only for the street where the applicant lives. Businesses and outside organizations apply for a festival permit instead. The request goes through your local aldermanic office, which is why each ward publishes its own packet. Several ward websites post block party instructions and a petition form.

## The rules in plain terms

- **Neighbor approval.** You must show that a majority of residents on the affected block approve. A signed petition is the usual way. CDOT's page does not set a signature count.
- **One block.** The permit is for a single block, intersection to intersection. CDOT's text also says block parties cannot extend for more than two blocks, so ask your ward which limit it applies.
- **One day.** Permits are not issued for more than one day in succession.
- **Metered parking.** Parking controlled by meters or pay boxes must stay accessible.
- **Approval is not guaranteed.** CDOT may deny requests for incomplete applications, traffic control issues, conflicts with nearby permits or public safety concerns.

Hours, barricade rules, alcohol and insurance are not spelled out on CDOT's page. Ask your ward office for its packet, and do not assume the answer from a different ward.

## What a block party actually needs

A block party is not a wedding, but it is a seated meal for 50 to 150 neighbors who all arrive at once. The most useful items are simple.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Item | Why it matters on a closed street |
|---|---|
| Long tables or rounds | Rounds keep neighbors talking across the table; long tables serve potluck food better |
| Folding chairs | Easy to stack and carry back to a garage |
| Shade | Asphalt radiates heat in July, and it is the first thing guests ask about |
| Tablecloths | Disposable or rented, they keep a plain table from looking bare |
| Trash and recycling | Neighbors will run out of bins before dessert |

</div>

For seating counts, our guide to [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [how many tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) give the numbers. ## Delivery on a closed block

The permit does not tell your rental company how to reach you. Ask the ward office when barricades go up, then give that time to your delivery provider. A truck needs to unload before the street closes or have a barricade opened for it. Plan to have neighbors on hand to carry items. It is easier to unload at one end and hand-carry down the block than to drive a truck into a crowd.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting until week-of.** CDOT's three-day window is a minimum, not a plan. Ward offices have their own deadlines.
- **Blocking a driveway or hydrant.** Keep access for residents and for emergency vehicles.
- **Assuming one ward's rules apply to another.** Check your own ward's packet.
- **Ignoring weather.** Chicago summer storms arrive fast. Have a rain plan, such as a garage or covered porch, and decide a rain date with your neighbors before you print flyers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get a block party permit in Chicago?

Contact your alderman's office, which handles the application and sends it to CDOT. You will need to show that a majority of residents on the affected block approve. Many ward websites post the form and instructions.

### How far in advance should I apply for a Chicago block party?

CDOT's page says requests must reach it within three business days before the event. In practice, ward offices set their own deadlines, so ask yours early. Apply weeks ahead for a summer weekend.

### Can I close more than one block for a block party?

CDOT's page gives two limits in different places: a permit for a single block, and a note that block parties cannot extend more than two blocks. Ask your ward which applies to you.

### Can a business hold a block party permit?

No. CDOT says block party permits are for Chicago residents on their own street. Businesses and outside groups apply for a festival permit instead.

### Do I need insurance or a liquor license for a block party?

CDOT's page does not address either. Your ward office may have its own rules, and serving or selling alcohol to the public can involve city licensing. Ask the ward before you plan a bar.

### Can I rent tables and chairs for a block party?

Yes. Rentals are common for block parties. Confirm the barricade times with your ward and give your rental company a clear drop-off point.
