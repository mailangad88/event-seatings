**Quick answer**

Count every item with the crew at delivery, compare it to the slip, and write any shortage or damage on the slip before you sign. Take photos of anything already scuffed, stained or broken. Before pickup, stack and group items so the crew can count them in minutes, and ask for a copy of the pickup slip. Lost-item charges come from counts that nobody can prove.

</div>

The slip you sign on delivery day is the document that decides who pays when the numbers do not match. Most hosts sign it while they are on the phone with the caterer. In a Chicago backyard or a rented hall, that is how 4 missing chairs become a charge a week later. A 5-minute count fixes it.

## At delivery: count before you sign

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Ask for the slip first.</strong> It lists items and quantities from your order.</li>
<li><strong>Count with the crew.</strong> Chairs by stack, linens by bag, glasses by rack.</li>
<li><strong>Look at condition.</strong> Scuffs, stains, cracks, loose legs.</li>
<li><strong>Write problems on the slip.</strong> Short two chairs, one torn linen. Initial the note.</li>
<li><strong>Take photos.</strong> Wide shot of each stack and close shots of damage.</li>
<li><strong>Keep your copy.</strong> A photo of the signed slip is enough.</li>
</ol>

## What to photograph

Photos are your proof of what arrived. Take them before you sign and before anyone sets up.

- Each stack or bag as delivered.
- Scuffs, cracks, stains or missing parts on any item.
- Boxed glassware and dinnerware, with a shot of the open crate.
- Anything stored outside: where it sits and how it is covered.
- The signed slip.

Time-stamped phone photos are fine. Keep them until the final invoice has been paid. The same applies if you do will-call, as [full service vs will-call](/blog/full-service-vs-will-call-event-rentals) explains.

## What most hosts find out too late

**You are asked to count items that are hard to count.** Glassware in racks, flatware in bins and napkins in bags are easy to miscount. Count by rack or tray, and note the number of full and partial racks.

**Counts in the dark are wrong counts.** If pickup is late at night or very early, counts happen in a driveway with a flashlight. Ask for the count to be done in daylight, or stack items where lights are on. See [late-night and early pickup fees](/blog/late-night-and-early-pickup-fees-event-rentals).

**Counting only the pieces you set out.** Chairs migrate to a garage, a neighbor's yard or the kitchen. Do a last walk before the crew arrives.

## Before pickup: set the crew up to count fast

<div class="table-wrap">

| Item | How to stage it |
|---|---|
| Chairs | Stack in equal piles, away from the rain, near the door the crew will use |
| Tables | Fold and lean, legs in, all in one place |
| Linens | Shake out, bag dry linens, put wet or stained ones in a separate bag |
| Glassware | Back in the racks, same direction, no loose glasses |
| Dinnerware | In the original crates, scraped, no wet food |
| Flatware | In the bin or tray, counted if possible |
| Heaters and gear | Off and cool, fuel noted |

</div>

## If the numbers do not match

- **Short at delivery.** Ask the crew lead what the plan is and when the missing items will arrive. Write it on the slip.
- **Damaged on arrival.** Note it, photograph it and ask for a replacement. Do not use damaged items.
- **Short at pickup.** Ask the crew to recount with you. Check the garage, the kitchen and the trash area.
- **A charge after the fact.** Ask for the pickup count sheet, your delivery slip and photos. Compare quantities line by line.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I count rentals at delivery?

Yes. Count with the crew before you sign, and write any shortage or damage on the slip. Your signature usually confirms that what is listed arrived in good condition.

### What if rental items arrive damaged?

Photograph the damage, note it on the slip before signing and tell the company right away. Ask for a replacement or a credit in writing.

### Do I have to be there at pickup?

Not always, but it helps. If no one is there, the crew counts alone and you cannot dispute the number. Ask whether a photo of your staged stacks is enough.

### How do I avoid lost item charges?

Keep items in one place, count them at delivery and again before pickup, and photograph the stacks. Charges usually follow when a count cannot be shown either way.

### What is a delivery slip?

It is the paper or digital sheet that lists the items and quantities delivered. Keep your copy and compare it with the pickup count.

### Who is responsible for items after delivery?

Usually you are, from the signed delivery until pickup. Check the contract for how it treats theft, weather and damage. See [damage waivers](/blog/damage-waiver-event-rentals-explained).
