**Quick answer**

For a Wigilia of 20 to 30 people, set one long table or two joined rows with about 24 inches of edge per person, a white cloth over a little hay, and one extra empty place setting. Plan one dinner plate and soup bowl per guest, plus smaller plates for the courses that come before the main dishes, and enough serving bowls and platters for the dishes you make. Start the dinner when the first star is out, which on Dec 24 in Chicago is just after sunset around 4:30 pm.

</div>

Wigilia is the Polish Christmas Eve supper, and in Chicago it is a big night. The city has one of the largest Polish communities in the United States, and neighborhoods like Avondale and Jefferson Park know the dinner well. Families often grow past what a dining room can hold. Grandparents, cousins and friends come from the suburbs, and a table for 8 turns into a table for 24. This guide covers how to set the table, what to rent, and how to plan the evening. Traditions differ by family and by region of Poland, so use what is true in your home and skip the rest.

## The traditions that shape the table

These are widely shared, though not universal.

- **The first star.** The meal begins after the first star appears in the evening sky. Children often watch for it. On Thursday, Dec 24, 2026 in Chicago, sunset is around 4:20 to 4:30 pm, so the dinner often starts in the late afternoon or early evening.
- **Opłatek.** Before eating, everyone shares a thin wafer with each person at the table, one at a time, with good wishes. This takes time and means people stand and move. Leave space behind chairs.
- **Meatless dishes.** Wigilia is a meatless meal. Many families serve 12 dishes, one for each of the 12 apostles. Some serve 7, 9 or 13. Typical dishes are beet soup with small dumplings, pierogi, fish such as herring and carp, and a sweet dish with poppy seed.
- **An extra place.** An empty setting stays at the table for an unexpected guest, a traveler or a family member who is gone. Some families treat it as a seat for anyone who might arrive.
- **Hay under the cloth.** Some families put a little hay under the tablecloth to recall the manger. It is a small detail, and not every household does it.

## Seating a long table

A single table is the heart of Wigilia, because the family eats together at once. A long table lets everyone see everyone. If your room cannot fit one table, two joined rows work, as long as the head of the family and the empty place are easy to see.

An 8-foot banquet table seats 8 to 10, and a rule of thumb is 24 inches of edge per person. Three 8-foot tables end to end make 24 feet, which fits up to 12 per side at 24 inches each. For 20 guests that leaves elbow room, with the head of the family and the empty place at the ends. A 30-inch table is a standard width. If you want a wider table for serving dishes down the middle, ask about 36-inch tables. Check the [long-table guide](/blog/long-table-layouts-in-a-narrow-room), [table spacing](/blog/table-spacing-and-aisle-clearance), and the [tables page](/rentals/tables).

Leave at least 36 inches behind the chairs, and 48 inches where people will pass with dishes. Opłatek means people get up, so give them room.

## Linens: white, with room for hay

A white cloth is traditional. Many families add a second layer or a runner. To cover the hay and keep it from shifting, use a cloth that drops to the floor. A 6-foot banquet table takes a 90 x 132-inch cloth, and an 8-foot takes a 90 x 156-inch. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart). For a long run, use several cloths overlapped at the center with a runner on top. See [linens](/rentals/linens).

Keep a lot of cloth napkins on hand. Beet soup stains. Cloth napkins in a dark red or white are fine, but test a sample for stain care.

## Serving ware and plates for 12 dishes

A dozen dishes means a lot of small plates and bowls on the table at once. You will use more serving pieces than you expect.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **The kitchen cannot hold 12 dishes at once.** Cook what you can ahead. Rent warming trays or chafing dishes for the hot dishes so the pierogi stay warm. See [chafing dishes for a buffet](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet).
- **Christmas Eve is a peak delivery day.** Rental companies close early or have short hours on Dec 24, so ask for delivery on Dec 22 or 23 and pickup after the holiday. See [event rental delivery day](/blog/event-rental-delivery-day-what-to-expect).
- **Count the coats.** A family of 24 brings 24 coats in a Chicago December. Plan a rack or a bed.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does Wigilia dinner start?

Traditionally when the first star appears in the evening sky. On Dec 24 in Chicago, sunset is around 4:20 to 4:30 pm, so many families sit down shortly after. Some set a clock time instead, which is fine.

### Why is there an empty place at the Wigilia table?

The empty place is for an unexpected guest, a traveler or a family member who is no longer with the family. Not every family keeps the custom, and the explanation varies. It is set like every other place.

### How many dishes are served at Wigilia?

Many families serve 12 meatless dishes, one for each apostle. Some serve 7, 9 or 13. Menus vary by family and region, so plan the number that fits your house.

### What is the wafer, and how is it shared?

Opłatek is a thin wafer, usually stamped with a nativity scene. Before the meal, each person breaks a piece from another person's wafer and exchanges good wishes. It takes a while with a big family, so leave room to move.

### How long should a Wigilia table be for 20 people?

About 20 feet to 24 feet of table, or three 8-foot tables end to end. A rule of thumb is 24 inches per person. Leave 36 inches behind the chairs for people to move.

### Do I need to put hay under the tablecloth?

No. It is a custom in some families, to recall the manger. If you want it, a thin layer under a cloth that reaches the floor keeps it neat.
