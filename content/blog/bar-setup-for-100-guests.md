**Quick answer**

For 100 guests, plan two bar stations or one 8-foot bar with two bartenders, a back bar for bottles and a prep table. Rule of thumb: one bartender per 40 to 50 guests, about 1 drink per guest per hour (2 in the first hour), and 250 to 300 bar glasses. Leave at least 4 feet of clear space in front of the bar so a line can form without blocking the room.

</div>

A bar is the busiest spot in the room. A line at the bar during cocktail hour is normal, but a line that blocks a door or runs through the dining area is a design failure. For a 100-guest wedding or party in Chicago, the layout is a small set of decisions: how many stations, how big, where, and what goes behind them.

## How many bars and bartenders

A rule of thumb is one bartender per 40 to 50 guests for a full bar and one per 75 for beer and wine only. At 100 guests that means two bartenders for a full bar. If your cocktail hour is short, one 8-foot bar with two bartenders works. If it is long or your guests drink heavily, add a second station.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Setup | Bars | Bartenders | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beer and wine only | 1 (6 ft) | 1 to 2 | Casual, daytime |
| Full bar, short cocktail hour | 1 (8 ft) | 2 | Plated dinner |
| Full bar, long cocktail hour | 2 (8 ft + 6 ft) | 3 to 4 | Cocktail-heavy party |
| Signature drinks only | 1 (6 ft) | 2 | Small drink menu |

## How much will they drink?

A common rule of thumb is about 2 drinks per guest in the first hour and 1 drink per guest per hour after that. For a 4-hour event with 100 guests, that is roughly 500 drinks. Your caterer or bar service will refine this by the crowd.

## What goes behind the bar

- **Bar front** (6 or 8 foot), with linen or a plain front.
- **Back bar** for bottles, mixers and garnishes, 6 to 8 feet long.
- **Ice bins or coolers**, at least two.
- **Glass racks** for storage, plus a trash can and recycling.
- **Cocktail napkins and bar tools**. See the [bar tools checklist](/blog/bar-tools-and-cocktail-napkins-checklist).
- **A prep table** at the end for cutting garnishes.
- **A tip jar and a drink menu sign** at the front.

## Placement tips for a Chicago room

Put the bar away from the dance floor and the entry so the line doesn't clog either. In many hotel ballrooms and banquet halls the venue will have outlets, ice access and a service door; ask where those are before you decide. For outdoor or tent events, bars need a flat, level surface and shade; a bar in direct sun melts ice quickly. For deeper layout thinking, see [bar placement in the floor plan](/blog/bar-placement-floor-plan).

## Dry hire or full service?

You can rent the bar equipment yourself or hire a company to provide bars, bartenders and stock. Each has different rules in Illinois; read [dry hire vs full service](/blog/dry-hire-vs-full-service-bar) and [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events) before you decide.

## Match the bar to the room

A dark wood bar fits a barn or a rustic tent; a white bar looks clean in a modern loft. Add chairs or stools only if you want guests lingering. For comfortable seating near the bar, a [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) or a bar-height option sits well with a natural wood bar.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many bartenders do I need for 100 guests?

Two for a full bar, one to two for beer and wine only. Add a third if the cocktail hour is short and guests all arrive at once.

### How big should the bar be for 100 guests?

An 8-foot bar handles one station with two bartenders. If you want two stations, an 8-foot main bar and a 6-foot second bar work well.

### How many drinks should I plan for 100 guests?

Plan roughly 2 drinks per guest in the first hour and 1 per hour after that. For a 4-hour event, that is around 500 drinks. Your bar service can adjust for your crowd.

### How many glasses do I need at the bar?

Plan 250 to 300 bar glasses for 100 guests, depending on whether guests reuse them. Our glasses per guest article gives the full count.

### Should I have one bar or two?

Two bars for 100 guests if the cocktail hour is short or you serve a full bar. One bar is fine for beer and wine only or a long, relaxed party.

### Where should the bar go?

Away from the entry and dance floor, near a service door or power source, with 4 feet of clear space in front. Avoid blocking aisles.

### Do I need a license to serve alcohol?

Rules depend on the venue and city. See our Illinois alcohol rules article, and always ask your venue first.
