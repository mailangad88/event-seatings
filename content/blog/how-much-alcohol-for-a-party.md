**Quick answer**

A common rule is two drinks per guest in the first hour and one drink per guest each hour after that. For a four-hour party with 50 guests, that is about 250 drinks. Split them roughly 40 percent beer, 40 percent wine and 20 percent liquor, adjust for your crowd, and add extra ice, mixers, water and nonalcoholic options. Check your venue's rules and local alcohol laws.

## The basic math

Estimate **total drinks = guests x (2 + hours after the first hour x 1)**. For a four-hour party, that is five drinks per guest. For 40 guests, 200 drinks. Heavy drinkers, hot weather and a celebration will push the number up, and a daytime family party with many kids will pull it down. Count only the adults who drink, not everyone.

## Turn drinks into bottles

- **Wine:** About five glasses per 750 ml bottle.
- **Beer:** One drink per can or bottle, 24 to a case.
- **Liquor:** About 17 standard pours per 750 ml bottle if you pour 1.5 ounces, and fewer in a mixed drink with two ounces.
- **Champagne or sparkling wine:** About six toast pours per bottle.

If you are using a signature cocktail or two, you need much less liquor than a full bar. ## Do not forget ice, mixers and water

Ice runs out first. Plan about one to one and a half pounds per guest for drinks and extra for chilling. See [how much ice for a party](/blog/how-much-ice-for-a-party). Add soda, tonic, juice, limes and plenty of water. Offer real alternatives such as sparkling water, iced tea or [mocktails](/blog/mocktail-bar-ideas), so guests who skip alcohol feel included.

## Glasses and equipment

You need more glasses than you think. Many hosts plan about two to three per guest at a long party if guests do not keep the same glass. See [how many glasses per guest](/blog/how-many-glasses-per-guest). Coolers, tubs, a drink table, a trash plan and bar tools matter too. [Bar tools and cocktail napkins checklist](/blog/bar-tools-and-cocktail-napkins-checklist) covers it.

## Dry hire, bartenders and rules

If you buy the alcohol yourself, ask whether your venue allows it and who must serve it. Read [dry hire versus full-service bar](/blog/dry-hire-vs-full-service-bar) and [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events). Venue contracts, insurance and local rules vary, so ask them first. Do not sell alcohol or charge for drinks without the right license.

## Plan for safety

Offer food, water and nonalcoholic drinks. Set an end time for the bar, and have a plan for rides home. Do not serve guests who appear intoxicated. If you are hosting, stay aware and ask a helper to watch the bar.

## Return policies

Ask the store whether unopened bottles or cases can be returned. Beer and wine are often easier than liquor. Buy extra only where you can return it.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area parties often run into rules about where and how alcohol can be served, especially in parks, forest preserves and some village halls, where alcohol may be limited or require a permit. Check with your park district or village before you plan a bar there. Summer heat means more ice and cold drinks, and winter events may call for warm drinks. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much alcohol do I need for 50 people?

For a four-hour party, plan about 250 drinks, or five per guest. That is roughly 100 beers, 20 bottles of wine and three or four bottles of liquor. Adjust for your crowd.

### How many drinks per person per hour?

A common planning rule is two drinks in the first hour and one per hour after. Heavy drinking crowds and hot days push this up. Daytime events with kids may need fewer.

### How many bottles of wine per person?

About one bottle serves two to three guests over a dinner, since a bottle pours roughly five glasses. At a long party, plan on one bottle for every two guests.

### How much liquor do I need for 100 people?

If you offer a full bar for four hours, six or seven 750 ml bottles is a starting range, along with beer and wine. Fewer bottles are needed if you offer a limited cocktail menu.

### Should I serve beer, wine and liquor?

Not necessarily. Beer and wine only is simpler and cheaper. A signature cocktail plus beer and wine is another popular choice.

### How do I avoid running out of alcohol?

Buy a bit more than you need in items you can return, and keep ice and mixers well stocked. Track consumption mid-event. Have a backup run planned.

### Do I need a permit to serve alcohol at a party?

It depends on where the party is and whether you sell drinks. Private events can have rules from the venue, park district or village. Check with them and review Illinois rules before you plan.
