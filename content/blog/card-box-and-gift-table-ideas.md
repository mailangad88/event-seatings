**Quick answer**

Choose a card box with a slot and a lock or a lid, big enough for roughly one envelope per 2 guests (couples and families give together), and set it on a 6-foot table or a 48-inch round near the entrance where someone can keep an eye on it. Assign a trusted person to move the box and gifts at the end of the night so nothing is left behind.

The card box looks like a small decision. It is also where money, checks and handwritten notes end up, and every year somebody realizes the box went missing at midnight. A good card box and gift table does three jobs: it is easy for guests to find, it looks good in photos and it is safe. This guide covers styles, sizes, layout and the security details, for weddings, showers and milestone parties in the Chicago area.

Open-top styles such as birdcages and wishing wells are charming but exposed. If you pick one, assign someone to watch it, or choose a model with a lid or lock.

## How big should it be?

A common planning guess is one card for every 2 guests, since couples and families give a single envelope. Fifty cards fit in most boxes, but bulky envelopes, gifts and notes fill them fast, so for 150 guests or more, choose a larger box. Measure the slot, and check that it will take a standard A7 or larger envelope. Add a few spare envelopes and pens for guests who forgot.

## The gift table

Set the table close to the entrance or the welcome area, with space around it for guests to stop without blocking the traffic. A 6-foot rectangular table holds a card box, a sign and several gifts. A 48-inch round table suits a compact display. Use a floor-length cloth, so you can store larger gifts underneath. Our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) gives exact sizes. For a checklist of every small table at an event, see the [cake, gift and guest book table checklist](/blog/cake-gift-guest-book-table-checklist).

## Security: how not to lose the cards

1. **Pick a box with a lid or lock** and a slot that is hard to reach into.
2. **Name a guard.** A relative or friend sits near the table during the event, in shifts.
3. **Move the box at a set time.** Many hosts move the box to a safe place after dinner, or to the couple's suite, before dancing starts.
4. **Keep valuables out of reach.** Never leave an open bag of cash.
5. **Hand the box to one person at the end.** A parent or best man takes it and the gifts to the car.
6. **Ask the venue** about secure storage.

If gifts are expected, such as at a baby shower, ask the venue where leftover gifts can wait and who is responsible for them.

## Styling

Keep the table simple. A sign with a clear message, such as "Cards and wishes," guides guests. Add a small arrangement of flowers, a framed photo or a candle (check venue rules on open flame). Match the box to the room: wood for a barn, acrylic for a loft, velvet for a ballroom. Choose chairs to match the room too, for example, a [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) at a rustic gift table or a [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) in a modern one.

## Where it goes at a Chicago event

At winter events, gifts and cards arrive with coats and snow, so keep the gift table separate from the coat check to avoid congestion. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago). At outdoor summer events, shade the table and anchor anything light, since wind lifts cards and signs. A [market umbrella](/blog/market-umbrellas-for-shade) helps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should the card box go at a wedding?

Near the entrance or welcome table, in a spot where someone can watch it. Avoid the dance floor and the bar.

### How big should a wedding card box be?

Large enough for about one card for every 2 guests, plus room for bulky envelopes. For 150 or more guests, pick a larger box.

### Should the card box have a lock?

A lid or lock is safer, especially for large events. If you choose an open-top style, assign someone to watch it.

### What size table do I need for gifts?

A 6-foot rectangular table or a 48-inch round, with a floor-length cloth so gifts can sit beneath it.

### Who should take the cards home?

One trusted person, such as a parent or a best man, designated before the event.

### Do I need a gift table for a birthday?

If guests bring gifts or cards, yes. A small table by the entrance keeps them in one place.
