Chicago storms build fast. A sunny afternoon can become a squall line with gusts that tear at a tent and send chairs flying. Tents are safe when they are anchored right and when someone is watching the sky. This guide explains the vocabulary so you can ask good questions. We do not give a universal mph number, because the answer depends on the tent and the company.

## What a wind rating means

A wind rating is the sustained wind speed the tent can resist with its specific anchoring and sidewall setup. It changes with the configuration:

- **Sidewalls up** make the tent act like a sail, so the rating drops.
- **Sidewalls off** lower wind load, so the rating rises.
- **More ballast or stakes** raise the rating.
- **Gusts** matter more than averages, because the load comes in bursts.

## What Chicago requires

For temporary tents, the Chicago Department of Buildings requires a high winds action plan. It must state the wind speed and timing that trigger evacuation, how weather will be monitored, and who is responsible for evacuation or disassembly. Larger tents also need wind resistance calculations from a licensed professional. See [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit). Other communities have similar expectations through their fire codes. Northwestern University's tent guidance asks organizers to monitor forecasts for high winds, lightning and storms and have a wind-action plan if speeds exceed tent limits.

## Questions to ask your tent company

1. What wind speed is this tent rated for as configured, with sidewalls up and down?
2. What is your evacuation trigger, and who decides?
3. Will someone from your company be on site or on call?
4. How are the legs anchored, and what weight per leg is used on pavement?
5. Do you have a lightning policy?

Get the answers in the contract. See [stakes vs ballast](/blog/tent-anchoring-stakes-vs-ballast).

## Lightning

A tent offers no protection from lightning. Lightning guidance is simple: when thunder is heard, move to a sturdy building or a hard-topped vehicle. Names of nearby shelters should be in your plan. Share it with the DJ and caterer too.

## Chicago weather habits

Late spring and summer bring thunderstorms, and gusty fronts can come through any season. The lake can change conditions along the shoreline within minutes. For ongoing weather, use the National Weather Service forecast for your ZIP code and a radar app. If a watch or warning is posted, treat it as a signal to move the party.

## During the event

- **Keep exits and paths clear.** Furniture should not block the doors.
- **Do not hang heavy decor** from the tent without approval. See [tent decor](/blog/tent-decor-draping-and-florals).
- **Secure loose items:** centerpieces, signs and linens can fly.
- **Do not lean on poles or walls,** and do not move stakes or ballast.
- **Report loose stakes, frayed ropes or flapping fabric** to the tent company right away.

## After a storm

Do not go back in until the tent company has checked it. Water pooling on the roof, shifted ballast or loose stakes are all reasons to stay out. Ask whether the event can continue and see our [rain plan guide](/blog/rain-plan-with-a-tent).

## Frequently asked questions

### What wind speed can a party tent handle?

It depends on the tent, the sidewalls and the anchoring, so there is no single number. Ask your tent company for the rating in writing for your configuration.

### When should guests leave a tent in wind?

At the evacuation trigger your tent company sets in advance, and always for lightning. Do not wait for damage to start.

### Is a tent safe in a thunderstorm?

No. A tent does not protect from lightning. Move guests to a sturdy building at the first sign of thunder.

### Do sidewalls make a tent less wind resistant?

Yes. Closed sidewalls add sail area, so crews often lower or remove them as wind rises.

### Who decides to evacuate the tent?

It should be decided in your wind action plan, usually the tent company's lead or the host, with a named weather monitor. Chicago's permit plan asks who is responsible.

### Does tent insurance cover storm damage?

Policies vary, and we do not give insurance advice. Read your tent rental contract and ask your homeowner's insurer or an event insurance provider.
