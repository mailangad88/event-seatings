**Quick answer**

For a winter event, plan about 1 coat hanger per guest and about 1 rolling rack for every 40 to 50 guests, with extra space for bulky parkas. Set the coat check just inside the entrance but out of the main traffic path, staff it with one attendant per 75 to 100 guests and add a bin or bench for boots, hats and bags. Confirm rack capacity with your rental company.

Winter events in Chicago start with a pile of coats. At O'Hare, the normal January high is about 31 degrees F and the normal low is about 16 degrees, according to the Illinois State Climatologist's published normals, so most guests arrive in heavy parkas, scarves and boots. Without a plan, coats end up draped over chairs, stacked in corners and blocking the aisles, and the first 20 minutes of your event look like a lost-and-found. This guide shows how to make a coat check that works.

## Self-serve or staffed?

**Self-serve racks** are cheaper and fine for under 50 guests: guests hang their own coats on racks near the door. The risks are mixed-up coats and no one watching belongings.

**Staffed coat check** gives guests tickets and keeps coats organized and safe. It is the better choice for 75 or more guests, formal events and galas, where guests bring valuables. An attendant also helps older guests and speeds up the line.

**Hybrid.** Use a staffed table at the door with a rack behind it, and add a few self-serve racks for the late rush.

## Where to put the coat check

1. **Just inside the entrance,** not in the doorway, so guests do not block the door.
2. **Away from the main path** to the bar and lounge.
3. **Close to the entrance but out of drafts.** Cold air pouring in from a propped door chills the room.
4. **In sight of staff.** Keep it visible to security or venue personnel.
5. **With enough queue space.** Allow about 10 to 15 feet for a line.

## What else to rent

- **Hangers.** Rental companies often include them; ask how many.
- **Tickets or tags,** numbered, with matching tags for each coat.
- **Boot trays and mats.** Salt and snow slush ruin floors, and venues often ask for mats at the door.
- **Bench or chairs.** Guests need to sit to change shoes. See [chair rental options](/chairs).
- **Stanchions or signs** to direct the line.
- **Umbrella stands** for slushy or rainy days. See [umbrella stands and rain plans](/blog/umbrella-stands-and-rain-plans).
- **Lockable bin or cabinet** for valuables and gift envelopes.

## Chicago details

Chicago winter events often involve snow, ice and salt. Ask the venue about door mats, floor protection and whether it has a coat room, since many hotels, banquet halls and historic buildings already have one. If the venue provides a coat check, find out about capacity and cost before you add your own. Cold weather also affects timing: guests arrive in a rush, so open the coat check 15 to 20 minutes before the start. ## Liability

Ask your rental company and venue about responsibility for lost items. Many coat checks post a sign saying they are not responsible, and tell guests to keep valuables with them. Check your contract and event insurance.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many coat racks do I need for 100 guests?

Two or three rolling racks and 100 hangers, plus one or two attendants. Ask the rental company how many hangers each rack holds.

### Do I need a coat check attendant?

For 75 or more guests or a formal event, yes. For small casual events, self-serve racks work.

### Where should coat racks go?

Just inside the entrance, along a wall and out of the main walking path, with space for a short line.

### Can guests bring coats to their tables?

They can, but coats on chairs crowd the room and block aisles. A coat check keeps tables clear.

### How do I protect floors from snow and salt?

Use mats at the entrance, boot trays near the coat check and ask the venue about its floor protection rules.

### Should I add a tip jar?

Policies vary. Many hosts cover the attendant's pay and ask guests not to tip. Decide in advance and post a sign if needed.
