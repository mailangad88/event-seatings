A sign tells guests where to go, where to sit and what to drink. It also sets the tone as they walk in. Signs need something to stand on, and that is where easels, stands and frames come in. This guide covers the signs most events use, the easels that hold them, and the mistakes that blow over in the wind off Lake Michigan.

## Signs most events use

<div class="table-wrap">

| Sign | Typical size | Holder |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome sign | 24 x 36 in or larger | Floor easel |
| Seating chart | 24 x 36 to 36 x 48 in | Floor easel or board |
| Bar menu | 11 x 17 to 18 x 24 in | Table easel or frame |
| Table numbers | 5 x 7 in | Table stand |
| Directional signs | 12 x 18 in | Stakes or stands |
| Memorial or photo display | 16 x 20 in | Table easel |

</div>

Sizes are common planning sizes. Ask your printer and rental company to confirm the exact dimensions.

## Choosing an easel

- **Wood easel** suits rustic and garden events.
- **Gold or black metal** suits modern and formal rooms.
- **Acrylic stands** suit clear, minimalist styles.
- **Wood A-frame** is stable on grass and holds chalkboards.

Choose a holder that can carry the weight of your sign. A foam board is light, while a framed mirror or a wood sign can tip an easel.

## Place them for flow

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Welcome sign:</strong> at the entrance, visible from the parking lot or door.</li>
<li><strong>Seating chart:</strong> next to the entrance, not in a hallway, so guests can step aside.</li>
<li><strong>Bar sign:</strong> at eye level on the bar.</li>
<li><strong>Directional signs:</strong> at turns and restrooms.</li>
<li><strong>Memorial sign:</strong> near the guest book or the head table.</li>
</ol>

## Make signs readable

Choose a font that guests can read from 6 feet away. Use two colors at most. Keep the text short. Place signs at about 5 feet so eyes land on them. In low light, add a clip light or a lamp.

## Outdoor signage

Outdoors, signs act like sails. Use weighted bases or stakes. Foam board folds in wind and warps in rain, so use a sturdier material such as wood, acrylic or aluminum. If you are at a park or forest preserve, check the park district's rules on stakes and signs, because many districts restrict what you can set up. Check with the district before you plan.

## Corporate and nonprofit signs

Corporate events use sponsor signs, check-in signs, step-and-repeat banners and agendas. See the [corporate photo backdrop guide](/blog/corporate-event-photo-backdrop-guide). Nonprofit galas often add auction item signs and bid sheets. Plan one easel per item category.

## Style notes

A [wishbone chair](/chairs/wishbone) or [bentwood](/chairs/bentwood) near a sign display keeps the look warm. Match the easel metal to the hardware in your venue, such as gold with brass fixtures.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Placing the seating chart in a hallway where lines block everyone.
- Printing text too small to read from 6 feet.
- Using a lightweight easel outdoors.
- Forgetting a sign for the restroom or the gift table.


## Printing and lead times

Most signs are printed on foam board, acrylic or wood by a local print shop or an online maker, and custom work can take a couple of weeks, so order after you finalize the guest count and menu. Keep the seating chart for last because it changes up to the final week. Proof the spelling of every name twice. If a sign will stand in a Chicago-area lobby or hotel entrance, ask the venue where they allow easels and whether they require a sign to be freestanding rather than leaned on a wall.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size should a wedding welcome sign be?

24 x 36 inches is common and readable from a few feet away. Larger signs can be 36 x 48 inches. Confirm the easel can hold it.

### How tall should a sign easel be?

Eye level is around 5 feet to the middle of the sign. Floor easels range from about 5 to 6 feet tall.

### Where should the seating chart go?

Near the entrance in a spot where guests can step aside, not in a narrow hall. Add a clip light if the room is dim.

### How do I keep signs from blowing over outdoors?

Use weighted bases, sandbags or stakes and avoid foam board. Place easels where buildings or tent walls block gusts.

### What material is best for signs?

Wood, acrylic and aluminum last outdoors. Foam board is light for indoor use. Choose by weather and weight.

### How many signs do I need?

One for each decision point: welcome, seating, bar, directions and restrooms. Skip signs that guests do not need.
