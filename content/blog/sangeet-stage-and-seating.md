**Quick answer**

A sangeet is a performance night, so build the room around the stage. Plan a stage of at least 16 x 12 feet for group dances, seating that faces the stage with no more than a 45-degree turn for any guest, a dance floor in front of the stage for open dancing later and rounds of 8 to 10 for dinner. For 200 guests that means about 20 rounds, a 24 x 24-foot dance floor and a clear 6-foot walkway to the stage on each side.

</div>

The sangeet is a music and dance celebration, usually held the night before or a few days before a South Asian wedding. Families rehearse choreographed performances, a DJ or MC runs the show and dinner often happens in between. The result is a hybrid of a theater and a banquet, and it needs both: good sightlines for a program and enough space for a dance party.

## Build the room from the stage

Start with the stage, then place everything else.

- **Stage size.** A group performance of 8 to 12 dancers needs about 16 x 12 feet. Larger groups need more. A 12 x 8-foot stage fits a small act or an MC. Ask your performers how many people will be on stage at once. See [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events).
- **Height.** A stage 18 to 24 inches high gives most guests a view. Higher stages need stairs and a railing for safety. Ask the venue what it allows.
- **Steps and wings.** Dancers need a way to get on and off without crossing the audience.
- **Power and sound.** Speakers, microphones, a DJ table and monitors all need power. See [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) and [sound systems for speeches](/blog/sound-system-for-speeches).

## Seating sized to the show

Most sangeets run 3 to 5 hours, with 30 to 90 minutes of performances. Guests sit for dinner and rise to dance. Rounds of 8 to 10 work.

Keep the nearest table at least 8 to 10 feet from the stage edge for sightlines and safety, and leave a clear lane for performers.

## Sightlines and table angles

Round tables with all chairs around the table leave some guests with their backs to the stage. Fix it by:

- **Leaving the stage side of each table empty** (seating 6 to 7 instead of 10 on front tables).
- **Angling tables** slightly toward the stage.
- **Placing family and elders** at the front where they see best.
- **Using high-top cocktail tables** at the back for guests who want to stand.

## Chicago notes

- Many Chicago-area banquet halls and hotels have built-in stages. Ask for measurements and weight limits, then decide whether to add a riser.
- Ask about load-in times for sound equipment and decor. Performers often want a sound check.
- Parking and loading docks in downtown Chicago can limit delivery times. Plan ahead.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big should a sangeet stage be?

A stage of 16 x 12 feet works for most group dances. For small acts or an MC, 12 x 8 feet is enough. Ask performers how many dancers are on stage at once.

### Where should family sit?

At the front, close to the stage, so elders can see and hear. Place the couple's table near them.

### How many tables do I need for 200 guests?

About 20 rounds of 10, plus tables for gifts, DJ and food. Use fewer seats at front tables to keep sightlines clear.

### Do I need a dance floor and a stage?

Usually yes. The stage is for performances, and the dance floor is for the open dancing that follows. Some venues have a built-in floor.

### How far should tables be from the stage?

At least 8 to 10 feet, so guests can see the whole stage and dancers have room. Follow your venue's rules.

### Can I host a sangeet outdoors in Chicago?

Yes, in warm months, with a tent, a stage and a weather plan. See outdoor event chairs and weather and [tent permits](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs).
