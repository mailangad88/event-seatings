Chair decor is where budgets quietly balloon. A bunch of flowers on every chair in a 120-guest room is a big order, and guests do not remember it. What they remember is the aisle, the couple's seats and the first view of the room. This guide shows what to decorate, what to skip and how to match the decor to the chair you have rented, so the result looks planned instead of busy.

For a ceremony, decorate the aisle end of the first three or four rows. For a reception, decorate the head table or sweetheart chairs. Everything else stays clean.

The principle is simple: if a chair already has detail, leave it alone. If it is plain, give it one accent.

## Five easy ideas

1. **Greenery sprig.** Eucalyptus, olive or ruscus tied with twine or ribbon. It is light, inexpensive and holds up outdoors.
2. **Floral cluster.** A small bunch of the same flowers as the bouquets, tied with a satin ribbon. Best on aisle chairs only.
3. **Hanging sign.** A small board for family seats or the couple. See [Mr and Mrs chair signs](/blog/mr-and-mrs-chair-signs).
4. **Napkin tie.** A cloth napkin tied to the back with a sprig. It costs almost nothing.
5. **Dried bundle.** Pampas, wheat or lavender. These last, are easy to prep and suit fall or boho weddings.

For what sashes and covers cost and whether they are worth it, see [chair sashes and covers: worth it?](/blog/chair-sashes-and-covers-worth-it) That guide focuses on fabric; this one is about natural decor.

## Practical rules

- **Use removable ties.** Ribbon, floral wire covered in paper tape or twine. Never glue or staple anything to a rental chair.
- **Test on one chair first.** Make sure the decor sits level and does not tip the chair back.
- **Keep it light.** A heavy arrangement on the back of a chair can make it tip when a guest leans.
- **Use fresh flowers late.** Tie them on shortly before the ceremony. In a Chicago summer, they wilt in direct sun in under an hour.
- **Remove everything at the end.** Have a person assigned to clear decor from chairs before the rental pickup.

## Fall and winter notes

In October and November, dried and preserved decor is more reliable than fresh. In winter, evergreen sprigs, cedar and berry stems last all evening in a heated room and bring scent. See [winter wedding seating](/blog/winter-wedding-seating) for how to handle coats and room flow.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best way to decorate wedding chairs?

Choose a small number of chairs, such as the aisle chairs and the couple's seats, and add one accent like greenery or a sign. Leave the rest plain.

### How much does chair decor cost?

Cost depends on flowers, season and the number of chairs, so ask your florist for a quote. Decorating only the aisle and head table keeps it modest.

### Can I tie things to rental chairs?

Usually yes with removable ties, but not glue, staples, nails or tape on the finish. Ask your rental company for its rules.

### Do I need to decorate every chair?

No. Guests focus on the aisle and the couple's seats. Decorating every chair adds cost and labor with little payoff.

### What decor works on ghost chairs?

A single stem or a small sign. Heavy decor pulls on the clear acrylic and spoils its clean look. See [ghost chair rental logistics](/blog/ghost-chair-rental-chicago).

### How far ahead can I attach flowers?

Fresh flowers should go on within an hour or two of the event. Greenery and dried decor can go on earlier.

### What can I use instead of sashes?

Greenery, napkin ties, small signs or nothing at all. A well-chosen chair often needs no decoration.
