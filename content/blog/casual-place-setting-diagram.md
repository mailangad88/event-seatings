**Quick answer**

A casual place setting has five pieces: a dinner plate in the center, a fork on the left, a knife and spoon (if needed) on the right with the blade facing the plate, a water glass above the knife, and a napkin on the left or under the fork. Skip the bread plate, charger and second glass unless the menu calls for them. It takes about 20 inches of table edge per guest.

</div>

Not every dinner needs a fork for each course. A Sunday supper in Oak Park, a backyard graduation lunch in Schaumburg or an office holiday meal in the Loop all call for a setting that looks tidy but does not intimidate anyone. This guide shows the casual layout, how to scale it up from five pieces to eight, and where it differs from a formal table. If you want the other end of the spectrum, see the [place setting spacing guide](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements).

## What each piece is for

**Dinner plate.** Sits in the center, about an inch from the table edge. If you serve salad first, bring it out on a salad plate set on top, or serve it from the buffet.

**Fork.** On the left, tines up. If you serve salad and entree with the same fork, one is fine.

**Knife.** On the right, blade turned toward the plate.

**Spoon.** On the right of the knife. Skip it if you are not serving soup, coffee or dessert that needs one.

**Glass.** Above the knife. A single water glass covers most casual dinners, and wine glasses sit to its right.

**Napkin.** On the left of the fork, or on the plate. Fold it in a simple rectangle; fancy folds are optional.

## When to add pieces

Use the menu to decide. If you serve soup, add a bowl on top of the dinner plate and a soup spoon on the right. If you serve cake, bring dessert plates and forks out with dessert instead of setting them at the start. If you serve wine, add one wine glass at the right of the water glass. The rule is to put out only what guests will use, because every extra piece is more to wash, more to count and more to break. Our count guide, [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest), helps with the numbers.

## Casual does not mean mismatched

You can mix patterns on purpose, such as plain plates with colored napkins. What you want to avoid is chipped or mismatched pieces that look accidental. Rental plates arrive matched, which solves the problem when you do not own 24 of the same. For ideas on mixing, see [colored and patterned plates](/blog/colored-and-patterned-plates-trend).

## Setting for a buffet

At a buffet, the guest picks up the plate at the start of the line. Many hosts set only the napkin, flatware wrapped in the napkin, and a glass at each seat, with plates stacked at the buffet. That removes the plate from the table and saves space. This works especially well at long farm tables in barns or backyards; see [dinnerware sizes for buffet, plated and family-style](/blog/dinnerware-sizes-buffet-plated-family-style).

## Outdoor tips for Chicago

Wind off the lake and open yards in the suburbs can send napkins flying. Place the napkin under the fork or tuck it under the plate edge. Use heavier glassware or a coaster on a breezy patio. If the table is under a tent, check that the cloth does not drag in damp grass.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a casual place setting?

A casual place setting is a simple layout with a plate, fork, knife, glass and napkin. It uses fewer pieces than a formal setting and skips chargers. It suits family dinners, barbecues and most informal parties.

### Which side does the fork go on?

The fork goes on the left of the plate. The knife and spoon go on the right, with the knife blade facing the plate. The napkin can go with the fork or on the plate.

### Do I need a bread plate for a casual dinner?

No. A casual dinner can serve bread on the dinner plate. Add a bread plate only if bread and butter are a feature of the meal.

### How do I set a table for a buffet?

Set the napkin with wrapped flatware and a glass at each seat. Stack plates at the start of the buffet line. This keeps the table clear and avoids having plates sit before the food is ready.

### How many forks do I need for a casual dinner?

One fork is enough for most casual menus. Add a second fork only if you serve a separate salad course or dessert that needs one.

### Can I use paper plates for a casual dinner?

Yes, and sometimes it makes sense. For parks and big yard parties, see the comparison in [compostable vs rented china](/blog/compostable-vs-rented-china).
