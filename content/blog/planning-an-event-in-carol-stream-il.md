Carol Stream is a busy DuPage village with large neighborhoods, many community groups and plenty of fundraisers, school events and church gatherings. Its events are often run by volunteers, which means budgets matter and planning time is short. This guide is built for that.

## Park district and village

The Carol Stream Park District runs the village's parks and facilities. The Village of Carol Stream manages public property and licenses, including a raffle license page on its website. If you run a fundraiser with a raffle, ask the village about the license early, because raffles have rules that vary by organization and size.

## Volunteer-run fundraisers

A fundraiser dinner for 120 might run on a volunteer committee and a modest budget. A few decisions save money. Borrow what you can from the venue, and rent only what you must. Ask whether the venue includes tables and chairs before you order more.

## A Carol Stream example

For a 120-guest silent auction dinner, plan 15 rounds of 8, a registration table, an auction table and a stage or podium. See [stage and riser sizes](/blog/stage-and-riser-sizes-for-events). A [velvet chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) (about $18-25) at the head table makes the honoree stand out.

## Fundraiser checklist

A fundraiser has more moving parts than a party. Check each of these early.

1. **Raffle or auction licenses.** Ask the village.
2. **Insurance.** Venues may require a certificate.
3. **Registration and check-in.** A table at the door with clear signs.
4. **Auction display.** Tables with floor-length cloths and good lighting.
5. **Sound.** A podium microphone and a place for the emcee.
6. **Volunteer schedule.** Who sets up, who greets, who cleans.

If your group is based in Chicago or elsewhere and holding the event in Carol Stream, confirm parking and accessibility ahead of time.

## Staying on a fundraiser budget

A fundraiser dinner should raise money, not spend it. A few habits help.

- **Count real attendance.** Ticket sales usually run lower than invitations. Plan for 80% of RSVPs and keep a short wait list.
- **Borrow from the venue.** Many halls include tables and chairs. Rent only what you must.
- **Use fewer, better pieces.** One good linen on each table looks better than three cheap ones.
- **Ask for a firm quote.** Ask what is included in delivery, setup and pickup. See [how event rental quotes work](/blog/how-event-rental-quotes-work).
- **Plan a clean breakdown.** Volunteers who know the pickup time avoid late fees.

Compare chair styles on cost and look, and test a few before you decide. If your group is based in Chicago and hosting in Carol Stream, ask the venue about loading times, since churches and schools often have strict hours.
