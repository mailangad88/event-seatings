**Quick answer**

Plan a 50th birthday six to ten weeks out. Choose a format that suits the guest of honor (dinner, cocktail party, backyard party or surprise), set a budget around food, drinks and rentals, and aim for a guest list of 30 to 75. Seat older guests comfortably, include a photo display or tribute, and keep a clear timeline for toasts.

A 50th birthday often brings together friends, family and coworkers from several decades of someone's life, and that mix is both the best part and the hardest part. Ages range from toddlers to grandparents, tastes differ, and everyone wants a chance to say hello. A smart plan makes space for conversation, comfortable seating and a moment that feels personal.

## Choose the format

**Seated dinner (30 to 60).** Round tables, a plated or family-style meal, and a short program. This feels most special and suits older relatives.

**Cocktail party (40 to 100).** Standing tables, appetizers, a bar and a few lounge groupings. It is social and flexible, but give older guests plenty of seats.

**Backyard or garden party (30 to 75).** A relaxed cookout with a tent, string lights and a mix of seating. See [backyard party layout plan](/blog/backyard-party-layout-plan).

**Surprise party.** Needs a trusted co-planner and a good cover story. See [surprise party planning rentals](/blog/surprise-party-planning-rentals).

**Trip or small gathering.** Some people prefer a weekend away with close family. Plan the guest list and budget the same way.

## Themes that feel personal, not cheesy

The best themes come from the guest of honor, not from a party store aisle. Consider the year they were born, a favorite era of music, a hobby, a place they love or a color they wear. A "50 years of" photo timeline is a crowd-pleaser. A favorite cuisine can set the menu. Many hosts also pick a simple black-and-gold or garden palette and let photos do the talking.

## Build the guest list thoughtfully

Divide guests into groups: family, close friends, neighbors, work friends and old friends. Decide which groups are in before you start counting. If friends will travel, send a save-the-date early. Use the [guest list and RSVP tracker](/downloads/guest-list-rsvp-tracker) to follow responses.

## A tribute without the awkwardness

A slideshow, a photo wall or a memory book gives guests a way to participate. Ask guests to bring a short note or a photo, and keep toasts to two to four people at two to three minutes each. A [guest book alternative](/blog/guest-book-alternatives-for-weddings) works well here too. See [milestone birthday party ideas](/blog/milestone-birthday-party-ideas-30-40-50-60) for more.

## Seating older guests and mixed ages

Provide comfortable chairs with arms for older guests and avoid placing them far from the restroom. Keep walkways clear for walkers and wheelchairs, with at least 36 inches between tables. See [comfortable chairs for older guests](/blog/comfortable-chairs-for-older-guests) and [ADA accessible event seating](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways). If kids are invited, set up a kids' table with activities.

## Food and drinks

Many hosts choose a buffet or stations for flexibility, or plated service for a formal feel. If you want help choosing, read [plated vs buffet vs family style equipment](/blog/plated-vs-buffet-vs-family-style-equipment). Plan the bar based on the crowd. Wine, beer and one signature cocktail often cover it. Add coffee and tea service for after dinner. See [coffee and tea service rental](/blog/coffee-and-tea-service-rental).

## Program and timeline

A simple schedule: guests arrive and mingle for 45 minutes, dinner is served, toasts happen during dessert, then cake, dancing or conversation. Keep the program short. The [run of show template](/downloads/run-of-show-template) helps you assign a person to each step.

## Budget

Set a total, then divide it among food, drinks, venue, rentals, entertainment and decor. Get two or three quotes for every major cost. See [event rental budget by guest count](/blog/event-rental-budget-by-guest-count).

## Chicago-area notes

Many Chicago-area 50th birthday parties happen in restaurant private rooms, banquet halls, backyards and brewery or winery spaces. Ask venues about minimums, deposits and what is included, since some supply tables and chairs and others do not. See [brewery and winery event venues](/blog/brewery-and-winery-event-venues-chicago). If you host at home in a suburb, check with your village for rules on tents and street parking. Plan for weather: summer afternoons can be hot and humid, and fall evenings can turn cool fast. For a winter party, plan for a coat rack and a clear entry path. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a 50th birthday party?

Pick a format, set a guest list and budget, reserve the space, book food and entertainment, and send invitations six weeks ahead. Add a photo tribute and a short program.

### How much does a 50th birthday party cost?

It depends on guest count, food and venue. A backyard party costs far less than a catered dinner in a rented hall. Many planners set a per-guest budget and get quotes for the rest, because Chicago-area prices vary.

### What are good 50th birthday party themes?

Popular themes include a decade or birth-year theme, a favorite cuisine, a garden party, black and gold, a hobby or sport, and a 'then and now' photo wall. Choose something the guest of honor loves.

### How many guests should I invite to a 50th birthday?

Most 50th parties have 30 to 75 guests. Set the number by your space and budget, and invite family, close friends and a few longtime friends.

### Should a 50th birthday party be a surprise?

It can be, if the guest of honor enjoys surprises. Ask a trusted friend or family member first. If in doubt, tell them there will be a dinner and keep the guest list a surprise.

### How long should a 50th birthday party last?

Three to four hours works well: one hour to arrive and mingle, an hour for dinner, then toasts, cake and conversation or dancing.

### What rentals do I need for a 50th birthday party?

Many hosts rent round tables, chairs, linens, glassware, a dance floor and a tent. Count a seat for every guest at a seated meal. See our [chair guide](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).
