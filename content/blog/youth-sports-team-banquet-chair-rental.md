**Quick answer**

For a youth sports team banquet, set round tables of 8 for players and families, with a coach's or awards table at the front and a clear aisle so every player has a short walk-up when it's their turn for a trophy or certificate. Count one seat per player plus their family, not just the roster, since most teams have two or more family members attending per kid. A church or school hall rarely has enough matching chairs for a group that size, so most teams end up renting.

</div>

Most Chicago-area youth sports leagues wrap the season with a banquet at a church hall, school cafeteria or park district room rather than a restaurant, since team sizes and family counts make a sit-down restaurant booking expensive fast. That venue choice is also why rentals come up: the room usually has folding tables but not nearly enough matching chairs for a room full of families.

## How to lay out a team banquet

1. **Put the coaches' table at the front**, with the trophies, certificates or a team photo backdrop close by.
2. **Set round tables of 8 for the room**, seating each player with their own family rather than splitting the team from the parents.
3. **Leave a clear aisle from the tables to the front**, so the walk-up for awards doesn't wind around other families' chairs.
4. **Keep the youngest players' families closest to the front**, since little kids get restless waiting for their name to be called.

## How many chairs to rent

Count by attending families, not by the team roster:

- **Plan on 2 to 4 seats per player**, covering parents, siblings and sometimes grandparents, not just the kid on the team.
- **Ask the league or team parent coordinator for an RSVP count** a week or two out, since turnout varies a lot by how the season went.
- **Add a few extra seats near the back** for anyone who didn't RSVP but shows up anyway, which is common at these banquets.

## What the venue usually does and doesn't supply

- **Church halls and school cafeterias** almost always have folding tables, but their chair count is sized for a normal weeknight crowd, not a full team plus every family.
- **Park district rooms** vary a lot. Some have stacking chairs for 100-plus; smaller fieldhouse rooms often have 30 or fewer.
- **Ask the venue for their exact chair count in writing before you order anything**, since "we have chairs" often means fewer than you'd guess once every family shows up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a team banquet?
Plan 2 to 4 seats per player to cover parents and siblings, not just the roster count. Get an RSVP estimate from the team parent coordinator a week or two before the event, since turnout varies by season.

### How should seating be set up for a youth sports banquet?
Round tables of 8 work well for players and their families together, with a coach's or awards table at the front and a clear aisle for the trophy walk-up.

### Does our banquet hall already have enough chairs?
Often not enough for the whole group. Ask the venue for their exact chair count in writing, since most halls are set up for a smaller weeknight crowd, not a full team banquet with every family attending.

### What's a good budget-friendly chair for a team banquet?
A teak-look garden folding chair is a simple, inexpensive step up from a plastic folding chair and stacks away fast after the event. Save a nicer chair, if you want one, for just the coaches' table.
