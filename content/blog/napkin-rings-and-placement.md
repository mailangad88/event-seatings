**Quick answer**

At a formal dinner the napkin goes to the left of the forks, or on the center of the charger. At a casual or buffet meal it can sit on the plate, under the fork, or wrapped around the flatware. A napkin ring holds a folded or rolled napkin and sits on the plate or the napkin. Pick a spot, repeat it at every seat, and make sure the ring and fold do not hide your menu or place card.

</div>

Napkin placement is a small decision that shows up in every photo. It also affects service: servers need to reach plates, and guests need to find their napkin without disturbing the table. This guide covers the common positions, how napkin rings work, and how to coordinate everything with the rest of a place setting at a Chicago-area wedding or banquet.

### Left of the forks
The traditional formal spot. The napkin sits folded in a rectangle with the open edge facing right. It frees the plate for a menu or place card, and servers can set plates without moving it.

### On the plate or charger
Common for weddings and photo-friendly tables. A folded napkin, sometimes with a ring, a menu or a sprig of greenery, sits in the center. The server removes it when the first course arrives or moves it to the left.

### Under or around the flatware
Casual and buffet settings often wrap the napkin around the fork and knife, so guests pick up one bundle at the end of a line. Pocket folds do the same job; see [napkin folds for events](/blog/napkin-folds-for-events).

### In a glass
A fan or pleated fold stands in a water glass. It looks formal but crowds the glassware and relaxes in humidity.

## How napkin rings work

A napkin ring is a band, usually metal, wood, rope or fabric, that holds a rolled or folded napkin. Choose the ring for the style first, then size it to the napkin.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Ring style | Best with | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gold or brass | Formal, glam, fall | Warm, pairs with gold chairs |
| Silver | Modern, winter | Cool, pairs with clear chairs |
| Wood or rope | Rustic, boho, barn | Casual, pairs with burlap or linen |
| Fabric or ribbon | Garden, romantic | Light, easy to match palette |
| Acrylic or crystal | Glam, modern | Catches light |

</div>

Rings hold a 20-inch napkin best when it is folded into a thin rectangle or rolled. A bulky napkin or a thick fold will not fit through a small ring. Ask your supplier for the ring's inside diameter, or test one with the napkin you plan to use.

## Place cards and menus

If you use a place card or menu, decide where it goes before you place the napkin. Common combinations:

- **Ring on the napkin, place card tucked under the ring.** Compact and tidy.
- **Menu on the plate, napkin on the left.** Gives guests something to read as they sit.
- **Place card above the plate.** Keeps the plate clean for the first course.

Do not stack three things on the plate. A napkin, a menu and a card on a charger look busy and are hard to clear.

## Chicago notes

At downtown hotels and large banquet halls, the catering team often sets napkins and rings, so tell them your preferred placement in writing. At backyard tents, setup is usually a team of friends and a rental crew, so a simple placement like the left of the forks or on the plate is easier to execute. In windy tents, place the napkin under a glass, a menu or a ring, because light napkins can blow off.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where does the napkin go on a formal table setting?

To the left of the forks, folded in a rectangle, or on the center of the charger or plate. Either is correct. Choose one and repeat it at every seat.

### Do you need napkin rings at a wedding?

No. They are a styling choice, not a requirement. A well-folded napkin on a plate looks neat on its own, and rings add polish and a way to hold a sprig or a place card.

### How do I fit a napkin through a ring?

Fold the napkin into a long, thin rectangle or roll it, then slide the ring over the center. Thick fabrics need larger rings, so test before ordering.

### Should the napkin be on the plate or beside it?

Either works. On the plate looks styled in photos; to the left leaves the plate clear for service. If you have a first course already plated, left of the forks is easier for servers.

### Can I use a ribbon instead of a ring?

Yes. A ribbon or twine tie holds a napkin or a bundle of flatware and fits a boho or rustic style. Be sure the ribbon is secure so it does not slide off.

### Who sets napkin rings at an event?

It depends on the vendor. Rental companies, caterers or decor crews can all do it, sometimes for a fee. Ask before your event and put the answer in writing.
