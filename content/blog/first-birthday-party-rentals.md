**Quick answer**

A first birthday is really a party for the adults who love the baby. Rent seats for most guests because grandparents and relatives stay longer than at a typical kids' party, a main table for food, a separate cake and smash cake table, and one or two child-size tables if toddlers attend. A 30-guest party usually needs about 24 chairs, 3 to 4 tables and linens, and a protected, shaded cake spot.

</div>

The guest of honor is one year old and won't remember a minute of it, but the photos will live on a mantel for decades. That makes a first birthday a hybrid: half family brunch, half photo shoot. The rentals you need follow from that, and so does the layout, which has to work for people carrying babies.

## Who is actually coming

Most first birthdays in the Chicago area draw 20 to 50 people. Expect:

- Grandparents and older relatives who want a comfortable chair with arms or a solid back.
- Parents of toddlers who will eat one-handed, standing, and be up and down.
- A few kids who can sit at a small table, and a lot who can't be pinned down.

Seat roughly 75 percent of adults and 100 percent of grandparents. Skip chairs for the toddlers and give them floor space with mats.

## What to rent

### Adult seating
Pick a chair that is comfortable for a long afternoon. Sturdy [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) (estimated $8 to $11) work for a rustic look, [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) ($10 to $14) stay out of the way of a colorful decor palette, and garden folding chairs ($5 to $7) are fine for a backyard. Whatever you choose, keep arms or a firm back in mind for older relatives.

### Tables
- One 6-foot or 8-foot table for the main food spread.
- A smaller 36- to 48-inch round or a cocktail table for the cake and smash cake. Round tables are easier to photograph from three sides.
- One or two child-size tables if toddlers will sit and color.
- 60-inch rounds for adult seating, 8 per table. See our [round table size guide](/blog/round-table-sizes-and-seating-48-60-66-72-inch).

### Linens
A floor-length cloth on the cake table hides crates, spare diapers and a cooler. Use the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) to match sizes. A tablecloth in a muted color works well with bright balloons.

### Dishes and serving
Rent plates and flatware only if you are serving a full meal. Many first birthdays serve finger food, small plates and cake, and disposable works fine for that. Ask for a quote on both.

## The smash cake corner

The smash cake photo is the moment everyone wants to catch. A good setup has three parts.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Light:</strong> face the baby toward soft indirect light, not a harsh midday sun. Shade from a tent or canopy helps.</li>
<li><strong>Surface:</strong> a clean washable mat or drop cloth under a high chair, with the backdrop behind it.</li>
<li><strong>Table:</strong> a round table for the main cake and extras, at 30 inches tall, so photos catch it from adult eye level.</li>
<li><strong>Cleanup:</strong> wipes, a towel and a change of clothes within reach.</li>
</ol>

## A timing plan

<div class="table-wrap">

| Time | What happens | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Hour 0 | Guests arrive, drinks served | Gives people time to settle in |
| Hour 1 | Lunch or snacks | Babies are usually fed before the cake |
| Hour 1.5 | Cake and smash photos | Before nap time and meltdown hour |
| Hour 2 | Open gifts or skip | Many families skip to keep it short |
| Hour 2.5 | Wrap up | Babies tire before parents do |

</div>

Most first birthday parties run 2 to 3 hours, and it's smart to schedule the cake before a typical nap time. Midday is usually best.

## Chicago-area notes

If you're hosting in a condo or townhome, check your building's rules about deliveries and elevators. Ask whether tables can be brought through the lobby at a set time, and whether you need to reserve a freight elevator. For outdoor parties, a shaded spot matters more than a perfect backdrop, since babies overheat quickly. For colder months, Chicago winters call for an indoor space with enough room for coats. A coat rack or a bed in a spare room works.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I rent for a first birthday party?

Chairs for adult guests, a couple of tables for food and cake, linens, and optionally a child-size table and a high chair. If you're serving a full meal, add plates and flatware. A tent helps if the party is outdoors.

### How many chairs do I need for a first birthday?

Plan chairs for about 75 percent of adult guests, plus a few extras. For a party with 30 total guests, about 20 to 24 chairs works. Toddlers usually don't need chairs.

### Do I need a high chair at a first birthday?

You need at least one for the baby, and it's often easier to bring your own. If you need more for guests with infants, ask whether your rental order can include them. Ask about it on a planning call.

### How long should a first birthday party last?

Two to three hours is typical. Schedule the cake before nap time so the smash cake photos catch the baby in a good mood.

### What size table works for a smash cake?

A 36-inch or 48-inch round table is a good size. It gives room for a main cake, a smash cake, and a few items without overwhelming the photo.

### Should I rent a tent for a first birthday?

If your party is outdoors in summer or has a fall chill, yes. A tent protects the baby from sun and the cake from wind-blown debris. Check [how to size a tent](/blog/what-size-tent-do-i-need) before you order.
