**Quick answer**

A day-after wedding brunch is easier than a reception: casual chairs, a mix of tables, simple linens, coffee and a buffet or family-style service. Plan one chair per guest, a table for each 8 to 10 people, two coffee stations for 40 or more, and 10 to 12 square feet per guest. Keep the look relaxed and book pickup for the same afternoon so the venue is clear.

</div>

The day after the wedding, out-of-town guests are tired, a little hungry and happy to linger. A brunch for 20 to 80 people gives them one more good meal with the couple and makes the travel feel worthwhile. It also asks for far less equipment than the reception, if you plan it right.

## Pick a style

The brunch should feel different from last night. Soft, bright, casual and warm usually works.

## What to rent

**Chairs.** One per guest. Light wood chairs suit a brunch: [bentwood bistro chairs](/chairs/bentwood), [cane-back chairs](/chairs/cane-back) or [garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) for a patio. If the wedding chairs are still on site, ask the rental company about keeping a set rather than a second delivery.

**Tables.** 60-inch rounds seat 8, 72-inch rounds seat 10. Long tables encourage conversation but make serving harder. A buffet table needs a 6- or 8-foot banquet table with a floor-length or lap-length cloth. See [banquet table sizes](/blog/banquet-table-sizes-6-ft-vs-8-ft).

**Linens.** Lap-length cloths or a runner on a bare table keep the look casual. Cloth napkins are nice, and paper napkins are fine at a patio brunch. Use the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) if you want floor-length.

**Dinnerware and glassware.** Brunch needs a plate, a fork and knife, a coffee cup and a water glass per guest, plus juice or mimosa glasses. Add a bread plate if you are serving pastries. See [dinnerware for brunch events](/blog/dinnerware-for-brunch-events).

**Coffee and drink service.** One 100-cup urn serves about 50 coffee drinkers; add a tea station and a juice dispenser. See [how many coffee urns](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party). For a mimosa bar, plan a table, an ice tub and glassware.

**Buffet equipment.** Chafing dishes, serving utensils and sneeze guards if the venue needs them. See [how many chafing dishes](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet).

## Where brunches are held

- **A restaurant or hotel:** simplest, since they supply everything. Ask for a private room.
- **The couple's family home or a rented house:** you rent most things; check parking and neighbors.
- **A park pavilion or garden:** check permits with the park district or forest preserve and ask about power and restrooms.
- **The wedding venue the next morning:** often cheapest in setup, because the tables are still in place. Ask if the venue allows it.

## Timing and cleanup

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Night before:</strong> confirm delivery and setup time with the rental company.</li>
<li><strong>Brunch morning:</strong> deliver and set up two hours before guests arrive.</li>
<li><strong>Brunch:</strong> typically 10 am to 1 pm.</li>
<li><strong>Midday pickup:</strong> book a pickup window that ends before the afternoon.</li>
</ol>

Tell your rental company the brunch is the day after a large event if you are ordering from the same venue. They can often combine returns.

## Chicago and suburban notes

Sunday morning delivery rules matter. Some Chicago buildings restrict loading hours on weekends, and some suburban neighborhoods have noise rules for early trucks. Ask your building or village. If the brunch is outdoors, watch the forecast: a patio brunch in October needs a heater or a fall-back inside. Sunday parking can be easier than Saturday, but street cleaning and special events like the Chicago Marathon (usually in October) can close roads, so check the city's event calendar if your date is near a big race.

## Ideas that feel like a brunch

- A pancake or waffle station, with a chef or self-serve.
- A mimosa bar with juices and fruit.
- A bagel board and lox table.
- A photo wall of last night's pictures on a screen or board.
- A gift opening area for early presents, if the couple is open to it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to rent chairs for a day-after brunch?

If the space doesn't have chairs, yes. One per guest, plus a few spares. If you keep wedding chairs, ask the rental company about a simple extended rental.

### How many guests come to a day-after brunch?

Usually a subset of the wedding: out-of-town guests, the wedding party and family. Many are 20 to 80 people. Ask your guests to RSVP so you can plan food and rentals.

### What linens work for a casual brunch?

Lap-length cloths, runners or even bare wood tables with cloth napkins. Floor-length cloths look formal and cost more than a simple runner.

### How much coffee do I need?

Plan about 1 cup per guest, and 2 for heavy coffee drinkers. A 100-cup urn covers about 50 guests; add a second at 80 or more.

### Can I hold the brunch at the wedding venue?

Often. Ask whether the room is available the next morning and whether tables and chairs stay up. Some venues charge for extra time.

### Is a buffet or plated brunch better?

A buffet is the usual choice, because guests arrive at different times. Plated brunch works for smaller groups of about 30 or fewer.
