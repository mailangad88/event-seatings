**Quick answer**

Rent 20 x 20-inch cloth napkins for a seated dinner. They are the standard dinner size, big enough to fold and to cover a lap. Smaller 17 to 18-inch napkins suit brunch or cocktail service. For color, choose a napkin that is either one shade deeper than the tablecloth, or a clear contrast, so the fold reads in photos. Avoid matching a napkin exactly to a white or ivory plate.

</div>

Napkins get picked last and noticed first. Guests hold them, set them down and tuck them in, and photographers shoot them on every plate. The two decisions that matter are size and color, and both are cheap to get right if you decide before the order is final. Here is how to choose for Chicago-area weddings, galas and corporate dinners.

## Picking the color

Think in layers. The tablecloth is the base, the napkin is an accent, and the plate and chair are the supporting cast.

### Three safe approaches

1. **Tone on tone.** Napkin one step darker than the cloth: ivory cloth, champagne napkin. Calm, elegant, photographs well.
2. **Clear contrast.** Ivory cloth with deep green, navy or burgundy napkins. The fold stands out, and the color links to your flowers.
3. **Match everything.** All white or all ivory. Clean and formal, best when you want the chairs and flowers to carry the color.

### The plate rule

A white napkin on a white plate disappears. If the plate is white, make the napkin a different tone or fabric, such as ivory, gray or any color. See [placemats, chargers and tablecloths](/blog/placemats-chargers-vs-tablecloths) for how the plate stack changes the picture.

## Fabric affects color

The same color looks different in different fabrics. Satin napkins reflect light and read brighter. Matte polyester and cotton-feel napkins look deeper. Velvet looks near black in shadow. If you can, look at a swatch in the lighting your venue has, not only in daylight. For a deeper look, our guide to [linen fabrics compared](/blog/linen-fabrics-compared) covers how each material reads in photos.

Seasons also matter. Chicago fall events often use rust, plum and forest tones; summer garden events lean sage and blush. See [linen colors by season](/blog/linen-colors-by-season) for palettes.

## Match napkins to the chair

A napkin sits near the chair back in a lot of photos. A warm natural [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) looks good with olive or terracotta. Gold [infinity chairs](/chairs/infinity) suit ivory, black or deep jewel tones. Clear [ghost chairs](/chairs/ghost) work with nearly anything. ## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing from a screen.** Monitors lie. Ask for a swatch or photo of the exact napkin.
- **Mixing fabrics on one table** without meaning to, such as satin napkins on a matte cloth.
- **Forgetting the staff.** Servers and bartenders may need black or dark napkins and bar towels; they are separate from guest napkins.
- **Ordering the exact count.** Spills and dropped napkins mean you need extra. See [how many napkins per guest](/blog/how-many-napkins-per-guest).

## Napkin fabric choices

Napkins are not all the same material, and the fabric changes how the napkin behaves in the hand and on the plate. Polyester napkins are the most common rental item: they wash well, hold color and come in dozens of shades, but they can feel slick, and they absorb less than cotton. Cotton and cotton-blend napkins feel softer and wipe better, which guests notice at a dinner with sauces. Linen napkins look natural and relaxed, with a visible texture, and they wrinkle on purpose. Satin napkins look glam, but they are slippery in a lap and show water spots.

A good rule is to choose napkins for the hand, not only for the eye. Ask what the napkin is made of and whether it feels absorbent. A napkin that looks great but does not work at the table leaves guests wiping their hands on their pants.

## Coordinating napkins with the rest of the setting

Napkins appear in a lot of close-up photos, so color choices matter more than their size suggests. If your tablecloth is ivory and your plates are white, try a napkin in your accent color. If your tablecloth is a color and your plates are white, make the napkin white or ivory so the plate has a clear frame. If you are using a patterned tablecloth, keep napkins solid.

At a Chicago fall wedding with rust and green flowers, an ivory cloth with deep green napkins reads as intentional and keeps the flowers as the focus. For a summer dinner on a lawn, white cloths with sage or blush napkins feel fresh. For black-tie winter events, black or navy napkins on ivory cloths give a formal contrast, but check that dark napkins do not shed lint onto formal clothing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size are cloth dinner napkins?

Most are 20 x 20 inches. That size covers a lap and holds a fold. Some luncheon sets use 17 or 18 inches, and fine dining sometimes uses 22 inches or more.

### Should napkins match the tablecloth?

They can, but contrast makes the place setting look more designed. A napkin one shade deeper than the cloth is the easiest safe pick, and a bolder color works if it matches your flowers.

### What color napkins go on a white plate?

Nearly anything except pure white. Ivory, gray, sage, navy, burgundy and blush all separate from white china and give the fold shape.

### Are 17-inch napkins too small for a wedding?

For a plated dinner they often look small and slip off laps. They are better for brunch, tea or dessert service. For a wedding dinner, choose 20-inch napkins.

### Can I mix napkin colors at one table?

Yes, if it is intentional. Alternating two colors by seat or by table can work, but it requires careful counts and labeling. For a simple look, use one color across all guest tables and change only the head table.

### How do I know the real napkin color?

Ask your rental provider for a photo of the actual napkin on a table, or request a swatch. Names such as "champagne" or "sage" vary between suppliers.
