## Garnish and consumables

- **Citrus:** lemons, limes and oranges, sliced and wrapped.
- **Cocktail picks** and straws. Choose paper if your venue discourages plastic.
- **Mixers:** soda water, tonic, cola, ginger beer, juices.
- **Simple syrup, bitters** and olives if you serve cocktails.
- **Salt and sugar** for rimming glasses, if needed.
- **Sanitizer wipes** and hand soap for the bartender.

Check what the bar company brings in the contract. Our guide on [dry hire vs full service](/blog/dry-hire-vs-full-service-bar) explains why that changes your list.

## Cocktail napkins: how many?

A common rule of thumb is 3 cocktail napkins per guest for a 4-hour event, and 4 to 5 per guest if passed appetizers are served, since each plate or bite uses another. Square cocktail napkins are about 5 inches square, folded. For 100 guests, buy about 300 to 500 napkins.

## The emergency box

Keep a small box under the bar with a spare corkscrew, bottle opener, scissors, tape, extra napkins, a sharpie, a first aid kit, spare cocktail picks and a flashlight. When something goes wrong at 7 p.m. at a Chicago banquet hall, you will not have time to run to a store. It's also useful to have a printed copy of your drink menu and contact details for the venue.

## Frequently asked questions

### What tools do I need for a home bar at an event?

A shaker, jigger, strainer, bar spoon, corkscrew, bottle opener, ice scoop, knife and cutting board, bar mat and trash bin cover most drinks.

### How many cocktail napkins do I need per person?

Plan about 3 per guest for a 4-hour event. Increase to 4 or 5 per guest if appetizers are passed, because each plate uses its own napkin.

### Do bartenders bring their own tools?

Professional bartenders usually bring shakers, jiggers and strainers but may not bring bins, mats or napkins. Ask what is included.

### What size are cocktail napkins?

Most are about 5 inches square when folded, or 10 inches square when open. Dinner napkins are larger, typically 20 inches.

### Do I need a bar mat?

It is a good idea. Rubber mats catch spills and protect the bar top and bartenders' hands from slipping glassware.

### What should I put in a bar emergency box?

A spare corkscrew and opener, scissors, tape, extra napkins, a marker, first aid items and a flashlight.
