## Step 1: Measure the real capacity

Walk the rooms you will use. Count the seats you already have, the standing room and the pathways. As a rule of thumb, many planners allow about 15 to 20 square feet per guest at a mixed party, less for a standing cocktail party and more for a seated meal. If the number is lower than your guest list, plan to use a patio, garage or tent. See [garage party layout ideas](/blog/garage-party-layout-ideas) and [backyard party layout plan](/blog/backyard-party-layout-plan).

## Step 2: Plan five zones

1. **Arrival:** space for coats, shoes and a welcome. Add a coat rack or a bed.
2. **Food:** a table in a spot with room on all sides. Keep it away from the front door.
3. **Drinks:** a separate station so lines do not cross.
4. **Seating:** clusters of four to six seats, so conversation forms naturally.
5. **Quiet corner:** a calm spot for older relatives, babies or anyone who needs a break.

## Step 3: Rent or borrow what you lack

Most homes lack enough chairs, tables, plates and glasses. Count them for the guest number, then rent the gap. Our guides on [dinnerware for a dinner party at home](/blog/dinnerware-for-dinner-party-at-home), [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest) give counts. A dinner for 12 to 24 is covered in [dinner party rentals](/blog/dinner-party-for-12-to-24-rentals).

## Step 4: Protect the house

Put mats at entries, move fragile items, cover or move rugs that slip, and close off rooms you don't want used. Use runners or mats on high-traffic paths. Set out coasters. Lock away valuables, and put pet supplies somewhere guests won't trip. For pets, see [dog-friendly event rentals](/blog/dog-friendly-event-rentals).

## Step 5: Food safety

Keep cold foods cold and hot foods hot, and don't leave perishables out for more than two hours (or one hour on a hot day). Use shallow trays, ice and warming trays. See [food safety at home parties](/blog/food-safety-at-home-parties).

## Step 6: Bathrooms, parking and neighbors

Stock bathrooms with extra toilet paper, soap, hand towels and a small trash can. A sign helps with directions. For parking, tell guests where to park and which spots to avoid, and talk to neighbors in advance. If your crowd is large, see [how many portable restrooms per guests](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests).

## Step 7: Music, lighting and temperature

Set a low-key playlist, adjust lights to be warm rather than bright, and check temperature, because a crowded house warms fast. Open windows or use fans and plan to adjust.

## Step 8: Cleanup plan

Place bins in obvious spots, a recycling bin near drinks, and a dish station. Ask a few friends to help at the end. See [event cleanup plan](/blog/event-cleanup-plan).

## Chicago-area notes

Many Chicago-area homes are two-flats, greystones, bungalows and condos where space is tight and street parking is competitive. Tell guests where to park, and check for permit parking or street cleaning. Condo and apartment buildings may have rules for elevators, noise and common areas, so ask your board or manager. In winter you need a coat plan and a clear, salted path. In summer, a yard or deck helps, but have a plan for storms. ## Frequently asked questions

### How do I prepare my house for a party?

Measure your space, plan five zones, rent what you lack, protect floors and furniture and set up bathrooms and trash. Prepare cleanup in advance and recruit a few helpers.

### How many people can I fit in my house for a party?

A common rule is about 15 to 20 square feet per guest at a mixed party, less for standing and more for a seated dinner. Walk your space and count real seating and walking room.

### What do I need for a house party?

Seating, tables for food and drinks, plates, cups, napkins, trash bins, coat space and a bathroom plan. Add music, lighting and cleanup supplies. Rent the items you don't own.

### How do I handle coats and shoes at a party?

Use a coat rack or a spare bed, and a shoe mat or tray if you ask guests to remove shoes. Tell guests in the invitation. In winter, plan more space.

### Where should I put the food at a party?

Put it in a spot with room on all sides and away from the front door. Place drinks in a separate station. This spreads guests out.

### How do I clean up after a house party?

Set bins out ahead, run a dishwasher, and stage a dish and glass station. Ask helpers to take on tasks. Do the quick resets that night and leave the rest for morning.

### Do I need to tell my neighbors about a party?

It is a courtesy. Tell them the date, time and parking plan, and ask them to call if there is a problem. Keep music at a reasonable level.
