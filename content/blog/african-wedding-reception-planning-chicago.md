There is no single African wedding. A Yoruba wedding in Lagos, an Igbo traditional marriage, a Ghanaian engagement and an Ethiopian or Eritrean celebration share some themes: family involvement, large guest lists, beautiful clothing, music and generous food. They also differ in ritual, language and structure. The planning pain is real: a guest list that can reach several hundred, two or three events, relatives flying in, and an MC and DJ who need to know exactly what to do. This guide gives a framework and leaves the specifics to your families.

## Start by naming the traditions

Ask both families what ceremonies are expected. A Nigerian wedding may include an introduction (introduction ceremony), a traditional engagement with family negotiations, a church or civil ceremony, and a reception. A Ghanaian wedding may include a knocking ceremony (kokooko) and an engagement, a church wedding and a reception. In Kenya, Cameroon, Sierra Leone, Senegal and elsewhere, the structures differ again. Some couples combine events into one weekend or one day. Some hold the traditional ceremony in the home country and a reception in Chicago.

Dress is part of the celebration. Many families choose fabrics for the wedding party and relatives (aso ebi in Yoruba tradition, and similar customs elsewhere) and may ask guests to buy it. Give guests notice. The color and style can shape your decor, linens and florals. See also the Chicago-specific [Nigerian wedding traditional engagement seating](/blog/nigerian-wedding-traditional-engagement-seating) and [Ethiopian and Eritrean wedding seating](/blog/ethiopian-eritrean-wedding-seating) guides for tradition-specific layouts.

## The program and the MC

The MC is the center of most African wedding receptions. They announce the entrance of the families and wedding party, introduce the couple, manage prayers, speeches and the cutting of the cake, and keep the energy high. Choose someone with experience, give them a written program and pronunciations for names, and agree in advance on timing. Some receptions include a spraying or "dollar dance" tradition, where guests dance and place money on the couple; if this is part of your culture, plan a way to collect it and keep it safe, and check with the venue about rules. Prayers or blessings from elders, a cake ceremony and special dances often have specific places in the program.

## Food

Menus may include jollof rice, fried rice, egusi or other soups with pounded yam, fufu, grilled meats, plantains, moin moin, suya, stews, kelewele, injera with wat and a wide variety of regional dishes. Many couples hire a caterer who specializes in their cuisine. Ask about service style: buffets for 300 need several lines and a careful flow, and see [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed). Plan drinks: soft drinks, water, malt drinks and other beverages your families like. Small chops (appetizers) during cocktail hour are popular. Provide vegetarian and allergy-friendly options, and consider halal if needed.

## Seating and the guest experience

Seat elders and honored guests near the front, with easy access to the restroom and good sightlines. Seat friends together, and divide families fairly. Provide a clear sign for each table. If aso ebi is worn, group by color or family for photos. A coat check is needed in winter. For layout ideas, see [wedding seating chart how-to](/blog/wedding-seating-chart-how-to).

## Common pitfalls

Underestimating the headcount, since invitations often reach beyond the listed guest, and extended family may bring guests. Planning a program that runs too long, so keep speeches short. Ignoring the sound system, because a large crowd needs a strong one. Forgetting a plan for gifts and money. Under-planning transportation for out-of-town guests. A wedding coordinator who understands the culture can help, and a family elder can handle sensitive questions about seating and protocol.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago and its suburbs are home to large African and African immigrant communities, with banquet halls, hotels and event centers used to hosting big, lively receptions. Weekend dates in summer and early fall book early, so reserve at least a year ahead. Ask venues about noise limits, end times, outside catering and rules about money spraying and decorations. Parking and shuttle needs are significant with 300 or more guests. In winter, plan for coats and snow. For tents and outdoor spaces, see the [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and check with your village or park district.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an African wedding?

There is no single African wedding. Weddings in Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Ethiopia and across the continent each have their own traditions. Many combine a traditional ceremony with a church or civil wedding and a reception.

### How many guests are typical at an African wedding?

Large guest lists are common, often 200 to 500 or more, because extended family and community are invited. Plan the venue and food around your real headcount.

### What is aso ebi?

Aso ebi is a Yoruba custom in which family and friends wear matching fabric to a wedding. Similar customs exist in other cultures. The couple or families choose the fabric and guests buy it.

### Do I need an MC for an African wedding reception?

Most receptions have an MC who runs the program, announces entrances and keeps the energy high. Choose someone experienced and share a written program.

### What food is served at an African wedding reception?

Menus often include jollof rice, soups and stews with pounded yam or fufu, grilled meats, plantains and small chops. Regional dishes differ. Hire a caterer who knows the cuisine.

### Can I combine the traditional and white wedding?

Yes. Some couples hold both on one weekend or the same day. Others hold them separately. Plan the schedule carefully and allow time for outfit changes.

### How far ahead should I book a venue in Chicago?

At least 12 months for summer and fall weekends, and earlier for large halls. Ask about capacity, outside catering and end times.

### What rentals does an African wedding reception need?

Round tables and chairs, linens, a head table, a dance floor, a buffet and a gift table. Add a tent for outdoor events. Count by the real guest list.
