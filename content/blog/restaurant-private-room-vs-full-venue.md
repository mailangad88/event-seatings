**Quick answer**

A restaurant private room is easier and often cheaper for small groups, with built-in food, staff and ambiance, but it limits menus, decor, music and timing. A full venue gives more space and control, but you handle more vendors and rentals. Choose the private room for dinners under about 40, and a full venue for larger or highly customized events.

</div>

Hosts often start with a favorite restaurant, then discover the room is too small, the menu is fixed or the minimum spend is higher than expected. Others choose a blank venue and find they are managing caterers, bartenders, rentals and cleanup. Understanding the trade-offs saves time and money.

## What you get with each

A private room usually comes with a menu, servers, a bar and a ready-made room. You pay mostly through a food and beverage minimum, with service charges and tax on top. A full venue, including a restaurant buyout, gives you the whole space, often with more flexibility on layout, music and hours, but at a higher cost and with more planning; see [restaurant buyout chair rental in Chicago](/blog/restaurant-buyout-chair-rental-chicago).

## Side by side

Details vary by restaurant and venue. Use the table to guide your questions.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Factor | Restaurant private room | Full venue or buyout |
|---|---|---|
| Group size | Small to medium, often under 40 | Medium to large |
| Cost structure | Minimum spend plus charges | Rental fee plus vendors |
| Food | Restaurant menu | Your caterer or theirs |
| Decor | Limited | Often flexible |
| Music and AV | Limited | More options |
| Timing | Within restaurant hours | More flexible |
| Rentals | Usually minimal | Often needed |
| Host effort | Low | Medium to high |

Always ask for the total with taxes, service charges and gratuity. Compare totals, not minimums.

## Making a restaurant event run smoothly

Confirm the menu, headcount deadline and dietary needs well ahead; see [final headcount deadline](/blog/final-headcount-deadline-event-rentals). Plan the schedule so that dinner service and speeches do not collide, and give the restaurant a run of show; see [run of show template](/downloads/run-of-show-template).

For rehearsal dinners, which often use restaurants, see [rehearsal dinner rentals](/blog/rehearsal-dinner-rentals). For celebrations that outgrow the room, consider adding a tent or patio setup.

## When to choose each

Choose a private room when your group is small, you want food handled by one team and you do not need much decor. Choose a full venue or buyout when you want dancing, a custom layout, longer hours or a larger crowd. If you are somewhere between, ask the restaurant about a semi-private area or a patio, and compare that against a hall with an outside caterer. Total cost and effort should decide, not only the room's charm.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago restaurants vary from tiny neighborhood spots to large downtown spaces, and private rooms may be booked months ahead on weekends and in December. Many restaurants close for private events with limited notice, and some city locations have tight loading and parking; see [chicago event delivery parking and loading zones](/blog/chicago-event-delivery-parking-loading-zones). Patio and rooftop options depend on weather and city rules; see [Chicago rooftop event rentals](/blog/chicago-rooftop-event-rentals). Ask your restaurant about licensing, outside cake and corkage, and check with your village or the city if you plan to extend onto a sidewalk or patio.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a restaurant private room cheaper than a venue?

For small groups it often is, since food, staff and the room are bundled. For larger groups, the minimum can rise quickly, so compare totals.

### What is a food and beverage minimum?

It is the amount you agree to spend on food and drink. If you spend less, you may owe the difference. Ask how tax and service are counted.

### Can I bring my own decor?

Some restaurants allow light decor, and many restrict candles, confetti and wall attachments. Ask for the rules.

### How many guests fit in a private room?

Ask for the seated capacity for your style. Standing receptions fit more than seated dinners.

### Can I bring a cake?

Many restaurants allow outside cakes with a cutting fee, and some do not. Ask in advance.

### When should I book?

For weekends and December, many hosts book a few months ahead. Smaller midweek events may be easier.

### What is a restaurant buyout?

It means renting the whole restaurant for your event, usually with a minimum spend. It offers more space and privacy, at a higher cost.
