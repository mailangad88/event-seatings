**Quick answer**

Plated service needs the most staff and plates but the least table space: just flatware, glassware and a charger. Buffets need chafing dishes, serving tables and long walkways but fewer servers. Family style needs large platters on every table, so tables must be 60 inches round or bigger, and you rent 2 to 3 platters per table. Pick the style first, because it decides tables, chairs and layout.

</div>

Couples ask whether to serve plated, buffet or family style and compare menus. The question that matters more for a rental list is what each one needs on the floor. A style that looks cheap on the menu can be expensive in tables, space and labor. Here is what each style actually requires.

Those staffing figures are common rules of thumb, not requirements. Confirm with your caterer.

## Plated: what to rent

- A **charger** (service plate) for each guest, plus dinner, salad and bread plates
- **Full flatware** for each course, and napkins
- **Water goblets and wine glasses**, plus champagne flutes for toasts
- A **serving cart or tray stand** in back of house, and a plate warmer for hot plates
- Round tables of 60 or 72 inches, and enough space between chairs for servers (about 60 inches between table edges)

Plated is the most elegant and uses the most dinnerware. For a 150-guest wedding, plan the number of plates by multiplying guests by courses. See our [dinnerware guides](/blog/topic/dinnerware).

## Buffet: what to rent

- **Chafing dishes** for every hot item, see [how many chafing dishes you need](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet)
- **Draped serving tables** and risers for height
- **Serving utensils**, see the [serving utensils checklist](/blog/serving-utensils-checklist-for-catering)
- **Dinner plates, napkins and flatware**, stacked at the line
- Sneeze guards, if your venue or health rules call for them

A buffet saves on wait staff but spends on tables and floor area. The layout matters, see [buffet line flow](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

## Family style: what to rent

- **2 to 3 platters or bowls per table**, 12 to 18 inches across
- **Serving spoons and forks** at each table
- **Bigger tables**: a 60-inch round with 8 guests has little room for platters, so use 72-inch rounds or long tables seating 10 to 12
- **Trivets** for hot dishes and a **sideboard** to stage backup platters
- A **runner** or two per section to refill

Family style leaves less room for centerpieces. Keep florals low, under 10 to 12 inches, or hang them overhead. See [round table sizes](/blog/topic/tables) for exact numbers.

## Chairs and seating by style

Plated and family-style dinners keep guests seated for 60 to 90 minutes, so comfort matters. A padded seat or an ergonomic design like the [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) is worth thinking about. For a buffet, guests come and go, and you can run a seat for about every guest with extra seats for standing areas. See [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Chicago notes

Winter weddings in banquet halls usually default to plated, since the room is set and heat is easy. Backyard parties and tents in the suburbs lean buffet or family style because the kitchen is a prep tent. Venues may require their own catering or a licensed caterer, and some do not allow open flame or outside staff, so check before you choose a style.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is cheaper, plated, buffet or family style?

It depends on the caterer, so ask for all three quotes. Plated needs more staff, buffet needs more equipment and food waste, and family style needs more food and platters. We do not publish catering prices.

### How much space does a buffet need compared with plated?

A buffet adds 12 to 20 feet of table plus 36 to 48 inches of clear walkway. Plated needs only the dining area and a service path of about 60 inches between tables.

### What size table is best for family-style dinner?

A 72-inch round seating 8 to 10, or a long farm table seating 10 to 12. A 60-inch round runs out of room for platters.

### Do family-style dinners need extra plates?

Usually one dinner plate per guest and an extra bread or salad plate, plus platters and bowls at the table. Ask your caterer if they bring their own platters.

### Can I mix styles?

Yes. Many weddings serve a plated first course, then stations or a buffet for dessert. Equipment needs add together, so list both.

### Which style do guests prefer?

Preferences vary. Plated feels formal and quiet, buffets are flexible, and family style is warm and social. Match the style to the tone of your event.
