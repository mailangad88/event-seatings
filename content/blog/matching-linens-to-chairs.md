**Quick answer**

Match linens to chairs by contrast and tone, not by exact color. Warm wood chairs like cross-back go with ivory, olive and terracotta. Clear ghost chairs go with almost any linen. Gold chairs go with ivory, black, navy and jewel tones. Velvet chairs want matte, quiet cloths. Avoid linens that nearly match the chair color, since the chair disappears into the table in photos.

</div>

Chairs and linens are two of the largest visible surfaces at a reception, and they sit in the same photo frame. A pairing that clashes can make an expensive room look random, while the right match makes a simple one look planned. Here is a clear method for choosing, with pairings for the chairs most Chicago couples consider.

## The three rules

1. **Contrast the chair and the cloth.** A tan chair on a tan cloth blends away. A wood chair on ivory pops.
2. **Match temperature.** Warm chairs (wood, gold, brass) pair with warm linens (ivory, champagne, rust). Cool chairs (silver, clear, white) pair with cool linens (white, gray, navy).
3. **One statement per table.** If the chair is ornate, keep the linen simple. If the linen is patterned or sparkly, choose a quiet chair.

### Cross-back (wood)
Warm and rustic. It sits well with ivory, cream, olive, terracotta, rust and navy. A white cloth works but looks crisp, and a tan or beige cloth loses the chair. See the [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back).

### Ghost (clear)
Neutral. Because the chair nearly disappears, the linen becomes the star. Patterns, sequins and bold colors all work. See the [ghost chair](/chairs/ghost) and [styling ghost chairs](/blog/ghost-chairs-wedding-styling).

### Gold infinity
Glam. Ivory, black, navy, emerald and burgundy let the gold stand out. Avoid yellow and mustard, which flatten it. See the [infinity chair](/chairs/infinity).

### Velvet dining
Rich and soft. Use matte, quiet cloths such as lamour or cotton in ivory, gray or black so the velvet is the highlight. See the [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining).

### Rattan or garden
Natural and relaxed. Linen or burlap and soft earthy colors suit it, along with white. See the [rattan garden chair](/chairs/rattan-garden).

## Do it in four steps

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Pick the chair first</strong> because it is the harder item to change.</li>
<li><strong>Choose a linen base</strong> that contrasts it in value (light or dark).</li>
<li><strong>Add an accent</strong> in napkins that links to your flowers.</li>
<li><strong>Look at both together</strong> in a photo or render before you commit.</li>
</ol>

## Photos and lighting

Chairs show most in ceremony photos, and linens show most at dinner. If your ceremony and reception are in the same room, you may need to plan the pairing for both. Remember that evening light shifts colors toward warm, so a wood chair and ivory cloth glow, while cool whites turn gray. Read [tablecloth colors for photos](/blog/tablecloth-colors-for-photos) for more.

## When the venue provides the linens

Many Chicago venues offer a limited palette of house linens. In that case, choose the chair to match the linens, not the other way around. Our [chair guide](/chairs) shows the range, and [Chiavari chair alternatives](/blog/chiavari-chair-alternatives) lists other options.

## Common mismatches and quick fixes

Most pairing problems fall into a few patterns, and most are cheap to fix by changing napkins or one layer of linen.

- **Beige chair, beige cloth.** The chair vanishes. Switch to ivory, white or a deeper color, or add a contrasting runner.
- **Gold chair, yellow napkin.** Both flatten. Try ivory, black or a jewel tone.
- **Busy chair, busy linen.** A carved or patterned chair with a lace or damask cloth looks fussy. Pick one to be plain.
- **Dark wood chair, dark cloth.** In dim rooms, both sink. Use a lighter napkin and candles to bring the table up.
- **Shiny chair, shiny cloth.** Two glossy surfaces fight for light. Make the cloth matte.

## Think about the whole room

A reception has more than one view of the chair-and-linen pairing: the table seen from the entrance, the head table, and close-ups of place settings. Walk through those views on your floor plan. If the head table is the focus, it can carry a special pairing, such as a velvet runner and the same chair as the rest of the room. If you are using more than one chair style, for example a different chair at the head table, keep the linen consistent so the change reads as a feature, not a mistake.

Finally, think about the aisle. At a ceremony, the chairs are rented without linens, so the pairing is between chair, floor and flowers instead. You can still use a runner color to echo the later table linens, tying the ceremony and reception together.

## Frequently asked questions

### What color tablecloth goes with wood cross-back chairs?

Ivory, white, navy or olive. These contrast with the wood and keep the chair visible. Avoid beige or tan, which blend with it.

### What color linens go with gold chairs?

Ivory, black, navy, emerald and burgundy. They contrast the gold and keep it from looking brassy. Avoid yellow and mustard.

### Do clear chairs go with any tablecloth?

Yes. Because they are transparent, the cloth shows through, so patterns and colors both work. Choose a cloth you love, and keep the napkins simple.

### Should linens match the chair color exactly?

No. An exact match removes the chair from the photo. A contrast in value or a coordinating tone looks more designed.

### Can I use patterned linens with decorative chairs?

Use caution. One strong pattern per table is enough. If your chair is ornate, choose a plain cloth.

### What if my chair and venue clash?

Change the linen first, since it is cheaper and easier to swap. Neutral cloths in ivory or white can bridge most clashes.
