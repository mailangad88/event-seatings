**Quick answer**

Lay out an open house as a one-way loop: entrance and gift table, then photo display, then food line, then drinks, then seating, with an easy exit. Put the food line at least 10 feet from the door so a queue doesn't block arrivals, keep seating for about half your guests, and leave 36 inches of aisle between tables. Spread the stations so no more than 8 to 10 people ever wait in one place.

</div>

An open house is a different animal from a seated dinner. People trickle in over several hours, say hello, eat, and move on. If every station sits next to the front door, you get a traffic jam during the first hour and an empty yard in the third. A good layout moves people through the space on purpose.

## Think in a loop, not a room

Guests behave like water. They follow the easiest path and stop where there is something to do. Use that.

## Where each station goes

### Entrance: gifts and guest book
Put a table at the entrance with a card box, a guest book or a signing board, and a sign that says where to go next. Use a cloth that reaches the floor so boxes can be tucked under. Check the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) so a 6-foot table gets a 90 x 132-inch cloth.

### Photo display
Photo boards, the cap and gown, a yearbook and a slideshow slow people down, so give them room. Place the display after the gift table, with 6 to 8 feet of clear space in front. If you have a slideshow on a TV or projector, shade it from afternoon sun.

### Food line
Food is the anchor. Set up the buffet so guests can approach from both sides if the table is 8 feet or longer. Read our [buffet line flow](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) guide for how long a line takes to move. As a rough rule, one serving line handles 25 to 30 guests comfortably, so split into two lines for 60 or more.

### Drinks
Keep drinks away from the food line. A separate beverage table in a different corner pulls people across the yard and shortens the food queue. Ice and water go with the drinks. For counts, see [how many beverage dispensers you need](/blog/how-many-beverage-dispensers-do-i-need).

### Seating
Put seating where people end up after plates are full, usually close to the food but out of the main path. A mix works best: round tables of 8 for families, a few small cocktail-height tables for people who won't stay, and lawn chairs along the edge for grandparents who want shade. [Garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) are estimated at $5 to $7 each.

## Spacing numbers

<div class="table-wrap">

| Area | Minimum | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Aisle between seated tables | 36 in | 48 in |
| Clear space in front of a buffet | 4 ft | 6 ft |
| Queue space before the buffet | 6 ft | 10 ft |
| Space around a photo display | 4 ft | 8 ft |
| Square feet per seated guest (tent) | 10 | 12 |

</div>

These are the common planning figures for event layouts. Your yard may not fit all of them, so use the minimums for the busiest stations only.

Plan the cake or a short toast about midway through, when most guests are still there. Make an announcement or ring a bell, not a long speech, so people who are leaving can slip out.

## Chicago-area layout notes

Many Chicago and suburban yards are narrow, with a garage or alley on one side. Put the entrance at the gate guests will actually use, not the one you prefer, and place the loop so it ends at the same gate or at a side path. If a delivery truck needs the driveway, schedule setup early so the truck is gone before guests arrive. For a tent, check [Chicago tent permit rules](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) and your village's own rules, since requirements vary.

## Frequently asked questions

### How should I arrange an open house graduation party?

Arrange it as a loop: gifts at the entrance, photos next, then food, drinks, seating and an exit. Separate the food and drink stations so lines stay short. Keep seating out of the main walkway.

### How much space do I need for 50 guests?

About 600 to 800 square feet for the combined seating, food and display zones, if only half the guests sit at once. A 20 x 30-foot tent covers seating for roughly 40 people at tables. Open lawn around it handles the rest.

### Where should the food go at an open house?

Put it near the center of the space and at least 10 feet from the door. That keeps arriving guests from clogging the entrance and gives the queue room to form.

### Should I have assigned seating at an open house?

No. Open houses are drop-in events and assigned seats slow things down. Reserve one table near the graduate for grandparents or older relatives so they have a comfortable spot.

### How many tables do I need for the food?

One 8-foot table per 25 to 30 guests, plus a separate table for desserts and one for drinks. Two sided access doubles the speed of a buffet line.

### How do I keep the graduate from being stuck at one spot?

Set a photo display in a place that is separate from the seating, and have the graduate move between it, the food and the tables. A photo area also gives guests a natural reason to wander.

### What if it rains during the open house?

Decide your backup before the day. Either put a tent over food and a core seating area, or move food indoors and keep the yard for overflow. Tell guests in your invitation where to look for updates.
