**Quick answer**

Leave at least 60 inches (5 feet) between the edges of round tables, which gives 36 inches of chair space plus a 24-inch walkway; use 72 inches where servers carry trays. Keep 4 to 5 feet in front of buffets, 3 to 4 feet around the dance floor and 36 inches minimum on exit paths. Local fire codes and your venue set the legal minimums, so ask them.

Crowded tables are the most common layout complaint in a banquet hall. Guests cannot pull out chairs, servers cannot pass, and wheelchairs cannot get through. Here is how much room each part of a Chicago-area event needs, with the numbers you can bring to your venue walkthrough.

These are common planning distances. Fire code and ADA rules set required widths for exits and accessible routes, so ask your venue or village what applies. Do not guess on exit routes.

## The 60-inch rule

When you pull a chair out to stand up, it uses about 18 to 24 inches. A person sitting uses another 6 to 10. So if two tables are 60 inches apart, each chair gets roughly 30 inches before blocking the aisle. A server carrying a tray needs about 24 inches. That is why 60 is the minimum and 72 is comfortable.

## How spacing changes your table count

Here is a quick way to test a room. A 60-inch round with chairs pulled out takes a 10-foot circle, and 60 inches between table edges adds a half-aisle on each side. In practice that means each round claims roughly a 12 x 12 foot square of floor once aisles are shared. A 40 x 60 foot room (2,400 square feet) therefore holds about 16 rounds before you add a dance floor, bar or buffet. Take away 600 square feet for a dance floor and bar and you are down to about 12 rounds, or 96 guests at tables of 8. A 72-inch round, which seats 10, needs about a 13 x 13 foot square, so the same room holds about 10 of them, or 100 guests. That is why large ballrooms favor 72s.

Always compare your actual room dimensions with the venue's floor plan. A pillar can change everything. For table counts see [how many tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests).

## Accessible paths

Plan at least one accessible route of 36 inches (more where wheelchairs turn) from entrance to restrooms, the head table and a dance floor edge. Place one or two tables near the path with a removable chair so a wheelchair user can join without blocking a walkway. The exact rules vary by building, so ask your venue and check ADA guidance.

## Special zones

- **Bar:** keep tables 8 feet from the bar so the queue forms away from seating.
- **Buffet:** 5 feet at the front; see [buffet layouts](/blog/buffet-table-layouts).
- **Dance floor:** a ring of 4 feet before the nearest chairs.
- **DJ or band:** see [DJ table setup](/blog/dj-and-band-table-setup).
- **Photo area:** 6 to 8 feet of free floor.

## Chicago specifics

Downtown and loft venues often have columns and radiators that eat floor space. Tent events add tent poles. Winter events add coats and boots. If your event is during a Chicago winter, add 10 percent more aisle space near the door for coat traffic. That is a rule of thumb, not a requirement.

## A walkthrough checklist

Bring a tape measure and a floor plan to your venue visit and check these before you finalize the layout.

- Measure from the wall to the first table, 36 inches minimum behind chairs.
- Measure distance from a table to the edge of the dance floor.
- Walk the server path from the kitchen door to the farthest table.
- Check that exit doors open fully and are not blocked by tables.
- Look for columns, radiators or low ceiling lights that change the layout.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far apart should round tables be?

At least 60 inches between table edges. Use 72 inches if servers carry trays or if guests use wheelchairs.

### How much space between banquet table rows?

60 to 72 inches between table edges, so guests can sit and others can pass.

### How much space do I need around a dance floor?

3 to 4 feet between the dance floor edge and the nearest chair.

### How wide must an exit aisle be?

It depends on the building and local fire code. 36 inches is a common planning minimum, but check with your venue or village.

### What spacing do I need for wheelchairs?

At least 36 inches along the route and more where a chair must turn. Ask the venue about ADA requirements.

### Can I squeeze tables closer to fit more guests?

Not safely. If you need more room, use 72-inch rounds, which seat 10, or remove a table.
