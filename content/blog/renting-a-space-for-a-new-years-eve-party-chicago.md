**Quick answer**

Book a New Year's Eve space as early as you can, since the best rooms go months ahead. Ask for the minimum spend, the hours and what is included, then list what the room lacks, such as chairs, tables, coat racks and a dance floor. Plan your rental delivery and pickup around the holiday, because pickup falls on January 1 and some companies charge extra or move it to the next business day.

New Year's Eve in Chicago is cold, busy and loud in the best way. If your home is too small, a rented room gives you space, a bar and a place to stay inside while it is 10 degrees out. It also adds a new set of questions: how much it costs, what is in the room and what you have to bring. This guide covers the booking side of renting a loft, a restaurant room or a hall. If you are hosting at home, see [New Year's Eve at home](/blog/new-years-eve-party-at-home). For lofts, see [Chicago loft party rentals](/blog/chicago-loft-party-rentals).

## Book early, then ask about the money

Many New Year's Eve rooms book up months ahead, since the date is the same for everyone and the room only has one night. Start looking in the fall. Don't assume a room will wait, and ask about a deposit and cancellation terms right away.

**Minimum spend.** Many restaurant rooms and some bars require a minimum amount on food and drinks, and some halls and lofts have package rules. Ask what it covers, whether tax, service and tip count, and what happens if you spend less. We won't guess at numbers, because they change by place and by year.

**Hours.** Ask when you can get in, when the music must stop and when you must be out. A midnight party with a 1 am end time leaves little time for pickup the same night.

**Cancellation.** Ask what happens if a guest list shrinks or a snowstorm hits. See [the date-change policy guide](/blog/event-rental-cancellation-and-date-change-policy).

## What the room lacks

Walk the room before you sign, or ask for a list. Most rooms are missing more than you think. Check:

- **Seating.** Is there enough for everyone, or do you want a mix of seats and standing space? See [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [chairs](/rentals/chairs).
- **Tables.** Cocktail tables, a buffet, a gift table, a cake or dessert table. See [tables](/rentals/tables).
- **Linens.** Many venues use their own or none. See [linens](/rentals/linens).
- **Coat space.** In Chicago winter, coats pile up. See [coat racks for winter events](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago).
- **Dance floor.** A hard floor is fine, but a rented floor helps at a bigger party. See [dance floor size by guest count](/blog/dance-floor-size-by-guest-count).
- **Lighting and power.** Uplights, string lights and extension cords. See [lighting](/rentals/lighting).
- **Glassware and a bar setup.** Toast glasses for midnight. See [glassware](/rentals/glassware).
- **Heat.** An outdoor smoking or photo area needs a heater, if the building allows it.

If the venue has an in-house rental list, compare it with outside options. [How to compare event rental quotes](/blog/how-to-compare-event-rental-quotes) helps you do it fairly. Ask whether the venue charges a fee for outside vendors, and whether they need paperwork.

## Delivery and pickup on a holiday

This is where New Year's Eve differs from other nights.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Delivery:</strong> Often December 30 or the morning of the 31st, when the room is free. Ask the venue when you can get in.</li>
<li><strong>Setup:</strong> Chairs, tables and linens in place before the caterer or DJ arrives.</li>
<li><strong>The party:</strong> Keep a person on site for the rental company's phone calls.</li>
<li><strong>Breakdown:</strong> Many companies don't collect at 2 am. Ask for a window on January 1 or the next business day.</li>
<li><strong>Pickup:</strong> January 1 is a holiday, so rental companies may be closed, charge a holiday or late fee, or move pickup to January 2 or later.</li>
</ol>

Read [late-night and early pickup fees](/blog/late-night-and-early-pickup-fees-event-rentals) and [setup and breakdown fees](/blog/setup-and-breakdown-fees-event-rentals) for how these are charged. Check the venue too: some rooms need to be empty by a certain time the next morning, and another event may be coming in.

## What most hosts find out too late

- **January 1 pickup is the real cost.** The rental itself is the same, but a holiday pickup can mean a fee, or a wait for the next business day. Decide whether the items can stay in the room, who is responsible for them overnight, and whether the venue allows it.
- **Loading in a high-rise or loft takes time.** Freight elevators have set hours and must be reserved. Chicago holiday traffic and snow slow trucks. Ask for a delivery window earlier in the day.
- **Rooms look bigger empty.** A loft that holds 100 standing may hold 60 with tables. Ask the venue for the seated and standing counts, and use [the seating chart planner](/downloads/seating-chart-planner).

## Midnight details

Plan the midnight toast as a task. Glasses should be in hands by 11:45, with sparkling drinks poured or on trays. Confetti and streamers can leave a mess that some venues charge for, so ask first. And plan the exit: coats, rides and a spot to wait out of the cold. [Holiday party planning kit](/downloads/holiday-party-planning-kit) has lists that fit a December party too.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far ahead should I book a venue for New Year's Eve in Chicago?

As early as you can. Many rooms book months ahead, so start touring in the fall. Ask about the deposit and cancellation terms before you sign.

### What is a minimum spend at a venue?

It is the least amount you must spend on food and drinks for the room. Ask what counts toward it, such as tax and service, and what you owe if you fall short.

### What rentals do I need for a loft party?

Usually chairs, tables, linens, glassware, lighting and coat racks, and sometimes a dance floor and heaters. Ask the venue what it has, then rent the rest.

### Will rental companies pick up on January 1?

Some do and some don't. Many are closed on the holiday, or charge a fee, or move pickup to the next business day. Ask when you order and settle where the items will stay.

### Can I use outside rentals at a restaurant private room?

Sometimes. Many restaurants supply their own tables and chairs, and some limit outside vendors. Ask what is allowed and whether there is a fee.

### How many people fit in a loft?

It depends on the floor plan. The number for standing is higher than for seated dinner, and the bar, a DJ and a dance floor take up space. Ask the venue for both counts.
