**Quick answer**

Give your rental company a final headcount by the deadline in your contract, which is often one to two weeks before the event. Until then, plan with a range, order a modest cushion for late RSVPs, and know whether you can increase, decrease or both after the lock. Ask for the deadline in writing when you book.

</div>

RSVPs trickle in. A cousin adds a plus one. A client confirms fifteen more. Your rental company cannot hold a moving target forever, because the truck is loaded, linens are laundered and place settings are counted for a specific date. The final headcount deadline is the point where your flexibility ends and the order becomes firm.

## Why the deadline exists

- **Inventory.** The company reserves exactly what you need and may turn away other customers.
- **Laundry and prep.** Linens, dishes and glassware need cleaning and packing for a specific count.
- **Labor.** Crews and trucks are scheduled based on quantity.
- **Peak season.** In busy periods, last-minute changes are hard to absorb. See [peak season in Chicago](/blog/peak-season-event-rentals-chicago).

## Increase vs decrease

Policies differ in what they allow after the lock:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Change | Typical treatment | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Increase before the lock | Allowed if inventory is available | Is the price the same? |
| Increase after the lock | Possible but not guaranteed | Is there a fee? |
| Decrease before the lock | Usually allowed | Any limit on size? |
| Decrease after the lock | Often not refunded | Is a credit offered? |

</div>

Increasing is generally easier than decreasing, but both depend on inventory and how close the date is. Read the clauses in [cancellation and date-change policies](/blog/event-rental-cancellation-and-date-change-policy).

## How to choose the number

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Start with your RSVP count</strong> of confirmed guests.</li>
<li><strong>Add the maybes</strong> using your own experience of how many usually show.</li>
<li><strong>Add vendors and staff</strong> who may need seats, such as a DJ or photographer.</li>
<li><strong>Add a modest cushion</strong> for late arrivals.</li>
<li><strong>Round up to full tables</strong> if you are using rounds.</li>
</ol>

## Padding without overpaying

A cushion protects you from awkward standing guests, but extras cost money. Some options:

- **Add a few spare chairs** rather than a whole table.
- **Ask about a "call-in" policy** for last-minute increases of a small number.
- **Use a flexible area** like a lounge or high-top zone to absorb extras.
- **Track RSVPs weekly** so your cushion shrinks as the date nears.

For quantity of other items per guest, see [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest) and [how many napkins per guest](/blog/how-many-napkins-per-guest).

## What to do when the number changes after the lock

1. **Tell the company immediately.** Early is better than day-of.
2. **Ask what is possible**: add, swap, or reduce.
3. **Ask the price** before you agree.
4. **Get the revised invoice** in writing.

If the change is an increase, ask whether the same style is available. If it is not, a different but compatible chair may be better than none. Check how [mixing chair styles](/chairs) would look.

## Chicago-area timing

Weddings and large parties in Chicago often have a headcount due to the caterer and venue at roughly the same time as the rental lock. That is no coincidence. Putting a single "decision date" in your calendar and sharing it with your caterer, venue and rental company keeps everyone on the same number. Ask each for their deadline, because they differ.

## Common mistakes

- **Locking too early** and then dealing with 20 late additions.
- **Locking too late** and missing the company's deadline.
- **Forgetting vendors** who need a chair or place setting.
- **Counting children** at the wrong rate. Decide whether kids need full seats.
- **Not tracking changes** in a single document.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the final headcount due for rentals?

It is set by your contract, often one to two weeks before the event. Ask for the date when you book and put it on your calendar.

### Can I add chairs after the final headcount?

Sometimes, if inventory is available. Ask whether a fee applies and how late you can request it.

### Can I reduce my order after the deadline?

Often not for a full refund. Some companies offer a credit or partial reduction, so ask what your policy says.

### How many extra chairs should I order?

A small cushion for late RSVPs and vendors is typical. The right number depends on your event, so use your RSVP trends rather than a fixed rule.

### Do vendors and staff need chairs?

Often yes. A DJ, photographer or band may need seats and tables. Include them in your count or confirm they bring their own.

### What if my headcount is still uncertain at the deadline?

Lock the number that you are most confident in, and ask about adding a small number later. Do not guess high on every item; choose where flexibility matters most.
