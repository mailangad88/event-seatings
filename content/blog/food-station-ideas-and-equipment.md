**Quick answer**

Plan one food station per 40 to 50 guests, and never fewer than three stations so lines stay short. Each station needs a 6 to 8-foot draped table, its own heat or ice, serving tools, a sign, and a staffed attendant for anything carved, fried or made to order. Pasta, taco, slider, carving, dessert and late-night snack stations are the easiest to run.

</div>

Stations feel generous and let guests move around the room, which is why they are popular for Chicago weddings, graduation parties and corporate receptions. They also multiply the equipment list. A buffet is one line; five stations is five tiny kitchens, each with its own table, linens, heat and trash. This guide helps you pick stations and count what each one needs.

## Ten station ideas and what each needs

**1. Pasta.** Two induction burners or a chafer for sauce, a pasta cooker or hotel pans of pre-cooked pasta, a 6-foot table, tongs and a sauce ladle. A chef or attendant tosses to order.

**2. Carving.** A heated carving board or chafer base, heat lamp, knife, fork, and a skirted table. Carving is the slowest station, so give it a second chef at 100 or more guests.

**3. Tacos.** Warming trays for meats and tortillas, cold bowls for toppings over ice, tongs and squeeze bottles. Plan 3 to 4 cold bowls per taco station.

**4. Sliders and sandwiches.** Chafers, a bread basket, and picks or skewers. Easy to run and doesn't need a chef.

**5. Mashed potato or mac and cheese bar.** Chafers for the base, small bowls for toppings and long spoons. A nostalgic favorite that suits cold weather.

**6. Raw bar or shrimp.** Needs ice, drainage trays and a way to keep seafood cold. See [keeping food cold at outdoor events](/blog/keeping-food-cold-at-outdoor-events).

**7. Dessert.** Cake stands, risers and small plates. Read [dessert table equipment](/blog/dessert-table-equipment-checklist).

**8. Coffee and tea.** An urn, cups, stirrers and a sugar caddy. See [how many coffee urns you need](/blog/how-many-coffee-urns-for-a-party).

**9. Late-night snacks.** Pizza, sliders or pretzels, often served at 10 p.m. in boxes. See [pizza oven rental](/blog/pizza-oven-rental-for-events).

**10. Popcorn, cotton candy or snow cones.** Small machines and a table; see [popcorn machine rental](/blog/popcorn-machine-rental-guide).

## The equipment every station shares

Whatever the food, each station needs:

- A draped table (a 6-foot banquet for most, 8-foot for carving)
- A table skirt or floor-length cloth to hide boxes and trash bins
- A sign naming the food and listing allergens
- A small trash bin or bus tub within reach
- Serving utensils, one per dish
- Plates, napkins and forks at that station, so guests do not carry from across the room
- Power or fuel, labeled and placed where nobody trips

## Power, fuel and open flame

Pasta and omelet stations often run on induction burners that draw power, so ask your venue which outlets are on which circuit and how many amps are available. Do not guess, because two induction burners and a coffee urn on one circuit can trip it. Many venues limit open flame, including chafing fuel, so check the venue's rules before you commit. In tents, use extension cords rated for outdoor use and tape them down; see our [lighting and power guides](/blog/topic/lighting-and-power).

## Seating for stations

A station reception still needs seats for most guests. A common guideline is a seat for about 70 to 100 percent of guests at a dinner station event, and you can trim to 60 or 70 percent for an hors d'oeuvre-style cocktail party. See [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) to count them.

## Chicago notes

In winter, stations near an outside door get drafts and cold plates. In summer, a patio station in the sun needs shade and ice. Some Chicago lofts and hotels have a rule that only licensed staff can cook on the premises, so ask your venue about outside caterers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many food stations do I need for 100 guests?

Four to five stations is a good plan, with at least three serving hot food. This keeps each line under about 20 people at peak. If your venue is small, combine the dessert and coffee stations on one table.

### Are food stations cheaper than a buffet?

Not always. Stations need more staff and more equipment, so a station menu often costs about the same as, or more than, a plated meal. Ask your caterer to price both. We do not publish catering prices.

### What equipment does a pasta station need?

Two induction burners or chafers, pots or hotel pans for pasta and sauces, tongs, a ladle, small plates or bowls and a 6-foot table. An attendant should toss pasta to order for freshness.

### Do food stations need their own tables?

Yes, one table per station, usually a 6 or 8-foot banquet table with a floor-length cloth. Hot stations need power or fuel; cold stations need ice and drainage.

### Where do I put food stations in a room?

Spread them along the walls, away from the dance floor and the bar. Leave 6 feet of clear floor in front of each one so lines do not tangle.

### Do guests still need chairs at a station reception?

Yes. Plan seats for at least 70 percent of guests at a dinner-length reception, because guests who are eating want to sit down.
