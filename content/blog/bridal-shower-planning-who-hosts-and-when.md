## Who hosts and who pays

Tradition says the maid of honor and bridesmaids host the shower, sometimes with help from the bride's mother or close relatives. In older etiquette, immediate family did not host because it looked like asking for gifts, but that rule has faded. Today the host can be anyone close to the bride: friends, aunts, coworkers or the groom's family.

Costs are often shared. A host team may split food, rentals and decor. If the group is large, set a clear budget and communicate it to everyone. Be open about what each person can contribute. The bride should not pay for her own shower.

## When to hold it

Most showers happen one to three months before the wedding. Avoid the last two weeks, when the couple is busy. If many guests travel, pick a weekend near other pre-wedding events. For a destination wedding, a shower in the couple's home city is common. Avoid the same weekend as the bachelorette party, if possible.

## Who to invite

A common rule is that anyone invited to the shower should also be invited to the wedding, with exceptions such as coworkers who are not invited to the wedding but join a work shower. Ask the bride for her list and any family sensitivities. Include the mothers and grandmothers of the couple. Co-ed showers are increasingly common, so ask the couple.

## Pick a style

**Brunch or luncheon.** The classic. Relaxed, seated and easy to host.

**Tea party.** Elegant and quiet.

**Garden party.** Works from late May through September.

**Themed shower.** Recipe cards, lingerie, bar cart or travel are popular ideas.

**Co-ed party.** A cookout or game night with both the bride and groom.

**Virtual or at-a-distance shower.** For guests who cannot attend in person.

## Timeline and tasks for the host team

<div class="table-wrap">

| When | Task | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 3 months before the wedding | Confirm hosts, date and budget | Host team and bride |
| 8 to 10 weeks out | Build the guest list and book the space | Maid of honor |
| 5 to 6 weeks out | Send invitations | Host team |
| 3 weeks out | Order food, cake and rentals | Host |
| 1 week out | Confirm headcount and games | Host team |
| Shower day | Setup, hosting, gift tracking | Bridesmaids |

</div>

Dates will shift to fit the wedding and the guests. Keep the sequence even if the dates move.

## Games, gifts and flow

A typical shower has arrival and mingling, a meal, a few light activities and gifts. Keep games optional. Popular choices include recipe cards, advice for the couple, a "how well do you know the bride" quiz, and a toast. A friend can write down gifts and givers for thank-you notes. Keep gift opening short and tell guests there is no pressure to bring a big gift. See [baby shower games and timeline](/blog/baby-shower-games-and-timeline) for flow ideas that also work for bridal showers.

## Registry and gift etiquette

Include a registry link on the invitation if the couple has one. Do not push for gifts. Guests who cannot attend may send a gift or a card, but it is not required. The bride should write thank-you notes soon after the shower.

## Space and rentals

Plan seats for every guest at a seated brunch and tables for food, gifts and cake. Many hosts rent tables, chairs, linens, glassware and serving pieces. For a backyard shower, add a tent. See [brunch party rentals](/blog/brunch-party-rentals-for-20-to-50) and [engagement party rentals](/blog/engagement-party-rentals).

## Keep the bride in mind

Ask what the bride wants, what she does not, and who she would like to meet. Some brides love surprise showers and some prefer to plan, so ask a close friend first. Keep the shower focused on her personality, not on a theme from a magazine.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago-area bridal showers happen in restaurant private rooms, banquet halls, backyards, tea rooms and community spaces. Spring and summer weekends fill up fast, so book early. If you plan a backyard shower, build in a rain plan, since spring storms are common. Winter showers need coat space and a way to keep entries dry. Parking can be limited in city neighborhoods, so share directions and ride-share tips. For park district pavilions and rooms, check with your village or park district about reservation dates, food and decor rules. ## Frequently asked questions

### Who traditionally hosts a bridal shower?

The maid of honor and bridesmaids traditionally host, sometimes with the bride's mother or relatives. Today anyone close to the bride can host.

### Who pays for a bridal shower?

The hosts usually pay, and costs are often shared among the host team. The bride should not pay for her own shower. Agree on a budget before you book anything.

### When should a bridal shower be held?

Usually one to three months before the wedding. Avoid the last two weeks, when the couple is busiest.

### How far in advance should I send bridal shower invitations?

Four to six weeks ahead is typical. Include the registry link and an RSVP date about ten days before the shower.

### Who should be invited to a bridal shower?

Generally women who are also invited to the wedding and close to the bride, including both families. Co-ed showers are now common, so ask the couple.

### How long does a bridal shower last?

Two to three hours: a meal, a few activities, gifts and cake.

### Can the bride's mother host the shower?

Yes. Older etiquette discouraged family from hosting, but today it is common. Co-hosting with bridesmaids is a good option.
