Many Chicago-area couples marry in a church and host the reception in a parish hall, a church basement or a nearby banquet room. These spaces often have strong bones, long tables and a built-in kitchen, but they also come with limited decor options and strict house rules. This guide shows what to ask the parish, what to rent and how to make a parish hall feel like a celebration.

## Questions for the parish or hall first

Parish halls are run by volunteers or a small staff, so get answers in writing:

- What is the capacity, and what is the fire occupancy limit?
- Which tables and chairs does the hall own, and in what condition?
- Is alcohol allowed? Many churches have rules about alcohol, and Illinois state law covers service. Check the parish and your caterer.
- Can we tape, nail or hang anything? Is there a ladder?
- What time can we load in, and what time must we be out?
- Who cleans up, and is a deposit required?
- Where are the outlets, and how many circuits?
- Are candles or flames allowed?

If you are not sure which liquor and permit rules apply, ask the parish and your caterer. Do not assume.

## Make a basement feel like a banquet

Low ceilings, paneled walls and linoleum floors are the usual challenge. Light is the answer. Turn off the harsh overhead lights if allowed and use warm lamps, uplights and string lights. Cover the walls with drape in a neutral color, and use linens and chair covers or better chairs to hide the school-hall look. A rug or dance floor in front of the DJ adds a focal point. Use plants or flowers in the corners to soften the edges.

## Tables for church halls

Long rectangular tables are common, either 8-foot banquet tables set in rows or a mix of rounds. Seat 8 at an 8-foot table, with 4 on each side, though 10 is possible when guests squeeze. Leave at least 36 inches between table backs for walking, and wider if there is a buffet line. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) for linens that fit rectangular tables and [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Chicago-specific notes

Many Chicago neighborhoods and suburbs have parish halls with deep history and good community, and a church reception is often more affordable than a banquet hall. Winter receptions need a coat solution, and snow can slow guests. Parking near a city parish can be tight, so check the lot size and the street rules. Ask the parish whether neighbors have rules about noise after a certain hour. Different churches have different customs, such as which hall can host a post-ceremony reception, so talk to the parish office early.

A [cross-back chair](/chairs/cross-back) warms up a plain hall, a [garden folding chair](/chairs/garden-folding) suits a budget, and a [Louis medallion chair](/chairs/louis-medallion) is a good fit for a traditional parish celebration.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Assuming the hall includes tables and chairs for your guest count.
- Not confirming alcohol and music rules.
- Ignoring the load-out time.
- Leaving trash and linens for the parish to deal with.


## Frequently asked questions

### How do I decorate a church basement for a reception?

Warm the lighting first, cover bare walls with drape, dress tables with floor-length linens and low centerpieces, and upgrade chairs. Add a dance floor or rug as a focal point.

### What should I ask a church about hosting a reception?

Capacity, which tables and chairs they own, alcohol and music rules, decor restrictions, load-in and out times, and cleanup responsibilities.

### Can you serve alcohol in a church hall?

It depends on the parish. Many have their own rules, and Illinois law covers service. Ask the parish and your caterer.

### How many people fit at a long table?

An 8-foot banquet table seats 8, with 4 per side, or up to 10 tightly. Leave at least 36 inches behind chairs for walking.

### Do I need to rent chairs for a parish hall?

Often yes if the hall's folding chairs are limited or worn. Upgrading chairs makes the biggest visual difference.

### What decor works in a low-ceiling hall?

Warm uplights, drape on the walls, low centerpieces and string lights. Avoid tall stands that touch the ceiling.
