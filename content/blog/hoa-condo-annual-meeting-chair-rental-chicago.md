## How to lay out the room

1. **Put the board or head table at the front**, facing the room, where officers and any property manager can be seen and heard.
2. **Set chairs in rows with a center aisle**, rather than one solid block, so a mic can be passed down the aisle for questions and people can leave early without climbing over neighbors.
3. **Keep a sign-in or proxy table near the entrance**, separate from the rows, so attendance and paperwork don't bottleneck the room.
4. **Leave space at the back** for anyone who arrives late or just wants to stand and listen without squeezing into a row.

## How many chairs to rent

Count chairs by expected turnout, not total units, since most annual meetings don't draw every owner:

- **Routine meeting, no contested vote:** plan for 20 to 30% of units. Most owners vote by proxy or skip a meeting with no major decisions on the agenda.
- **Quorum required or a contested issue on the agenda:** plan closer to 40 to 50% of units, since a special assessment, a bylaw change or a board election tends to pull out more owners than a routine update.
- **Add a few extra chairs** near the back rather than packing the front rows tighter, so late arrivals have an easy, unobtrusive seat.

## If your building already has a clubhouse or meeting room

Many Chicago-area condo and HOA buildings have their own clubhouse, party room or community space with chairs already on hand. Before you book outside rentals, check with building management or the board:

- How many chairs does the room already have, and do they match your expected turnout?
- Is the room big enough for rows with a center aisle, or will it only fit a smaller roundtable-style meeting?
- If you're renting a separate hall or clubhouse for the night, what's the load-in window before owners start arriving?

Renting makes sense when your building's own chairs don't cover a higher-turnout meeting, when the regular room setup doesn't allow for rows and an aisle, or when you're holding the meeting somewhere without built-in seating, like a rented community hall.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for an HOA annual meeting?
Plan for about 20 to 30% of units for a routine meeting, and closer to 40 to 50% of units when a quorum is required or a contested vote is expected to draw a bigger crowd than usual.

### How should chairs be arranged for a condo association meeting?
Set rows facing the board or head table with a center aisle, so a microphone can be passed during Q&A and people can leave without disrupting the room. Keep a separate sign-in or proxy table near the entrance.

### Does our building already have chairs for the annual meeting?
Many condo and HOA buildings have a clubhouse or community room with chairs on hand. Check with building management on how many are available and whether the room allows for rows with an aisle before you book rentals.

### What chairs work best for a board or annual meeting?
A comfortable, stackable chair works best for a meeting that runs an hour or more, since the room likely needs to flip back to daily use right after. A folding chair is the fastest option if you need to set rows and clear them quickly.
