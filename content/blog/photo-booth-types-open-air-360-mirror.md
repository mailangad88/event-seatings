**Quick answer**

Open-air booths suit most weddings and parties: they take about 8 by 8 feet, fit groups and run fast. A 360 booth makes spinning video clips and needs a larger clear circle and a flat floor. A photo booth is one of those rentals everyone has an opinion about. Guests love them, venues sometimes worry about them, and couples often pick by Instagram appeal without checking space, power or how many people can use them in an evening. This guide compares the four main types so you can decide with real details.

## The four main booth types

<div class="table-wrap">

| Type | What guests get | Typical group size | Pace | Space needed* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open-air | Photos, GIFs, boomerang clips against a backdrop | 1 to 10+ | Fast | About 8 x 8 ft |
| 360 video | Slow-motion spinning video from a rotating arm | 1 to 4 | Medium | About 10 x 10 ft clear circle |
| Mirror booth | Touchscreen on a mirror with prompts and animation | 1 to 6 | Slower | About 8 x 6 ft |
| Enclosed booth | Classic photo strips in a curtained box | 1 to 4 | Medium | About 6 x 6 ft |

</div>

*Space numbers are typical vendor ranges, not rules. Always confirm the exact footprint, including the backdrop, props table and line, with your vendor.

## Open-air booths

An open-air booth is a camera and lights facing a backdrop, with a screen or tablet. There is no enclosed box, so it fits large groups and there is no wall to sit behind. It is the most flexible type for weddings with a mix of generations, because grandparents and kids can jump in together. It also moves quickly: a typical session takes under a minute, so lines clear.

Best for: weddings, birthdays, corporate parties, any event with 50 or more guests.

## 360 video booths

A 360 booth uses a camera arm that circles a small platform where guests stand. The output is a short slow-motion video that can be shared immediately. It creates energy and works well in rooms with a clear dance-floor feel.

Watch for these needs:

- A **flat floor** for the platform to spin properly.
- A **clear circle** around the platform for the arm to swing safely.
- **Ceiling height** high enough for lights and for guests who raise their arms.
- Less ability to hold a big group, as the platform is small.
- **Safety:** guests on the platform need to stay seated or stand still. Ask the vendor for rules and an attendant.

Best for: high-energy parties, corporate events with a younger crowd, sweet sixteens and quinceañeras.

## Mirror booths

A mirror booth shows a full-length mirror with a touchscreen. Guests are prompted with messages and then it takes the photo. It looks elegant when it is turned off and works well as part of a luxe design.

Things to know: the screen interaction takes longer, so the line moves slower, and fingerprints on the glass need a quick wipe now and then. It also needs a stable stand and a spot out of direct sunlight.

Best for: formal weddings, galas, brand events with a custom message.

## Enclosed booths

The classic cabin with a curtain gives privacy and a nostalgic photo-strip print. It takes less floor space than a 360 and can be placed in a corner. Its main limit is capacity: usually three or four people fit.

Best for: smaller events, retro themes, hallways and nooks.

## How to choose

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Count guests.</strong> Under 50: any type. Over 100: favor open-air to avoid long lines.</li>
<li><strong>Check the space.</strong> Measure the corner. See <a href="/blog/photo-booth-space-and-power-needs">photo booth space and power needs</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Check the vibe.</strong> Formal: mirror or open-air. Party: 360.</li>
<li><strong>Ask about output.</strong> Prints, sharing, GIFs, overlays and gallery delivery vary.</li>
<li><strong>Get an attendant</strong> to manage the line, props and equipment.</li>
</ol>

## Where booths fit in a Chicago event

Place the booth near the lounge or cocktail space, not beside the dance-floor speakers or in the path to the restrooms. A booth in the cocktail area gets used early, when guests have time and hair is fresh. Coordinate [coat racks](/blog/coat-racks-for-winter-events-chicago) so they do not block the line in winter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which photo booth is best for a wedding?

An open-air booth is the safe default because it handles groups, runs fast and suits all ages. A mirror booth is a good fit for formal weddings, and a 360 booth works if the crowd wants a party feel.

### How much space does a 360 photo booth need?

Typically around 10 by 10 feet of clear floor, though models differ. Ask your vendor for the specific platform and arm size, and check ceiling height.

### How many guests can use a booth in an evening?

It depends on type and session length. A booth that takes about 30 to 60 seconds per group can serve many groups in an hour, but a slower touchscreen booth serves fewer. Ask the vendor for their typical throughput.

### Do I need an attendant?

Usually yes. An attendant manages the line, props and equipment, and reduces damage risk.

### Can a photo booth go outside?

Many can, with shade and a stable surface. Direct sun makes screens hard to read, and wind can tip backdrops. Check power access too.

### Do photo booths need to be near an outlet?

Almost always. See the space and power guide for the details to confirm with your vendor.
