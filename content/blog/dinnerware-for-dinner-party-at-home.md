**Quick answer**

Most home cooks own 8 to 12 matching plates, so a dinner party over 10 needs rentals or borrowed sets. For a three-course dinner, count 3 plates, 3 forks, 1 knife and 2 glasses per guest. Rent the pieces you are short of, not the whole set, and mix a few serving platters. Deliver two days before so you can wash, count and set the table the day before the party.

</div>

A dinner party at home sounds simple until you count. Twenty guests means 60 plates for three courses, 60 forks, 20 knives and 40 glasses. A typical home kitchen in a Chicago two-flat or a suburban ranch house does not own that much, and a dishwasher cannot cycle fast enough. This guide helps a host work out what to rent and what to skip.

## What most homes already own

A typical set holds service for 8 to 12. The odd pieces, such as serving bowls and a gravy boat, fill the gaps. Before you rent anything, count what you have by piece. Then decide whether guests will use the same plate for salad and entree, or switch.

## Simplify to cut the order

Hosts often reduce the count with a few choices:

- **Skip the salad plate** by serving salad on the dinner plate or in a bowl.
- **Serve dessert on the same plate** after clearing, with a small wash between.
- **Use one fork for salad and entree** if the menu allows.
- **Serve family-style**, so you need fewer plates and more platters; see [family-style serving ware](/blog/family-style-serving-ware).
- **Offer one wine glass** instead of two.

These choices keep the table clean and the sink empty.

## Space and seating

Home dining rooms rarely seat more than 8 to 10. For 20, you need extra tables, which brings chairs and linens into the plan. Our [chair counting guide](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) help you plan. Allow about 24 inches of table edge per seated guest, as in [place setting spacing](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements).

## Washing and pickup

Ask the rental company whether you need to wash dishes. Many ask only that you scrape food and stack plates in the crates. Do not run rental china through your dishwasher unless told it is safe. See [do you wash rental dishes](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-dishes). Breakage policies vary, so read the contract and the [breakage fees guide](/blog/rental-breakage-and-loss-fees-explained).

## Delivery to a home

Deliveries to a home mean stairs, narrow doors and street parking. Chicago city blocks have limited curb space, and some buildings need a freight elevator reservation. Tell the company about steps, elevators and parking. Crates are heavy, so have space ready to stage them near the kitchen. Our [delivery and packing guide](/blog/dinnerware-delivery-and-packing-crates) explains what to expect.

## Little problems at home

Home kitchens run out of three things: counter space, fridge space and dishwasher capacity. Clear a whole counter for the staged plates, keep the fridge for food rather than crates, and plan to hand wash nothing. A rental crate on a rug can scratch floors, so slide it on a towel. If the dining table cannot fit all guests, borrow or rent a second table and match the cloths so the whole room reads as one table.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many plates do I need for a dinner party of 20?

You need 20 of each plate type you will use. For a three-course dinner, that is 60 plates. Add 5 to 10% for breakage.

### Should I rent or buy dishes for a dinner party?

Rent if you host once or twice a year. Buy if you host monthly and can store the set. Renting avoids storage.

### Can I mix my own plates with rentals?

Yes. Use your own for the main course and rent only the salad and dessert plates. Pick a color that matches.

### How early should I order for a home party?

Order three to four weeks before, and sooner for holiday weekends. Check deadlines with the company.

### Do I need to wash rental dishes?

Usually you scrape and stack them, and the company washes. Confirm the rules, since some require a quick rinse.

### What if my guest count changes?

Tell the rental company as early as possible. Many allow small changes up to a deadline.
