**Quick answer**

A Chinese wedding tea ceremony has the couple serve tea to relatives in order of seniority, usually with the elders seated in a row and the couple kneeling or standing in front. Plan one comfortable chair for each person being honored (most families seat 2 to 12 elders), a small tea table with a tray, cushions if the couple kneels, and about 10 x 12 feet of room. Ask each family for its order of service, because customs differ by region and generation.

</div>

The tea ceremony is a short, warm ritual in which a couple thanks and honors family. Many Chinese-American couples in Chicago hold it in a hotel suite, a family home or a side room at the venue, either before the ceremony or between the ceremony and the reception. The rental list is small, but seating is the whole point: elders sit while the couple serves, so the chairs have to be comfortable, sturdy and arranged so the couple can reach each person.

## How the ceremony shapes the room

Families vary, but the pattern is common. The couple serves tea to the groom's parents first, then the bride's parents, then other elders, grandparents and sometimes siblings and cousins. In return, relatives often give red envelopes or jewelry. Whatever your version, three physical needs repeat:

- **Seats for elders.** Each honored relative needs a chair, ideally with arms and a firm seat.
- **A place to put the tea.** A small table or tray stand within the couple's reach.
- **A way for the couple to serve comfortably.** Some kneel on cushions, others stand or bow.

Guests who are not part of the service stand or sit behind in a second row.

## Choose chairs that elders can use

Think about how an elder gets in and out of a chair, because grandparents may sit for 20 minutes or more. Chairs with arms and a seat height of about 17 to 19 inches are easier for most older guests. If the room is small, ask each relative to bring their own preference for armchair versus side chair.

- The [Louis Medallion chair](/chairs/louis-medallion) (estimated $20 to $30) is a formal option with a supportive back.
- The [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) ($18 to $25) in a red, gold or neutral tone is comfortable and photographs well.
- For a pair of honored seats for parents, a [throne-style pair](/chairs/royal-throne) ($75 to $150 per pair) adds ceremony without needing a stage.

See our [comfortable chairs for older guests](/blog/comfortable-chairs-for-older-guests) guide for what to look for.

## Table, tray and cups

A small round table about 30 to 36 inches across or a 24 x 48-inch rectangular table is plenty for the tea tray, cups and red envelopes. Ask each family what to place on the table, and bring your own tea set unless a venue supplies one. The table can sit to the side of the couple or in front, whichever gives the photographer a clear line.

## Room size and sightlines

A suite or private room of 10 x 12 feet fits a row of six elders and a tea table. For larger families, hold the ceremony in two rounds (groom's side, then bride's side) or in a private room at the venue. Place chairs in a shallow arc so the couple does not have to reach across. For photos, keep a 3-foot gap between the elders' row and the wall.

## Linens, color and decor

Red and gold are common colors in Chinese weddings, but not universal. Many families choose red linens, gold chair accents or a simple neutral setup. ## Chicago notes

- Many Chicago-area hotels have suites or a bridal room that works well, but ask about the room's furniture before renting. Some suites already have armchairs.
- If you hold the ceremony at a home in the suburbs, check delivery access and parking, and measure doorways for chair width.
- Confirm timing with your photographer. The tea ceremony is often squeezed between the ceremony and the reception.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who sits at a Chinese tea ceremony?

The couple serves tea to elders, usually starting with the groom's parents, then the bride's parents and other relatives by seniority. Each honored person needs a chair. Other guests stand or sit behind. Families have their own order, so ask them.

### How many chairs do I need?

One for each person being served, plus a few extra for family members who need to sit. Most couples need 2 to 12 armchairs for elders and a few chairs behind. Count the people on both sides before you rent.

### Do we have to kneel?

Not always. Some couples kneel on cushions, others bow or stand. Ask your family and choose what is comfortable. Cushions are easy to add if you want them.

### How big is the tea table?

A small table about 30 to 36 inches across or 24 x 48 inches is enough for a tray, cups and red envelopes. It should be within easy reach of the couple.

### Can I hold the ceremony at the reception venue?

Yes, if the venue has a private room or a quiet corner. Ask early because rooms book quickly. Many couples also hold it in a hotel suite.

### How long does a tea ceremony take?

Most take 20 to 30 minutes, depending on how many relatives are served. Add time for photos and for elders to move in and out of the room.
