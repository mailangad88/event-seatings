**Quick answer**

In the City of Chicago, a temporary tent of 400 square feet or less (and no more than 15 feet high) does not need a building permit, but it must still meet the Chicago Construction Codes. A tent over 400 square feet needs a permit from the Department of Buildings. Tents up to 600 square feet can use the Express Permit Program. Apply at least 14 days before setup, or the permit fee triples.

Hosting a party under a tent in Chicago feels like a backyard decision, but the city treats a big tent as a temporary structure. The good news is that the rules are clear and the process for smaller tents is fast. This guide summarizes what the Chicago Department of Buildings publishes. Rules change, so confirm the details on the department's tent permit pages or with your tent company before you book.

## The three size tiers

The city's temporary tent procedures sort tents by area and height for structures that stay up 180 days or less.

- **400 square feet or less, no point over 15 feet:** no building permit. A 20 x 20 tent is exactly 400 square feet.
- **401 to 600 square feet, or taller than 15 feet:** a building permit is required. Tents up to 600 square feet and 15 feet high can use drawings from any responsible person and the Express Permit Program.
- **Over 600 square feet:** drawings must come from an Illinois-licensed architect or structural engineer, and the application goes through standard plan review or self-certification on the city's E-Plan system.

A 20 x 30 tent is 600 square feet, so it just fits the express tier. A 30 x 30 tent is 900 square feet and needs the full process.

## Tents close together count together

The city generally adds up the area of tents that sit less than 12 feet apart. Two 20 x 20 tents placed side by side count as 800 square feet, not two small tents. For a special event permit, the gap drops to 6 feet only if the organizer certifies in writing that no cooking or heating devices, fuel or open flames will be within 10 feet of the tents.

## What the city asks for

For permitted tents, expect the application to include:

- **A fire resistance certificate.** The tent fabric must have a certificate showing compliance with NFPA 701. If the flame-retardant treatment is not permanent, the certificate must be dated within 360 days before the last date of use.
- **Anchorage details.** Tents up to 600 square feet need anchorage drawn by a responsible person. Larger tents need structural details and wind calculations from a licensed professional.
- **A high winds action plan.** It must state the wind speed and timing that trigger evacuation, how weather will be monitored, and who handles evacuation or takedown.
- **Fire extinguishers** where cooking happens under or within 20 feet of the structure.
- **Battery-backup emergency lighting** if the tent stays in place more than 48 hours.

## The 14-day rule

If a complete permit application reaches the city less than 14 calendar days before installation, the permit fee is tripled. Reinstatement applications follow the same rule. In peak June and September weekends your tent company's schedule also fills up, so two weeks is a minimum, not a goal. ## What about a backyard or alley?

The city's pages we reviewed do not single out backyards, so assume the same area rules apply. A tent that crosses onto a sidewalk, alley or street needs separate approval. That is a separate approval from a building permit, so ask the Department of Buildings which city office handles it before you plan on the public way. Your landlord or condo board may also have rules that are stricter than the city's.

## Heaters and cooking

Heating and cooking devices change the separation rules, as above. Do not plan a gas heater or grill inside a tent. Read [heating a tent in fall](/blog/heating-a-tent-in-fall) for safer choices.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit for a tent in my backyard in Chicago?

If the tent is over 400 square feet, yes. A tent of 400 square feet or less and under 15 feet high does not need a building permit, but it still has to meet the Chicago Construction Codes. The city's tent page does not give separate backyard rules, so confirm with the Department of Buildings.

### How long before my event should I apply?

Apply at least 14 calendar days before installation. Applications that arrive later pay triple the permit fee. Ask your tent company to start sooner if the date falls on a busy weekend.

### Who pulls the tent permit, me or the rental company?

It varies. Many tent companies pull the permit as part of their service, and others expect the host to. Ask before you sign the contract and get the answer in writing.

### What is the Express Permit Program?

It is a faster path for tents and stages up to 600 square feet and no more than 15 feet high. Drawings can come from any responsible person rather than a licensed architect or engineer.

### Does a pop-up canopy need a permit?

A 10 x 10 canopy is 100 square feet, well under the 400 square foot line, so it needs no building permit. It must still follow the code, including fire safety and anchoring. Read [pop-up canopy vs rented tent](/blog/pop-up-canopy-vs-rented-tent) for the differences.

### Do the suburbs follow the same rules as Chicago?

No. Each village or city sets its own rules, though 400 square feet is a common starting point. Our [suburban tent permit guide](/blog/tent-permit-chicago-suburbs) explains how to check yours.
