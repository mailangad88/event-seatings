**Quick answer**

Set up the tamalada as a line: husks and masa at one end, fillings in the middle, and wrapped tamales at the other end near the steamers. Use banquet tables covered with plastic or butcher paper, with one 6-foot table for every 4 to 6 people. Standing work is easiest at about 34 to 36 inches high, so seat the elders at standard 30-inch tables and put the rest of the crew at raised tables or counters.

A tamalada is a tamale-making gathering. Family and friends spend a day around the table, spreading masa, adding filling and folding. It is common in the weeks before Christmas, and in Chicago neighborhoods like Pilsen and Little Village it is part of the season. A good tamalada makes hundreds of tamales in a day. A bad one means people hunched over a low table, a sticky mess and one stove for the steamer. This guide covers the setup, from table height to cleanup. Methods vary by family and region, so adjust to how your family makes them.

## Lay it out as a line

Think of an assembly line. Each step has its own spot, and people can pass tamales down the line instead of crossing in front of each other.

For 8 to 10 workers, two 6-foot or 8-foot tables placed end to end or side by side work. For 15 or more, add a third table for fillings and a second for wrapped tamales waiting to be steamed.

## Table height and chairs

Standard rental banquet tables stand 30 inches tall. That is right for sitting, and low for standing. Most people prefer to stand to spread masa, and a low table is hard on backs and shoulders. Many adults are comfortable at roughly 34 to 36 inches, about elbow height, but it depends on who is working.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Option | Height | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Standard banquet table | 30 in | Seated workers, folding, elders |
| Counter or kitchen island | about 36 in | Spreading masa, standing |
| Highboy cocktail table | 42 in | Standing, tall crew, fillings |
| Table risers or bed risers | adds a few inches | Raising a standard table |

</div>

Mix heights. Put elders and children at seated tables with folding chairs and give them the folding job, which is easy to do sitting down. Keep standing stations for people who like to stand. Risers can raise banquet tables, but only if the legs are stable. Ask a rental company which options exist. See [table heights](/blog/table-heights-standard-counter-bar) and [cocktail tables](/blog/cocktail-highboy-vs-lowboy-tables).

For chairs, folding chairs are fine for a day, but padded ones are easier for a 4-hour session. See [chairs](/rentals/chairs) and [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need). A rule of thumb is 24 inches of table edge per worker.

## Cover the tables

Masa sticks, and corn husks stain. Cover each table before anyone starts.

- Use a **plastic or vinyl tablecloth** or heavy butcher paper, taped under the edge.
- Tape down corners so a sleeve does not drag them.
- Put a sheet pan or a rimmed tray under bowls of filling.
- Keep a roll of paper towels and a damp cloth at each station.
- If you rent linens, ask about stain policies first. A rental cloth may not be right for masa. See [linen stain and damage policies](/blog/linen-stain-and-damage-policies) and [linens](/rentals/linens).

## Steamer and stove space

The steamer is the bottleneck. A big tamale pot, or tamalera, may take 2 hours or more to cook a batch, and each batch holds a limited number. Plan the steaming while the crew is still wrapping.

- **A tall steamer pot** needs a sturdy burner and room around it. Check the weight when full of water.
- **A second burner** doubles your output. Some hosts use a propane burner outside. Use gas burners only where it is safe, and check your building and village rules.
- **Power.** A roaster oven or an electric steamer needs its own outlet. See [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist).
- **Resting space.** Cooked tamales need to rest on a tray before serving. Keep a warm spot nearby.

For a large batch, ask about [catering equipment](/rentals/catering-equipment) such as roasters and warming trays.

## Cleanup

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Before</strong> Cover tables, set bins, lay out towels.</li>
<li><strong>During</strong> Toss husk scraps into a lined bin as you go.</li>
<li><strong>After steaming</strong> Wipe tables, then lift the covers and fold inward.</li>
<li><strong>Dishes</strong> Soak bowls and spoons. Masa hardens fast.</li>
<li><strong>Return rentals</strong> Scrape, stack and count before pickup.</li>
</ol>

Keep masa out of drains. Scrape it into the trash. See [event cleanup plan](/blog/event-cleanup-plan) and [returning rentals](/blog/returning-event-rentals-condition-and-cleaning).

## What most hosts find out too late

- **Cold basements and garages.** A Chicago garage in December can be near freezing, and masa gets stiff. Work indoors in a warm room if you can, and plan heat if you cannot. See [heating rentals](/rentals/heating-cooling).
- **The weight of steamers.** A tamalera full of water and tamales is heavy. Put it on a stove or a stable stand, not a folding table.
- **Lines for the sink.** A crowd of 15 can back up one sink fast. Set out a tub or two for soaking bowls and utensils.
- **Weekends before Christmas book early.** Ask for your date as soon as you know it. If the crew is large, extra tables and chairs run out first. See [peak season event rentals](/blog/peak-season-event-rentals-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### What height should tables be for making tamales?

Standing work is usually comfortable at 34 to 36 inches, about elbow height. Standard rental tables are 30 inches, which suits sitting. Mix heights so everyone has a place.

### How many tables do I need for a tamalada?

One 6-foot table per 4 to 6 workers is a good rule of thumb. For 10 workers, plan two tables for the line and one for fillings and wrapped tamales. Add a table for serving.

### How do I protect tables from masa?

Cover them with plastic or vinyl and tape down the edges. Use sheet pans under bowls and keep damp cloths at each station. If you rent linens, ask about stain rules first.

### How many tamales can one steamer make?

It depends on the size of the pot and the stack. A big tamalera holds many dozens, and a batch takes about 2 hours or more. A second burner and pot helps a lot.

### Do I need chairs for a tamalada?

Yes, for people who sit to wrap and fold, and for elders. Folding chairs work for a day. Padded chairs are easier for a 4-hour session.

### How do I clean up after making tamales?

Scrape masa into the trash, not the drain, soak bowls right away and wipe tables before the masa hardens. Roll up paper covers from the edges inward.
