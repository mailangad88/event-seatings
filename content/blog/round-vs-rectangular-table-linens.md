**Quick answer**

Round tables use one round cloth per table (120 inches for a 60-inch round, 132 inches for a 72-inch round) and need no runner, while rectangular tables need a rectangular cloth (90 x 132 inches for a 6-foot banquet, 90 x 156 inches for an 8-foot banquet) and look best with a runner. Rounds are easier to dress and better for conversation. Rectangles seat in rows, use more linen per seat and suit long-table, buffet and head-table styles.

</div>

Choosing between round and rectangular tables changes your linen order more than most people realize. Shapes need different cloth sizes, different napkin placements and different decor. They also change how a room reads in photos. Here is what shifts when you pick one layout, or combine both, at a Chicago-area wedding or banquet.

## Round tables

Rounds are the classic wedding table. Every guest sees every other guest, a single cloth covers the whole thing, and there is no head or foot. Linens are simple: one cloth, one napkin per seat, and a centerpiece in the middle. A 60-inch round with a 120-inch cloth hangs 30 inches on every side. A 72-inch round needs a 132-inch cloth. See the [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).

Rounds also drape evenly, which hides wrinkles better and moves less in a draft. They are more expensive in floor space, though. A 60-inch round takes about the same footprint as a 5-foot square, so the room looks fuller with many rounds. For counts, see [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need).

## Rectangular tables

Rectangles seat guests in rows. They work for buffet lines, head tables, family-style dinners and any layout where you want a long single table or several in a row. They need a rectangular cloth, and that makes size and fit more critical: an 8-foot banquet table needs a 156-inch-long cloth to reach the floor, and a cloth that is 6 inches short shows the legs at each end.

A runner is the most common add-on. It breaks up the long expanse of cloth and gives you a place for candles and greenery. See [table runner sizes and styles](/blog/table-runner-sizes-and-styles).

Rectangles can also be joined end to end for a feast layout. Ask your supplier how to handle the seam: two cloths overlapping at the join, or one long cloth. For linen on a long rustic table, see [farm table linens](/blog/farm-table-linens-runner-only).

## Mixing round and rectangular tables

Many Chicago rooms mix both: rounds for guests, rectangles for the head table, buffet, bar and gift tables. The rule for linens is to keep fabric and color consistent across shapes so the room reads as one. Use the same base color for rounds and rectangles, and put contrast on the head table or buffet with a runner or skirt. See [table skirting](/blog/pleated-table-skirting-head-table-buffet) for buffet and head table ideas.

## Cost and logistics

Linen price depends on size and fabric, not shape alone, so ask your supplier to itemize round and rectangular cloths separately. Rectangular cloths in large sizes can be harder to find, and large rounds, like 132-inch cloths, may be stocked in fewer colors. In general, a room with one shape needs fewer different cloth sizes, which simplifies counts and delivery. A mixed room needs more line items on the order.

## When each shape works best

- **Rounds:** plated dinners, receptions with dancing, rooms where conversation is a priority.
- **Rectangles:** buffets, long-table dinners, cocktail parties with food stations, rustic and barn weddings.
- **Both:** most large weddings, with rectangles for the head table and buffet.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are round or rectangular tables easier to dress?

Round tables are easier. One round cloth covers the table, it drapes evenly, and no runner is needed. Rectangles need the right cloth length and often a runner for a finished look.

### Do round tables need table runners?

No. They are optional on 60 and 72-inch rounds, laid across the middle. Many planners skip them and use a centerpiece and napkins for color.

### How many people sit at a rectangular table?

An 8-foot banquet seats 8 to 10 guests with chairs on both sides, depending on how much room each person needs. Adding ends can raise it, but the ends are tighter.

### Can I use round cloths on rectangular tables?

Not as a base. A round cloth will not cover a banquet table to the floor. A round cloth can be a topper on a rectangular table for a layered look.

### Which shape uses more linen?

It depends on the size. Rectangles use more fabric per seat for floor-length cloths, while rounds use less cloth per seat but take up more floor space.

### Can I mix round and rectangular linens in one room?

Yes. Use the same fabric and base color for both and add contrast through runners or skirts on the rectangles.
