**Quick answer**

Pack one labeled tote with first aid, stain remover and spare napkins, scissors, tape, safety pins, zip ties, a charger and power strip, trash bags, snacks and water, sunscreen or a poncho, and a printed sheet of vendor phone numbers and the day-of timeline. Give one person the job of carrying it. Most day-of problems are small, and a good kit makes them invisible.

Something always goes sideways on event day. A zipper splits, a candle drips, a vendor runs late or the ice runs out. None of this ruins an event if you can fix it in two minutes. A well-packed kit and a calm person in charge are what separate a smooth party from a stressful one. Here is what to pack, grouped by what it solves.

## First aid and personal care

Pack bandages, antiseptic wipes, pain relievers, allergy tablets, antacids, hand sanitizer and tissues. Include a few blister pads for shoes and lip balm. Add any personal medication for the host. If your event has many children or older guests, tell your venue where its first aid kit is. Do not give guests medication unless you are trained.

## Fix-it tools

Scissors, a box cutter, packing tape, duct tape, painter's tape, zip ties, binder clips, a small screwdriver, a lighter or matches (where candles are allowed), a flashlight and extra batteries. Command strips and a stapler help hang signs. Fishing line is handy for hanging decor.

## Clothing and appearance

Safety pins, a mini sewing kit, a lint roller, stain remover pen, deodorant, hair ties, bobby pins, a comb, double-sided fashion tape and shoe inserts. If the guest of honor will be photographed, a few small touch-up items go a long way.

## Spills and cleanup

Paper towels, baby wipes, club soda, extra napkins, trash bags in two sizes, a small broom and dustpan, and disposable gloves. Our [event cleanup plan](/blog/event-cleanup-plan) covers the end of the night. If you rent dishes, check how they should be returned in [do you wash rental dishes](/blog/do-you-wash-rental-dishes).

## Tech and power

Phone chargers, a battery pack, a power strip, extension cords rated for the use, a Bluetooth speaker as a backup, and an HDMI or audio adapter if there is a presentation. For outdoor power, see [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist) and [outdoor extension cord safety](/blog/outdoor-extension-cord-safety-events).

## Weather gear

Sunscreen, bug spray, ponchos or umbrellas, small towels, hand warmers in cold months and a few extra clips or weights for tent walls and decor. Read [mosquitoes and bugs at outdoor events](/blog/mosquitoes-and-bugs-outdoor-events) and [rain on event day](/blog/rain-on-event-day-chicago).

## Comfort and food

Bottled water, snacks for you and your helpers, a few granola bars, and a plan for vendor meals. Hungry hosts make worse decisions at 4 p.m.

## Paperwork

Print the day-of timeline, vendor names and phone numbers, deposit receipts, rental order, floor plan, headcount, permits and insurance papers. Keep a copy in the kit and a copy in your phone. A [run of show](/blog/four-hour-party-timeline) with names beside each task helps others act without you.

## Decide who owns the kit

Pick one calm person who is not the host. Show them where the kit lives and what is in it. That person handles small fires so the host can enjoy the party.

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago weather can swing in a single day, so pack for sun, wind and rain at any outdoor event. Add snow melt or salt and a boot tray in winter, and an umbrella or poncho in spring and fall. If you park on a city street, keep coins or a parking app ready, and check loading rules for deliveries. Lakefront and open venues can be windy, so add clips and weights. For permit questions, keep a copy with you, and check with your village or park district for any conditions on site.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should be in an event emergency kit?

Pack first aid, fix-it tools such as scissors and tape, clothing repair items, stain and cleanup supplies, chargers, snacks and a printed contact list. Add weather gear for outdoor events.

### What do wedding planners keep in their kit?

Commonly safety pins, sewing items, fashion tape, stain pens, bandages, tissues, scissors, tape, snacks and chargers. Many also pack mints and hairpins. Adapt the list for your own event.

### Who should carry the emergency kit?

One reliable person who is not the host or guest of honor. They should know where it is and what it holds. Keep the kit near the main work area.

### What do I do if a vendor is late?

Call them right away, check their ETA and tell your timeline owner. Move flexible tasks like photos earlier. Keep the vendor's number printed.

### How do I handle a spill or stain?

Blot, don't rub, use club soda or a stain pen, and cover with a napkin or runner if needed. Keep extra linens or napkins nearby. Tell the caterer if it affects service.

### What should I do if it rains?

Move guests to the tent or indoor backup and use towels and mats at entrances. Keep ponchos handy. See your weather plan and decide early rather than late.

### Should I bring extra supplies for guests?

A few extras help, such as bottled water, sunscreen, bandages and phone chargers. Do not offer medication. Direct anyone with a medical issue to professional help.
