**Quick answer**

In a narrow room 20 to 24 feet wide, run one or two long tables lengthwise with at least 4 to 5 feet of clear aisle on each side. A single 36-foot table of 8-foot banquets seats about 34 to 36 guests. Put the bar and buffet on the long walls, and the dance floor or lounge at one end.

</div>

Narrow rooms are common in Chicago: storefront event spaces, long restaurant private rooms, church fellowship halls and brick loft rooms. They rarely look like a ballroom, and round tables do not fit well. Long tables do. They create the family-style feeling people want, and when they are planned correctly they make a narrow room feel intentional.

## How wide does the room need to be?

The width of the room decides the layout. A banquet table is 30 inches wide (some farm tables are 36). Add chairs, which extend about 18 to 24 inches back, then the aisles.

| Layout | Minimum room width | What fits |
|---|---|---|
| One long table | About 17 ft (3 table + 4 chairs + 10 aisles) | Seats on both sides, 4 to 5 ft aisles |
| One table plus wall buffet | 20 to 22 ft | Table with a serving line along one wall |
| Two long tables side by side | 26 to 28 ft | Two tables, 5 ft between chair backs |
| Three long tables | 34 ft or wider | Or use a wide room with rounds |

Round tables in a 20-foot-wide room waste space; a row of 6-foot rounds with 4 feet of aisle uses about 22 feet. See [long tables vs round tables for weddings](/blog/long-tables-vs-round-tables-wedding) and [farm tables for weddings](/blog/farm-tables-for-weddings).

Give each guest at least 24 inches of table (the standard for a comfortable place setting; 20 to 22 inches is tight). Seat guests on both sides, and leave the ends open for the couple or a host.

## Two long tables

If the room is 26 feet wide or more, two parallel tables seat double. Keep at least 5 feet between the chair backs of the two tables so servers can pass. Space is easily lost: with two 30-inch tables and four rows of chairs, you use 2.5 + 2.5 + 3 + 3 feet and then the aisle. Walk through it with a tape measure in the room.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Measure the width.</strong> At the narrowest point, not at the door.</li>
<li><strong>Subtract the walls' features.</strong> Radiators, benches, columns, doors that swing in.</li>
<li><strong>Draw the table.</strong> 30 or 36 inches wide, centered.</li>
<li><strong>Add chairs.</strong> Two feet on each side.</li>
<li><strong>Leave aisles.</strong> 4 to 5 feet, with 6 feet where servers and a bar share a wall.</li>
</ol>

## Making a narrow room feel wide

A narrow room can feel like a hallway if the table is too thin or the lighting is flat. Three tricks help. First, run the table down the center line so the room feels symmetrical. Second, add height with low centerpieces and tall candlesticks at intervals, not a single row of flowers. Third, use the end walls: a draped backdrop, a sign or a photo wall at the far end draws the eye down the room, and a seating chart at the near end tells guests where to start.

Lighting also matters. String lights or pendant lights over the table line reinforce its length; see [string light layouts for events](/blog/string-light-layouts-for-events). Make sure the aisles are not the darkest part of the room, since servers and guests need to see the floor.

When the room is narrower than 17 feet, a long table may not work at all. In that case, use two rows of smaller tables against the walls, or switch to a cocktail format with a few seated areas. Measure first, then choose the layout, not the other way around.

## Where do the bar, buffet and dance floor go?

In a narrow room, use the long walls for service, such as the bar, buffet and coat rack. Use one end for the entrance and the other for dancing. The point is to keep the central aisles clear and avoid placing the bar where guests walk in. If you have a buffet, the line runs along the wall, not across the aisle. See [buffet line flow and serving speed](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) and [dance floor placement](/blog/dance-floor-placement-reception-layout). For 40 guests or fewer, see [floor plan for 50 guests](/blog/floor-plan-for-50-guests) for round-table options.

## Chairs and linens

Long tables show every chair, so the chair matters. A [bentwood bistro chair](/chairs/bentwood) or [cane-back chair](/chairs/cane-back) suits a long rustic table. Rental tables that are 30 inches wide can have a runner of 14 to 16 inches. See [table runner sizes](/blog/table-runner-sizes-and-styles).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people can sit at a 36-foot table?

About 34 to 40 guests on both sides, at 24 inches each. Leave the ends for hosts or the couple.

### How wide should a room be for one long table?

At least 17 feet, with 4 to 5 feet of aisle on each side. More is better for servers.

### Can I use two long tables in a 24-foot room?

It is tight. Two tables with chairs need about 26 to 28 feet. In a narrower room use one table and a lounge or bar.

### How far apart should long tables be?

At least 5 feet between chair backs for service, 4 feet at the very least.

### How much space per guest at a long table?

About 24 inches of table length. Fewer than 20 inches feels crowded.

### Where do I put the dance floor in a narrow room?

At one end, beyond the tables, so the floor is not cut off from the bar.
