**Quick answer**

A 250-guest seated banquet with dancing needs about 5,000 to 6,000 square feet: 25 rounds of 10, a 28 x 28 foot dance floor, a stage or DJ riser, three bars and 5-foot service aisles. That is 20 to 24 square feet per guest. At this size, service logistics decide the layout more than table count.

</div>

At 250 guests, you are in ballroom territory: large hotel rooms in downtown Chicago, big banquet halls in the western suburbs, or a 60 x 100 tent. Seating is straightforward. What gets complicated is serving 250 people one plate at a time, giving the bar enough capacity and making sure nobody has to cross the whole room for anything.

## Why service decides the layout

For a plated dinner, caterers often work in "teams": one server for two tables, each team starting at the kitchen door. With 25 tables, you want four or five serving teams. That requires a straight aisle to each row, no bar lines crossing the route, and no dance floor between the kitchen and the farthest table.

Chairs matter here too. Chairs placed back to back with only 3 feet between rows make serving impossible. At least 4 feet from chair back to chair back, ideally 5. See our guide to [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) and [how many tables for 50 to 200 guests](/blog/how-many-tables-for-50-100-150-200-guests) for the base counts.

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Find the kitchen door.</strong> Mark where food comes in and where dishes leave.</li>
<li><strong>Draw service lanes.</strong> One lane per two rows, at least 5 feet wide.</li>
<li><strong>Place tables.</strong> Rows parallel to the lanes, 12-foot centers.</li>
<li><strong>Add bars and stations.</strong> On the opposite end from the kitchen, with 6 feet clear in front.</li>
<li><strong>Add the stage and dance floor.</strong> At the end of the room, with a short route to the bar.</li>
</ol>

## Reserved seating at 250

With 250 guests you need a seating chart that people can find quickly. Table numbers must be visible from the door, and a display with the alphabetical list on an easel or a large board prevents a bottleneck. We cover this in [reserved seating and signage layout](/blog/reserved-seating-and-signage-layout).

## A sample service timeline

Numbers help a caterer more than adjectives. For a plated dinner, a common rhythm is salad out and cleared in about 30 minutes, entree out and cleared in about 45, then toasts and cake. With 25 tables and five serving teams, each team handles five tables. If your caterer says they need a 15 x 15 foot staging area near a door, give it to them in the drawing, because a plan without it forces servers to plate in the aisle.

If your event includes speeches, put the microphone where the most tables face it and keep the dance floor clear until the program ends. A pipe-and-drape divider can hide the staging area from guests. Ask your caterer what they need, then add it to the plan before you add decor.

## What if you use a tent?

A 250-seat dinner under canvas needs around 5,000 to 6,000 square feet: a 60 x 100 tent, or a 60 x 80 with the dance floor outside the roofline, depending on your tent company. Do not size it from guest count alone. Ask your tent company for the plan they recommend, and ask the village, city or park district about permit rules, since tents above a certain size usually need one. Our [Chicago tent permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) explains what to ask.

## Safety and accessibility

Large rooms need clear paths for guests with wheelchairs or walkers. Keep at least one 36-inch-wide route to every table and reserve a few spaces at each row that are not in the aisle. The [ADA accessible seating guide](/blog/ada-accessible-event-seating-and-pathways) shows how.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big a room do I need for 250 guests?

About 5,000 to 6,000 square feet for a seated dinner and dancing. Smaller rooms work only without a dance floor.

### How many tables do I need for 250 guests?

Twenty-five 72-inch rounds of 10. Add small tables for the cake, gifts, guest book and sweetheart or head table.

### How many bars for 250 guests?

Three bars is the usual answer. Two can work if you also run a cocktail hour in a separate space.

### How wide should the aisles be at a 250-person banquet?

At least 5 feet between rows for plated service. Four feet is the absolute minimum most catering teams accept.

### How big should the dance floor be for 250 guests?

About 28 x 28 feet, or 784 square feet, enough for roughly 100 people dancing at once.

### Is buffet realistic for 250 guests?

Yes, with three or four serving lines or stations. A single line can take over an hour to serve 250 guests.

### How many servers are needed for 250 guests?

Caterers typically staff one server per 15 to 25 guests for plated service. Ask your caterer for their ratio.
