**Quick answer**

A table skirt is a pleated, gathered or flat panel fastened around the edge of a rectangular table, hiding the legs and storage under a buffet, head table or cake table. Skirt three sides of an 8-foot banquet (front plus two ends) with about 13 feet of finished perimeter, or all four sides with about 21 feet. Skirts attach with clips or Velcro, and a floor-length cloth is simpler if guests only see one side.

</div>

A buffet looks tidy or messy depending on what is under it. A skirt turns a folding banquet table into a finished piece and hides the boxes, chafing fuel and extension cords that sit below. For the head table and the cake table, it provides a front panel that photographs cleanly. Here is how to size and style skirting for Chicago-area weddings and banquets.

- **Against a wall:** front plus the two ends.
- **Freestanding buffet:** all four sides, but a back panel can be plain if the caterer works behind it.
- **Head table:** usually the guest-facing front and sides only.
- **Cake table:** usually all around, since guests circle it.

## How much skirting do you need?

Skirt length is the perimeter you cover. Pleated skirts also use extra fabric for the pleats, so suppliers cut and price them differently. The finished perimeter is simple arithmetic.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Table | Sides skirted | Finished perimeter covered |
|---|---|---|
| 6 ft banquet (72 x 30 in) | Front + 2 ends | 11 ft (72 + 30 + 30 in) |
| 6 ft banquet | All 4 | 17 ft |
| 8 ft banquet (96 x 30 in) | Front + 2 ends | 13 ft (96 + 30 + 30 in) |
| 8 ft banquet | All 4 | 21 ft |
| 48 in round | All around | about 13 ft (circumference) |

</div>

Many suppliers stock skirts in set lengths, so you may be given a longer one. Confirm what you will receive and how it is fastened.

## Skirt length and drop

The drop should match the table height: about 29 inches for a standard 30-inch table, just brushing the floor. A skirt that is too long gets stepped on; one that is too short shows a gap. Ask for the table height your supplier uses.

## Styles

### Box pleat
Evenly spaced flat folds. Tailored and formal. The usual head table style.

### Shirred or gathered
Soft, full ruffles. Romantic and less structured.

### Flat or swag
Smooth panels with fabric swags at the top. Traditional, banquet-hall style.

### Spandex and stretch skirts
A fitted, modern look without pleats. See [spandex covers](/blog/spandex-covers-cocktail-tables) for the same idea on tall tables.

## How skirting attaches

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Cover the tabletop</strong> with a base cloth or topper.</li>
<li><strong>Clip or Velcro</strong> the skirt to the table edge, starting at a back corner.</li>
<li><strong>Space pleats evenly</strong> and keep seams at corners.</li>
<li><strong>Check floor clearance</strong> and fix any dragging.</li>
<li><strong>Add the top cloth or runner</strong> so clips and Velcro are hidden.</li>
</ol>

Ask who attaches skirts. Some rental companies include it, others deliver it flat for a crew to fit. A pleated skirt takes noticeably longer to set up than a floor-length cloth.

## Skirt or floor-length cloth?

A floor-length cloth is simpler and fine for most guest tables. Choose a skirt when:

- You want texture or pleats at the head table
- The cloth would puddle or show seams
- You need to open and close the table front for storage
- The buffet is long and you want a clean line

Choose a cloth when guests sit on all sides, setup time is short or the budget is tight. Our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) lists floor-length sizes.

## Head tables, buffets, cakes

- **Head table.** Skirt the guest-facing front and ends. If you want a layered look, add a runner. See [sweetheart table chair ideas](/blog/sweetheart-table-chair-ideas) for the seating side.
- **Buffet.** Skirt the front and any exposed ends. Leave room under the table for supplies, and ask your caterer where they need access.
- **Cake table.** A small round skirted table is classic. Add a topper in a contrast color.

## Chicago venue notes

Hotel ballrooms and banquet halls often have skirting in house stock. If you bring your own, ask whether they will attach it. Tents on grass may need a ground cover or a floor so the skirt does not soak up moisture. Check how your caterer sets up buffets so your skirt works with their equipment.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much skirting do I need for an 8-foot table?

About 21 feet of finished perimeter for all four sides, or about 13 feet for the front and two ends. Pleated skirts use extra fabric, so ask your supplier what length they will send.

### Do I need a skirt on a head table?

Not required, but it is common. The front of the head table is the part guests see and photographers capture. A skirt or floor-length cloth hides the legs.

### How do you attach a table skirt?

With clips or Velcro on the table edge. Cover the attachment line with a tablecloth or runner so clips do not show.

### Can I put a skirt on a round table?

Yes. Round skirts wrap the whole perimeter and are common for cake and gift tables.

### Should a buffet table be skirted on the back?

Usually not. The caterer needs access from behind, and guests do not see it. Skirt only visible sides.

### Is a skirt or a floor-length tablecloth cheaper?

It depends on the supplier. Skirts add labor and may be priced separately, so compare quotes for each.
