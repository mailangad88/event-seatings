A lot of Chicago-area high schools hold a grand march before prom: an hour where couples walk a runway in front of family, photographers and often the whole school community, before everyone heads to the actual dance. It's usually in the school gym, which means it needs its own chairs even though the dinner-dance later that night happens somewhere else entirely.

## How to lay out the grand march

1. **Run the aisle or runway down the center of the gym**, with enough width for couples to walk through comfortably and pause for photos.
2. **Set chair rows facing in on both sides**, so everyone has a clear view without craning around the person in front.
3. **Put the photographer or video stand at one end**, opposite where couples enter, so every walk-through ends facing the camera.
4. **Leave an aisle at the back of the rows** for anyone arriving late or stepping out, without crossing in front of the runway.

## How many chairs to rent

Count by families attending, not by the number of students in the grand march:

- **Plan one seat per attending adult**, and remember most students bring two or more family members, not just one.
- **Check with the school on expected turnout** from past years, since a grand march usually draws more family than the dance itself.
- **Add a short row near the entrance** for grandparents or anyone who needs a seat close to the door.

## The grand march isn't the dinner-dance

It's easy to assume one set of rentals covers the whole night, but most Chicago-area proms split into two separate events with two separate seating needs:

- **The grand march** happens at the school, usually in the gym, and needs its own chairs since the gym doesn't have built-in audience seating for this.
- **The dinner-dance** happens afterward at a hotel, banquet hall or other venue, which almost always provides its own banquet chairs as part of the room rental.

If your school is planning both, confirm with the dinner venue what's included before ordering anything extra, and keep the grand march order separate since it's a different room, a different guest count, and a different kind of seating.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many chairs do I need for a prom grand march?
Plan one seat per attending family member, not per student, since most couples bring two or more guests. Check with the school on turnout from past years, since the grand march often draws a bigger crowd than the dance itself.

### How should chairs be set up for a grand march?
Set rows on both sides of the runway, facing in, with a photographer or video stand at one end. Leave a clear aisle at the back of the rows for anyone arriving late.

### Does the school already have chairs for the grand march?
Some schools have enough chairs or bleacher seating for a smaller turnout, but many gyms need rented chairs for the crowd a grand march draws. Ask the school what's already available before you order.

### Do I need separate chairs for the grand march and the prom dinner-dance?
Usually yes. The grand march happens at the school and needs its own seating, while the dinner-dance venue almost always provides its own banquet chairs as part of the room booking. Confirm what each venue includes before ordering.
