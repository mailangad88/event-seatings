**Quick answer**

Keep cold food at 41 degrees F or below. Use a cooler with a 2-to-1 ratio of ice to food by volume, pre-chill both, and set salads in bowls nested in larger bowls of ice. Replace food after 2 hours out of ice, or 1 hour when the air is above 90 degrees F. Plan about 1 pound of ice per guest for drinks and a cooler per 25 guests for food.

</div>

A Chicago July afternoon can turn potato salad into a problem in an hour. Cold food is harder outdoors than hot, because the sun, the grass and the open lid all work against you. Good coolers and a few habits make it simple. Here is the plan.

The drink figure follows the common rule of about 1 pound of ice per guest. Hot afternoons, long events and open-air parties call for more. Block ice melts slower than cubes, but cubes cool faster, so many caterers use both.

## Set the table so food stays cold

- **Nested bowls:** Put a serving bowl into a larger bowl or hotel pan filled with ice. Refresh the ice every 30 to 45 minutes.
- **Shade:** A tent, umbrella or tree. Shade can lower temperatures noticeably, and sun on a metal tray can heat food quickly.
- **Small batches:** Fill small serving bowls and refill from the cooler, rather than putting out everything at once.
- **Lids and covers:** Cover dishes between servings. A clear dome keeps flies off.
- **Elevated trays:** Tiered trays on ice work for shrimp and fruit.

## Which foods are the riskiest

Foods that need refrigeration are called time and temperature control foods. This includes dairy, eggs, cut melon, cooked rice and pasta, potato and macaroni salads, deli meat, sliced tomatoes, seafood and cut leafy greens. Whole fruit, bread and most baked goods do not. If you cannot keep potato salad cold, serve a vinaigrette-based salad instead.

## Beyond the cooler: other cold equipment

- **Refrigerated trucks and trailers:** For events of 200 or more guests, a caterer may bring their own. Ask what is included.
- **Ice bins and tubs:** For beverages and bottled drinks, a galvanized tub or a rolling cooler works. See [how many beverage dispensers you need](/blog/how-many-beverage-dispensers-do-i-need).
- **Dessert chillers:** Ice cream and whipped cream need extra attention; see [ice cream sundae bar setup](/blog/ice-cream-sundae-bar-setup).
- **Raw bar and sushi:** Need a drainage tray and constant ice replacement.
- **Dry ice:** Handle with gloves and ventilation, and never in a sealed container. Ask your caterer before using it.

## Chicago summer notes

Chicago can have long stretches of hot, humid weather in July and August, and heat index can pass 90 degrees F in the suburbs. Park district shelters and backyards often lack electrical hookups for refrigerators, so plan coolers. The sun at an outdoor wedding in June sets late, around 8:30 p.m., and the hot part of the day stretches. Put the food out later rather than earlier. For weather and seating tips, read [outdoor event chairs and weather in Chicago](/blog/topic/seasons-and-venues).

## Drainage and cleanup

Melting ice becomes water, and water on a rented linen leaves stains. Put trays or pans under every ice bowl, and run a drain hose or dump bucket in the back of house. Choose a spot for water disposal that does not run onto the lawn near guest chairs. See [trash, recycling and composting stations](/blog/trash-recycling-composting-stations-for-events).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I keep food cold at an outdoor party?

Pre-chill coolers and food, pack with ice above and below, and set serving bowls inside larger bowls of ice. Keep everything in shade and refresh ice every 30 to 45 minutes.

### How much ice do I need for a party?

About 1 pound per guest for drinks, plus extra for food coolers. Hot or long events need more, so buy up to a quarter extra.

### How long can food sit out in the heat?

Two hours, or one hour if the temperature is above 90 degrees F. Time adds up across the whole event, not each serving.

### What temperature should cold food be?

41 degrees F or below. Use a probe thermometer to check the center of the food, not the surface.

### Should I use block ice or cubes?

Both. Blocks last longer in the cooler, and cubes cool food and drinks faster. Put blocks at the bottom.

### Can I put potato salad on ice?

Yes, in a bowl nested in a larger bowl of ice. Swap in a fresh bowl from the cooler every hour.
