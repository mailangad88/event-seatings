## How much room you need

- **Seated dinner:** 10 to 12 square feet per guest.
- **Seated with buffet:** 8 to 10 if you rely on the walls.
- **Standing cocktail:** 6 to 8.
- **Lounge:** 10 or more with sofas.

A 20 x 20 foot room is 400 square feet, which is 20 seated guests at best, or 50 standing. Use the walls: long tables flat against the wall give you room in the center.

## Measure before you order

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Doors</strong> Measure width and height of every door the table passes.</li>
<li><strong>Stairs and elevators</strong> Rental tables with fixed legs are long.</li>
<li><strong>Turns</strong> A 6 ft table cannot turn a tight corner.</li>
<li><strong>Parking</strong> Where does the delivery truck stop?</li>
<li><strong>Building rules</strong> Many Chicago condos require a certificate of insurance and elevator reservations.</li>
</ol>

Folding tables with collapsible legs ride in elevators more easily. Ask your rental company about delivery in a condo building, and book the freight elevator early.

## Layout tips

- **Use the walls.** Put banquet tables against the wall and chairs on one side only.
- **Go small.** 30-inch rounds fit more places than 48-inch tables.
- **Think vertical.** Highboys use less floor than seated tables.
- **Stagger seating.** Mix 2 or 3 seat groups, so guests do not crowd.
- **Move furniture.** Take out sofas and coffee tables for the night.

See [table spacing and aisle clearance](/blog/table-spacing-and-aisle-clearance) for walkway guidance, and [how many cocktail tables](/blog/how-many-cocktail-tables-for-standing-reception) if you plan to stand.

## Chairs for small spaces

## Backyards and patios

Small Chicago backyards, often 25 x 100 feet, are narrow. Use long tables along a fence and keep the center for flow. Check your lot size, the grill, trees and the neighbor line. A tent may need space around it; ask your tent company.

## A plan for 12 guests in a city apartment

You live in a one-bedroom in a Chicago high-rise and want a dinner for 12. Your living and dining area is about 300 square feet, and the building has a freight elevator that you reserve.

1. Move out the sofa and coffee table the day before.
2. Rent one 8-foot banquet table and one 6-foot table, and join them in an L along a wall. That seats 12 with chairs on one side and the ends.
3. Add a 30-inch round at the end of the room for drinks.
4. Choose folding chairs that store flat in a closet or hallway after pickup.
5. Ask the rental company for delivery and pick-up windows that match the elevator reservation.

The dinner works because the table follows the wall, which keeps the center free for people to move. If you have more than 12, shift to a standing menu with cocktail tables.

## Questions to ask before you rent

- What are the table dimensions folded and open?
- Can delivery staff bring tables up the stairs, or do I need to meet them at the curb?
- Is there a minimum order or delivery fee for small orders?
- Can I get a late pickup so the party does not end early?
- Do you provide protective feet for wood floors?

Clear answers to these questions often matter more than the price.

## Frequently asked questions

### What tables work best in a small apartment?

30-inch rounds, 18-inch narrow banquets and cocktail tables. They fit through doors and tuck against walls.

### How many people fit in a 400-square-foot room?

About 20 seated at tables or 50 standing, using the rules of thumb above.

### Can rental tables fit in an elevator?

Many fold and fit. Ask for table dimensions and building rules.

### Do I need a certificate of insurance for a condo party?

Often yes, for deliveries in managed buildings. Ask your building manager.

### Should I rent tables or use my own?

Rent if you need more than your own tables can seat. Rentals add matching linens and folding legs.

### Are folding tables okay for small events?

Yes. See [folding vs heavy wood tables](/blog/folding-vs-heavy-wood-tables).
