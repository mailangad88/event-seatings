**Quick answer**

Plan a conference about six months out for 150 to 500 attendees. Months six to five cover goals, budget and venue. Months four to three are for speakers, sponsors and registration launch. Months two to one handle the agenda, food, signage and room setups. The final two weeks are for headcounts, speaker prep and a run of show. Smaller one-day events can compress this to 12 to 16 weeks.

</div>

A conference is several events stacked together: a registration experience, a series of sessions, meals, networking and often a sponsor expo. Each runs on a different timeline, which is why planners feel behind even when they are doing everything right. The fix is a plan that shows all the tracks in one place, with dates, so nothing silently falls behind.

## Lock the foundation first

The first month is about three decisions: purpose, budget and date. Purpose shapes the agenda and the speakers. The budget shapes the venue and the number of days. The date must avoid major holidays, competing industry events and local happenings that fill hotels. Once those are settled, you can request venue proposals with firm numbers.

Think about the sessions you want and the rooms they need. A main stage room, two or three breakout rooms and a networking space is a common mix. Each room has its own setup style, and the venue's capacity changes with every style. See our guide to [conference seating styles](/blog/conference-seating-styles-theater-classroom-banquet-u-shape) for how many people fit in theater, classroom and banquet rooms.

## Speakers, sponsors and registration

Speakers and sponsors take longer than people think. Invite headline speakers early, because their calendars fill fast, and give them a clear brief: topic, length, audience and tech needs. Sponsors need a package, a price and a deadline, which our [event sponsorship packages guide](/blog/event-sponsorship-packages-guide) explains.

Registration should launch about three to four months out, with a clear early-bird deadline. Collect dietary needs and accessibility requests in the form. For the check-in plan, see [registration and check-in setup](/blog/event-registration-and-check-in-setup). Promotion matters too, and our piece on [how to increase event attendance](/blog/how-to-increase-event-attendance) covers channels that tend to work.

## Food, rooms and the last month

In the last month, shift from big decisions to details: final agenda, signage, badges, speaker run-throughs, vendor schedules and room setups. Meals are the biggest logistical piece. Breaks need coffee stations placed to avoid bottlenecks, and lunch needs space and enough serving lines. Our [buffet line flow guide](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed) shows how many lines a crowd needs. Send final headcounts to the caterer two weeks out, and keep a list of last-minute changes.

## How to run the plan

<ol class="flow">
<li><strong>Set goals and budget.</strong> Know what the conference must achieve and what it can cost.</li>
<li><strong>Book the venue and date.</strong> Confirm capacity by room layout and hotel blocks.</li>
<li><strong>Recruit speakers and sponsors.</strong> Send briefs and packages with deadlines.</li>
<li><strong>Launch registration.</strong> Open the site with an early-bird deadline and dietary questions.</li>
<li><strong>Finalize the agenda and rooms.</strong> Match each session to a room setup.</li>
<li><strong>Order rentals and vendors.</strong> Chairs, tables, linens, stage, registration desks, lounge pieces.</li>
<li><strong>Run rehearsals.</strong> Walk through check-in, sessions and meals before day one.</li>
</ol>

## Chicago-area notes

Chicago conferences compete with citywide events for hotel rooms, so check the calendar before you pick a date. Many attendees fly into O'Hare or Midway, so a venue near transit makes travel easier. In winter, weather can delay flights, so build a flexible first morning. If you need an off-site reception, ask about transportation and permit requirements with your village or the city. Downtown venues often have strict load-in schedules and freight elevator reservations, and the [Chicago loading zone guide](/blog/chicago-event-delivery-parking-loading-zones) helps you plan deliveries. Suburban conference centers often offer easier parking but fewer nearby hotels.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far in advance should I plan a conference?

About six months is a common window for a few hundred attendees. Larger or multi-day events can need a year, while a one-day regional event can be planned in 12 to 16 weeks.

### When should conference registration open?

Many planners open registration three to four months before the event and set an early-bird deadline about a month later. Expect a surge in the final two weeks.

### What are the steps to plan a conference?

Set goals and budget, choose a date and venue, recruit speakers and sponsors, launch registration, finalize the agenda and rooms, order rentals and food, and run rehearsals before day one.

### How many breakout rooms do I need?

It depends on your agenda. A common setup is one main room and two or three breakout rooms, but you can use fewer if sessions run in sequence.

### How do I choose a conference date?

Avoid major holidays, competing industry events and local happenings that raise hotel rates. Check with your audience and with key speakers before you commit.

### How much food do I need for conference breaks?

A common rule is to plan coffee, water and snacks for every guest at each break, with several stations to avoid lines. Ask your caterer for quantity guidance based on your schedule.

### Should I plan a backup for speaker cancellations?

Yes. Keep a short list of alternates, and build some flexibility into the agenda so a panel or short talk can fill a gap.
