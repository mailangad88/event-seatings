**Quick answer**

Set the table in the order of use, working from the outside in: the first course uses the outermost fork, spoon or knife. Forks go on the left, knives and spoons on the right with the knife blade facing the plate, dessert utensils above the plate, and glasses at the upper right of the knife. Never set more than three of any utensil at the start; add others with the course that needs them.

</div>

A formal dinner can make guests nervous: five forks, three glasses, a spoon above the plate. The system behind it is simple, and once you know it, you can set any table in minutes. This guide explains the rule, the order and the shortcuts, with a layout for a four-course wedding dinner in a Chicago banquet hall. If you want the simpler version, see the [casual place setting diagram](/blog/casual-place-setting-diagram), and for spacing see [place setting spacing](/blog/place-setting-spacing-measurements).

## The outside-in rule

Utensils are placed in the order guests will use them. The first one is farthest from the plate, and the last is closest. If you serve salad, then fish, then the main, the salad fork is outermost on the left, then the fish fork, then the dinner fork.

## What goes where

<div class="table-wrap">

| Piece | Position | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Salad fork | Far left | Salad course |
| Fish fork | Middle left | Fish or second course |
| Dinner fork | Closest left | Main course |
| Soup spoon | Far right | Soup course |
| Fish or salad knife | Middle right | Second course |
| Dinner knife | Closest right | Main course, blade in |
| Dessert spoon and fork | Above the plate | Dessert, brought up when served |
| Bread plate and butter knife | Upper left | Bread |
| Water glass | Above dinner knife | Water |
| Wine glasses | To the right of water | Wine by course |

</div>

## The three-piece rule

Many etiquette guides advise no more than three forks, three knives or three spoons on the table at once. If a menu has five courses, bring the extra utensil out with its course. This prevents crowding and avoids guests picking the wrong piece. See our [china rental guide](/blog/china-rental-guide-what-pieces-exist) for the plates that go with each course.

## Glass order

Glasses go from the upper right of the knife in order of use, or in a diagonal line. Water sits nearest the knife. A white wine glass sits to its right, then a red wine glass, then a champagne flute if it is for the toast. The wine glass for the first course is farthest to the right on some layouts, so match your caterer's plan. See the [glassware and bar topic](/blog/topic/glassware-and-bar) for sizes.

<li><strong>List the courses</strong> with your caterer.</li>

<li><strong>Place utensils</strong> outside in, in order of use.</li>
<li><strong>Add dessert pieces</strong> above the plate or with the dessert.</li>
<li><strong>Place glasses</strong> by use from the knife outward.</li>
</ol>

## Clearing and resetting between courses

Servers clear each plate and its matching utensil after the course, leaving the next ones in place. If a guest uses the wrong fork, servers bring a replacement. A buffet or family-style dinner needs fewer pieces, as described in [dinnerware sizes for buffet, plated and family-style](/blog/dinnerware-sizes-buffet-plated-family-style).

## A shortcut for busy tables

On a crowded table, simplify. A good rule: set a salad fork, a dinner fork, a dinner knife and a spoon, and bring dessert pieces with the dessert. Use one wine glass and one water glass. Skip the bread plate; see [bread plates](/blog/bread-and-butter-plates-do-you-need). The table looks cleaner and servers have room. Because every extra piece also adds to the count, review [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest).

## The napkin

The napkin sits on the charger, left of the forks or in a glass. Guests place it on their lap when seated. A used napkin goes to the left of the plate when they leave the table temporarily. These conventions are widespread, and different hosts may follow slightly different customs.

## Who sets the table?

At a wedding, the caterer or venue sets tables, working from your floor plan and menu. Confirm which pieces you are providing and which they supply. For setting a table fast, see [how to set tables fast with a crew](/blog/how-to-set-tables-fast-with-a-crew).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which fork do I use first?

Use the fork farthest from the plate on the left for the first course. Work toward the plate as courses arrive.

### Where do dessert utensils go?

Above the plate, with the fork handle to the left and the spoon handle to the right. They can also be brought with dessert.

### How many forks should I set?

No more than three at the start. Bring extras with their courses.

### Where do the glasses go?

Above the knife, starting with water and moving right. Match them to the order of the courses or drinks.

### What if a guest uses the wrong fork?

The server brings the right one. It is not a problem, and a good host would not point it out.

### Is this the same everywhere?

The outside-in rule is widely used in the US. Details vary slightly by region and host.
