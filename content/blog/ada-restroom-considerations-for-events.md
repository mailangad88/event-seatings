**Quick answer**

An accessible portable restroom needs roughly 60 by 60 inches of interior turning space, a door opening of at least 32 inches, grab bars and a firm, level route to the door. Industry practice is about 5 percent of total units, or 1 accessible unit for every 20 standard units, with at least one at any public event. Confirm the exact requirements with your vendor and your municipality, since they differ by event type and location.

</div>

Accessibility is a requirement for many public events and a basic courtesy for private ones. A guest who uses a wheelchair, walker or cane needs a restroom they can enter, turn around in and use independently, and a path to get there. This guide summarizes common standards and practical steps, so you can ask the right questions. It is planning guidance, not legal advice. For legal requirements, check the Americans with Disabilities Act standards and your local authorities.

## What makes a restroom accessible

Rental industry resources that summarize ADA-based guidance list these typical features:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Feature | Typical requirement |
|---|---|
| Interior turning space | About 60 x 60 in |
| Door opening | At least 32 in clear (36 in preferred) |
| Rear grab bar | At least 36 in long |
| Side grab bar | At least 42 in long |
| Toilet seat height | 17 to 19 in |
| Toilet centerline from side wall | 16 to 18 in |
| Sink rim, if present | No higher than 34 in |
| Route to door | Firm, stable, level, no steps |

</div>

Treat these as a checklist to confirm with your vendor, not a substitute for the standards. Ask the vendor to confirm in writing that their accessible units meet ADA guidelines.

## How many accessible units

United Rentals and other providers describe an industry practice of at least 1 accessible restroom for every 20 portable restrooms, roughly 5 percent. That means a 40-unit festival has 2 accessible units, and a 3-unit wedding has 1. Public events usually need at least 1 regardless of size. Local rules may require more, so ask your village or event permit office.

## The route matters as much as the unit

A perfect restroom is useless if a guest cannot reach it. Plan the route:

1. **Surface.** Firm and stable, such as pavement, compacted gravel or boards over grass. Loose gravel and soft turf stop wheelchairs.
2. **Slope.** Level or very gentle. Avoid curbs and steps, or add a ramp.
3. **Width.** Wide enough for a wheelchair and a companion.
4. **Lighting.** A lit path for evening events.
5. **Distance.** Place accessible units close to the main tent and parking.
6. **Signs.** Mark the accessible unit and the route.

Place the accessible unit at the end of the row on a level pad, with room beside the door to maneuver.

## Seating and the rest of the event

Accessibility goes beyond restrooms. Think about accessible parking and drop-off, ramps into tents, clear aisles between tables, a few seats near the front for those who cannot stand long and spaces for wheelchairs at tables. Our guide on [how many chairs you need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) can help with seat counts. Use at least 36-inch aisles around tables, and more near the main route.

## Indoor venues and trailers

If you use indoor restrooms or a restroom trailer, ask for the venue or vendor's accessible features, including door width, grab bars, ramps and whether a wheelchair can enter. Many trailers offer an accessible stall or ramp, but not all. See [restroom trailer: when you need one](/blog/restroom-trailer-when-you-need-one).

## Chicago notes

Chicago winters bring snow and ice, which make routes unusable. If your event is in cold weather, plan clearing and salting. In summer storms, wet grass becomes a barrier, so use boards or mats. Public events in the city and suburbs may face additional accessibility requirements, so ask your permitting office. See [how many portable restrooms per guests](/blog/how-many-portable-restrooms-per-guests) for counts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to rent an ADA portable restroom?

For public events, you should, and at least one is the usual minimum. For private events, it is good practice and may be required by a venue or permit. Ask your vendor and municipality.

### How big is an ADA portable toilet?

Larger than a standard unit, with a turning space of about 60 by 60 inches inside and a door at least 32 inches wide. Ask your vendor for exact dimensions.

### How many accessible portable toilets do I need?

About 1 for every 20 standard units, with at least 1 at public events. A small wedding with 3 units needs 1.

### Can an accessible unit sit on grass?

Only with a firm, level path to it, such as boards or mats. Soft grass can stop a wheelchair.

### Are restroom trailers accessible?

Some include accessible stalls or ramps, others do not. Ask the vendor to confirm in writing.

### Who do I ask about local rules?

Your village or city permit office, county health department, and the venue. Rules differ by event type and location.
