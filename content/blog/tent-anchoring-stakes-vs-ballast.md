**Quick answer**

On grass and soil, tents are anchored with steel stakes driven into the ground, which resist wind far better than weights. On concrete, asphalt, pavers and decks, where stakes cannot go in, tents are held down with ballast: concrete blocks, steel plates or water barrels strapped to each leg. Ballast must be heavy enough for the wind rating, so the weight per leg can run into the hundreds of pounds. Never lean on a rule of thumb for this; ask your tent company for its anchoring plan in writing.

</div>

A tent is a big sail. The anchoring is what turns it from a weather problem into a safe place to eat dinner. Chicago-area weather is famous for sudden gusts off the lake and storms in the summer, so a tent anchored badly is a real hazard. This guide explains the two methods, when each applies and what to check on setup day. We do not rent tents, so use this as a list of questions for your tent company.

## Stakes: the standard on soil

Crews drive long steel stakes into the ground next to each leg or at the end of each guy line. Soil grips the stake, and that grip is what keeps the tent from lifting.

- **Strength:** Properly driven stakes hold much more than the same weight of ballast.
- **Utilities:** Every stake is a hole in the ground. You must have utilities located first. See [JULIE utility locates in Illinois](/blog/julie-utility-locate-tent-illinois).
- **Soil:** Very soft, sandy or saturated ground reduces holding power. After heavy rain, crews may add stakes or switch methods.
- **Sprinklers and invisible fences:** Mark irrigation heads and dog fences. Stakes can break them.

## Ballast: the answer for hard surfaces

On pavement, you cannot drive stakes, so weight holds the tent down. The usual choices:

- **Concrete blocks** strapped or chained to each leg.
- **Steel plates or water barrels** filled on site, which are lighter to bring in.
- **Weighted base plates** for pop-up canopies, rated for the canopy only.

## Why ballast weight matters

Wind lifts a tent and pushes it sideways at the same time. The ballast has to resist both. The bigger the tent and the more sidewalls it has, the more sail area it presents, and the more weight each leg needs. We will not give a number here because it depends on the tent model, size, sidewalls and the rating the company works to. Ask for the weight per leg in writing, and ask what wind speed that plan covers. Related reading: [tent wind ratings and weather safety](/blog/tent-wind-ratings-weather-safety).

Chicago's permit rules show how seriously this is treated: tents up to 600 square feet need anchorage details drawn by a responsible person, and larger tents need structural details and wind calculations from a licensed professional. See [Chicago tent permits](/blog/chicago-tent-permit).

## Surface protection

Heavy ballast and sharp base plates can scar pavers, stain driveways and crush edges. Ask the company about:

- Rubber pads or plywood under blocks.
- Whether water barrels will leak or leave stains.
- Where sprinkler lines, drains and septic covers sit.
- Hot asphalt in July and August, which dents under point loads.

See [tent on a driveway or parking lot](/blog/tent-on-driveway-or-parking-lot) and [tent and grass damage](/blog/tent-and-grass-damage).

## Seating on the anchored surface

Concrete surfaces are firm, and grass is not. [Garden folding chairs](/chairs/garden-folding) work on both, and slim [cross-back chairs](/chairs/cross-back) look right on a patio. If your tent is on soft ground, a floor or mats help chair feet; see [tent flooring and subfloors](/blog/tent-flooring-and-subfloors).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you put up a tent on concrete without stakes?

Yes, using ballast such as concrete blocks, steel plates or water barrels strapped to each leg. The weight per leg must be enough for the tent size, sidewalls and wind rating, so get the plan from your tent company.

### How much weight does a tent need on concrete?

It depends on the tent and the wind rating, and you should not guess. Ask the rental company for the weight per leg in writing and what wind speed it protects against.

### Are stakes better than weights?

On soil, yes. Stakes grip the ground and hold more than ballast of similar size. Weights are for places where stakes cannot go, like driveways and patios.

### Can I drive tent stakes into my lawn without calling anyone?

No. Contact JULIE (811) first so buried utilities are marked. In Illinois the request must be made at least two working days before digging.

### Do water barrels work as tent weights?

They can, when they are the right size and tied to the leg. They are lighter to transport and fill on site. Ask the company how many and what size they use.

### Will anchoring damage my lawn or driveway?

Stakes leave small holes that grass closes up, and ballast can mark soft surfaces. Ask about pads and protection, and take photos before setup.
