**Quick answer**

Open the coffee bar about 30 to 45 minutes before the end of the event and plan 1 cup for roughly half your guests: 50 cups for 100 guests, from one 50-cup urn or two airpots, plus a hot water urn for tea. Add syrups, milk options, cups with lids and a small sweet or salty snack. Keep it near the exit so it works as a send-off.

</div>

A late-night coffee bar is the last act of the evening: guests rest from the dance floor, warm up before a Chicago winter drive and grab something to take with them. It is also less expensive than a full dessert bar and needs little staff. These are planning rules of thumb; confirm counts with your caterer.

Add some decaf. Late at night, many guests prefer it. The general coffee counts in [coffee and tea service rental](/blog/coffee-and-tea-service-rental) apply here too, adjusted down because fewer people drink coffee after dinner and dessert.

## Menu: keep it small

- **Coffee, regular and decaf**
- **Hot tea:** black, herbal and decaf.
- **Hot chocolate** or cocoa, see [hot cocoa bar for winter](/blog/hot-cocoa-bar-winter-event).
- **Milks and creamers:** whole, oat and almond, plus sugar and sweetener.
- **Syrups:** vanilla, caramel and hazelnut.
- **A treat:** mini donuts, cookies, pretzels or pizza slices for salty snacks.

A signature touch, such as a spiked coffee, is possible but needs bartenders and licensing; see [Illinois alcohol rules for private events](/blog/illinois-alcohol-rules-private-events). Many couples keep the late-night bar non-alcoholic for the drive home.

## What to rent

1. **A 6-foot table** and a floor-length cloth. Use our [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart).
2. **Coffee urns or airpots,** with an outlet or an extension cord that is rated for them.
3. **Cups, sleeves, lids and stir sticks.** Paper cups with lids suit walking out the door.
4. **Cream and sugar sets, syrup bottles,** napkins and small plates.
5. **A tray or tiered stand** for snacks.
6. **A menu sign** and a trash bin.
7. **Chairs** if your coffee bar doubles as a lounge. A few [velvet dining chairs](/chairs/velvet-dining) make a cozy corner.

## Layout and flow

Place the station away from the dance floor speakers so people can talk, and near the exit so it becomes the send-off. Keep the line moving by putting cups at the start and snacks at the end, with trash at the exit. Allow 4 feet in front for the line. If your room is a ballroom or hall, ask the venue where the outlets are. For more layout thinking, read [bar placement in your floor plan](/blog/bar-placement-floor-plan).

## Staffing and cleanup

A late-night bar needs one person at most. A caterer's server, a venue attendant or a trusted friend can refill the urn, wipe the table and replace cups. Agree on who will do it before the day, since the original bar staff may already be packing up. Plan cleanup: coffee urns are hot and heavy, so ask whether the rental company wants them emptied and cooled or collected as they are, and where used grounds and liquids can go. Many venues do not allow pouring coffee into a bathroom sink, so ask.

## Winter and cold-weather notes

A Chicago winter wedding is the best use of a coffee bar: guests face a cold walk to a car or the L, and a warm cup makes the exit pleasant. Place a coat check or a warm lobby near the station. Add to your plan with ideas from [winter wedding seating](/blog/winter-wedding-seating). For summer evenings, add an iced coffee option with ice and cold brew from a dispenser.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should a late-night coffee bar open?

About 30 to 45 minutes before the event ends. This catches guests as the dancing slows down and works as a signal that the night is wrapping up.

### How much coffee do I need for 100 guests?

About 50 cups, or half the guest count, from one 50-cup urn. Add decaf and hot water for tea.

### Can I serve coffee and alcohol together?

Only if your venue and bartenders allow it. Spiked coffee needs a licensed bartender in most venues, so check rules first.

### What snacks go with a coffee bar?

Mini donuts, cookies, pretzels or small slices of pizza. Salty snacks are popular after drinks.

### Do I need a barista?

No. A self-serve urn setup is common. A barista or espresso cart adds cost and variety, so ask caterers for quotes.

### Do coffee urns need electricity?

Most do. Ask your venue about outlets near the station, or use insulated airpots filled in the kitchen.
