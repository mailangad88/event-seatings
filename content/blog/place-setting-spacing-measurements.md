**Quick answer**

Give each guest about 24 inches of table edge for a formal setting, or 20 inches if the table is tight. Set the charger or plate 1 inch from the table edge, flatware about 1 inch from the plate, and glasses at the top right of the knife. A 60-inch round holds 8 formal settings, and a 72-inch round holds 10. These are widely used rules of thumb, not strict laws.

</div>

Most place settings fail on spacing, not on style. Forks that crowd the plate, glasses that touch a neighbor's, a centerpiece that eats half the table. In a Chicago banquet hall, where 60 and 72-inch rounds are the norm, a few inches decide whether the table feels generous or cramped. This guide gives measurements you can tape out before the crew arrives. If you want the full formal layout in one picture, our article on the [casual place setting](/blog/casual-place-setting-diagram) shows the simpler version.

## The 24-inch rule

The rule of thumb is 24 inches of table edge per person for a formal seated dinner. At a 60-inch round, the circumference is about 188 inches, so 8 people get 23.6 inches each. A 72-inch round has a circumference near 226 inches, so 10 people get 22.6 inches. Both are fine, but 12 at a 72-inch round drops to 18.8 inches, which is tight for a three-course setup with multiple glasses.

## Piece by piece

<div class="table-wrap">

| Item | Where it goes | Spacing |
|---|---|---|
| Charger or dinner plate | Center of the seat | 1 in from the table edge |
| Forks | Left of the plate | About 1 in from the plate, 0.5 in apart |
| Knife | Right of the plate, blade in | About 1 in from the plate |
| Spoons | Right of the knife | Lined up with the knife |
| Bread plate | Above the forks | Level with the glasses |
| Water glass | Above the knife | Tip of the knife points to it |
| Wine glasses | Right of the water glass | Slightly angled, about 0.5 in apart |
| Napkin | On the plate or left of forks | Folded flat to avoid crowding |

</div>

A common method is to measure from the table edge: charger 1 inch in, flatware lined up with the bottom of the plate, glasses placed an inch above the knife. If you want a deeper look at courses, our guide on [multi-course place setting etiquette](/blog/multi-course-place-setting-etiquette) covers the order of forks and spoons.

## Centerpieces and the middle of the table

A 60-inch round with 8 formal settings leaves a center circle about 18 to 24 inches wide before the plates start. Keep centerpieces low (under 12 inches) or tall and narrow (over 24 inches tall, thin stem) so guests can see each other. Candles and bud vases fit inside the circle without touching the chargers.

## Chair spacing at the table

Allow 18 to 24 inches behind a seated guest so servers can pass. For floor plans and aisle width, our guide to [how many chairs do I need](/blog/how-many-chairs-do-i-need) shows how seating counts affect the room. A chair that sits 4 inches too close to the table will crowd the plate, so check that the chair's seat edge meets the plate's edge with room to pull in.

<li><strong>Mark the center</strong> of each chair position with a chair placed at the table.</li>

<li><strong>Step back and check</strong> that no piece touches a neighbor's.</li>
</ol>

## When the table is smaller than the plan

If a 60-inch round must seat 10, drop to a simpler setting: remove the bread plate, use one wine glass and place dessert utensils after the main course. You can also switch to a 72-inch round or a long table. Our tablecloth guide at [tablecloth size chart](/blog/tablecloth-size-chart) shows how the cloths change with table size.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space does each person need at a dinner table?

Each person needs about 24 inches of table edge for a formal dinner. For casual dinners, 20 inches is workable. Below that, plates and glasses start to overlap.

### How far should a plate be from the table edge?

Set the plate about 1 inch from the edge, so the rim does not hang over. This is a common convention and not a strict rule. Chargers follow the same rule.

### How many people fit at a 60-inch round table?

A 60-inch round holds 8 comfortably, and 10 if the setting is simple. The extra two guests lose roughly 4 inches each of table edge.

### How close should the fork be to the plate?

About 1 inch. Forks should sit parallel and evenly spaced so the setting looks tidy. Place them an inch from the edge of the plate, not touching.

### Where do the glasses go?

Place glasses above the knife, starting with the water glass and moving right. For two or three glasses, angle them slightly. Keep glasses a half inch apart.

### Does the napkin go on the plate or the left?

Either works. Place it on the charger for a formal look, or to the left of the forks for a clear plate. The left side adds width, so only use it if you have the room.
