**Quick answer**

Keep Mother's Day and Father's Day gatherings simple and personal. Choose a format (brunch, cookout or restaurant), set a guest list of 8 to 30, plan food that lets the guest of honor relax, seat everyone comfortably, and book restaurants or rentals early because both weekends are busy. A two to three hour gathering is about right.

</div>

These two holidays share a problem: the people being honored often end up doing the work. Mom is cooking for her own party or Dad is manning the grill. A good plan flips that, with food and setup handled by someone else, and gives the day a shape so it feels like a celebration and not just another family dinner.

## Pick the format

**Mother's Day brunch at home or in a backyard.** A buffet with quiche, fruit, pastries, a mimosa bar and flowers. Easy for 8 to 30 guests.

**Mother's Day restaurant brunch.** Book early, as restaurants fill up. Confirm the seating time, group size and menu. Expect set menus and time limits.

**Mother's Day tea or garden party.** A softer, quieter gathering with finger sandwiches, tea and flowers. See [garden party rentals and layout](/blog/garden-party-rentals-layout).

**Father's Day cookout.** Grill, sides, beer and lawn games. Perfect for a backyard or park pavilion.

**Father's Day day-out.** A ballgame, golf or fishing outing followed by a meal. See [golf outing rentals](/blog/golf-outing-rentals-chicago).

**Combined family gathering.** In some families one party covers both holidays, or celebrates grandparents together.

## Make the honoree the guest

The best gift is a day off from hosting. If the party is at their home, bring food, set up and clean up. If it is at yours, get them there with a seat of honor and a drink in hand. Ask what they want: a quiet brunch, a big party, time with grandkids or just a nap. Include short toasts or a card reading. A scrapbook, family photos or a recipe book makes a lovely gift.

## Invite the right people

Count the immediate family first, then extended family and friends. Check for conflicts with other holidays, graduations and travel. Many families host Mother's Day on Saturday or Sunday morning, and Father's Day on Sunday afternoon.

## Food ideas

For Mother's Day: quiche, frittata, French toast casserole, fruit platters, pastries, salads, and a mimosa or tea bar. For Father's Day: burgers, brats, ribs, grilled vegetables, potato salad, corn on the cob and a dessert such as pie or ice cream. See [backyard BBQ equipment for 100 guests](/blog/backyard-bbq-equipment-for-100-guests) for scaling and [grill rental for events](/blog/grill-rental-for-events). For drink amounts, see [how much ice for a party](/blog/how-much-ice-for-a-party).

## Seating and layout

Seat the honoree where they can see and talk to everyone, near the center of the table. Seat grandparents close to restrooms. Use a long table for a family-style feel, or rounds for easier conversation. See [long tables vs round tables](/blog/long-tables-vs-round-tables-wedding). Add a kids' table with games.

## Activities and keepsakes

Ideas include a family photo session, a recipe swap, a letter or toast from each child, a game of cornhole, a horseshoe tournament, a trivia game about the guest of honor and a slideshow. A card box or a memory jar collects notes. See [lawn games for weddings](/blog/lawn-games-for-weddings) for yard games.

## Weather plan

May and June in Chicago can be warm, cool or stormy. A tent protects the food and the honoree. See [tent rental setup timeline](/blog/tent-rental-setup-timeline).

## Day-of timeline

Arrive and mingle for 30 minutes, eat for an hour, share toasts and cards for 15 minutes, then relax with dessert and games.

## Gifts that fit a gathering

Group gifts often land better than a pile of small ones. A framed family photo, a memory book with a note from each person, a gift card for a favorite restaurant or a day of help around the house all fit the day. Some families draw names, and some give nothing but time. If the honoree prefers experiences, plan a short outing after the meal, such as a walk at a forest preserve or a visit to a garden. Keep gifts simple so the focus stays on the people.

## If the family is complicated

Not every family fits a single gathering. Some people are grieving, some are estranged and some have two homes to visit. Ask the honoree what would feel good, offer more than one option, and avoid making anyone explain themselves. A short brunch at one house and dinner at another can solve a scheduling problem without hurting anyone's feelings. Keep invitations warm and low pressure.

## Chicago-area notes

Mother's Day and Father's Day weekends are popular for Chicago-area restaurants, so book early. Backyard events in May can be cool and wet, and June brings heat and storms. A tent or canopy provides shade and rain cover. See [outdoor events in Chicago in May](/blog/outdoor-events-chicago-in-may) and [outdoor events in Chicago in June](/blog/outdoor-events-chicago-in-june). Park district pavilions fill up for these weekends, so check with your village or park district for reservation dates and rules about grills and alcohol. If you host in a city yard or on a patio, plan parking and tell neighbors in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I plan a Mother's Day brunch at home?

Pick a menu you can prep ahead, set a guest list, plan seating and flowers, and set up a mimosa or tea bar. Ask someone else to help with setup and cleanup so the honoree can relax.

### What is a good Father's Day gathering idea?

A backyard cookout with burgers, brats and lawn games is a classic. A ballgame, golf outing or fishing trip followed by a meal also works.

### How far ahead should I book a restaurant for Mother's Day?

Book as early as possible, often several weeks ahead, since it is one of the busiest days for restaurants.

### How long should a family gathering last?

Two to three hours. Longer for a cookout with games.

### What should I serve at a Mother's Day brunch?

Quiche, fruit, pastries, salads and a mimosa or tea bar. Offer non-alcoholic options.

### How many guests should I invite?

Eight to thirty is common, depending on your space and family.

### Do I need rentals for a family gathering?

If you have more than about 12 guests, you may need extra tables and chairs. Add a tent for shade or rain.
