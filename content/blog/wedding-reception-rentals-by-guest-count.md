**Quick answer**

For a seated wedding reception, rent one chair per guest plus 2 to 5 spares, one 60-inch round per 8 guests (or 72-inch per 10), and one floor-length cloth per table plus spares. A 50-guest reception needs about 7 guest tables, 100 needs about 13, 150 needs about 19 and 200 needs about 25 at 8 per table. Add support tables for the cake, gifts, DJ and bar, and plan roughly 10 to 12 square feet per guest plus a dance floor.

</div>

Once you have a guest count, the rest of the rental order is arithmetic. The math is simple but easy to get wrong when you juggle kids, vendors and plus-ones. This guide gives the counts at four common sizes, a room-size guide and the points where the order changes shape as the list grows. It uses standard industry rules of thumb, not prices.

Room sizes are rules of thumb: about 10 to 12 square feet per seated guest, plus a dance floor and space for the bar. Always check with your venue about its capacity.

## 50 guests: intimate and flexible

A 50-guest reception fits a restaurant buyout, a small hall or a 30 x 40 foot tent. Seven rounds of 8 or five of 10 is plenty, and long farm tables are popular at this size. You can afford a more distinct chair, such as a [velvet dining chair](/chairs/velvet-dining) or [wishbone](/chairs/wishbone), since the chair count is low. Check our [long tables vs round tables guide](/blog/long-tables-vs-round-tables-wedding) before choosing.

## 100 guests: the common middle

At 100 guests you need about 13 rounds of 8 or 10 of 10. A dance floor of about 12 x 12 feet is typical. This is the size at which a single bar line stretches to 15 minutes, so a second bar front or a signature drink helps. Consider a 72-inch round for part of the room to save floor space.

## 150 guests: the logistics step

Here the support tables multiply: cake, gifts, guest book, escort cards, DJ, dessert and coffee. Rent extra cloths and napkins for those. A 150-guest dinner usually needs two buffet lines or a plated service. The room is large enough that tent sidewalls, lighting and heat or cooling become real line items.

## 200 guests: plan like an event

At 200 guests, the counts grow and so do lead times. Ask about delivery windows, because a crew that places 200 chairs and 25 tables needs a clear loading route and enough hours. Plan for aisle width: 5 feet between rounds so servers can pass with trays. Seat the head table where it can be seen from every section.

## Dinnerware and glassware by count

For plated dinner, plan a dinner plate, salad plate, bread plate and dessert plate per guest; flatware for each course; and water, wine and champagne glasses. Guest counts of 150 or more benefit from a washing and pack plan. ## Where counts go wrong

The most common miss is the head table. Whether you use a long head table, a sweetheart table or a regular round, count its seats separately from the guest tables and remove those guests from your table math. The second miss is the children's table and vendor meals: photographers, bands and planners often eat on-site, and a vendor table needs chairs and a cloth. The third is table size changing late. If your venue cannot fit 19 rounds of 60 inches, you may need to swap to 72-inch rounds for part of the room, and the linen order changes with it.

## Chicago-specific details

Chicago ballrooms and Gold Coast or West Loop lofts often limit load-in to certain hours and require elevator reservations. Suburban banquet halls may already own tables and chairs; ask what's included. Outdoor receptions on the north shore or in the western suburbs may need a tent permit at larger sizes. Always ask your village or the venue directly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many tables do I need for 100 wedding guests?

About 13 round tables of 8 or 10 rounds of 10, plus 5 to 6 support tables. Use the 72-inch rounds if floor space is tight.

### How many chairs do I need for 150 guests?

150 chairs plus 2 to 5 spares for the reception. Count the head table or sweetheart table within that number.

### How big a room do I need for 200 guests?

Plan about 2,800 square feet for seated dinner and dancing, as a rule of thumb. A large tent or ballroom works; confirm the venue's fire capacity.

### Should I use 60-inch or 72-inch rounds?

Sixty-inch rounds seat 8 comfortably and feel more intimate. Seventy-two-inch rounds seat 10 and save floor space, though centerpieces and conversation get harder.

### How many extra chairs should I rent?

Two to five, depending on guest count. Spares cover late additions and family who bring a plus-one.

### Do I need to rent tables if the venue provides them?

Maybe not for guest tables, but ask about size, condition and quantity. Support tables and linens are often rented separately.
