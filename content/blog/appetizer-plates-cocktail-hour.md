**Quick answer**

Plan on 2 to 3 appetizer plates per guest for a cocktail hour with stations, and 1 to 2 if the food is all passed. A 6 to 7-inch plate is the right size for standing guests. Stack the plates near each food station, not in one spot, and add 10% for guests who set one down and grab another.

Cocktail hours are where plates disappear. Guests hold a drink in one hand and a plate in the other, set the plate on a ledge or a cocktail table, and come back with a clean one. If you host a cocktail hour in a Chicago hotel or a suburban reception hall, the plate count often surprises the host. This guide gives counts by style, sizes, and a station layout.

## Passed bites vs stations

Passed hors d'oeuvres are served on trays with napkins; guests do not need plates for small bites like mini crab cakes or skewers. Stations (cheese, carving, pasta, tacos) require plates. A mixed cocktail hour, with passed bites and one or two stations, needs plates for about two-thirds of the guests at a time. Ask the caterer how many stations there are and how much food will be served at each.

## Counting by style

| Passed bites plus 1 station | 1 to 2 | 6 to 7 in |
| Multiple stations, no dinner | 2 to 3 | 7 in or dinner size |
| Heavy hors d'oeuvres replacing dinner | 3 | 7 to 10 in |

</div>

If hors d'oeuvres replace dinner, see [how many plates per guest](/blog/how-many-plates-per-guest) for counts by event type.

## Size matters for standing guests

A 10-inch dinner plate is too large for a standing guest balancing a drink. A 6 to 7-inch plate holds two or three bites without wobbling. A salad plate in the 7 to 8-inch range is the largest that works. For cocktail hours with carving stations, ask the caterer for a 9-inch plate for the heavier food.

## Where to put the stacks

Put plates at the start of each station, at the right side of the food so right-handed guests pick up with their left hand. Keep the stacks small, about 20 to 30 plates, so the server can refill them. A full 100-plate stack is hard to pick from and tips easily on a rolling cart.

## Cocktail tables and ledges

Guests need a place to put plates. At least one cocktail table for every 8 to 10 guests is a common planning rule, and more helps. See our [table guides](/blog/topic/tables) for sizes. Without surfaces, guests hold plates for the whole hour, which reduces how much they eat and drink.

## Rental plates vs disposable

Cocktail hour is the most common time for disposable plates. A high-quality compostable plate works for a casual party, but a wedding cocktail hour in a ballroom usually looks better with china. Weigh the trade-offs in [compostable vs rented china](/blog/compostable-vs-rented-china). Some venues restrict china on terraces or lawns; check before ordering.

## Chicago specifics

Rooftop and riverside cocktail hours in Chicago can be windy, which affects plates and napkins. Use heavier plates or add small weights to napkins. In winter, cocktail hour often moves indoors near a coat check, which can limit where stations go. Ask your venue for the room dimensions and station sizes.

## Napkins, forks and trash

Appetizer plates need forks or picks for some foods, and a place to throw things away. Set small trash bins near stations and cocktail tables, and have staff clear them often. Cocktail napkins go next to the plates, not under them. At a Chicago hotel cocktail hour, ask whether the venue clears plates for you or whether your caterer provides that service, since it changes the number of people you need on the floor.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many appetizer plates per person?

Plan for 2 to 3 plates per person if you have food stations, and 1 to 2 if most food is passed. Guests often set down a plate and pick up a new one. Add a 10% cushion.

### What size appetizer plate is best?

A 6 to 7-inch plate is best for standing guests. It holds a few bites and is light enough to carry with a drink. A 10-inch dinner plate is too large.

### Do I need plates for passed hors d'oeuvres?

Not always. Small bites on napkins need no plate. Bites that drip or need a fork, such as mini pasta, should come with a small plate.

### Can I use cocktail napkins instead?

For finger foods, yes. Cocktail napkins are fine for bites that need no cutlery. Napkins and plates together cover most menus.

### Where should I put the plates?

Put a small stack at each station, on the guest's side. Keep extra stacks in a back cart for quick refills. Avoid one large stack in a corner.

### Who refills the plates?

The caterer or venue staff usually refill stations. Ask in advance, and add a note to your timeline so someone is responsible.
