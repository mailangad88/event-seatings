Northbrook is a residential northern suburb with generous lots, mature trees and plenty of homes that host graduation parties, anniversaries, holiday gatherings and weddings in the backyard. It also has banquet space and a park district. When you host at home, the yard becomes the venue, so the best plan starts with a map. This guide shows how to build one and size the rental order.

## Map the yard into four zones

Most backyard events fall apart not because of the chairs, but because of flow. People bunch at the bar, the buffet blocks the path and the dance area sits on a slope. Zoning prevents this.

## Sizing the tent

A common planning rule gives about 10 to 12 square feet per seated guest at rounds, and 15 or more when you add a buffet or dance floor inside the tent.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Guests | Seating type | Rough tent size |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | Seated at rounds | 20 x 30 ft |
| 80 | Seated at rounds | 30 x 40 ft |
| 80 | Seated plus dance floor | 40 x 40 ft |
| 120 | Seated plus buffet | 40 x 60 ft |

These are planning ranges. A tent company will confirm sizes, and the village will set permit rules. In the Chicago area, many villages require a permit or inspection for tents over a certain size; ask the Village of Northbrook what applies to you, and read our [permit guide](/blog/chicago-tent-permit) for questions to ask.

## HOA rules and neighbors

Many Northbrook neighborhoods are part of homeowner associations or have informal rules. Check for limits on tents, noise and parking. Tell your neighbors in advance, share an end time and give them a phone number. A friendly note often avoids a complaint. Also check where guests will park; narrow streets may fill fast, and some blocks have restrictions.

## Rain, heat and bugs

Plan for the weather you can predict. In summer, shade is the first need: a tent with open sides, a large umbrella or the shadow of a tree line. Use fans or cooling for peak afternoon hours, and keep water available. In fall, evenings drop quickly, so bring wraps or heaters (check clearances with the tent company). In spring, the ground is wet, so use flooring under the tent or choose a patio. Bug control matters at dusk near wooded lots: citronella candles or fans help, but keep open flames away from tent fabric, and ask the tent company about fire rules.

## Setting a realistic budget

Make a list of every category: chairs, tables, linens, tableware, glassware, tent, flooring, lighting, restrooms, staffing. Rank them by guest impact. Guests notice seating comfort, food and drinks, lighting and restrooms; they rarely notice napkin folds. Spend first on what guests touch. Then ask for a quote and compare itemized lines. If you must cut, cut the extras that serve few people.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big a tent do I need for 80 guests?

About 30 x 40 feet for a seated dinner at rounds, or 40 x 40 feet if you add a dance floor or buffet inside. Rules of thumb vary, so confirm with the tent company.

### Do I need a permit for a backyard tent in Northbrook?

Possibly. Many villages require permits for tents over a certain size, so ask the Village of Northbrook before you book.

### How many chairs for 80 guests?

About 84 chairs, which is 80 guests plus 5% spares. Add chairs for lounge areas if you plan one.

### Which chair is best on grass?

Wide-footed wood chairs such as the cross-back are stable on turf. Use foot pads when the ground is soft.

### Can I mix round and long tables?

Yes. Mix a few long tables for family with rounds for the rest. Keep the heights the same and use matching linens.

### What is JULIE?

JULIE is the Illinois utility locating service. Call or submit a request before digging or driving stakes, and confirm your tent company has done it.
