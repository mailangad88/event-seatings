The Feast of the Seven Fishes is an Italian-American Christmas Eve dinner. It is meatless, and the table fills up with seafood. Italian-American families across Chicago and the suburbs often cook for 15, 20 or more. The number of fish and the dishes vary by family. Some serve 7, some 9, 11 or 13. This guide is about the practical part: how to set the table, count plates and keep the kitchen moving. For Dec 24, 2026, that means a Thursday, so plan your rentals around a busy holiday week.

## What the dinner looks like

The menu changes by family and by region of Italy, but a typical spread includes baccalà (salt cod), fried smelts or calamari, shrimp, clams or mussels, an octopus or seafood salad, a pasta with a fish sauce and a baked or broiled whole fish. Some families add eel or other dishes tied to a village. Treat the list below as a pattern, not a rule.

## Plates per course

A rule of thumb is one plate per person per round if you clear, and fewer if people keep one plate and take seconds. Rental plates are easy to count. The question is how many you want to wash or stack in the sink.

| Keep one plate all night | 1 plus spares | 24 | Casual, family style |
| Clear after fried and pasta | 2 to 3 | 40 to 60 | Most family dinners |

Add a few spares in case of breakage. Small plates and a pasta bowl per guest, plus one dinner plate, is a common mix. Fish needs sharp little forks and a spot for bones. Put out a small bowl on the table for shells, and plenty of napkins. See [how many napkins per guest](/blog/how-many-napkins-per-guest).

## Serving ware

Seafood is messy and some of it is served in a shell, so the serving ware matters.

- **Platters**, long and oval, for whole fish and fried dishes. One per fish.
- **Large shallow bowls** for pasta and seafood salad.
- **Small bowls** for lemon wedges, sauces and tartar.
- **Serving spoons, forks and tongs**, one per dish, so flavors do not mix.
- **Warming trays or chafing dishes** for fried fish and pasta. See [chafing dishes](/blog/how-many-chafing-dishes-for-a-buffet) and [serving platters and bowls](/blog/serving-platters-and-bowls-rental).
- **Crushed ice and trays** for the cold dishes. For how much, see [how much ice for a party](/blog/how-much-ice-for-a-party).

## Table length and seating

Plan about 24 inches of table edge per person. For 12 guests, that is 12 feet, so one 8-foot table and one 6-foot table end to end. For 20 guests, it is 20 feet, so two 8-foot tables and a 6-foot table. For 30, it is 30 feet and usually means two rows or a room that can take a T shape. A 30-inch-wide table needs a center line of platters, so leave 18 to 20 inches in the middle.

Leave 36 inches behind each chair so people can pass. For a tight room, a narrower table layout helps. See [long-table layouts in a narrow room](/blog/long-table-layouts-in-a-narrow-room).

If the table is family style, put each fish near the person who knows it best. If the table is too crowded, set up a serving table by the wall and pass dishes in waves. See [buffet line flow](/blog/buffet-line-flow-and-serving-speed).

## Plan the kitchen in waves

The biggest problem is not the table, it is the kitchen. Fried fish loses its crunch in minutes. A home oven can hold one baked dish at a time.

1. Make cold dishes the day before and chill them.
2. Put out cold dishes first, and serve with bread and lemon.
3. Fry in batches, and keep the cooked fish on a rack over a tray in a warm oven, not in a covered pan.
4. Boil pasta while the fried dishes are on the table, and toss with the sauce at the last minute.
5. Bake the last fish while people eat the pasta.

Extra burners or an outdoor fryer may help. Check your local rules and any building rules about frying and open flame. For the equipment, see [catering equipment](/rentals/catering-equipment) and [event power needs](/blog/event-power-needs-checklist).

## Chicago notes

In December, sunset is around 4:20 to 4:30 pm, and many families start cooking at noon. Plan for coats and snow boots. Check the path for the delivery truck, and clear the walk. Narrow gangways and stairs in bungalows and two-flats can be hard on 8-foot tables, so measure first. See [holiday party at home rentals in Chicago](/blog/holiday-party-at-home-rentals-chicago).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many dishes are in the Feast of the Seven Fishes?

Seven is the name, and many families serve seven fish dishes. Some serve 9, 11 or 13. It varies by family and region of Italy, so there is no wrong number.

### How many plates do I need for a seafood dinner for 20?

If you clear between rounds, plan 2 to 3 plates per guest, so 40 to 60 plates. If guests keep one plate, 24 is plenty. Add a pasta bowl per guest.

### How long should the table be for 20 guests?

About 20 feet, at 24 inches of edge per person. That is two 8-foot tables plus one 6-foot table end to end. Leave 36 inches behind the chairs.

### How do I keep fried fish hot for a crowd?

Fry in batches and hold the cooked fish on a rack in a warm oven or on a warming tray. Do not cover it with foil or a lid, which traps steam. Serve it in waves instead of all at once.

### Can I serve the Feast of the Seven Fishes buffet style?

Yes. A buffet works well for 20 or more, with the cold dishes first and the baked fish last. Use warming trays and one serving utensil per dish.

### What time should dinner start on Christmas Eve?

Many families sit down in the late afternoon or evening. Sunset is around 4:20 to 4:30 pm in Chicago, and cooking often runs several hours before. Pick a time that gives you room to serve in waves.
